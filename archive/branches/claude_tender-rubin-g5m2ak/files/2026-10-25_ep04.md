# FICTION. Episode 4: A quarter, not a minority

**Sunday 25 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Kai · Vote day. IPS due in 13 days (Sat 7 Nov 09:00).**

---

Kai had his speech ready by 9 a.m. Fifty per cent stocks. He'd practised it on his nan.

Then he did what he'd been doing all week: he ran Jun's script himself, to find the hole. He found something worse.

**Kai:** (to the group, 9:40) I came here to argue for more stocks and the numbers just argued me out of it. Medians
in 2033: all bonds $200k, a quarter in stocks $205k, half $209k. Every extra quarter of stocks buys about five grand.
Jun, check me.

**Jun:** It's my script. You're right. MODEL, 21 October curve, payment-date basis. And here's the bit you didn't ask for.

He posted a new table at 9:52, on Friday's close.

**Jun:** The ladder on the holdings basis, 23 October: $300,992. Still $992 over. Yields went up a bit, so it's $1,207
cheaper than Thursday. Fine. But the floor at 25% stocks is $141,800, not $150,000.

**Nora:** Since when?

**Jun:** Since always. The $150k sat on Wednesday's curve and the wrong basis. Switch to the bonds we'd really hold and
it loses three grand. The five-year falling from 4.95 to 4.82 takes another thousand. The gap's only the last three
and a half. Even with no gap, 25% gives about $142,800. If the rule is "floor at least $150k", the answer today is 20%.

---

### Library, 2 p.m. The vote.

Tess spoke first. She'd printed IPS v2: both of Friday's fixes in, four weak phrases out.

**Tess:** Both fixes alone put us over 500 on the strict count once the heading counts. I cut "with different
natures", "by construction", "only ever ... never", and half a sentence about the reserve. Now it's 480 with the
heading, 477 without. And the case says Laura has
"been willing to take thoughtful risks", not "always taken". So it says that now.

**Nora:** And the judge?

She'd had their mock judge read v1 overnight: a portfolio manager's eye, scored against Wharton's five criteria.

**Nora:** Fours on strategy, client and presentation. Three on portfolio analysis. Weakest sentence: our certainty line.
It says the only risk is a U.S. default, and the next sentence names a second risk. So what? A judge stops trusting us
right there. And "a minority" isn't a policy. Give me a number.

**Kai:** "A minority" is forty-nine per cent wearing a hat. Here's my motion: the sail is one quarter of the 2028 money
*left after the ladder is complete*. We write "a quarter" and say why.

**Jun:** And if January's gap is big? Last week's bad case had a $25,600 gap.

**Kai:** Then three quarters and one quarter of a smaller pile. Floor and sail shrink together, so "a quarter" stays true.
If we fix the floor and make the sail take the whole hit, a big gap squeezes the sail to ten per cent and our sentence is
lying.

**Amara:** And Laura's half? Every extra stock dollar is half a dollar off the building and half in her drawer. On today's
prices, the middle case gives the building about $168k at 25% and about $147k at 50%, before the cap. Line 111 says a
meaningful contribution may make co-sponsors more willing to contribute. I'll take the bigger floor.

**Felix:** One check. "Thoughtful risks" is line 70. Line 72 asks for "an appropriate balance between pursuing growth and
protecting the capital required for her goals." Her risk already sits in her career, which is where the 2028 money
comes from. One sentence of ours can say that.

**Jun:** I'm voting no. Not on the quarter. On what we'll say in 2031. The floor won't be $150k. On today's prices it's
about $142k.

**Amara:** Then we say $142k, or whatever she owns that day. Principle 3 never promised $150k. It promised what she owns.

Jun thought about that for a long time, which for Jun was about four seconds.

**Jun:** Still no. Write my reason down.

Tess wrote it down.

Five to one. Kai looked almost disappointed to have won.

**Kai:** Bold. Brave. A quarter. My nan's furious.

**Nora:** Decision, owner, date. Read it back, Tess.
