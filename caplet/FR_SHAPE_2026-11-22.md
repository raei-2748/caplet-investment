# The Final Report's shape, for you to steer before Fri 27 Nov (Sun 22 Nov 2026)

**What it argues.** From January 2028, every dollar Laura has promised is paid by Treasuries held for her before she
promises it, and only money promised to no one takes market risk. The report tests that rule against each thing Wharton
asks us to recommend, and shows what it costs.

**What it opens on.** Section 2 opens with a test a judge can check against the holdings table: if every share in
Laura's plan were worth nothing, all ten payments would still be paid on their dates and the building would still get
$95,000. Then the reason (the order in which her money is spent), the finance name for this kind of plan, what pencil
and ink mean, and the table. We chose this over opening on Laura's career, which would repeat our IPS, or on the
conceit, because an image before its meaning loses readers.

**Section 2 (3 pages).** Each holding and why it is there; a short note lining our WInS portfolio up with the plan (it
was built before the operating reserve was sized); diversification across dates rather than issuers, with the
concentrations that remain named; and how carefully it was bought (the price check, the costs in dollars, the one-trader
rule after the mis-click).

**Section 3 (8 pages), in Wharton's order.**
- The commitment and the reserve: all ten payments are paid even if cash waiting earns nothing. The price is about
  $39,000 of the 2028 deposit, and if rates turn out as today's prices imply, it all comes back to her by 2042.
- The contribution and her flexibility: the building gets what the late-2032 Treasury fund pays, plus the half of the
  shares moved into it before 2031. The other half is hers, and we say what it can and cannot cover.
- The 2031 range: from today, the figure she will be able to state is uncertain (a top of at least $100,000 in about 91
  of 100 simulated futures, $115,000 in about 57). Once the shares are moved, what she states is already held.
- Laura's words: one figure ($115,000) in a signed pledge letter, with her whole commitment of about $615,000.
- Assumptions and what to watch: only a missed deposit or a Treasury default changes the plan, then three short lists.
- Team Reflection: three moments (the first plan set aside for how a writer is paid; the knife edge of buying the ten;
  Darren's "copout"), with one figure.

**Where the 'aha' moments fall (ten, about one a page).** In diversification: the deposit can arrive about
three-quarters smaller with every payment still met. In buying: on only one day in nineteen years did $300,000 buy all
ten. Opening Section 3: a market fall can shrink the next figure she states, never one already stated. The reserve:
bonds held to maturity still left one forecast, and the extra Treasuries remove it. The contribution: fewer shares would
give the building less, not more ($110,000 against $115,000). The range (two): by 2031 nothing is left to estimate, and
speaking a year early is the real risk. Laura's words: owning the money is why it will be paid, signing is why a funder
can count it. Assumptions: a share slump followed by a cost burst can catch her flexibility. Reflection: once the 2028
deposit is in, going "one step further" took a missed payment from about 1 in 31 to none.

**Where pencil and ink turn (one turn per part).** Defined at first use in Section 2; beside the table, every Treasury
row is ink and the world fund the only pencil; a click is ink; the one line that waited in pencil (the reserve); ink
only goes one way (the contribution); inked in January 2028, inked before the 2031 meetings (the range); none in
Laura's words, because the printed page shows only ink; rub out every pencil line (assumptions). The reflection is told
plainly.

**What it leaves out.** Most of the historical parallels (at most one in each part of Section 3, none in Laura's
words), outside "90% certain" standards, the WInS return, odds rarer than 1 in 100, and anything that repeats the IPS or
the Trading Notes.

Unless you say otherwise by Wed 25 Nov, we write to this shape.
