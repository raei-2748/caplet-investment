## 1. Check whether the hedge held, using our own WInS book

**The idea.** Wharton says the WInS return is not scored, so we use the book to test the plan instead. We take each locked rung and set its value against the cost of the payment it funds, priced on the Treasury curve on the same dates. The bonds' prices moved in October, and so did the cost of what they owe. The test is how far apart the two moved.

**Why judges care.** This is Portfolio Analysis: it combines numbers with judgement and tests whether the payments are reliable when markets move. It ties the Trading Notes, the IPS and the Final Report together using the team's real trades. A practitioner judge will recognise it as a hedge-effectiveness check, which few school entries attempt.

**Evidence.**
- `competition/wins/2026-10-23_snapshot.md`: bond profit about +$5,967, VT -$386, book +1.85%.
- `competition/official_market_data/daily-treasury-rates_2026-10-23.csv`: the 10-year fell from 5.28 on 7 Oct to 5.00 on 23 Oct, and the 20-year from 5.71 to 5.37.
- The five T-bonds will repay $96,000 of face value, with coupons of $4,027.50 a year. These amounts were fixed on the day of purchase.

**Where.** Final Report (Portfolio Analysis).

**Sample sentence.** "In October our bonds rose, but buying what Laura owes rose by nearly as much, and the small gap that remains comes from the 2032 floor, which is meant to mature sooner than the payments."

**Caveats.**
- The book is Laura's plan scaled to $300k. Compare each rung with the scaled payment it funds, not with ten $50k cheques.
- Keep the floor part of IBTM, and VT, off the main line or show them separately.
- Use a few real dates: the fills on 6-8 Oct, the 23 Oct export, and a 6 Nov export if Ray takes one. There is no daily WInS bond history, and whether WInS reprices bonds daily is unverified.
- Script the calculation so every figure has a source, and report the gap honestly.
- Use a small table to show that the T-bonds' payments are fixed. Say that the iBond payouts are close to fixed but not exactly fixed, and that reinvesting coupons is the plan's named weak point.
- Do not write "no richer, no poorer" until the calculation shows it.

## 2. Write the co-sponsor text as a table of Laura's commitments, each marked by status

**The idea.** Funders read capital projects from a table of where the money comes from. We draft Laura's part of the fundraising material the same way, with one row per commitment and a status word for each:
- **Bought:** her ten $50,000 operating payments, already in Treasuries, each maturing before the January it pays.
- **Owned:** the bottom of her building range, held since 2028.
- **Moved on naming day:** the top of the range.
- **Open:** whatever co-sponsors, grants and program fees are asked to fund.

A co-sponsor can see the two kinds of money without anyone explaining them.

**Why judges care.** Client Knowledge and Creativity & Presentation, applied to text the case requires ("draft part of Laura's fundraising materials", case p.3-4). It also puts the gift on top of an operating commitment that is already funded, which is the strongest true fact the plan has.

**Evidence.**
- Case p.3: co-sponsors, grants and program fees may fund any remaining facility cost.
- Case p.4: teams are not expected to estimate facility cost or the funding gap.
- The current IPS draft holds the plan facts.
- The claim that funders use this kind of table is unverified. Find one public template (for example a community-development lender's or a capital campaign's) before calling it standard.

**Where.** Final Report (co-sponsor section).

**Sample sentence.** "In the table we drafted for Laura's co-sponsors, three of her rows already read 'bought' or 'owned', and the only row that reads 'open' is the part they are being asked to fund."

**Caveats.**
- Leave out any facility total or funding gap (case p.4).
- Do not write "running costs paid for". Only her $500,000 commitment is funded, not the residency's running costs. Write "her operating commitment is fully funded".
- Do not write "mature each December". The T-bonds mature in February, May and November.
- Never add the ten payments to the gift to make a headline total, and never compare $500k paid out with $450k put in. That adds dollars from different years and reads as naive.
- One clause on currency can go here: the range is a promise in US dollars, the exchange rate changes what it buys in Taiwan, and her kept half absorbs that. Do not claim the co-sponsors are Taiwanese.

## 3. What certainty cost: three prices for the same ten payments

**The idea.** Laura's ten payments have three prices:
- $500,000 if you simply add them up.
- About $294-298k to buy them for certain as Treasuries (re-run on a dated curve).
- About $250k if you price them on the 7.0% a year we expect from world shares.

The roughly $45k difference is a return the plan refuses to count on before it has been earned. Our own model shows what counting on it would cost: the share-heavy plan missed a payment in 3.2% of paths, 13.7% if the 2028 deposit were only $75k, and 40.8% if it never came.

**Why judges care.** Portfolio Analysis and Investment Strategy. It puts a dollar figure on what "a high degree of certainty" cost and links that cost to the team's own modelled failure rates. That is evidence a judge can check, not just "bonds are safer".

**Evidence.**
- Present values at 7.0% ($250,386) and 7.5% ($239,062) of ten $50k payments from 2033 to 2042, valued at 1 Jan 2027, computed 3 Nov.
- Ladder cost: `keel/library/ips_numbers_2026-10/IPS_number_sheet.md`. The figure moves with the curve and was $298,087 on the 16 Oct curve.
- Miss rates: `keel/library/verified_2026-09-27/strategy_mc.py`.
- An optional public-pension parallel (Novy-Marx and Rauh, Journal of Finance 2011) is unverified until the paper itself is read.

**Where.** Final Report. For the IPS, at most one plain line with no numbers and no names: "We price each payment at what a Treasury costs today, not at what we hope shares will earn."

**Sample sentence.** "Priced on what we expect shares to earn, Laura's ten payments would look about $45,000 cheaper, but in our model the plan that relied on that saving missed a payment in about one path in thirty, so we paid the Treasury price instead."

**Caveats.**
- Pick one share assumption (7.0% from J.P. Morgan or the IPS's 7.5%) and use it everywhere.
- Call the gap an unearned return, not "risk".
- Say that the ladder still depends on reinvesting coupons.
- Drop any "public-pension mistake" framing and any GASB claim unless read from the primary source.

## 4. In the IPS, describe the shares in the team's own submitted words: "where we allow ourselves to be wrong"

**The idea.** Our submitted Trading Note says, in the team's own voice, that VT is where we allow ourselves to be wrong. The current IPS ends its shares paragraph with a flatter sentence ("We assume about 7.5% a year from them, but no payment relies on it"). Replace it with a lightly reworded form of the team's line that states the plan's central split: the shares can change the size of the gift, never whether a payment is made.

**Why judges care.** Creativity & Presentation and Articulation. Judges read the Trading Notes and the IPS together, and this makes them sound like the same people. It is also the most memorable plain-English statement of the plan.

**Evidence.**
- `keel/drafts/writing_room_2026-11-03/tna_as_submitted.txt` (reflections 1 and 3, verbatim).
- The flat sentence is in `keel/ips_rewrite/drafts/D35/ips_V55.txt`, shares paragraph.

**Where.** IPS. It replaces one sentence, so the word count stays about the same, and the 7.5% assumption moves to the Final Report.

**Sample sentence.** "The world fund is the one part of Laura's plan where we allow ourselves to be wrong, because if it falls her gift shrinks and no payment does."

**Caveats.**
- Do not use "the only holding that doesn't stand between her and a payment". The IPS's reinvestment paragraph uses her kept shares as the first backstop, so that wording would contradict it.
- Reword the line rather than copy it, and do not tag it "as we wrote".
- Do not present this as a cure for the AI-voice problem. The Trading Notes themselves scored high on a detector.

## 5. Take the plan's balance from the case's own words: "the capital required for her goals"

**The idea.** The case asks for a balance between growth and "protecting the capital required for her goals". It does not ask to protect all her capital. The plan protects what her goals require (the ten payments and the floor we set for her gift), and only what is left over goes into shares. So the small share slice is what is left after her goals are covered, not a percentage picked for taste.

**Why judges care.** Investment Strategy and Client Knowledge. It answers the objection a mock judge already raised: why so few shares? The answer comes from the brief the judges wrote.

**Evidence.** Case p.2, verbatim: "an appropriate balance between pursuing growth and protecting the capital required for her goals."

**Where.** Both.
- **IPS:** fold the case's words into the existing 2028 sentence at no extra word cost.
- **Final Report:** show that the share slice comes out under a tenth of her money only because of the interest rates when the ladder was bought, and how big it would have been at other starting rates.

**Sample sentence.** "What remains after the capital her goals require, roughly the interest the floor we set for her gift will earn, goes into one world index fund."

**Caveats.**
- Do not write "exactly". The floor is the team's choice, and the half of her shares that moves to Treasuries before 2031 and the reinvestment set-aside also protect capital.
- The phrase is already used in the team's research files, so present it as a sharper version of an existing line, not a new idea.

## 6. Overruns on arts buildings, and why Laura's range can only move before she names it

**The idea.** A University of Chicago study of new US arts buildings found that theatres ended on average about 92% over their first budget (museums about 46%, other performing-arts centres about 62%). Managers also chose when to announce budgets so that the public only saw about a 19% overrun. Laura's plan does the opposite in timing:
- Her payments are bought before the building's price is known.
- Overruns land first on her kept half and the co-sponsors' share, never on the ten payments.
- Her range is set aside before it is named, so it can only move before she announces it.

**Why judges care.** Client Knowledge from the funder's point of view. It is real evidence on how such projects go over budget. It also speaks to the case's question about inflation and facility costs, and gives the kept half a concrete job.

**Evidence.** Woronkowicz, Joynes, Frumkin et al., *Set in Stone* (University of Chicago Cultural Policy Center, 2012). The 92%, 46% and 62% figures and the "did not appear outrageous" passage (about 19% perceived overrun) were checked against the PDF on 3 Nov.

**Where.** Final Report.

**Sample sentence.** "In a study of US arts buildings, theatres ended on average about 92% over their first budget, which is why Laura's payments are bought before the building's price is known and why her range can only change before she names it, never after."

**Caveats.**
- Say "US sample" every time.
- The report calls its worries about operating costs anecdotal, and says many overruns came from features added on purpose.
- Do not claim the ladder keeps the residency able to run. It protects only her fixed $50k a year.
- Drop "most" and "the best study".

## 7. Draw the co-sponsor exhibit in Laura's own medium, a four-panel strip

**The idea.** The case calls Laura a graphic novelist and illustrator, and says her best-known work began as a comic. Next to the prose fundraising text, the students draw four panels themselves:
1. **2028:** the floor is bought.
2. **2031:** she names the range, and the promised half moves into Treasuries.
3. **A bad market:** the gift is still the floor and the payments are untouched.
4. **2033:** the gift is paid while the ladder keeps paying each January.

**Why judges care.** Creativity & Presentation and Client Knowledge. The exhibit is designed for this client's audience. Panel 3 makes the key point visually: a crash does not move the named bottom.

**Evidence.** Case p.1 ("bestselling graphic novelist, illustrator"; *The Wuhan I Know* is "a comic"; *Messy Roots* is a "graphic memoir"). Case p.3-4 (draft part of the fundraising materials).

**Where.** Final Report only (no charts are allowed in the IPS).

**Sample sentence.** "Because Laura tells hard stories in pictures, we drew the plan for her co-sponsors as four panels, and the third shows a market crash while the bottom of her range stays where it was."

**Caveats.**
- Keep it only if the Final Report instructions (out 9 Nov ET, Tue 10 Nov 01:00 Sydney) allow images, and only if the students draw it by hand and draw it well.
- Do not imitate her style.
- Every caption number needs a "we modelled" source in the text.

## 8. Laura's name earns her living, so her word to co-sponsors is protected like her money

**The idea.** The case says over-promising "could damage her credibility". It also lists her income as publishing advances, speaking and licensing, and names "continued business income" as one of the facility's funding sources. Our reading is that the trust a shrunken gift would spend is the same trust that brings in her income. That gives one more reason the bottom of her range is money she already owns.

**Why judges care.** Client Knowledge. It joins two lines on different pages of the case (p.2 on her income, p.3 on credibility) into one view of the whole person.

**Evidence.** Case p.2 (sources of the 2028 deposit), p.3 (the credibility warning and the facility's funding sources). The link between the two is our own inference and must be written as reasoning.

**Where.** Final Report (Client Knowledge), one sentence. In the IPS, at most a few words, and only if they replace existing ones.

**Sample sentence.** "Laura's income depends on people trusting her name, and the case lists that income as one of the facility's sources of money, so we treated her word to co-sponsors as something to protect in the same way as her money."

**Caveats.**
- Drop the link to *The Wuhan I Know* and misinformation. It reads as reaching.
- Do not echo the IPS's existing "hurt her twice" wording.
- Never present the link as a fact from the case.

## Strongest ideas rejected

- **Laura's offer is about $650,000, not $150,000:** it adds undiscounted dollars from different years and inflates the promise, the over-promising the case warns about. Its true part is the opening line of idea 2.
- **The product manager's rule (fixed launch date, flexible scope):** a thin link to one line of her biography, it adds no mechanism, and read loosely it suggests a named range could shrink.
- **Every rung matures before its January, so a late Treasury payment would not matter:** the iBond funds leave only about two weeks of cushion, the timing was never designed for this, and the 1979 details are unverified. At most it is one row in a Final Report risk table.
- **The world fund's foreign share as a Taiwan-dollar hedge:** worth a few hundred dollars once sized, it was not the reason VT was chosen, and saying it was would be invented history.
- **Certainty in the currency of the promise, and the Taiwan life-insurer mirror:** the case puts purchasing power outside her commitment on purpose, the insurer comparison points the wrong way, and it is unverified. The one useful clause goes into idea 2.
- **The fixed payment buys a fifth less by 2042:** already known, it aims at the wrong part of the case, and its 2.5% growth claim is wrong. The useful inflation angle (facility costs) is folded into idea 6.
- **Opening the Final Report with the Trading Notes question "how generous can the contribution be?":** the question was a statement of VT's job, not left open, and opening with it is a writing move rather than an idea. Use it as a light clause once the Final Report instructions arrive, with the same word for the gift used throughout.
- **Defending a "dull" WInS return:** +1.85% in about two weeks is not dull, and defending it raises an objection judges are told not to raise. Its fixed coupon table is folded into idea 1.
