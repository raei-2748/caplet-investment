## Part 1. Why Wharton runs this competition

- **It is outreach and education, not a trading contest.** It started in 2012 with a few Philadelphia-area teams. It now draws about 2,300 final reports from 79 countries (2025-26). The executive director has called the Global Youth Program Wharton's platform for talking to high school students about business. [Public: alumni.wharton.upenn.edu story (2019); globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu "A winning season" (Jan 2026)]
- **Returns stopped counting.** In 2015 ROI was "important" but not the main factor. Today the FAQ says WInS standings have "little to do with the final outcome". The 2026-27 Guide lists ranking, trade count and profit as things you are **not** judged on. [Public: 2015-16 guidebook PDF; FAQ page · Official: Competition Guide p.3]
- **What they say they reward:**
  - "client-focused objectives, a long-term-investing mindset" [Public: abrdn press release, Jan 2023]
  - the reasoning, assumptions and tradeoffs behind decisions [Official: Guide p.5]
  - "no single correct strategy" [Official: Guide p.2; IPS Instruction p.1]
- **Who judges:**
  - Wharton evaluators plus an Aberdeen professional panel pick the Top 50. [Public: "A winning season" article]
  - The public rules page says the Top 50 are chosen on the IPS and the Final Report. [Public: rules-roles page]
  - The 2026-27 materials also score the Trading Notes Analysis. [Official] Treat all three as scored.
- **This year's case is a new kind of problem.**
  - Past cases were a growth pot plus a small fixed slice: 20% liquid (2019-20), a $10k buy-in (2020-21), $5k a year for 10 years (2021-22). [Public: past case pages]
  - Laura's case is the first where the fixed payments ($500k) exceed the deposits ($450k). It is also the first to use the words "certainty" and "co-sponsor". [Earlier Caplet research, D7]

## Part 2. Portrait of a finalist or winning team

### What judges and clients have said, season by season [Public; URLs in the source list]

| Season | Winner | What was praised |
|---|---|---|
| 2019-20 | Tie: UWC South East Asia (Singapore) and Maclay School (US) | Strategy judged on strength and creativity, not profit; finance is about helping people reach their goals |
| 2020-21 | Marvin Ridge HS (US) | Monte Carlo simulations; the client would use the teams' recommendations himself |
| 2021-22 | Sailing to Success (Arizona) | "cut slides, cut words, cut minutes" (J.P. Morgan judge); understanding of the client "top-notch" |
| 2022-23 | Thomas Jefferson HS (US) | Judges deliberated over an hour; the winners had reached the previous year's finale without placing |
| 2023-24 | Bergen County Academies (US) | Efficient frontier and machine learning noticed; *but* judges said to consider how the client feels in a downturn and look at "this person's total financial picture"; "connect the client's objectives to an investment strategy" |
| 2024-25 | Deerfield Academy (US) | "clear thoughtfulness and reasoning behind all the decisions"; the teams understood people, not just numbers; "simple, elegant ideas" |
| 2025-26 | Stuyvesant HS (US) | No strategy detail published |

### The portrait [Inference from the above]

1. **Client first, visibly.** Every choice traces back to one client fact. Generic portfolios with a client paragraph on top don't place.
2. **One simple, central idea that everything else follows.** Judges repeatedly reward clarity and brevity over volume.
3. **Serious numbers, used to answer a client question.** Monte Carlo, optimisation and ML get noticed when they serve the client. The 2024 judges pushed back toward the human side: how will she feel in a bad year?
4. **Honest tradeoffs and what the team learned.** Winners explain what they gave up and why. A team that learned something shows it.
5. **Persistence and a pipeline.** The same schools keep reaching the finale (Bergen County, Thomas Jefferson, Stuyvesant), and one winning team had lost the year before. Finalists are mostly US teams. No Australian team made the Top 50 in 2024, 2025 or 2026; the last one found was Brisbane State High in 2022.

### What we could not find (gaps)
- **No winning written report is public.** Nor is any account of how the 2021-22 finalists funded Jordan's fixed $5k payments.
- **No full finale recording.** Only a few team-uploaded pitch videos turned up [Snippet].
- **No stated reason for the format changes.** That covers the IPS, the Trading Notes and dropping ROI.
- **Conflicting numbers:** 2025-26 registrations appear as both 5,400+ and 6,300+. The $500k virtual cash applied to 2025-26 only; ours is $300k [Official].

### What this means for Caplet [Inference]
- **Already fits:**
  - client-first design (payments bought before any risk)
  - a simple rule we can state ("never promise money you don't own")
  - quantitative work tied to the 2031 range question
- **Risk:** our plan looks like a pile of engineering (four dates, a cap rule, a half-of-fund rule). Judges reward simple, elegant ideas. The IPS should read as one idea plus a few rules, with the modelling saved for Final Report charts.
- **Add the human side:** one clause on how the plan behaves for Laura in a bad market year (the "bad-year rule"). The 2024 judges asked for exactly this.
- **Be realistic:** about 50 of 2,300 final reports reach the Top 50 (roughly 2%), and the pipeline is mostly US. Success = compliant, on time, every criterion addressed, and an outside reader can state our strategy in one sentence.

## Part 3. Self-scoresheet

Built from the Guide, IPS Instruction and Trading Note Instruction [Official]. The criterion detail comes from past-season teacher guides [Snippet, for 2026-27].
Each scorer fills this in **alone** before discussing. Scale: **0** missing · **1** present but generic · **2** clear and specific to Laura · **3** specific *and* the tradeoff is stated.

### Gates (pass/fail; failing = excluded from semifinal selection)
**IPS**
- [ ] Title page: official team name, names as "First Name, Last Initial", WInS username, all matching the roster
- [ ] Pitch ≤ 50 words · IPS ≤ 500 words · on pages 2–3 only
- [ ] Times New Roman 12pt, double-spaced, 1" margins, PDF ≤ 5 MB
- [ ] No graphics, charts, images, links, footnotes or citations

**Trading Notes Analysis**
- [ ] 3 notes from real WInS trades, copied exactly
- [ ] Each reflection ≤ 100 words and covers why / how it fits the strategy / how it serves the client

### 1. Investment Strategy
| Question | Score |
|---|---|
| After the pitch alone, can a reader state the central idea in one sentence? | |
| Does it show *how* growth, risk, liquidity, funding reliability and flexibility are balanced (not just list them)? | |
| Does it say how the allocation changes as 2033 approaches and as payments are made? | |
| Do the WInS trades visibly follow the strategy? | |

### 2. Client Knowledge & Objectives
| Question | Score |
|---|---|
| Could this text be pasted onto a different client? (Yes = score 0) | |
| Does it protect the operating commitment first and say why? | |
| Are "responsible facility contribution" and "flexibility" given a concrete meaning? | |
| Does it say how Laura experiences a bad market year? | |

### 3. Portfolio Analysis
| Question | Score |
|---|---|
| Does each holding have one stated job (future funding / risk management / growth / liquidity)? | |
| Are the numbers sound and at the right level (IPS = framework, not calculations)? | |
| Are the main risks (rates, the 2028 deposit, a stock fall) named, with the plan's response to each? | |

### 4. Articulation of Competition Experience
| Question | Score |
|---|---|
| Do the reflections show a decision that supported, tested or refined the strategy? | |
| Is there a real change or discovery the team can point to, with its reason? | |

### 5. Creativity & Presentation
| Question | Score |
|---|---|
| Is there one idea a generic team would not have? | |
| Is the voice our own: plain and confident, without jargon or false precision? | |
| Is uncertainty communicated credibly, with no overpromising? | |

### Judges' stated test (Guide p.5)
| Question | Score |
|---|---|
| For each major decision, are the reasoning, assumptions and tradeoffs all visible? | |

**How to use it:** Three or more scorers, including at least one outside reader who hasn't seen our research. Compare the scores and fix anything scored 0–1, or wherever scorers differ by 2 or more.
