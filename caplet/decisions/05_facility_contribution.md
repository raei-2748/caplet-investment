# D5: How much can Laura responsibly contribute to the facility, and how much flexibility should she keep?

**Status:** DECIDED · **Evidence:** `tables.md` → "Flexibility buffer", "Inflation and currency"; `results.json` → `grid[A, base]`, `fx_real`

## Question
After the reserve is set aside in 2033, what share of the remaining portfolio should go to the facility? How do we justify the part she keeps?

## Options
Contribute 100%, 90%, 80%, 70% or 60% of the 2033 surplus (a buffer of 0–40%).

## Evidence
**Surplus after the reserve, Strategy A, base case:** p10 $147k · p50 $209k · p90 $287k (nominal, start of 2033).

| Buffer kept | Facility p10 | p50 | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | $147k | $209k | $287k |
| 10% | $132k | $188k | $259k |
| **20%** | $118k | **$167k** | $230k |
| 30% | $103k | $146k | $201k |
| 40% | $88k | $126k | $172k |

Reasons for a buffer:
- **Currency:** the facility is paid in Taiwan dollars. Since 2000, the USD/TWD rate has moved between −11% and +26% over 6-year periods (5th–95th percentile, FRED DEXTAUS monthly). A 15% weaker dollar cuts the median contribution's purchasing power from $167k to the equivalent of $142k.
- **Inflation:** in 2027 money, the median $167k is worth about $144k (US breakeven ~2.5%). Taiwan CPI is 1.8–2.2% (CBC forecast / DGBAS May 2026). We assume construction costs rise 2.5% a year.
- **Cost uncertainty:** architects typically carry a **20% contingency at pre-design** and 5–8% during construction. The facility's cost isn't even estimated yet (the case says so).
- **Life flexibility:** the case says committing everything "could limit her financial flexibility as the project develops."

## Debate
- **Advocate (20% buffer):** One number covers three separate, research-backed risks: a plausible currency move (~15%), a pre-design cost contingency (~20%), and early-years operating overruns above the fixed $50k. At the median, the buffer is about $42k, roughly 0.8 years of operating payments.
- **Opponent (0–10%):** Co-sponsors respond to a big lead gift (List & Lucking-Reiley). Every dollar held back weakens the signal.
- **Advocate:** Credibility matters more than size. A gift Laura later has to cut is worse than a slightly smaller one she delivers. The buffer is also *available* for the residency if costs run over; it isn't gone.
- **Opponent 2 (30–40%):** Be even safer.
- **Judge:** 20%. The reasons are specific, each is sourced, and it is not an arbitrary round number. Present the full buffer table so judges see we considered the whole range.

## Decision
**Contribute 80% of the 2033 surplus (portfolio value minus the reserve's value). Keep 20% as Laura's flexibility buffer.**

Projected contribution, Strategy A:

| Case | p10 | p50 | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | $118k | **$167k** | $230k |
| Bear + inflation | $103k | $152k | $214k |
| Bull | $127k | $181k | $247k |
| Base, in 2027 dollars | $101k | $144k | $198k |

**The buffer** (median ≈ $42k):
- stays invested conservatively (for example, 50/50);
- is kept outside any promise made to co-sponsors;
- is first in line for facility cost overruns or residency needs Laura chooses to cover.

(The case does not ask us to size its composition, so we don't.)

## What would change our mind
- Co-sponsors require a larger lead share to proceed. Then consider a 10% buffer *only if* the 2031 committed minimum (D6) already covers most of it.
- A clear facility cost estimate appears, which would reduce the need for a pre-design contingency.

## Plain English for Laura
When the reserve is set aside in 2033, we suggest giving 80% of what's left to the building, most likely around $167k (somewhere between about $118k and $230k). The other 20% stays with you to cover things nobody can predict yet: currency moves, construction surprises, and the residency's early years. It's still there to help the project if it's needed; it just isn't promised in advance.
