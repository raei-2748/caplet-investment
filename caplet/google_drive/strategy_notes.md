## Tab: Tab 1

_Tab ID: `t.0`_

Laura is building a creative residency in Taiwan in 2033. Her portfolio cannot pay for all of it, so she will ask other people or groups to chip in. The case names grants, collaborators and others, and calls them co-sponsors.

**The rule that ties it together.** The range must not put the ten $50k operating payments at risk. Those are funded first, and only what's left counts toward the facility.

**Where our plan comes in.** This is the reason for the "bought floor". About $150k is locked into a Treasury by 2028, so the bottom of the range is money she already owns rather than a forecast. In 2031 she can tell co-sponsors "at least this much is already secured, and up to this much if markets go well". Because the floor is bought, a crash between 2031 and 2033 can't break her word.

The fundraising research supports the idea that a credible lead gift raises other people's giving. One field experiment found that raising the seed gift from 10% to 67% of the goal led to nearly six times more giving from others. But no source measures how much a secured floor specifically helps, so that part is our reasoning. It's also why the "timid plan" criticism is still open: a small floor is safe, but it may make a less impressive pitch.

## Tab: Project Management Techniques

_Tab ID: `t.j746r5ikv9s`_

Project Management Techniques Success

- **Pre-mortem** — Assume the project has failed spectacularly. Ask: “What caused it?” Then prevent those causes now.
- **Post-mortem / Retrospective** — After a project or milestone, analyse what worked, what failed, and what should change next time.
- **Red Teaming** — Assign someone to deliberately attack the plan: find flaws, counterarguments, loopholes, unrealistic assumptions.
- **Devil’s Advocate** — Similar to red teaming, but usually one person temporarily argues against the current proposal.
- **Backcasting** — Start with the desired final outcome and work backwards: “What must be true immediately before this? And before that?”
- **Scenario Planning** — Build several plausible futures, e.g. best case, base case, bad case, extreme case, and see whether the project survives each.
- **Assumption Mapping** — Write down everything the project assumes is true, then rank assumptions by **importance × uncertainty**. Test the dangerous ones first.
- **Kill Criteria / Exit Criteria** — Decide in advance what evidence would make you stop or pivot. Prevents sunk-cost thinking.
- **Stage-Gate Planning** — Divide the project into stages. At each gate, decide: continue, modify, pause, or kill.
- **FMEA — Failure Mode and Effects Analysis** — List possible failures and score each by severity, likelihood, and detectability. Focus on the worst combinations.
- **RAID Log** — Continuously track **Risks, Assumptions, Issues, and Dependencies**.
- **Risk Matrix** — Rank risks by probability × impact so you know what actually deserves attention.
- **Decision Tree** — Map important choices, possible outcomes, probabilities/costs, and subsequent decisions.
- **Decision Journal** — Record why you made major decisions and what you expected to happen. Later you can distinguish bad decisions from bad luck.
- **Dependency Mapping** — Identify what relies on what. Especially useful for finding single points of failure.
- **Critical Path Method** — Identify the sequence of tasks that determines the earliest possible completion date. Delays on this path delay everything.
- **Theory of Constraints** — Find the single biggest bottleneck in the system and concentrate resources there instead of optimising everything.
- **OODA Loop** — **Observe → Orient → Decide → Act**, then repeat rapidly. Good when circumstances change quickly.
- **PDCA** — **Plan → Do → Check → Act**. Similar idea but more focused on continuous improvement.
- **Build–Measure–Learn** — Build the smallest useful version, collect evidence, and change direction based on what you learn.
- **Minimum Viable Test** — Before building the entire project, run the cheapest experiment capable of disproving your idea.
- **Pilot / Prototype** — Test the project at small scale before committing fully.
- **Benchmarking** — Compare your plan against successful comparable projects and identify gaps.
- **Lessons-Learned Repository** — Keep failures, solutions, templates, and decisions from previous projects so future teams don't repeat mistakes.
