**Investment Policy Statement**

This policy governs Laura Gao’s $300,000 deposit in January 2027 and $150,000 deposit in January 2028 for her Taiwan creative residency, opening in 2033. Our primary objective is to fund ten $50,000 operating payments, each January from 2033 through 2042, with high certainty from these deposits alone. Each payment is matched to a Treasury bond or defined-maturity Treasury ETF maturing before its due date. Our secondary objective is to establish a minimum facility contribution Laura can communicate to co-sponsors, with potential growth above it.

Laura’s living expenses are funded separately, and no portfolio withdrawals are required before 2033, supporting a high capacity to bear risk. We assess her willingness to accept risk separately for each objective. Her statistical training and entrepreneurial experience suggest comfort with measured risk, but her tolerance for missed operating payments is low because artists would bear the consequences. For capital above the facility floor, we assess her tolerance as above average.

Root-and-Branch funds the “roots”—operating payments and the facility floor—before investing in the “branch,” a global equity index fund. The 2027 deposit purchases the latest maturities first, prioritising the payments most sensitive to interest-rate changes. Our late-September 2026 estimate puts the ladder’s cost at $292,000; if yields fall by more than 0.3 percentage points before January, the 2028 deposit completes it. Our analysis of month-end Treasury curves since 2000 indicates that only yields comparable to 2020 levels would leave any payment partially unfunded after both deposits. From the remaining 2028 capital, we purchase enough Treasuries maturing in late 2032 to repay that amount, establishing the facility floor. Capital left after this purchase funds the global index fund. Sovereign concentration in the roots is deliberate: we prioritise predictable dollar payments, while diversifying the growth allocation across thousands of companies.

Until 2033, we hold the portfolio without discretionary trading and reinvest income in the holding that generated it. We review the policy with Laura each January, acting only if her goals change or a holding becomes unavailable. An unavailable Treasury holding is replaced with one maturing earlier. From 2033, the operating reserve remains invested to maturity and declines as scheduled payments are made. Our WInS portfolio represents the allocation after both deposits, scaled down.

Equity-market declines may reduce the facility contribution above the floor. Funds backing the floor remain outside equities to protect Laura’s stated commitment. Inflation and Taiwan-dollar appreciation may raise facility costs even though the scheduled dollar payments remain unchanged. Laura’s retained equity allocation provides a contingency reserve against higher costs and co-sponsor shortfalls.

In 2031, Laura communicates a contribution range from the funded floor to that floor plus half the equity fund’s value. In 2033, she contributes the floor plus half the fund’s then-current value, capped at the upper bound communicated in 2031. This rule limits the contribution’s variability and supports a credible commitment to co-sponsors. The remaining equity capital stays with Laura as a financial cushion.
