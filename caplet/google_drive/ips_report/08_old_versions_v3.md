**Investment Policy Statement**

This policy governs the portfolio Laura Gao funds with $300,000 in January 2027 and $150,000 in January 2028 for the Taiwan creative residency she opens in 2033. Its first objective is to fund ten $50,000 operating payments, each January from 2033 through 2042, with high certainty and no outside money. High certainty means each payment is matched by a Treasury ETF or bond maturing before it, so once bought it depends mainly on the US government paying its debts. The second objective is a responsible facility contribution that leaves Laura flexibility.

Laura, a statistics graduate and entrepreneur, takes thoughtful risks, but her capacity for risk differs by goal. Her tolerance for a missed payment is low, because artists depend on that money. For facility money above a floor she can promise co-sponsors, her tolerance is above average. Her living costs are met elsewhere, so the portfolio needs no liquidity before 2033.

Our approach, Root-and-Branch, secures the roots, the payments and a facility floor, before anything is invested in the branch, a return-seeking fund. The 2027 deposit buys the payments latest first, locking the most rate-sensitive ones at today's yields. At late-September 2026 yields they cost about $292,000; if yields fall more than 0.3 percentage points before January, the 2028 deposit completes them. We priced the payments on every month-end Treasury curve since 2000: only 2020-level yields would leave even part of one unfunded after both deposits. From the 2028 remainder, we buy just enough Treasuries maturing in late 2032 to repay the whole remainder; that is Laura's facility floor. The rest forms the branch, a global index fund of thousands of companies. We diversify where we take risk; the roots rely on one issuer deliberately, because no dollar asset is safer.

Until 2033 the portfolio is held, not traded, with income reinvested in the holding that paid it. Each January we review it with Laura, acting only if a holding becomes unavailable, when we substitute a Treasury maturing earlier, or if her goals change. In 2033 the roots become the operating reserve, held to maturity and shrinking as each payment is made. Our WInS portfolio shows this allocation after both deposits, scaled down.

Markets can shrink the facility contribution, but never below the floor. Moving floor money into equities would raise the expected contribution but could break a number Laura has already quoted, so we do not. Inflation or a stronger Taiwan dollar leaves the payments unchanged but raises facility costs; the equity half Laura keeps is her defense.

In 2031 Laura tells co-sponsors a range from the floor to the floor plus half the equity fund's value then. In 2033 she contributes the floor plus half the fund's value, capped at that top, so she cannot miss the range. A range certain to hold protects her credibility better than a narrow one she might miss. The other half stays with Laura as a cushion if costs rise or co-sponsors fall short.
