**Investment Policy Statement**

This policy governs the portfolio Laura Gao funds with $300,000 in January 2027 and $150,000 in January 2028 for the Taiwan creative residency she opens in 2033. Its first objective is to fund ten $50,000 operating payments, each January from 2033 through 2042, with high certainty from these two deposits alone. We match each payment with a Treasury bond or defined-maturity Treasury ETF maturing before it, so once bought, each payment depends mainly on the US government paying its debts. The second objective is a facility contribution with a floor Laura can promise co-sponsors and growth above it.

Since other income covers her living costs and the portfolio requires no withdrawals before 2033, Laura's ability to bear risk beyond the payments is high. Her willingness depends on the objective. As a statistics graduate and entrepreneur she accepts measured risk, but her tolerance for a missed payment is low, since the artists would bear the loss. Above the facility floor, we judge her tolerance above average.

Our approach, Root-and-Branch, secures the roots, the payments and a facility floor, before investing in the branch. The 2027 deposit buys the payments latest first, given that the most distant ones are the most rate-sensitive. At late-September 2026 yields they cost about $292,000; if falling yields push that above $300,000 before January, the 2028 deposit completes them. From the 2028 remainder we buy a 2032 Treasury ETF sized to return that remainder, which backs Laura's facility floor. The rest forms the branch, a global index fund of thousands of companies. Relying on one issuer for the roots may look like a weakness, but we accept it, since no dollar asset is safer.

Until 2033 the portfolio is held without trading, with income reinvested in the holding that paid it. Each January we review it with Laura, acting only if her goals change or a holding becomes unavailable, when we substitute a Treasury maturing earlier. In 2033 the payment Treasuries become the operating reserve, shrinking as each payment is made. Any shortfall expected in 2033 comes first from the 2027 leftover, then the half Laura keeps, then the contribution above the floor. Our WInS portfolio shows this allocation, scaled down.

Markets can shrink the facility contribution, but never below the floor. Moving floor money into equities would raise the expected contribution, but we do not, as it could break a number Laura has quoted. Inflation or a stronger Taiwan dollar raises facility costs, and the equity half Laura keeps is her defense.

In 2031 Laura tells co-sponsors a range from the floor, quoted rounded down after any expected shortfall, to the floor plus half the equity fund's value then. In 2033 she contributes the floor plus half the fund's value, capped at that top, so she cannot miss the range. We prefer a range built to hold, since missing a narrower one would harm her credibility more. The other half stays with Laura as a cushion if costs rise or co-sponsors fall short.
