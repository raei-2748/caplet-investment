**Gaps, most important first (against criteria)**

- **The "zero-coupon Treasuries" claim doesn't match WInS.** WInS has no zero-coupon bonds, and your floor holding, IBTM, pays monthly income. The Final Report has to "evaluate implementation" against this IPS, so a judge could point to the difference. Your earlier draft said "Treasuries maturing in late 2032", which is accurate. Put that back or say something equivalent.
- **Diversification is in the rubric and you barely cover it.** The PDF dropped "of thousands of companies" from the equity sentence. It also never explains why holding only one issuer (the US government) is right for the payments: no credit risk, and the payments are in dollars. One sentence would cover both points.
- **Changing time horizons and governance are thin.** The IPS says nothing about:
  - what the team does between 2028 and 2031 (nothing, on purpose? say so)
  - who reviews the plan, how often, and what would trigger a change, such as Laura's situation changing, a co-sponsor pulling out, or a holding becoming unavailable (IBTR is a live example)
  - what happens to Laura's retained half after 2033
- A "review and triggers" clause is standard in an IPS. It also gives you material for the competition-experience part of the Final Report.
- **Laura as a person.** The profile describes a Wharton statistics graduate, a storyteller whose work is about belonging, and a residency where artists, writers and educators live and work together. The IPS mentions "artists rely on that money" once. Linking one design choice to who she is would help "earn her confidence." For example: why a numerate entrepreneur would accept dull bonds for the payments but want equities on top.
- **The range rule needs one line of defence.** By design, your range is certain to contain the contribution, from the floor up to floor plus half the stock fund. A sharp judge will ask whether such a wide range is useful to co-sponsors. You locked this design on purpose, since it has better outcomes in bad years than the 2031 lock-in plan. Spend about 10 words on why a guaranteed range beats a narrow one that might be missed.
- **Small fixes:**
  - "latest payments first" has no reason given, and readers will stop at it.
  - "unlike immunization" spends words on a concept you don't use. Cutting that clause frees about 9 words.
  - Currency: the facility's costs are in Taiwan and the portfolio is in dollars. It's optional, but it would sit well next to the inflation sentence.
  - The pitch has 3 spare words. Consider adding the reason, the artists' certainty, rather than only the mechanics.
