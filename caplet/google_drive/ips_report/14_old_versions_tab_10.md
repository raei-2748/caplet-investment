This policy (the "Policy") governs the portfolio (the "Portfolio") that Laura Gao funds with $300,000 in January 2027 and $150,000 in January 2028 for the Taiwan creative residency she opens in 2033. Its first objective is to fund ten $50,000 operating payments, one each January from 2033 through 2042, with high certainty and from these two deposits alone. Each payment is matched by a Treasury bond or defined-maturity Treasury ETF maturing before it, so that once bought, it depends mainly on the US government paying its debts. The second objective is a facility contribution with a floor Laura can promise co-sponsors and growth above it that remains hers to direct.

Since other income covers her living costs and the Portfolio requires no withdrawals before 2033, Laura's ability to bear risk is "above average." Her willingness depends on the goal. As a statistics graduate and entrepreneur she accepts measured risk where the consequences are her own, but where they would fall on artists, as a missed payment would, her willingness is "low." Above the facility floor it is "above average," and the Policy therefore sets risk separately for each objective.

Root-and-Branch secures the roots, meaning the payments and a facility floor, before a dollar reaches the branch, which is a return-seeking fund. We buy the payments with the 2027 deposit, latest first, given that the most distant ones are the most rate-sensitive and are best locked at today's yields. At late-September 2026 yields they cost about $292,000, and should yields fall more than 0.3 percentage points before January, the 2028 deposit completes them. We priced the payments on every month-end Treasury curve since 2000. Only at 2020-level yields would even part of one go unfunded after both deposits. From the 2028 remainder we buy just enough Treasuries maturing in late 2032 to repay that whole remainder, and this becomes Laura's facility floor. What is left forms the branch, a global index fund holding thousands of companies. We diversify where we take risk. The roots rely on one issuer by design, since no dollar asset is safer.

Until 2033 the Portfolio shall be held without trading, with income reinvested in the holding that paid it. Each January we review it with Laura, and we act in only two cases. If a holding becomes unavailable, we substitute a Treasury maturing earlier, and if her goals change, we revise the Policy with her. In 2033 the roots become the operating reserve, which is held to maturity and shrinks with each payment. Our WInS portfolio shows this allocation after both deposits, scaled down.

Markets can shrink the facility contribution, but never below the floor. Moving floor money into equities would raise the expected contribution, yet it could break a number Laura has already quoted, so we do not permit it. Inflation and a stronger Taiwan dollar leave the payments untouched while raising facility costs, and the equity half Laura keeps is her defense against both.

In 2031 Laura tells co-sponsors a range running from the floor to the floor plus half the equity fund's value at that date. In 2033 she contributes the floor plus half the fund's value, capped at the top of that range, so she cannot miss it. A range certain to hold protects her credibility better than a narrower one she might miss. The other half stays with Laura as a cushion, should costs rise or co-sponsors fall short.
