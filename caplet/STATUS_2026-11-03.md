## The five best ideas (what a judge should remember)
1. **Buy the promises, don't invest for them.** Laura's ten $50,000 payments are bought in 2027 with Treasuries that
   mature one before each January. Share prices can't touch them.
2. **Her job is already a share-market bet.** Her 2028 deposit comes from book advances, speaking fees and licensing,
   which dry up in the same downturns that crash shares. So her portfolio shouldn't bet on shares a second time.
3. **A home-made "capital-protected note".** Banks sell products that guarantee your money back and pay you a bit of
   market upside. We do the same thing without the bank: Treasuries guarantee the building gift's minimum, and only
   the interest those Treasuries would earn goes into shares. Unlike the bank's option, shares can't expire worthless.
4. **Lock the number before you say it.** In 2031 Laura tells co-sponsors a range. Just before she does, half her
   shares move into Treasuries, so every dollar she offers is already in bonds and can't be halved by a crash.
5. **We name our own weak spot.** If interest rates fall and stay low, the interest the bonds pay gets reinvested at
   worse rates and the ladder ends up a bit short. We price that before 2031 and cover it from her spare shares first.
   Judges trust a plan that says where it can break.

Plus the name: **Caplet XR**, like an extended-release tablet: it releases one payment a year and only works if
nobody "crushes" it by selling early.
