# D6: What should Laura tell co-sponsors in 2031, and how confident can she be?

**Status:** DECIDED · **Evidence:** `tables.md` → "2031 announce-then-protect (Strategy A)"; `results.json` → `announce.A`; Chart 04

## Question
In 2031, two years before the facility contribution, Laura must give co-sponsors a **dollar range** and a **confidence level**, and explain how good and bad markets could affect it. The range must not put the operating commitment at risk.

## Options
1. **Wide range, no action.** Announce the 10th–90th percentile of the projected 2033 contribution and leave the portfolio as it is.
2. **Announce then protect.** In 2031, move a share *p* of the surplus into Treasuries maturing Dec 2032 (e.g., iBonds IBTM, or 2-year notes). The protected part becomes a fixed amount; only the rest is still exposed to markets.
3. **Single number.** Lock 100% of the surplus and announce one exact figure.

## Evidence
Strategy A, median 2031 outcome, team's base-case assumptions. The range = 80% of (protected surplus + unprotected surplus × 10th/90th percentile of 2-year growth).

| Share locked | Committed minimum (can't be missed) | 80% range | Width | Expected contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | $0k | $136k – $205k | 41% | $171k |
| 50% | $81k | $148k – $183k | 21% | $167k |
| **70%** | **$113k** | **$153k – $174k** | **13%** | **$165k** |
| 90% | $145k | $159k – $165k | 4% | $163k |
| 100% | $161k | exactly $161k | 0% | $161k |

**How reliable the 70% rule is:**
- **Committed minimum:** never missed in any simulated path (bear, base or bull, both correlation regimes).
- **Two-tier statement "at least committed, at most high end":** holds 90% of the time (base), 92% (bear + inflation), and 72% in the historical replay. In history the misses are *upside* surprises: US returns 1928–2025 beat our conservative base case.
- **Delivered at or above the low end of the range:** 90% (base), 84% (bear + inflation), 91% (history).
- **Cost of protection:** locking 70% lowers the expected contribution by about $6k (from $171k to $165k). That is the price of a narrower, more credible promise.
- **Protects the operating commitment:** under A the reserve has been fully hedged since 2027, so the 2031 promise uses *only* surplus money.

**Research:**
- **List & Lucking-Reiley (2002, JPE):** raising the visible "seed" gift from 10% to 67% of a capital campaign's goal produced a *nearly sixfold* increase in contributions.
- **Vesterlund (2003); Andreoni (1998):** early, visible commitments signal the project's quality to later donors.
- **Krasteva & Saboury (2021):** seed money is most valuable when later donors know little about the project. That fits a new residency approaching Taiwanese co-sponsors.
- **Kitces:** clients understand concrete dollar amounts and rules (guardrails) better than probabilities.

## Debate
- **Advocate (option 2, lock 70%):** A firm committed minimum is exactly the signal the fundraising research says matters. The 13% range is narrow enough to be taken seriously. Laura keeps some upside, and the cost is about $6k in expectation.
- **Opponent (option 3, single number):** Why not lock everything and remove uncertainty completely? It is simpler, and $161k exactly is the most credible statement possible.
- **Advocate:** The case explicitly asks for a *range* and a *confidence level*. A single number also removes any share in 2031–33 market gains, and it would ignore the case's wish that she can explain good and bad outcomes. We'll mention option 3 as a possible choice if co-sponsors need an exact figure.
- **Opponent 2 (option 1, wide range):** Keep all the upside, because markets have historically beaten the base case (history median facility $206k vs $167k simulated).
- **Advocate:** A 41% wide range with no floor is exactly the "promise more than she can deliver" risk the case warns about. In bear + inflation markets it misses its own low end 16% of the time.
- **Judge:** Option 2 at 70%. Present it as a two-tier statement: *committed minimum* (certain) + *expected range* (80% confidence). Show the table so judges see the trade-off, and name option 3 as a fallback.

## Decision
**At the start of 2031:**
1. Confirm the reserve ladder is intact (it is under A).
2. Calculate the surplus: portfolio value minus the reserve's market value.
3. Move 70% of the surplus into Treasuries maturing Dec 2032 (IBTM or 2-year notes).
4. Tell co-sponsors: **"Laura has already set aside at least $[80% of the locked amount, grown to 2033]. She expects her total 2033 contribution to be between $[L] and $[H], and we are about 80% confident it will fall in this range."** On today's median projection: *at least $113k; expected $153k–$174k.*
5. Explain outcomes both ways: if markets fall 2031–33, the contribution drifts toward the committed minimum and never below it. If they rise, it can exceed the range, and Laura may choose to give more or keep the extra in her buffer.

**What we can say *today* (2026) about the 2031 announcement:** under the base case, the committed minimum is most likely around $113k, and in a bad 2027–31 (10th percentile) the low end of the range would be around $117k. In the bear case, the median committed minimum is $106k and the median range is $145k–$164k.

## What would change our mind
- Co-sponsors or Taiwanese partners require a fixed pledge amount → option 3.
- The Final Report instructions (9 Nov) specify a confidence level (e.g., 90%) → widen the range using the model's quantiles.

## Plain English for Laura
Two years before the building money is due, we'll move most of what you've earned for it into safe bonds that mature right on time. That lets you tell co-sponsors a guaranteed minimum plus a narrow likely range. Research on fundraising shows that a firm early commitment is what persuades other donors, so a promise you know you can keep is worth more than a bigger number you might have to walk back.
