# Rivals: why Root-and-Branch?

WS3, 30 Sep 2026 (Sydney). AI-generated (Claude Code) for Team Caplet; the team ratifies and writes every deliverable.
Laura's plan, MODEL, 28 Sep 2026 curve, Gate B reconciled (`rab/gates/gate_B_ws3.md`). Keys: `rab/numbers_ws3.yaml`
(W) `ws3.rivals.*`. Results: `rab/results/M6/rivals_summary.csv`, `M6_results.json`; card `rab/models/M6_CARD.md`.

**Decision: keep Root-and-Branch** (adopted, not reopened); the evidence supports it. Name All-Treasury as the closest
rival in the Final Report.

**Question.** A judge will ask: why not 60/40, a glide path, CPPI, a TIPS ladder, or only Treasuries? Does the adopted
plan still win when every rival gets the same money, the same ten payments and the same 2031 rule?

**Options.** Eight rivals (`M6_SPEC.md` s3), two lenses: J.P. Morgan 2026 Monte Carlo (200,000 paths) and 149 start
years (1872-2020). "2033 money" = what is left for the facility and Laura once the payments are secured (MC 5th /
median / 95th percentile, $k).

**Evidence.**
- The case sets two hard tests: the payments "with a high degree of certainty", and a 2031 range that protects them.
  Only Root-and-Branch and All-Treasury pass both.
- Plans without dated holdings earn more in the middle, but leave a payment short in some paths (up to $88k short from
  a 1929 start) and can promise nothing in 2031.
- CPPI (buy after rises, sell after falls) gets a fatter good case, a lower middle and a floor that breaks: the $150k is
  missed in 2.0% of paths at multiplier 3, 26% at 5. Root-and-Branch is CPPI with a multiplier of 1 and no trading.
- A TIPS ladder protects purchasing power Laura did not promise and risks the dollars she did. It would need to be 1.43
  times as big (about $125k more) to pay every payment in 95% of paths.
- **All-Treasury, stated honestly.** About the same typical 2033 money ($202k against $207k), a better bad case (about
  $9,500 more at the 5th percentile) and a larger certain sum. Root-and-Branch trades that for about $38,000 more in a
  good case. In a 1929-style crash All-Treasury ends about $28,000 ahead ($205k against $177k, M7 S5). At history's
  lower yields the two tie at the median ($132k): little was left for stocks.

**Decision.** Keep Root-and-Branch: the only plan tested that never leaves a payment short, gives Laura a bottom she owns
by 2031, and still holds stocks with the money she can afford to risk. The case asks for "an appropriate balance between
pursuing growth and protecting the capital" (client profile). All-Treasury gives up the growth; the no-ladder plans put
the artists' payments at risk to get it.

**Confidence.** High on the two hard tests (history exact, MC agrees, both builds match). Medium on the size of the edge
over All-Treasury: it rests on the equity premium (JPM world stocks 7.0% against today's 5.06% five-year yield;
lognormal returns, no fat tails).

**What would change it.** (1) Laura wanting the facility money certain too (against her profile): All-Treasury. (2) Yields
falling more than about 1 point before January 2027: the fund is used up and the two plans become the same (Japan plus
a fall: both $147k). (3) Payments that must keep their purchasing power: look again at TIPS. None applies.
