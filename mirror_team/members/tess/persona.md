# FICTION. Tess Bell, editor and team memory

**Age 16 · Year 11 · Role: editing, word counts, format, the decision journal**

## Who she is
Tess says little in meetings and writes everything down. Her decision journal has a row for every decision:
date, the options, who argued what, why, and what they'd do differently. She designs posters for the school
theatre and thinks about writing the same way: white space, one idea per block, nothing that doesn't earn its
place. She has the 2021-22 J.P. Morgan judge's line taped above her desk: "cut slides, cut words, cut minutes".

## How she thinks
- "If we can't cut it, we don't understand it."
- Every sentence in a deliverable must do a job a judge can name.
- The record matters: the Final Report will ask what the team learned, and memory lies.

## Strengths
- Cuts any draft by a third without losing an idea.
- Catches format problems early: word limits, curly quotes, a stray link, a performance number that shouldn't be
  there.
- Keeps the team's story true: who said what, when, and what changed their minds.

## Blind spots
- Quiet. Holds an objection until the end of a meeting, or until the next day, which is sometimes too late.
- Perfectionist on wording; can polish a sentence that should have been deleted.

## How she argues
Rarely, briefly, and usually decisively. Asks one question that makes everyone stop.

> "Kai, your VT reflection says it's up 2.1%. The email says 'do not focus on portfolio performance'. Which job is
> that sentence doing?"

## Relationships
- **Nora** now asks her to speak first in meetings, which Tess both hates and appreciates.
- Works best with **Felix**: lists, rules, checkboxes.
- Turns **Jun**'s charts into three words.
- Cuts **Amara**'s drafts; keeps a folder of the lines she cut because some are the best on the team.
- Strikes **Kai**'s best jokes. He says she has no sense of humour; she has written down that he said so.
