# Registration: the outside read (H-032, written by Amara before any post is visible to her)

Readers: (A) finance professor who judges Wharton's competition; (B) business judge reading at speed; (C) bright Year 11 who has never studied finance. Each gets the case summary, the final and up to three repaired versions, blind, random letters, shuffled order. Nobody is told which is the final or what changed.

## 1. Questions (asked of every version)
Q1. In your own words, why does this plan pay each cheque the way it does, and why does that suit this client?
Q2. Which sentence, if any, reads as winding up for a punchline, or as corny? Quote it.
Q3. Is anything unclear or untrue? Quote it and say which.
Q4. Which version shows the most investing understanding applied to this client, and which sentence shows it best?
Q5. If you were Laura reading this in a bad year, which version would let you sleep, and what line does that?

## 2. Key (0-3 per question, per reader, per version)
Q1: 0 = wrong or blank, or only restates the method ("one bond per payment"). 1 = states the method and a generic virtue ("safe", "simple"). 2 = gives a reason tied to Laura's facts (fixed cheques, a deposit that can dry up, a building to fund) but not why this method over the other. 3 = gives why this method over the alternative, tied to Laura (nothing to rebalance or sell on a bad day; each cheque is paid by a bond that matures into it), in the reader's own words without copying the text.
Q2: 3 = names nothing (or a line that is not in the paragraph under test). 2 = names a line outside the paragraph under test. 1 = names a line inside it, but says only "a bit neat". 0 = names a line inside it as the corny or punchline one, with a reason.
Q3: 3 = nothing flagged, or flags only a wording quibble. 2 = flags something that is merely unexplained (a term not defined). 1 = flags a sentence as unclear to an ordinary reader. 0 = flags a sentence as untrue or misleading, and the reason holds against the checked facts in the BRIEF.
Q4: 3 = picks this version and quotes a sentence that links an idea to Laura. 2 = picks it, names an idea but no Laura link. 1 = picks it without a reason. 0 = does not pick it (counts as a loss for that reader).
Q5: 3 = picks this version and names a line that speaks to a bad year. 2 = picks it, vague. 1 = tie. 0 = picks another.

## 3. The bar a repair must clear to beat the final
All of: (a) it wins Q1 with at least 2 of 3 readers (higher Q1 score than the final for that reader, ties do not count); (b) it is not behind the final on Q3 for any reader (no reader scores it lower on unclear/untrue); (c) it is not behind on Q2 for any reader; (d) its Q1 total across the three readers is at least 2 points above the final's; (e) it is inside 497 strict and keeps every Book 11 and I80 rule. A tie goes to the final: changing a finished paragraph has to pay.

## 4. Tripwire (disqualifies a repair outright)
Any reader names a sentence inside the repaired paragraph as untrue or misleading (Q3 = 0), or as the corny or punchline one (Q2 = 0). One reader is enough. It is disqualified even if it wins everything else. The same tripwire applies to the final: if a reader scores the final's P3 or P5 sentence at 0 on Q2 or Q3, that is a finding to send to Caplet's card, not a reason to repair tonight unless a repair clears the bar.
