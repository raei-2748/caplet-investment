### Judging "exactly" (the chair asked me to)
The final's sentence is "A Treasury bond held to maturity pays exactly what it promised, whatever markets do." Its words
are true of a bond (the Window's fix this morning). But it sits one sentence after "a ladder of Treasury bonds and
Treasury funds with fixed end dates", and one sentence before "So ... high certainty means no payment depends on
shares", so the paragraph draws its conclusion about the whole ladder from a word that covers only half of it. The five
iBonds rungs are funds, and iShares says the fund "does not seek to return any predetermined amount at maturity". So the
professor's reason holds against the checked facts: "exactly" is misleading where it stands, though not untrue as
written. That is a 0 under my key. The Year 11's "a fund has no maturity date" does not hold (the fund "will
automatically redeem your shares for cash" in its final year), so that flag scores as unclear, not untrue.
