# The look-alike card

AI DRAFT (Claude, 4 Nov 2026). Keel is a simulated team, AI-written.

**How to use it.** Reword the IPS however you like; your own voice is the point. Before you finish a sentence, find its row below and check you haven't landed on the look-alike: the capital letters mark the part that makes it false.
Words in the "true" line are not words to keep, only the fact to keep.

---

**A. What the plan holds**
- True: there is a holding for every payment, and each one is paid back before its January. Some are Treasury bonds and some are Treasury funds.
- False: "a TREASURY for every payment" / "Treasuries that each REPAY a fixed sum"
- Why: some of the holdings are funds, and a fund pays what it is worth at the end, not a fixed sum.

**B. Not crushing it**
- True: crushing means selling a holding before it matures.
- False: selling it before its JANUARY / before the PAYMENT is due
- Why: every holding matures before its January, so by then there is nothing left to sell.

**C. How we match the payments**
- True: each payment has its own bond (or fund) to pay it. The alternative is matching the average timing of the bonds to the payments and rebalancing as rates move, which we do not do.
- False: "we DURATION-MATCH" / "our ladder is DURATION-MATCHED" / "we DON'T duration-match"
- Why: the average timing of our holdings does not match the payments (interest comes back early), so neither "we match" nor "we don't match" is the right claim. Our submitted trading notes use the phrase loosely, so don't lean on it.

**D. What a Treasury bond pays, and what the funds pay**
- True: a Treasury bond held to maturity pays exactly what it promised. The funds pay what they are worth when they end.
- False: "the funds pay EXACTLY what they PROMISED" / "every holding pays a FIXED SUM"
- Why: our own trading notes tell the judges the funds pay their value at the end, "as opposed to a promise of par".

**E. What "high certainty" means here**
- True: no payment depends on shares.
- False: "NOTHING can go wrong" / "RISK-FREE" / "GUARANTEED"
- Why: the plan still leans on the government paying and on interest being reinvested; that is why the weak point is named later. We claim certainty from shares, not from everything.

**F. Time and share losses**
- True: waiting makes a loss less likely, but not smaller when it happens.
- False: "time makes a loss SMALLER" / "shares are SAFE if you wait"
- Why: a long wait changes the odds, not the size of a bad outcome, and a fixed cheque cannot wait for a recovery.

**G. Her deposit and the downturn**
- True: her deposit comes from income that can dry up in the same downturns that hit share prices.
- False: "her income ALWAYS falls with shares" / "her income MOVES WITH shares" / "her income RECOVERS with shares"
- Why: the evidence shows it falling in slumps, not that it always does, and not that it comes back when shares do.

**H. What the floor is**
- True: the floor is a Treasury fund that ends the year before the payments start.
- False: "a TREASURY that ends the year before..." / "a Treasury BOND"
- Why: Laura's plan holds a fund there, and a fund's final payout is not fixed.

**I. How the bottom of the range is stated**
- True: the bottom is the floor, rounded down, money she already owns.
- False: "her FLOOR" on its own / "a GUARANTEED minimum" / "a FIXED floor"
- Why: the floor's payout is not fixed, so the number we say aloud has to sit under it.

**J. What goes into shares**
- True: only roughly the interest the floor will earn goes into the world index fund.
- False: "THE REST goes into shares" / "most of the deposit buys shares"
- Why: most of the deposit buys the floor. Shares only set how far the gift rises above its minimum.

**K. The ladder's weak point**
- True: its main weakness is that it counts on reinvesting what comes back.
- False: "its ONLY weakness" / "ONE weakness" / "no weakness"
- Why: our trading notes name a second catch (the funds' payout is a value, not a promise) and a wait between a bond maturing and its January.

**L. What the shortfall is sized for**
- True: we price the shortfall as if every dollar waiting for a cheque earned nothing.
- False: "as if no more INTEREST were reinvested" / "as if later COUPONS were lost"
- Why: the funds also hold cash and matured bond money while they wait. Counting only interest sets aside too little, and a cheque could be short.

**M. Where the shortfall money goes**
- True: that much moves from her kept shares into Treasuries before she names the range.
- False: "SELL shares to cover the gap WHEN it appears" / "set it aside" (nowhere)
- Why: selling shares to meet a payment puts a cheque back in the shares' hands, against our trading notes. Money set aside with no home is still in shares.

**N. What shrinks first**
- True: her spare money shrinks first, and if it cannot cover the gap the range comes down, before the gift does.
- False: "before her PROMISE does" / "the CHEQUES shrink"
- Why: "promise" can be read as the cheques, which never shrink. The gift is the thing that gives way.
