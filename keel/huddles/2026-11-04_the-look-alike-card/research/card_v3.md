# The look-alike card

AI DRAFT (Claude, 4 Nov 2026). Keel is a simulated team, AI-written.

**How to use it.** Reword the IPS however you like; your own voice is the point. Each row gives a fact in our words (you needn't keep them), a false look-alike and why it's false. Capitals show the difference: in the look-alike when a word is swapped, in the fact when one is left out.

**This card isn't everything.** If a changed sentence has no row, check each word you swapped, added or cut against its IPS line. In our tests, most breaks hid in added finance words and cut small words.

**A. One for each January.** Each holding is redeemed FOR a January's cheque and matures BEFORE it.
- False: "one redeemed each January" / "one maturing each January"
- Why: each ends earlier (the funds in December, the bonds in February, May or November).

**B. Matching the payments.** One bond or fund per payment, held to the end, rather than matching the bonds' AVERAGE timing to the payments' and rebalancing.
- False: "our ladder is DURATION-MATCHED" / "the other way is DURATION MATCHING"
- Why: our money comes back sooner than the cheques, on average, so the first is false; the second clashes with our submitted notes' "through duration-matching".

**C. Bonds.** Held to the end, a Treasury BOND pays exactly what it promised; a fund pays its value.
- False: "a Treasury held to maturity pays exactly" / "the funds pay EXACTLY what they PROMISED"
- Why: our submitted notes say the funds pay their value, "as opposed to a promise of par".

**D. What the cheques rest on.** No cheque depends on shares, but they can still need the second deposit: it completes the ladder first, if needed, and what it buys covers any later gap.
- False: "every cheque is GUARANTEED" / "the second deposit NEVER touches the cheques"
- Why: the IPS says the ladder can fall short if rates fall and stay low, and our WInS trade notes say the second deposit "would top up the ladder". Our submitted notes only say "near-ironclad".

**E. Her income.** It funds the second deposit and CAN dry up in the same downturns that hit shares.
- False: "which dry up in the same downturns" / "her income RIDES ON shares"
- Why: the case doesn't say her income falls in slumps, so we can only claim it might.

**F. The floor.** It's a Treasury fund, and the bottom of the range is the floor ROUNDED DOWN.
- False: "the floor is a Treasury BOND" / "the bottom is her floor"
- Why: the floor's payout isn't fixed (our submitted note says so), so the bottom must sit under it.

**G. The bank's note.** A capital-protected note is a bond plus an option bought with the interest.
- False: "they spend the interest to pay for the PROTECTION"
- Why: the bond is what protects the money; the option is only a chance of a gain.

**H. The weakness, and its price.** The ladder's MAIN weakness is that it counts on reinvesting its bonds' interest; we price the gap as if every dollar waiting for a cheque earned nothing.
- False: "the ladder's weakness is ..." / "as if no more INTEREST were reinvested"
- Why: our submitted notes name another catch (the funds pay their value), and the funds also hold cash and matured bond money, so pricing only interest can leave a cheque short.

**I. The gap money.** Before the range is named, it moves from her kept shares INTO TREASURIES.
- False: "sell shares to cover the gap WHEN IT APPEARS" / "set it aside from her shares"
- Why: our submitted notes keep every cheque off shares; money with no home is still shares.

**J. Shrinks first.** Her spare money (her kept shares); if that's not enough, the whole range does.
- False: "EXCESS CASH absorbs the gap first" / "spare CASH shrinks first"
- Why: the IPS names no spare cash, so "cash" sends a reader looking for a pile that isn't there.
