This one matters, so it goes first. Can I read you something? "The United States are as much bound by their
contracts as are individuals." It isn't Perry's own line. Hughes quotes Chief Justice Waite in the Sinking-Fund
Cases (1879), and Brandeis had used the same line in Lynch (1934) ([Perry](https://openjurist.org/294/us/330);
[Sinking-Fund Cases](https://openjurist.org/99/us/700); [Lynch](https://openjurist.org/292/us/571)). So we cite it as
"Perry, quoting the Sinking-Fund Cases". S2's heading also gets the words wrong ("is as much bound by its contracts").
FUSIONS has them right.

**Device: "Which clause broke in 1933, and does Laura's bond have one?"** It needs two fixes.
1. "The only Treasury term ever broken was a gold clause" goes too far. In April and May 1979 some T-bill redemptions
   to individual investors were paid late, and bill rates rose about 60bp ([Marron, Tax Policy Center](https://taxpolicycenter.org/taxvox/day-united-states-defaulted-treasury-bills),
   citing Zivney and Marcus). So say "rewritten", not "broken": 1933 rewrote a term, and 1979 was late.
2. C says "real returns". Goetzmann and Jorion measure real *appreciation*, price only: the US "by far the highest",
   and a median elsewhere of "about 1.5 percent" ([NBER w5901](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5901)). Both figures are on
   the same basis, so the point holds. The word "returns" doesn't.
