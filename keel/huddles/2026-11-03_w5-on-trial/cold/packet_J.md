# Reader sheet

You will read two short texts, labelled X and Y. Each is a plan written for one client, Laura Gao, who will pay ten yearly cheques from an investment portfolio and may also give money toward a building. You have never seen either text. Use only the texts. Do not look anything up and do not ask anyone.

Answer every question for X and for Y separately, in your own words, in a sentence or two. Do not copy the text's phrases unless you must. If the text does not say, write "the page doesn't say". If you are guessing, write "guess" and then give your guess. After each answer mark one of: **sure** (I could explain this to someone else and act on it), **half** (I think so, but I could not act on it), **lost** (I could not say it back).

## Questions (answer for X, then for Y)

1. What does Laura's first $300,000 buy, and how is it arranged?
2. What does her 2028 deposit buy? Say what each part is for.
3. The plan admits one weak point. What is it, and what does the plan do about it before she names her range?
4. After she names her range in 2031, can any of the ten yearly cheques still come up short? Say why, or why not.
5. Some money is set aside that the cheques turn out not to need. What happens to it?
6. Why does a downturn hurt Laura twice?
7. The text ends "Do not crush." What does that ask of whoever runs the money?
8. List every word or phrase you had to guess at or skip over. Beside each, write your guess at what it means, or "no idea".

## Text X

In January 2033, Laura's portfolio writes the first of ten cheques: $50,000 each, one every January, toward running her Taiwan residency. She also hopes to contribute to the building.

We call our plan Caplet XR. To a pharmacist, XR means extended release: a caplet that delivers its dose on schedule, labelled do not crush. Crush it and the schedule is gone. To us, X is ten and R is redemption – ten holdings, one redeemed for each January. Crushing one means selling it before it matures, even for the building.

In January 2027, her first $300,000 buys a ladder of Treasuries, some held directly and some through funds, one rung falling due before each cheque. A Treasury held to maturity pays exactly what it promises, whatever markets do. That, to us, is what "a high degree of certainty" means.

Laura's career already behaves like a stock. Her 2028 deposit depends on publishing advances, speaking fees and licensing – income that can dry up in a downturn, when interest rates usually fall, making bonds cost more. If that deposit arrives smaller, the gift shrinks. A cheque funded by a Treasury already bought does not.

Her 2028 deposit finishes any rung the first $300,000 couldn't buy. Most of the rest buys a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, the floor under her gift. The remainder, roughly the interest the floor should earn, goes into one world index fund, spread across thousands of companies. That is under a tenth of her $450,000. A bank's capital-protected note also starts with a bond, but spends that interest on an option, a bet that expires worthless unless the market rises. Stocks don't expire. We assume world stocks earn about 7.5% a year, but the cheques never depend on it.

In 2031, Laura gives co-sponsors a range for her gift to the building. The bottom is her floor, rounded down: money she already owns. The top adds half her stocks. Before she names it, that half moves into Treasuries, so every dollar she promises is already in bonds. The other half stays with Laura, liquid any day, as her flexibility, in case the building costs more in Taiwan dollars.

We could put the floor into stocks and chase a bigger gift. But a number said aloud to co-sponsors cannot be quietly halved if markets turn.

The weak point is reinvestment. The ladder costs less than the cheques because its bonds pay interest along the way, which we reinvest. If interest rates fall and stay low, that interest earns less, and the ladder falls short. Before she names the range, we price that gap as if later bond payments sat idle, and move that much from the stocks Laura keeps into Treasuries, so her flexibility shrinks before her gift does. Whatever the stocks can't cover lowers the whole range.

From 2033, the ladder and the money set aside for its gap become the operating reserve, paying out one rung per cheque.

Do not crush.
