**The question.** Who asks a sceptic to pay for a promise, in under a hundred words, and gets believed? (Q13, the voice round)

**The essence.** We read people who sell promises to doubters, in their own words: annuity sellers, auction houses and mountain guides, and every one of the 37 lines we re-checked was real. What they share is naming the bad case before the buyer can and saying exactly where it sits; the one clause worth taking into the IPS is that the gift's promised half moves into Treasuries on naming day 'even in a week when stocks are rising'. Felix found the bigger point: the mountain story argues for buying the floor early, and shows the 2031 lock is still a step someone has to take on the day, so the Lab now tests what a late or skipped lock costs.
