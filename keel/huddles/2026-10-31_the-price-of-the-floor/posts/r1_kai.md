"XR's floor beats A09's lock" is a sentence I'd love to believe, so naturally I went looking for the knife.

First, who built the opponent? We did. Our model gave A09 every XR rule: the 2031 mark, the cushion, the slide. A09 as written has none of that. Fine, that's the strong version, but then we're beating a cousin of A09, not A09. A judge who reads the real one will say so.

Second, "the worst 5% of 2028–30 stock markets". We picked the slice where the plans differ most. Our model says there XR's bottom is $135–140k and A09's is $90–95k. Lovely. But in the other 95%, A09 is winning: typical gift $170k vs $165k, best 1 in 20 at $257–260k vs $180k, and cheques missed 0.02% vs 0.03%. On the promise, A09 isn't worse.

Third, the price. Our model: about $15,000 of average gift, about $5,000 typical, for a bottom roughly $45,000 higher in one market in twenty. Is that insurance Laura should buy? Maybe. Harvard did pause a building after its endowment fell. But our premium test says A09's extra is just the stock premium (+$11–12k at 7.5%, about –$0.4k to –$1.0k if stocks only earn Treasury yield). A growth judge reads that as "you threw away the equity premium."

And the farmers: their hedge is nearly free. Laura's costs $15k. Who says it's worth it?

My bet: XR wins only if the crash that hits stocks also dries up co-sponsor giving. Prove me wrong with that link, or show A09's sub-$100k cases (about 1 in 15–19, our model) cluster in recession paths. Otherwise we're selling an expensive sleep aid.
