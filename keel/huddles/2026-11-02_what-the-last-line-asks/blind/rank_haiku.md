**Overall order:** DYEY > UHPT > KMGK

**Reasons:**

**DYEY:** "We don't invest toward these payments. We buy them" is the easiest central idea to repeat after one read, and the caplet metaphor carries it through to "Do not crush." It also says what happens when the stocks cannot cover the reinvestment gap, so on content it is only a step behind UHPT. Its weak spots are "one bond per cheque", which clashes with the earlier funds, and a reserve definition that leaves out the set-aside cushion; the Creativity 5 is a one-line lift, and when I judged the whole text again it is otherwise the same as UHPT, so this order is close.

**UHPT:** The most complete of the three: it says nothing is sold early ("none sold"), it says what happens when the stocks cannot cover the gap ("lowers the whole range"), and it defines the operating reserve as the ladder plus the set-aside. It is less quotable than DYEY, so it ranks second on a close call.

**KMGK:** Otherwise the same text, with a clear rule that ties the no-sell promise to the building: "Crushing one means selling it before its January, even for the building." It drops the sentence on what happens when the stocks cannot cover the gap, so the range's protection in a bad market is left open. That is a one-sentence difference; I re-judged the whole text and it still earns 3 on Portfolio Analysis, so it ties UHPT on score and ranks third on that sentence.

**Score note:** Articulation is 2 for all three. None mentions the team, the WInS trades or what the team learned; the only reflection is "The weak point is reinvestment."
