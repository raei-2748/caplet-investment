**Better overall:** UCPK

**Reasons:**

Note on method. The two versions share every paragraph but three sentences (the definition of "crushing" in paragraph 2, a repeat of the pitch's "we buy them" line in paragraph 3, and the closing paragraph). I noticed that any difference would therefore rest on those lines, so I judged each text as a whole, pitch included, and asked what it taught me beyond a standard ladder. The shared idea that earns both a high mark is not the ladder (table stakes) but what is built on it: a capital-protected note with the option replaced by shares, a 2028 deposit treated as correlated human capital, a 2031 promise that is moved into bonds before it is spoken, and a reinvestment gap that is priced and taken from flexibility before it touches the promise. Shared weaknesses: projections are thin even for an IPS, the reserve's composition over time is only "one rung per cheque", and there is nothing on teamwork, research process or learning, so Articulation sits at 3 for both.

UCPK. The metaphor is made operational: crushing is defined as selling a rung before its January, even for the building, so the closing "Do not crush" is a rule, not a slogan, and it answers Wharton's "protect the commitment while preserving flexibility" question by prohibition. The last paragraph closes the loop opened by the reinvestment paragraph: the operating reserve is the ladder plus the money set aside for its gap, which is the one thing this version taught me that the other did not. The narrative runs introduce, define, apply, close, with no repetition of the pitch, which is why Creativity and Presentation reaches 5.

DYEY. Same analysis and the same voice, but the whole reads slightly looser: it repeats the pitch's hook ("We don't invest toward these payments. We buy them") inside the IPS, never says what crushing means, and ends with two closing devices that compete, a priority order ("the cheques, then the floor, then everything above it") that the structure already implies and paragraph 8 already states for floor versus stocks, and then "Do not crush". Its reserve definition drops the gap money and says "one bond per cheque" after describing rungs as bonds and funds, a small loose end. Nothing in it taught me something UCPK did not, so it loses one point on presentation and the pairwise call.
