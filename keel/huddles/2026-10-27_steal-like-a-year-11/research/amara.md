## 1. IDEO's brainstorming rules (and where they come from)

**What they actually are.** IDEO.org's Design Kit page "Brainstorm Rules" lists seven: "Defer judgement", "Encourage wild
ideas", "Build on the ideas of others", "Stay focused on the topic", "One conversation at a time", "Be visual", "Go for
quantity". Exact lines worth keeping:
- "In conversation, we try to use "and" instead of "but.""
- "Your team is far more likely to build on an idea and make a creative leap ... if everyone is paying full attention to whoever is sharing a new idea."
- "In a good session, up to 100 ideas are generated in 60 minutes." and "Crank the ideas out quickly and build on the best ones."
- IDEO U's version of the same list: "Creative spaces are judgment-free zones" and, from instructor Brendan Boyle, "Attaching a single person to a single idea hinders collaboration and greatness."

**Where they come from (not IDEO).** Per Wikipedia's Brainstorming page: Alex Osborn, an advertising executive, developed the
method from 1939; "His two principles were to defer judgment and reach for quantity." (paraphrase of the page; Wikipedia
is secondary, so the 1939/1942/1948 dates are UNVERIFIED against Osborn's own books). IDEO's list is a refinement.

**Does group brainstorming work? The research cuts the other way.**
- Diehl and Stroebe (1987, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 53(3), 497-509): reviewing 22 studies, 18 found
  "nominal groups" (people working alone, results pooled afterwards) beat real brainstorming groups, 4 found no difference.
  The original finding: "nominal groups produced nearly twice as many different ideas as the real groups." Their best
  explanation was production blocking: "production blocking accounted for most of the productivity loss of real brainstorming
  groups" (only one person can talk at a time, so others forget or drop their idea). They doubted fear of criticism
  ("evaluation apprehension") as the main cause, and found little free-riding. (Source: the paper PDF, read via a summary tool.)
- Wikipedia (paraphrase): the loss grows with group size; hearing others' ideas can narrow what everyone thinks of
  ("collaborative fixation"). Quote: "overwhelmingly, groups brainstorming together produce fewer ideas than individuals
  working separately."
- Meta-analysis, Mullen, Johnson and Salas (1991): I could only reach a short, auto-translated note (Scrapbox), which says
  groups do worse than nominal groups and the loss is "particularly noticeable" in bigger groups, and that writing ideas
  does better than saying them aloud. No effect sizes verified. Treat the detail as UNVERIFIED; the direction is consistent.
- Brainwriting (write ideas on paper/slips, pass them round): Paulus and colleagues (reported by the Association for
  Psychological Science): in one study of 57 employees at a global tech company, groups that worked together first made 37%
  more ideas than groups that worked alone first ("not significant", the authors say); in a second study an alternating
  write-alone / read-the-group method "yielded more ideas than did the group condition" (71% higher rate). The BPS Research
  Digest says early evidence "suggests that it, unlike brainstorming, helps groups to spawn more ideas than the same number
  of people working alone." Caveats: these count idea quantity, not quality; Wikipedia says brainwriting still "does not
  perform as well as individuals using the nominal group technique" (that page's claim, not checked in the primary study).
- Debate beats "no criticism" for creativity? A Hult blog post about Charlan Nemeth's Berkeley study (the post says 2003; the
  paper itself I did not read) says debate groups produced "far more creative solutions", with Nemeth quoted: "debate and
  criticism do not inhibit ideas but, rather, stimulate them." This attacks "defer judgment". No numbers; secondary; UNVERIFIED in detail.
- The same post mentions a 1958 Yale experiment where people alone produced about twice as many solutions as groups
  (secondary; UNVERIFIED).

**Honest summary.** Rules 1, 3, 7 sound warm and are loved. The evidence says: for raw quantity, six people shouting ideas in a
room do worse than six people thinking alone for ten minutes first; writing beats talking; and some argument helps
rather than hurts. IDEO's own list, to be fair, doesn't claim a lab result; it's craft knowledge from design work.

## 2. Improv's "yes, and"

**What it does.** Accept what your partner offered, then add to it. Wikipedia ("Yes, and ...") says the principle "does not
forbid disagreements between the improvisers' characters" (the rule is about the performers accepting the scene, not the
characters agreeing), and it is complemented by a "No, but..." technique that refines and challenges ideas.
**Origin.** I could not verify Keith Johnstone or Second City as the source from the page I read: UNVERIFIED.
**Criticism.** Gary Schwartz (quoted on the Wikipedia page) argues it "runs counter to the most basic tenets of improvisation",
reduces improv to "information sharing" and gives work that is "uninspired, 'talky' and not very theatrical"; he suggests
Viola Spolin's "Follow the follower!". The page adds there is "hardly universal consensus regarding its authenticity and value."
Also (my reasoning, not a source): "yes, and" is a rule for making things, not for deciding things. It's the wrong tool for a
number that has to be right.

## 3. Patagonia

**Mission.** "We're in business to save our home planet." (patagonia.com/ownership). In Sept 2022 the company's ownership moved
to a trust and a nonprofit: "Earth is now our only shareholder." (same page).

**Let My People Go Surfing.** Chouinard's 2005 book (title from the book; publication year UNVERIFIED here). CNBC (2016) quotes him:
"We have a policy that when the surf comes up, you drop work and you go surfing." and "I don't care when you work as long as
the job gets done." (CNBC repeats these; I did not confirm in the book itself.)

**On-site childcare.** Patagonia's own story page ("Family Business: Weighing the Business Case") and the US Chamber of Commerce
Foundation entry: opened 1983 at Ventura; "offering on-site child care, we believe, is the right thing to do for employees,
working parents." Numbers from Patagonia's own page: "we estimate that we recover 91 percent of our calculable costs
annually"; turnover for parents using it "has run 25 percent less"; "100 percent of moms return to work after maternity
leave" (over five years). The company says it never cut the programme in hard times. These are Patagonia's own figures,
not independently audited.

**Criticism and limits.**
- Fortune (28 Jun 2024): Patagonia told about 90 of 255 customer-service staff to move to a hub city or leave. Its
  communications head said the team had been "200 to 300% overstaffed" and "Many times staff only had about two hours of work a
  day." An employee: "I think that the company has changed a lot since it sold to Mother Earth." Staff called the deadline
  rushed. So the "people first" brand also cuts jobs when the work isn't there.
- Flextime only works if the work is done: "as long as the job gets done" is a condition, not a gift. (reading of the CNBC quote)
- Patagonia's culture is owned by a founder with money and a mission; a school group doesn't have that cushion. (my reasoning)

## 4. Stealable habits, translated for six Year 11s at Fernleigh

Context: Nora has the bakery Sat 6 a.m. and minds Lina; Felix has band; Jun runs at 6 and has piano; Kai has the phone shop
and basketball; I tutor Tuesdays and help in the gurdwara kitchen on Sundays; Tess does theatre lighting. Exams Mon 9 - Fri 13 Nov.
IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00 Sydney.

**From IDEO / brainwriting (fix what the research broke)**
1. **Write first, talk second (brainwriting in the group chat).** Question posted in the chat at 7 p.m.; each person sends
   three ideas alone in ten minutes, *before* they read anyone's. Costs ten minutes on the 389 bus or in a free period; works
   when someone's at a shift because it's asynchronous. Fixes blocking and collaborative fixation.
2. **Quantity round, alone.** Everyone writes 10 ugly versions of one IPS sentence (e.g. the pin-up line) on their phone in
   one lunch. Nobody's name on them when pasted. Then we pick together.
3. **"And" first, "but" second, in that order.** In the Long Room, the first reply to an idea must add something ("and what
   if..."). Only after one round may anybody say "but". Keeps Rule 3 and keeps the debate that Nemeth's point suggests we need.
4. **One conversation at a time on the late call.** Jun and Tess already do this; make it a rule for the six-way call: hand-raise
   emoji, no talking over. Costs nothing.

**From improv**
5. **"Yes, and" for first drafts only; "no, but" for numbers.** When I draft Laura's lines I get a "yes, and" round. Anything with a
   number goes to Felix and Jun with their "no, but." One rule per kind of work.

**From Patagonia**
6. **Surf time is real.** Nora's bakery morning and Jun's 6 a.m. run are not "free time"; they are when the brain does its
   best work. Agreed: nobody is asked for work between 9 p.m. and 7 a.m. except by their own choice, and no one is judged for
   a missed lunch. (Flextime = "as long as the job gets done", so each person says Sunday night what their job is for the week.)
7. **Exam week gets a lighter plan, written down.** Mon 9 - Fri 13 Nov: only the IPS-in tasks and the 6:58 post; nothing else.
   Patagonia's lesson is that the support is planned in advance, not promised in a crisis (their childcare was never cut).
8. **A purpose sentence over the door.** We already have a mission (CULTURE.md). Patagonia shows a mission works when it picks
   decisions, not when it decorates. Test: does Laura's promise win an argument this week?

**Refused**
- **Unlimited "wild ideas, no judgment" for numbers.** Our rules say a number needs a date and basis; deferring judgment on a
  number would let my best sentence outrun the evidence again (my known weakness).
- **A 100-ideas-in-60-minutes target.** We are six people with exams; a quantity target is for design studios with a day off.
- **Copying Patagonia's perks.** We have no childcare centre and no money. Steal the idea (plan for people's real lives), not the building.
- **Treating improv as consensus.** "Yes, and" can hide a wrong idea under politeness. The Schwartz criticism is fair.
