## The job
Fight A: does the owner decide, or do we vote? My round-1 line was Pixar's: the Braintrust "should have no authority",
so the director decides. Nora conceded wording in round 2 and kept strategy for a vote. That was too easy, so I went

## 1. Pixar takes the pen away, and does it often
The Braintrust has no authority. The studio does. Those are two different things, and I only quoted the first.

- **Brave (2010).** Brenda Chapman, who created the story, was taken off as director and Mark Andrews took over.
  SlashFilm (18 Oct 2010, citing Cartoon Brew's sources): Chapman "was pushed aside from full directing duties a while
  back" and "the change officially happened last week, although it had been inevitable for some time" (verbatim). No
  reason was given. Chapman, New York Times op-ed (Aug 2012, as quoted by Cartoon Brew): "When Pixar took me off of
  'Brave' — a story that came from my heart, inspired by my relationship with my daughter — it was devastating." Also:
  "Animation directors are not protected like live-action directors." and "But in the end, my vision came through in
  the film." (all verbatim via Cartoon Brew).
- **The Good Dinosaur (2013).** Bob Peterson was removed as director. Empire, quoting Catmull (via the LA Times):
  "Sometimes directors ... are so deeply embedded in their ideas it actually takes someone else to finish it up"
  (verbatim, ellipsis in the source). The film was delayed and rebuilt; several outlets call it "dismantled and
  completely reimagined" (The Film Stage headline, title only, not read). UNVERIFIED: that it was Pixar's first
  money-loser (often said; I did not read a source for it).
- **Toy Story 2 (around 1998-99).** Shortform's summary of *Creativity, Inc.* (paraphrase, no book quotes): after
  Lasseter reviewed the work and told Catmull the process was failing, Catmull removed the two first-time directors,
  gave Lasseter control, and brought back the original *Toy Story* people, who rebuilt the film in about nine months.
  UNVERIFIED in Catmull's own words; I did not read the chapter.
- **Ratatouille.** Mindset Online (the author's own statement, not a quote): it "was overhauled so completely that the
  director was replaced partway through." The same page quotes Catmull: "After a Braintrust meeting, it is up to him
  or her to figure out how to address the feedback" (verbatim).

**What this does to my argument.** Pixar's real rule has two halves. Notes have no authority: the director can ignore
anyone. But the director serves at the studio's pleasure, and when a film stays broken the studio hands the pen to
someone else. "The owner decides" was only ever the inside half. The outside half is a removal clause. And Chapman's
words show the price: taking the pen is "devastating" when it is done late and without a stated reason. Pixar's
evidence is its own story, again (one company, its president's book); I found no study of whether replacing
directors works. Toy Story 2 says yes; The Good Dinosaur at best says "it shipped".

## 2. What real investment committees do: who proposes, who approves
- **University of Kentucky endowment policy (Dec 2024 version, read).** The Investment Committee is responsible for
  "(1) formulating and reviewing investment policies; (2) appointing, monitoring and evaluating investment managers and
  consultants; (3) reviewing and approving plans for the general management of the endowment funds" (verbatim). "Any
  modifications to the policy shall be approved by the Committee." Staff work "for the investment asset allocation and
  strategies approved by the Committee." Staff "will prepare analysis and recommendations for the Committee",
  "is responsible for executing the policies and decisions enacted by the Committee" and "is responsible for
  developing and implementing a rebalancing plan", then "will report the results of all rebalancing activity" to the
  Committee (all verbatim). Managers "exercise discretion over the securities".
- So the split is: **staff propose, the committee votes on the policy and the allocation, staff then execute and
  rebalance on their own and report back.** The committee approves the plan, not the wording of staff's memo. Nobody
  in that document votes on a sentence.
- **Yale.** The search turned up Yale's 2005 endowment report, which I expected to show the same split (committee
  approves the policy portfolio, staff pick managers). UNVERIFIED: the fetch budget ran out before I could read it.
- **Why vote on allocation but not wording?** My reading, not a source: the allocation is the decision that is hard
  to undo and that the whole fund lives with; the memo is how staff argue for it. Bezos says the same thing in
  company terms (2015 shareholder letter, as reproduced on Farnam Street; I did not read the SEC copy):
  one-way doors "must be made methodically, carefully, slowly, with great deliberation and consultation", while
  "Type 2 decisions can and should be made quickly by high judgment individuals or small groups." Using the heavy
  process on everything ends in "slowness, unthoughtful risk aversion, failure to experiment sufficiently" (verbatim
  via fs.blog).

## 3. What it means for six Year 11s
1. **Split by door, not by word count.** A one-way door is anything that changes what Laura owns or what we promise
   her: the floor, the range and its confidence, the shortfall plan, the sail size. Those are votes (and, for the
   real IPS, Caplet's votes, not ours). Everything else, including every sentence, is the owner's.
2. **The owner proposes the strategy too.** Kentucky's staff write the recommendation and the committee votes on it.
   So whoever says "mine" on a section brings the strategy call to the vote with a one-line why. The five don't
   redraft it in the meeting.
3. **Steal Pixar's removal clause, and fix Pixar's manners.** If a section stays broken, the pen can move. But out
   loud, with the reason, and early, never by DM and never at 2 a.m. (Northwind's summary was rewritten at 2 a.m. and
   missed the portal by 47 minutes; that's my red folder, and it is the late-rewrite failure in our own Archive.)
   Concretely: if a section blows its appetite and a CORD on it is still open, any two of us can call a vote to pass
   the pen. The owner speaks first. The new owner is named before the vote ends.
4. **Refuse:** a "studio" above the owner that is one person. At Pixar, Catmull and Lasseter were the studio. If our
   studio is the person with the marker, Nora's vote is just the marker with extra steps.
