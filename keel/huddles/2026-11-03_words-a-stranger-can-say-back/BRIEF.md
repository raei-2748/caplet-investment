## The answer key (written by the chair before any reader runs; Nora's habit: pass mark first)
A reader passes a question only by saying it back in their own words in a way they could act on (EU readability
guideline: "answer each question correctly and act appropriately"). Four readers are a pilot: one who fails sends the
sentence back; all passing is "not failed", never "passed". Every tally says how many readers were strangers (all four).
- **K1 lock (P8, arms with road B):** the bonds still pay their interest (yes); money already reinvested keeps
  earning (no, it does not stop); the rule: enough is set aside now that the cheques are covered even if money that
  arrives later earns nothing more.
- **K2 "what the cheques don't need" (arms that have it):** goes to Laura (not the gift, not co-sponsors); in 2036 it
  means money no remaining cheque needs, not that year's leftover.
- **K3 "until only a quarter of them is left" (arms that have it):** a quarter of the stocks she keeps; a fixed rule:
  past that point the range is lowered instead.
- **K4 shares and cheques:** a fall in her shares can do nothing to a cheque (it can shrink her flexibility or the
  top of the gift). The gap money between 2031 and 2033 sits in Treasuries.
- **K5 after naming day:** what could still leave a cheque short? Road B arms: nothing a fall in yields does (the gap
  is bought as if later money earns nothing). V54: a reader may honestly say "if yields fall more than the cushion".
  **Amended 16:55, before any reader ran (Kai, r1 (d)):** "nothing markets do" was too wide: five rungs are funds that
  pay out their net asset value, "not fixed" (submitted IBTM note). A reader who says "nothing" is recorded as reading
  the text that way, not as passing a fact; whether the text claims too much is Tess's call in round 3.
- **K6 the six steps:** (1) the bank puts most of the money in a bond that returns it and spends the rest on a bet that
  pays only if the market rises; flat market, the bet pays nothing; our slice buys shares instead, which don't
  expire. (2) In a downturn interest rates usually fall, and when rates fall bonds cost more. (3) A fund with an end
  date that pays its money out then, like a bond. (4) Under a tenth of everything she puts in (both deposits). (5) The
  interest the floor (the Treasury fund) should earn between 2028 and late 2032. (6) Stocks, shares and the index fund
  are one thing; building and facility are one thing; the gift is her contribution to it.
- **K7 promise (pitch + P6 + TNA):** the cheques are what Laura has committed to pay her residency each year; "every
  dollar she promises" is the range she tells co-sponsors; the TNA's "promised minimum" is the range's bottom. Pass =
  the reader can tell which promise is which, to whom.
