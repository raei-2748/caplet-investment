# FICTION. Team Keel: the story bible

> The world, the people around the six, the images the story keeps returning to, and the season's plot to the Final
> Report. Written 23 Oct 2026 at Ray's request ("like we are making a movie ... a fictional, literature world").
> Load it only for episodes that open an act or turn a plot point (`TOKEN_RULES.md`); for ordinary days,
> `CAST.md` and `episodes/INDEX.md` are enough. Rooms come from `OFFICE.md`; time from `WORLD.md`. Keel's people
> are Sydney students. Nothing here is from another world, and nothing here goes into Caplet's deliverables.

**The one rule (Ray, 23 Oct): the world exists to give the characters depth, and the competition is the story.**
Every one of the six wants one thing this season: to reach the Top 50 semifinal. Every place, family member,
rival, motif and subplot below is here because it changes how someone argues, decides or writes for Laura. If a
scene doesn't change the work, cut it, however good it is. Test each scene by asking: *what does this change in
the plan, the vote or the page?*

**And it is not a smooth ride (Ray, 24 Oct).** The six have lives outside Wharton, and those lives take time when
the competition needs it: an exam week, a shift, a sick grandmother, a production tech week. Things also go wrong:
votes are lost, numbers move, mistakes get through. The story shows both, and it shows what they cost. The same is
true of Caplet's real students, and the office plans for it (section 7a).

## Overture

There is a door at the top of Fernleigh's library that nobody under seventeen was supposed to open. Behind it, a
long room runs the length of the building under a skylight the colour of weak tea. The shelves still carry the
school's old bound magazines, 1962 to 1998, and a brass library ladder on a rail, with ten rungs. Felix counted
them on the first day. Nobody has said what everyone thought.

Six students go up those stairs at lunch. From the playground it looks like a study group. Inside, the clocks
stretch and an afternoon turns into a week: they read, model, argue, sleep in the bunks behind the end bookcase,
wake up and argue better. They come down again at 1:25 with crumbs on their blazers, and their friends ask why they
look as if they've been away.

They are trying to keep a promise for a woman they will never meet. She has ten payments of $50,000 to make,
one at the start of each year from 2033, and she wants to build something in Taiwan with whatever is left. The six
have named themselves after the part of a boat nobody sees. A keel sits under the water and keeps the boat upright
when the wind fills the sail. Keel's whole idea is that the promise is the keel and growth is the sail, and that
you build the keel first.

That is the story. Underneath it is another one: six people learning what they owe each other.

## 1. Sydney, as Keel sees it

Keel's Sydney is the inner west and the long line west along the river: train platforms at 7:40 with the light
coming in sideways, the 389, jacarandas, Saturday netball, and the smell of a bakery at 6 a.m. The places are real
kinds of places, but every shop, school and person in them is invented.

- **Harris Park (Nora).** Wigram Street at night, the sweets shops lit up, her father's ute parked with tile
  offcuts in the tray. The bakery where Nora works Saturdays from six is two streets away. On the family fridge is
  the bill calendar she made at twelve, in three colours. She still updates it. It is why she runs the season as a calendar of promises, and why she will never let a deadline become "good news later".
- **Pyrmont (Felix).** Felix and his mother live in a flat above the light rail, and the trams sound like pages
  turning. Por Por's mahjong table is on Thursday nights, with Felix keeping score in a ruled book because nobody
  trusts anyone else. He hears the harbour before he sees it. The scorebook is where he learned that a rule nobody checks isn't a rule, and that is how he reads Wharton's files.
- **Epping (Jun).** A split-level house with a running track behind it. Jun runs at six, before the day has an
  opinion of him. The kitchen bench holds his sourdough starter, Duration, in a jar with a date on masking tape. He
  has fed it every day since March and can't leave it alone, which is the problem. A thing you feed every day and can't leave is how he treats his model, and why he works alone at night.
- **Canley Vale (Kai).** The phone shop on the main street, where Kai works afternoons under the fluorescent lights
  next to a wall of cases. His mother's real-estate signs are in the boot of the car. Bà watches Vietnamese dramas
  at a volume the whole street can follow. His father's trading screens are in the back room with the door shut. Kai sells phones on margin and watched his father trade on it. That is why every order he places is a limit order with Felix watching.
- **Glenwood (Amara).** A brick house on a curved street, with her father's chemistry marking on the dining table.
  On Sundays there is the gurdwara kitchen, where Nani's old fund raised money for a new stove and the opening came
  two years late. On Tuesdays she tutors Priya and Leo at the local library at 3:30. Two years late and still a gift: Nani's fund is why Amara tests every sentence by asking what Laura's co-sponsors would feel if it came true late.
- **Marrickville (Tess).** Terraces, planes low overhead, and her father's set-building shed full of half-made
  doors that open onto nothing. The second-hand bookshop on the corner, where she works Sundays, smells of dust and
  oranges. She keeps her mother's court-reporter habit of writing down what people actually said. It is why Keel's decision journal records who said what, word for word, and why the TNA quotes were exact.
- **Between them.** The train. Most of Keel's best ideas started on a platform, because a platform is the only
  place nobody can take your time. The Parramatta River ferry is where Nora goes when she needs to be unreachable
  for forty minutes. Nora decides on the ferry. The big calls of the season (the sail vote, the 2031 wording) are made on the water, then brought back to the Long Room to be argued.

## 2. Fernleigh Selective High School

A co-ed public selective school in the inner west, in red brick with a 1970s wing nobody loves. Fernleigh is good
at debating, decent at music and has never had a team in anything financial reach a final. The main quad has a
Moreton Bay fig that the Year 12s claim, and the library is a three-storey building with a lift that works in theory.

**The top floor is now the Office** (`OFFICE.md`). Until August it was storage: bound magazines, broken projectors
and a staff-only sign. Mrs Lim gave Nora the key on one condition: "Leave it better than you found it." It became
value 5.

**The people of the building**
- **Mrs Lim**, the librarian. Small, unhurried, sixty-one. She says less than anyone in the school and remembers
  everything. She once ran the school's debating program and never says why she stopped. She doesn't go up to the
  top floor, and she knows exactly what happens there.
- **Ms Adeyemi**, economics teacher and Keel's school supervisor. Her role is admin only, as the competition requires:
  she signs forms, books rooms and asks the school for the documentation letter. She never tells them what to buy,
  and when Kai asks her what she would do, she says, "That's your question, and you know it." She trained as an
  actuary before she taught, and she is the first adult to call the plan "liability matching" in front of them.
- **Mr Okafor**, music. Felix has played in his band for three years. He notices absences, and he sends one message
  rather than three.
- **Dr Hartley**, principal. Appears twice a term and always at assemblies. To her, Keel is "the investment club".
- **Bev**, at the canteen. Sells pies to Kai. Somehow the canteen is always open when the Long Room is, and nobody
  can explain it.
- **Hamish Ward**, Fernleigh class of 2025, now first-year commerce at a Sydney university. He was captain of the
  team that didn't submit (section 4). He appears in the story by email, twice.

## 3. The rivals: Spinnaker

**Harbourside Grammar** is a fictional private school on the lower north shore with a rowing shed on the water and
a careers office that books Bloomberg tours. Its Wharton team calls itself **Spinnaker**, after the big balloon
sail a boat flies downwind. They are good. Their captain, **Oscar Lindqvist**, is charming, quick and on every
forum, and he posts their WInS return each Friday night as if it were a score. Spinnaker's plan for Laura is mostly
sail: a heavy stock tilt, a "barbell", and confident numbers for 2031.

Keel meets them once in person: at an inter-school economics evening in the City on Thu 29 Oct, where Oscar asks
Nora what Keel is "up" this month. Nora answers, "We don't keep score that way." She regrets how smug it sounded
before she reaches the lift. After that, Spinnaker is mostly a voice in the Courtroom: Kai plays Oscar, and plays
him well, because part of Kai has always wanted to be on that team.

Spinnaker is not a villain. It is the road Keel didn't take, and the story should make it look attractive. The
Week 3 email says teams are not judged on whether the portfolio makes money (`wharton_email_week3_2026-10.md`),
and Felix has that line ready. Even so, every Friday, Oscar's number goes up and Keel's ladder doesn't, and it
stings.

## 4. The Unsent: the loss Keel is redeeming

Fernleigh entered the competition for the first time in 2025-26. Five Year 12s, captained by Hamish Ward, called
themselves **Northwind**. They added one Year 10 for "the trading": Kai.

Northwind was brilliant and late. By December they had a 31-page Final Report, a model nobody but Hamish
understood, and an idea Kai still thinks was the best in the competition. On the last night they decided, at
2 a.m., to rewrite the summary. They worked to Sydney's clock and forgot that the deadline was in New York time.
When Hamish pressed submit, the portal had closed. Forty-seven minutes late.

Nobody at school talked about it. The draft report sits in the Archive's top drawer in a red folder labelled, in
Hamish's handwriting, *FINAL FINAL v9 (unsent)*. Kai brought it in August and put it in the drawer without a word.
Tess is the only one who has read it all the way through.

This is what Keel is quietly about:
- Felix's **two clocks** are the Unsent's lesson in physical form. He hung them before he knew the story.
- Value 1 ends with "deadlines are promises too". Amara wrote that line, but Kai argued for it.
- Kai has been the most careful hands in the room (the button rule, the screenshots) because he has seen what
  brilliance does without a clock.
- The folder is never quoted, and none of its numbers are used. Keel's rule is to build its own plan. The red folder
  shows them only what failure looks like.

Wharton reports that more than 6,300 teams registered in 2025-26 and about 2,300 submitted final reports
(`CLAUDE.md`, Key facts). Northwind was one of the other four thousand. Keel intends not to be.

## 5. Why these six, and what each stands to lose

Nora built Keel one pitch at a time (`ARCS.md`, the founding). All six want the semifinal; each needs it for a
different reason, and that reason shapes how they work.

| | What the semifinal would prove | What they'd lose if Keel misses it | The private stake nobody says aloud |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nora** | A team that decides well, together, on time | The proof that she can lead without steamrolling | The fundraiser: she announced $10,000 and raised $6,400. She will not announce a number again |
| **Felix** | Being right in time to matter | The belief that care is worth what it costs | The uniform review he won after the policy was printed. Rightness that arrives late is just a footnote |
| **Jun** | Numbers that mean something to someone | The excuse that he is "just the calculator" | Hana, who studies medicine because their parents hoped she would. Jun doesn't want to be the next safe choice |
| **Kai** | One bold, true idea, delivered | His second chance after the Unsent | His father's leveraged week. If Kai is reckless, he is his father; if he is timid, he is no one |
| **Amara** | Words that move people and are true | Trust in her own sentences | Nani's kitchen fund, which opened two years late and was still a gift. Promises can be late and kind, or on time and cold. She wants on time and kind |
| **Tess** | To be a maker, not just the person who writes things down | The folder of things she saw and didn't say | Her mother's line, "memory lies; the record doesn't", and her fear that a record is all she'll ever be |

## 6. Motifs, symbols and recurring images

Use these sparingly. One or two in an episode is enough, and they should do work, not decorate.

- **The keel and the sail.** The plan, and the team. Nora and Felix are keel; Kai and Amara are sail; Jun and Tess
  are the hull that holds both. In the last act, Kai says it the other way round: "The sail is only worth
  something because the keel lets you lean into the wind."
- **The ladder.** Ten rungs on the library ladder; ten payments on Laura's bond ladder. Someone always climbs it to
  fetch a magazine at the moment a rung (a payment) is under discussion. In the final episode, Jun climbs it and
  counts out loud.
- **Two clocks.** Sydney and the room, with New York shown once a week on Felix's phone. Every deadline scene opens
  with a clock face. After the Unsent, a clock is never just set dressing.
- **The door.** The values are on the Long Room door: four in August, five since 17 Oct, value 4 in Tess's hand.
  Whoever touches the door before a vote is about to change their mind.
- **The folder of cut lines** (the Writing Room). Eleven cards so far. The lines come back only when they become
  true; that is the season's slow-burn promise between Amara and Tess.
- **The red folder** (the Archive). The Unsent. It is opened exactly once in the season (Act IV).
- **Jacarandas.** Sydney's student clock: the joke is that when the jacarandas flower, it's too late to start
  studying. They are bare in September, purple through the IPS and the HSC, a carpet on the footpaths by December.
  Each act changes the trees.
- **The southerly buster.** A summer cool change that comes up the coast in an hour and drops the temperature ten
  degrees. Act IV's crisis night has one. Afterwards the air is clean.
- **Small objects that carry people:**
  - **Amara's thermos.** It is there when she is all right and missing when she isn't.
  - **Duration**, Jun's starter. Feeding it is how he says he is fine. Giving it to someone else to feed is how he
    says he isn't.
  - **Tess's film camera.** A roll of 36 that she hasn't developed since August. The prints become the timeline in
    Keel's own Final Report section on its competition experience.
  - **Kai's unposted videos.** Practice for the semifinal's short video presentation (`competition/COMPETITION.md`).
    In the coda he records the first take, and it is about bonds.
  - **Felix's clarinet reed.** It cracks the week he misses band. He replaces it the week he ranks his checks.
  - **Nora's bill calendar.** The first image of the season, and the last.
- **Light.** The skylight goes amber when a stretch has run too long. Deep stretches end at "skylight amber", and
  the six use the phrase as a warning.

## 7a. Pressure: outside load and setbacks

**Outside load (fictional calendar, Sydney dates).** The world knocks on the door at the worst times. When it
does, someone is short of time, the work is re-split out loud, and something is dropped or late.
| Who | What pulls them away | When | What it costs the work |
|---|---|---|---|
| All six | Fernleigh's Year 11 yearly exams (fictional school calendar) | Mon 9 to Fri 13 Nov | The Final Report instructions land at 01:00 on the Tuesday of exam week. The first full draft slips four days, and Nora cuts one planned section |
| Nora | Her mother's night-shift weeks (one in three), so Lina is hers after school; bakery every Saturday | Weeks of 26 Oct and 16 Nov | Meetings move to 4:30 with Lina at the table. Nora hands chairing to Tess twice and hates how well it goes |
| Felix | The band's end-of-year concert, with rehearsals every evening | Week of 16 Nov; concert Thu 19 Nov | The rules gate list for the Final Report is late. Jun runs it from Felix's checklist, and it misses one item Felix would have caught |
| Jun | Exam week, plus the 6 a.m. runs he won't drop | 9-13 Nov | For the first time he can't do the numbers alone. Kai learns to rerun the ladder script, slowly |
| Kai | Extra shifts at the phone shop for the Black Friday sale; his father's "sure thing" | Week of 23 Nov | Misses the first Courtroom session; prosecutes the second one tired, and too hard |
| Amara | Nani in hospital; tutoring on Tuesdays | Mid-November | The co-sponsor text is drafted without her and comes out flat (Act IV) |
| Tess | Lighting the school's end-of-year production: tech week, then three shows | Mon 23 to Sat 28 Nov | The busiest editing week of the season. Amara and Felix do the counts; a formatting slip gets through to a late draft and Tess finds it on the Sunday |

**Setbacks so far (Acts I-II), and the marks they left.** The near mis-click (Ep 5) left the button rule. The
2037 vote was won and then reversed (Ep 8, 10), so the team learned a vote can be wrong. The "guarantees" note can't
be edited and stays in WInS forever; the correcting note sits beside it (Ep 18, 22). Room fell from 42bp to 18bp in
ten days (Ep 9-20), and nobody could do anything about it. Amara forgot her tutoring kids (Ep 17). Felix's "can
wait" pile held the FAQ change for two days (Ep 23, 25). The curly apostrophe nearly broke an exact quote (Ep 26).

**Setbacks to come (Acts III-V).** These beats are planned. The "if" beats happen only if the real data does.
- **Kai loses the sail vote** (Sun 25 Oct) and goes quiet, which is worse than arguing.
- **If the ladder's headroom falls under about $2,000** on a real curve, Keel's own plan leans on its January-gap rule
  for the first time, and the IPS sentence about it has to be rewritten two days before the freeze.
- **The IPS is 520 words on Wed 4 Nov.** The paragraph Amara loves most is the one that goes.
- **Exam week collides with the Final Report instructions** (above). The plan slips, and the slip goes on the
  Noticeboard in Nora's handwriting.
- **A number in Keel's own Final Report draft is on a stale curve.** Felix finds it after the team has called the
  section done. It's fixed the same day, and it goes in the decision journal as a mistake, not a typo.
- **Spinnaker gets praised in public** (a forum post, a teacher's comment), and for a week the team doubts the keel.
- **The southerly night** (Thu 3 Dec): the power flickers and forty minutes of unsaved work are gone.
- **The ending isn't promised.** The semifinal list is not out when the season ends, and the story never says Keel
  made it.

**The bigger storms (creative beats, each tied to the work).** Busy weeks wear a team down. These are the turns
that break something open. Use at most one per act. Each one has to change a vote, a page or how the team works.
- **The floor is taken back.** In the first week of November, the school announces that the library's top floor
  will be emptied over the summer for a refurbishment, starting the Monday after the Final Report. A facilities
  manager starts measuring the Long Room while they work. Keel has a deadline the competition didn't set: everything
  worth keeping has to fit in the Archive's grey cabinet. Tess decides what goes in it, and the team learns what its
  record actually is.
- **The laptop on the 389.** Jun leaves his laptop on the bus the night before the IPS freeze. The models are in the
  repo, but his unsaved notes on why each margin was sized that way aren't. Kai rebuilds the reasoning from the
  decision journal in front of Jun, and realises for the first time how much of the plan lives only in Jun's head.
  The house rule after that: if it isn't written down, it doesn't count.
- **Kai thinks about leaving.** After he loses the sail vote, Spinnaker's Oscar messages him: they want "a real
  trader". For four days Kai doesn't answer, and he doesn't tell anyone. Jun finds out from Kai's face. The Courtroom
  session that week is Kai prosecuting Keel's own plan as if he were already on Spinnaker. It's the best case anyone
  makes against the keel all season, and his answer to Oscar is a single line: "We're not that kind of team."
- **Felix's dad wants him in Hong Kong for the summer, from 1 December.** His flight is booked for the morning of
  Fri 4 Dec, the day Keel submits. He offers to cancel it; Nora says no, and the team brings its own deadline forward
  to the morning of Fri 4 Dec, so Felix's last check happens before he boards. Felix reads both clocks one last time at the airport, and
  he is the one who sends the "submitted" message, from the gate.
- **Amara's line gets stolen.** A sentence of hers from a public forum post Kai once made ("a promise you can price
  is a promise you can keep") turns up in Spinnaker's pitch, word for word. The team wants to call it out. Amara
  doesn't. She writes a better line instead, and that's the one in Keel's Final Report.
- **Nora loses her voice.** Literally: laryngitis in the last week of November. For four days she can't chair, can't
  argue and can't read "decision, owner, date" aloud. She writes on the whiteboard, and the meetings get shorter
  and better. She learns the team doesn't need her voice. It needs her calendar.
- **The mistake that went out.** Keel's own IPS, already submitted, has a sentence whose number Jun later finds was
  on a stale curve. It can't be fixed. The question for the Final Report is whether to say so. They do, in one plain
  sentence, and it becomes the most honest line in their articulation section.
- **Two clocks, one wrong.** On Sun 1 Nov the New York clock on the wall is still on daylight time, because nobody
  but Felix knows how to change it and Felix is at a concert. For a day, the countdown on the Noticeboard is an hour
  wrong. Nobody gets hurt, but Kai turns white, because that is the exact mistake that sank Northwind.

**The rule for a setback:** it has a believable cause, it costs something real (time, a line, a vote, a night's
sleep, a friendship moment), the fix is partial, and it leaves a mark on how the team works. At least one per act;
most weeks, one.

**Humans too.** Caplet's six real students have school, exams, jobs and families. The office never assumes they
have time. It asks who's short of time in the Sunday stand-up, keeps two-day buffers before every Wharton
deadline, and when someone is busy it proposes a re-split or a smaller scope out loud (`keel/pm/RISKS.md`, R10).
It never nudges in a way that guilt-trips.

## 7. The season: Keel to the semifinal

Dates are Sydney days. Wharton's dates come from `WORLD.md` and the Week 3 email; everything else is fiction. Acts I
and II have happened (Ep 0-26). Acts III to V are the plan, and each beat must still be earned on the day it airs.
A beat may move if the real competition moves. The semifinal announcement date is UNVERIFIED (Wharton says only
"after judging"), so the season ends while they are waiting.

### Act I. The keel (August to Thu 8 Oct) — done
The founding pitches; the name; mission and values on the door (Ep 0); trading opens and Nora says read first
(Ep 1); the bond list's hole (Ep 2); "funds don't mature" (Ep 3); the button rule (Ep 5); Markowitz rejected
(Ep 6); the 2037 rung, voted 4-2 and then re-voted 5-1 (Ep 8, 10); the first orders; the truce (Ep 11).
**Turning point:** the night orders, Wed 7 Oct. Nora was right to wait and Kai was right to reopen, and both
of them know it. From here the plan is real.

### Act II. The long middle (Fri 9 Oct to Sat 24 Oct) — done
The roster; Sharpe and Kelly rejected; the day outside (Ep 13); the halfway journal (Ep 14); "guarantees" found
and sat on (Ep 18); nineteen basis points (Ep 19); value 4 on the door (Ep 20); Caplet's first question comes
through the Window (Ep 21); the deep stretch (Ep 22); the mock judge (Ep 23); the Week 4 email (Ep 25); the last
read and the TNA submitted (Ep 26).
**Turning point:** value 4, Sat 17 Oct. The team learns that saying it late costs more than saying it badly.

### Act III. Freeze (Sun 25 Oct to Sat 7 Nov): the IPS
The jacarandas come out, and the plan has to fit in 500 words and a 50-word pitch.
- **Sun 25 Oct, the Rooftop.** The first Sunday on the roof since the floor became the Office. The sail vote:
  Jun's 25% of the 2028 money against Kai's "more". Kai loses and doesn't reopen it. Nora notices, and it is the
  most worrying thing he has done all season.
- **Tue 27 Oct, the Kitchen at 1 a.m.** Jun answers Nora's question from 21 Oct ("need or afraid?") in front of
  everyone: Hana, medicine, the safe path, and why he works alone. The house rule changes so that nobody works
  alone, and the night shift becomes two people. Jun gives Duration to Amara to feed for a week.
- **Thu 29 Oct, the economics evening.** Spinnaker in person. Oscar's question; Nora's smug answer; the long lift
  ride down.
- **Sun 1 Nov.** U.S. clocks go back. Felix moves the New York face on his phone, and he finds that the gap is now
  16 hours, so the last day's freeze moves an hour. He tells the team in one sentence. That's the first time he has
  ranked something without being asked.
- **Tue 3 Nov, the Courtroom.** The 2031 range wording. Nora wants it closed; Kai is right to keep it open, because
  the bottom of the range isn't fixed. Tess tells Nora, about Nora, on the day: "You're closing it because you're
  tired, not because it's ready." Nora holds the question open overnight on purpose, for the first time.
- **Wed 4 Nov.** Kai brings the leap he has been looking for all season, and it isn't more stocks. It is the honesty
  rule: never tell a co-sponsor a number Laura doesn't already own, applied to the range. It is Nora's old
  fundraiser line, which Kai turns into the plan's spine. Nora sees what he has done and says nothing for a long
  time.
- **Fri 6 Nov, 20:00 Sydney.** Keel submits its own IPS, thirteen hours early. Nobody cheers. Tess puts the receipt
  in the Archive, in the drawer below the red folder.
**Turning point (midpoint):** the IPS freeze. The plan is locked, and the story turns from what Keel believes to
whether Keel can show it.

### Act IV. Instructions (Sun 8 Nov to Sat 5 Dec): the Final Report
The jacarandas drop, and the footpaths go purple and slippery.
- **Tue 10 Nov, 01:00.** The Final Report instructions arrive. Felix's hour (arc 5): he wakes the floor, reads for
  forty minutes, and says, "Three things matter. The rest can wait." He is right. His reed holds.
- **Mid-November, outside.** Someone has to choose between Keel and something outside it (arc 7). It is Amara: Nani
  goes into hospital the week of the co-sponsor draft. Amara chooses Nani. The team rewrites the rota so she
  can, as a rule and not a favour, and drafts the co-sponsor text without her for the first time. The draft comes out
  flat. When she comes back, the team understands what the client lead actually does. Her thermos is missing for a week. Kai brings soup in it when she comes back.
- **Late November, Laura's Room.** Amara writes the co-sponsor text. A line from the folder of cut lines comes back,
  now with Jun's model behind it, and Tess hands her the card without a word (arc 3).
- **Wed 25 Nov, an email from Hamish.** He has heard what Keel is doing. He asks whether they want "the v9 model",
  and Kai says no. That night he opens the red folder for the first time since August and reads it with Tess. They
  find the mistake was never the model. It was the clock.
- **Late November, the Courtroom.** Spinnaker on trial: Kai's best performance as Oscar. The report survives.

## 8. Wharton potential: shown, never said

admits. So each of the six is written as a budding Wharton student, through what they do and how they change, never
through labels. Nobody in Keel says "leadership" or "curiosity" about themselves, and the story never mentions
admissions.

**How Caplet's own writing can carry the same signals** (Caplet's facts, Caplet's events, Caplet's words; nothing
from Keel's story goes in):
- Show a decision and what was given up, not just an outcome. "We chose X over Y because Z; it costs us W."
- Show a change of mind with its cause. A corrected mistake reads as self-improvement. A plan that never changed
  reads as untested.
- Connect fields once, where it earns its place: a concept from outside finance that explains the client better.
- Put the person first. Laura's promises and the community the facility serves should be the reason for every
  number.
- Let the facts claim the strength. Words like "innovative", "rigorous" and "leadership" are claims; the judge
  should come away thinking them without reading them.

## 9. How to use this bible
- **The competition is the story.** Every scene must change the plan, a vote or a page. Life outside Keel appears
  as the cost or the fuel of the competition work, and it can take real time away (section 7a).
- **No smooth ride.** Check section 7a every week: who is short of time, and which setback is due.
- **One episode, one beat.** An episode moves one plot beat and one arc (`ARCS.md`). It doesn't skip ahead, and it
  doesn't play two turning points in one day.
- **Earn it on the day.** A beat above is a plan, not a script. If the real competition, the real curve or a
  Wharton email says otherwise, the story bends.
- **Images do work.** A motif appears when it means something in that scene, never as a signature.
- **Rivals stay fictional.** Harbourside Grammar, Spinnaker and Oscar Lindqvist are invented. They are never based
  on a real school or team, and never Caplet.
- **The usual hard rules hold.** FICTION at the top; no mapping onto Caplet students; Keel's events never go into
  Caplet's deliverables; facts with file:line; numbers labelled MODEL; and Wharton's dates only from Wharton's files.
