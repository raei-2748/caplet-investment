# The stranger sheet (H-025 round 2; written by the chair before any reader ran)

> 17:25, before any reader ran: question 3's items were reworded so they don't quote either text's own words (the first
> wording quoted V54 and would have hinted to readers who get W3 first).

Every reader gets: who they are (one line), the pitch above a full IPS text (FIRST TEXT, read cold), the questions
below, then a SECOND TEXT, then the changed sentences side by side in a random order, then the sentence block. Two of
the four also get the submitted Trading Notes (three notes and reflections) before the first text, because judges read
the three deliverables together. Readers see no file, no room name, no draft number and no model output.

## Part 1, on the FIRST TEXT only (read it once, at normal speed, before answering)
1. List every sentence you didn't understand the first time you read it. Quote it, and say in a few words what you
   couldn't follow. If none, say none.
2. In 2-4 sentences of your own, what does the plan do with Laura's money?
3. In your own words (if you would be guessing, say "guess" and give the guess):
   a. What is the banks' "capital-protected note", and why doesn't the plan use one?
   b. Why would a downturn make the ladder cost more to buy?
   c. The ladder holds some funds as well as bonds. What does one of those funds do at the end?
   d. The line about how much goes into the world index fund says it is "under a tenth" of something: a tenth of what?
   e. The interest that pays for the world index fund: whose interest, and over what stretch of time?
   f. Are "stocks", "shares" and the "world index fund" one thing or different things? Are "the building", "the
      facility" and "the gift" one thing or different things?
4. What can a fall in Laura's shares do to one of her $50,000 cheques? Between 2031 and 2033, where is the money set
   aside for the gap kept?
5. The paragraph that starts "The weak point is reinvestment": say its rule in your own words. Under that rule, do the
   bonds still pay their interest? Does money that was already reinvested stop earning?
6. Once Laura names her range in 2031, can anything still leave one of the cheques short? What?
7. The pitch says the cheques "are a promise". The paragraph about 2031 says "every dollar she promises". What is
   Laura promising in each, and to whom? (Readers with the Trading Notes: what is "her promised minimum"?)
8. Only if the text has these words: "until only a quarter of them is left": a quarter of what, and is it a fixed rule?
   "returning to Laura what the cheques don't need": who gets the money, and in 2036 what money is it?
9. What does the last line, "Do not crush.", ask Laura not to do?

## Part 2, the SECOND TEXT
10. Read it once. List every sentence you didn't understand the first time.
11. For each pair of sentences below (the places the two texts differ, order shuffled): which one did you understand
    on first read, "1", "2" or "same", and why in a few words?

## Part 3, the sentence block (variants of one sentence, order shuffled per reader)
12. Each version of the lock sentence: say it back in one line. Do the bonds still pay their interest? Does money
    already reinvested stop earning? Which version would you act on with the least guessing?
13. "...returning to Laura what the cheques don't need" v "...returning to Laura what no remaining cheque needs": in
    2036, what money goes back to Laura under each? Which is clearer?

Rules for readers: answer only from the texts; don't use finance knowledge the person you play wouldn't have; never
look anything up; keep each answer short.
