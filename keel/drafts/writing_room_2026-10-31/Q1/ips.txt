Laura Gao has one promise and one hope. The promise is $50,000 every January from 2033 to 2042, paid from this portfolio alone with a high degree of certainty. The hope is a gift towards the residency's building in Taiwan.

So we don't invest the promise. We buy it.

In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys all ten cheques – or nearly – as a ladder of Treasury bonds, and Treasury funds that mature like bonds, one rung falling due before each January.

A high degree of certainty is a list of ways to fail, not an adjective. The US government defaults. Or a recession arrives before we buy: it pushes yields down, so the ladder costs more, and it shrinks the advances, speaking and licensing behind the 2028 deposit that must cover the gap. One leak remains: coupons paid early earn less if yields fall. In 2031 we price that shortfall and set it aside from the hope, before she names any gift.

Sell low, by appointment

There is a serious case against all this, and we made it first: our first plan was three-quarters stocks, until our own model sank it. Laura is willing to take thoughtful risks, and in our model, with world stocks at 7.5% a year, a 60/40 portfolio usually ends 2042 almost $200,000 ahead of ours.

But "usually" is the whole trick: about one time in three the 60/40 misses a cheque or breaks its word to co-sponsors; ours, under one in sixty. Laura doesn't owe one ten-year bill. She owes ten one-year bills, and a crash in the wrong year forces a sale at the bottom – selling low, on a timetable printed years in advance.

Most of the 2028 deposit buys Treasuries maturing late in 2032, just enough to grow back into all of it: her floor. What's left – the interest those bonds should earn – buys one world index fund. Banks sell this as a capital-protected note. Ours swaps the bank's option for shares, which can't expire worthless.

Why not keep everything above the cheques in stocks until 2031? In our model that rival usually gives about $4,000 more. But one time in twenty stocks are down a quarter by then, and its gift sinks to about $115,000 while ours holds near $155,000. A floor found in 2031 is a forecast.

In 2031 she gives co-sponsors a range. The bottom is the floor she has owned since 2028. The top is the floor plus half her stocks. The other half stays hers, to sell any day if a building priced in Taiwan dollars comes in dearer.

From 2033 the ten holdings are the operating reserve, and it runs itself down: each turns to cash before its cheque. If anything changes, the cheques come first, then the floor, then the hope. We call the plan Caplet XR, after the extended-release capsule: ten redemptions, one a year, and it only works whole.

Do not crush.
