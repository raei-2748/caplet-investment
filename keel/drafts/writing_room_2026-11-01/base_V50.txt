In January 2033, Laura's portfolio writes the first of ten cheques – $50,000 each, one every January, to fund her Taiwan residency. Nobody else can help. She also hopes to contribute to the building. But the cheques come first.

We call it Caplet XR. To a pharmacist, XR means extended release: a capsule that delivers its dose on schedule, labelled do not crush. Break it open and you ruin how it works. To us, X is ten and R is redemption – ten Treasuries, one redeemed in time for each January.

In January 2027, her first $300,000 buys a ladder of Treasury bonds and funds that mature like bonds, one rung falling due before each cheque. We do not invest toward these payments. We buy them. A Treasury held to maturity pays exactly what it promises, regardless of what markets do in between. That is what "a high degree of certainty" means to us – not a probability, but a purchase.

Laura carries more stock-market risk than she may realise. Her 2028 deposit depends on publishing advances, speaking fees and licensing – income that dries up in exactly the kind of downturn that makes bonds more expensive. If that deposit arrives smaller, the gift shrinks. A cheque funded by a Treasury already bought does not.

Most of the 2028 deposit buys Treasuries maturing in late 2032, sized to grow back to the deposit by maturity. That is her floor: money she will own outright even if every share in the world fell to zero. The rest, roughly the interest those Treasuries are expected to earn, goes into one world index fund. A bank would call this a capital-protected note; we prefer shares, which cannot expire worthless.

In 2031, Laura gives co-sponsors a range for her facility contribution. The bottom is her floor: money she already owns, a commitment rather than a forecast. The top adds half her stocks, valued that day. The other half stays with Laura, liquid any day, whether the building costs more in Taiwan dollars or she simply needs flexibility.

We could put the floor into stocks and chase a bigger gift. But time makes a stock-market loss less likely without making it smaller, and a number said aloud to co-sponsors cannot be quietly halved if markets turn. The building deserves a floor that was bought, not hoped for.

The weak point is reinvestment. If yields fall and stay low, bonds that mature early earn less while waiting, and any gap comes from Laura's kept half before the floor. We accept that. The alternative – exposing the floor to the share market – would swap a small, known weakness for a large, unknowable one.

From 2033, the ladder becomes the operating reserve, unwinding one bond at a time as each cheque is written. Whatever else changes, the order will not: the cheques, then the floor, then the gift. Ten capsules, ten Januaries – every one bought before the first is opened.

Do not crush.
