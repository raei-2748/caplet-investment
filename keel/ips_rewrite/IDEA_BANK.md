# Idea bank: every good idea, and the deliverable it lands in

> Ahaan, 27 Oct (BIBLE Book 9): "Every single good idea you have ... must end up in either the IPS or the Final Report or
> both." This page makes sure none vanish. **IPS** = in the 500 words or the pitch, as a principle (Book 7: framework,
> not calculations). **FR** = Final Report (cited, with numbers). **Both** = the IPS states it, the FR proves it.
> **Out** = dropped, with the reason. Each idea keeps its honest weakness, because Book 9 asks us to critique, not dump.
> Numbers are MODEL (22 Oct curve) unless marked. Sources: `THINKING_LOG.md`, `lab/`, `research/R02`, `R05`, `R11`.
> Keel adds a row the day an idea appears; the synthesis and every draft are checked against this page.

## The spine (what the IPS is built on)

| # | Idea | Where | Why it earns its place | Honest weakness / critique |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Debts paid with debts owed to her.** The ten $50k cheques are fixed, dated debts, so they are bought with Treasuries maturing before each January, before any share | Both | The clearest possible answer to "high degree of certainty"; V25's opener was the panel's favourite line | Every serious team matches the payments; it's table stakes, so it can't be the aha alone |
| 2 | **Zero-order vs first-order release = fixed dollars vs fixed percent.** An endowment spending a fixed % can hold stocks and never run dry; ten fixed-dollar cheques are what sequence risk punishes, so only a matched ladder delivers them. This is XR doing investing work | Both | Turns the motif into a reason; sequence risk was one of four lines blind judges ranked V25 first for | Pharmacology vocabulary must stay light ("dose", "patient" banned in the IPS) |
| 3 | **The diagnosis: after 2031 the cap leaves the contribution with every fall and Laura with every rise** (a covered call nobody is paid for). So the day she names the range, the promised half of stocks moves into Treasuries | Both | Rivals lock out of caution (9 of 12); we lock because we found the asymmetry. The lock itself is standard liability-driven de-risking | The cure is common; only the diagnosis is ours. Don't say "free", "$4,406" or "covered call" in the IPS |
| 4 | **The order of commitments.** Cheques bought 2027, floor bought 2028 (three years before anyone names it), top owned once named. "No number Laura names rests on a share price: each is owned in Treasuries by the day she names it" | Both | A framework that generates every action without a calculation (Book 7) | "Bought on the day" is false; keep "by the day" |
| 5 | **The two-cost range (newsvendor / therapeutic window).** The case names both costs of the 2031 range: too little fails to signal viability, too much damages credibility. Each edge is set where its two costs balance; the top reached ~3 in 4 means we treat over-promising as ~3x worse than under-promising, a choice we defend | Both | Ahaan: "amazing". 0 of 36 rivals have it. Stable at 2.7-3.3 across correlation and a 1pp lower return | With the lock, top-reached rises to ~87-93%, so the IPS states the principle; the FR shows the ratio before and after. Credibility loss is lumpy, which is why the bottom is owned outright |
| 6 | **The donor study.** Raising announced seed money from 10% to 67% of the goal gave a nearly sixfold rise in giving (List & Lucking-Reiley 2002); matching above 1:1 adds nothing (Karlan & List 2007) | Both (IPS uncited: "a bigger lead gift draws more giving") | The evidence for the under-promise cost in #5: why the ratio is 3:1, not 10:1. Ahaan: "absolutely BRILLIANT" | A university capital campaign, not a Taiwan arts residency; external validity is the FR's caveat |
| 7 | **Human capital.** Laura's 2028 deposit comes from her career income, which shrinks in the same slump that makes bonds dearer. So the payments are bought first, from the 2027 money, and the 2028 money carries the risk | Both | 0 of 36 rivals have it; blind judges flagged it | The case gives no detail on her job; stated as a reason, not a forecast |
| 8 | **Certainty defined by where it breaks.** The payments fail only if yields fall more than ~2pp in the ten weeks before the Jan 2027 build AND the 2028 deposit falls below ~$50k (unfunded 0.02% at $50k, none at $75k+; a 2pp two-month fall is ~0.23% of history, all 1980-82) | Both (IPS in words, no numbers) | A certainty claim with an address beats "high certainty"; one of V25's four winning lines | Needs one clean sentence; numbers to the FR |
| 9 | **One rule for "as needs change": cheques first, then the floor and stocks shrink in proportion** (stocks stay ~21% of what's left, the floor's expected interest) | IPS | Answers Book 7's "as the client's needs change" with a rule, not a forecast | – |
| 10 | **The reserve changes itself.** The case asks how the reserve's composition should change "if at all": each holding turns to cash the December before its January; nobody touches anything. One scheduled re-match in Jan 2033, then not at all | IPS | Answers a required item AND carries XR (an extended-release tablet needs nothing after it's swallowed) | The 2033 re-match is a planned stock sale in most paths; say so plainly |
| 11 | **The 2033 re-match with a cushion sized by the forecast's own error.** Projected coupon gap + a cushion into T-bills, from leftover cash, then Laura's half, then the contribution above the bottom only. The bottom is spent on nothing but the gift (99.25% inside; payments >$1k short in ~1.1%) | Both (IPS: the rule; FR: the sizing) | Keeps the TNA line "VT not standing between Laura and a payment deadline" | Cushion lowers top-reached to ~87% |
| 12 | **Coupons buy more of their own bond.** Every published number assumes it; parking coupons in bills loses ~$7.6k even with no rate move | IPS (half a sentence) | A real rule a PM would check | Small; half a sentence at most |
