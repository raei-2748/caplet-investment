## Trial 1 (WO-R6): the day-1 ideas

### (a) Zero-order against first-order release: THE reason for Treasuries, and XR's job
**Prosecution.** Fixed-dollar against fixed-percent withdrawals is retirement canon (sequence risk, endowment spending
rules), and pharmacy words don't make it new. The log overstates it twice. "Certainty is a property of the release law,
not of how much you hold" is false: an overfunded stock pot meets fixed cheques almost always. "Only a matched ladder
delivers zero-order" is false too: an insurer's annuity delivers it. The real reason is plainer: the liability is
nominal, fixed and dated, so its hedge is default-free with the same cash flows.
**Defence.** Add the fact the log left out, the budget, and the argument holds. The ten payments cost about $298,900
against $300,000 (22 Oct, 4bp headroom). Laura has no surplus to buy certainty by overfunding, so at her budget only a
match releases fixed dollars. The endowment contrast also explains her other half: a share-of-what's-left rule can hold
stocks forever, and that is her facility money.
**Verdict: KEEP for the IPS, as the motif's hinge, not as "THE reason".** One sentence, plain words first (a fixed sum
against a fixed share), "zero-order" named once, with the budget fact making it bite. It's the one XR reading that
does investing work: the tablet and the ladder share a design goal. **Costume, but the costume that justifies the
name.** No judge will repeat it as a discovery, though every judge will remember why the plan is called Caplet XR.

### (b) The 2031 range as a newsvendor decision (top reached ~73-77%, so over-promising priced ~3x)
**Prosecution.** It's post-hoc: the "half the stocks" rule came first, and 3:1 was read backwards out of it. "Why
3:1?" "Because our rule implies it" is circular. Credibility loss is lumpy, not linear. Money under the top isn't
lost, because it stays Laura's. And the IPS can't show the maths.
**Defence.** Reverse the direction and the circle disappears. State the ratio as policy, with a reason. Over-promising
breaks trust a later update can't repair, while under-promising costs a weaker signal, and that cost is real: the donor
study found that raising seed money from 10% to 67% of the goal gave nearly six times the giving. So the ratio is 3:1,
not 10:1, and the critical fractile is 1/(1+3) = 25%: set the top where the gift reaches it three times in four. Our
rule lands there, for a reason a co-sponsor can check without a model. The gift is floor + half the fund in 2033,
capped at floor + half the fund in 2031, so the top is reached whenever world stocks are no lower in 2033 than in
2031 (Lab: 72.9-76.7% across six runs, including rates and stocks falling together). The bottom's over-cost is
ruinous, which pushes its fractile to about 0, so we own it. The case names both costs (:110-115). This is a Wharton
operations staple applied to the case's own sentences.
**Verdict: KEEP for the IPS, and it is the strongest idea here.** The log has the direction wrong when it says the
plan "implicitly prices" over-promising at 3x. Write that the ratio is our choice and the top follows from it.
"Newsvendor" can go in one clause; the derivation and the 2.7-3.3 band go in the Final Report. **Aha: yes**, the one
idea here a judge would repeat. Condition: check the historical share of positive two-year world-stock returns
