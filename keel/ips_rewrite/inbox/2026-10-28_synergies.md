   - **Plan change:** re-measure the coupon gap in **January 2031** at that day's yields, and deduct it before the

   - The Courtroom's lock is not needed for this. 2031 is already the date the range is named.
   - Lineage, for the Final Report: Solvency II Art. 77(4) (replicate or reserve), Germany's interest reserve
     (reserve early), and Equitable Life (the promise nobody reserved for).
2. **One storm, named by its cause and a year** (6-0). Laura's defences are the 2027 headroom, the 2028 deposit, the
   2033 re-match and her half of the stocks. All four fail in one kind of slump: her income can shrink while yields
   fall. The nuclear rule counts multiple failures from a single occurrence as a single failure, and certainty is
   really a claim that failures are independent. Name the one shared cause. That is our cause, and the rivals don't
   name it.
   - **Drop the 2028 hold-back.** It works, but it costs about $9k of mean gift (T2).
3. **Laura's half is a standby, called last** (6-0). Kill "the project's second cheque". The kept half covers a full
   inflation top-up in only about 5% of worlds (Model Suite M4). The order of call is written before the money
   exists: cheques, then the bottom, then the top, then her half. Flexibility gets a role, which the panel found
   missing.
4. **The deposit's first dollar buys cheques; the next ones back the coupons** (4-0-2).
   - "More than halves" is wrong. At yields 2 points lower, the cheques break when the deposit is below about $85k,
     a fall of about 43% (T7).
   - When the ladder costs more than $300k (46% of paths), it is always the 2033 rung that waits for the deposit (T8).
5. **Two standards of proof: the floor needs no history, only the top does** (6-0).
   - The bottom rests on a contract. The US rewrote a Treasury term once (the 1933 gold clause, which Laura's bonds
     don't have), and some bills were paid late in 1979.
   - The top is the plan's only number that rests on history. So the US-only odds are never quoted: the US is the
     survivor market.
   - The top's "about 3 in 4" survives a 0.6-point haircut on the stock return everywhere (74-77%). It survives a
     2-point haircut in the house and storm worlds (71-75%) (T4).
6. **The sceptic, answered with a number nobody else has** (4-0-2). Cut the stock return 2 points: our median gift
   loses about $1k, while a 60/40 rival's loses about $41k (T4). Certainty doesn't depend on the forecast. Drop Peters'

## Logic a judge will remember (pick at most one for the IPS; the rest go to the Final Report)
- **"Who is allowed to be wrong?"** The bank, never. The sponsor, in the good case. The cheques are the bank's; the
  gift is the sponsor's. This was the huddle's pick for the IPS: one pass, no set-up.
- **Fukushima.** Most of the emergency generators sat in the flooded basements; the one air-cooled set kept running.
  Count causes, not copies.
- **Equitable Life**, told to its end: a free option (1957-88), no reserve (1993), the Lords rule against it (July
  2000), doors closed (December 2000).
- **Studebaker, 1963-64:** retirees were paid in full, older workers about 15 cents in the dollar, younger ones
  nothing. Laura sets the order before the pot exists. Re-read the Sass PDF before citing.
- **Brady bonds (1989):** the national-scale twin of the capital-protected gift.
- **Pitt's 1786 sinking fund:** a promise to save is not a vault (Ricardo: "derived from the excess").
