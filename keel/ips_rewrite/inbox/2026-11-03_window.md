## 4. W-08b, Felix's primary-source find for Q15 (the stranger round)
SEC Office of Investor Education and Advocacy, *Interest rate risk: When interest rates go up, prices of fixed-rate
bonds fall*, SEC Pub. No. 151 (6/13), https://www.sec.gov/files/ib_interestraterisk.pdf, read Tue 3 Nov 2026 through
the fetch tool (near-verbatim): "If you intend to hold a bond to maturity, the day-to-day fluctuations in the bond's
price may not be as important to you. The bond's price may change, but you will be paid the stated interest rate, as
well as the face value of the bond, upon maturity." And, in brackets, before it uses the word: "interest payments are
called coupon payments". **What the regulator explains first:** prices and rates move in opposite directions; then
that a bond held to the end pays two things, the interest along the way and the face value at the end. **The D
sentences it tests:** P3 "pays exactly what it promises" (CLEAR: the SEC's own claim) and P8 "The ladder costs less
than the cheques because its bonds pay interest along the way, which we reinvest" (GUESS: "costs less than the
cheques", because V54 never says a bond pays two things, so a stranger can't see why interest that arrives early
needs reinvesting). The missing step: the rungs' face values add up to less than the cheques; the interest, paid
years early, makes up the rest. **Not** a source on reinvestment risk: the bulletin's only "reinvest" is about
selling early. No sentence to paste; the words are the Writing Room's.
