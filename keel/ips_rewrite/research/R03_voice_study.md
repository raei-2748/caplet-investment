## Twenty moves in Ahaan's essays that AI prose almost never makes
1. **Opens on a concrete scene or number, not a thesis.** "66 million years ago ..."; the thesis comes third.
2. **Voices the other side in its own words, in short blunt sentences.** "You knew what you were doing. You could have
   done otherwise." Then dismantles it.
3. **Concede properly, then turn on one word.** "does, however, have its critics, and they are not without
   ammunition" ... then "But".
4. **Steals the opponent's metaphor and turns it.** The rising tide lifts all boats, until the boats are "chained to the
   bottom". AI invents its own metaphor; Ray hijacks theirs.
5. **A one-word or one-line paragraph after a long one.** "Miserably." "We do." The rhythm is lumpy on purpose.
6. **Fragments as a list.** "Poorer suburbs. Racially marginalised neighbourhoods." No "including X, Y and Z".
7. **Numerals, even at the start of a sentence.** "3 legitimate goals would survive"; "4 airlines now control 80%".
8. **Names a concept with its owner in passing, then explains it in plain words and uses it.** "what the philosopher
   Galen Strawson called the Basic Argument"; externalities "as conceptualised by economist Arthur Pigou". This is
   north star 4's method exactly: concept, plain meaning, application, no fuss. (The IPS can't name owners; it can
   still name the concept and explain it in one breath.)
9. **Beats an objection with an analogy from another field.** The carcinogen that not everyone reacts to.
10. **Wry, colloquial similes inside a formal register.** "revenge with a wig on"; bailing with "a teaspoon";
    "furniture arrangements on a very small raft"; "hold my beer".
11. **Reversal (chiasmus) as a verdict.** "not between the poor and the planets ... between the planets and continued
    poverty."
12. **Headings are puns on sayings, with a bracketed twist.** "There's gold in them thar asteroids (and platinum)";
    "Show me the money (it's all at the top)"; "Ark not included".
13. **Joking brackets.** "(A non-exhaustive list)"; "(though small)". The bracket is where he winks.
14. **Rhetorical questions, rarely, and answered at once.** "What could possibly go wrong?" (`VOICE.md` bans them; Ray
    uses them. Keep to one, answered.)
15. **Strong words left strong.** "damning", "terrifying", "a resounding no", "to put it gently". He overclaims a
    little and doesn't apologise.
16. **Personal connectives.** "Alas", "Very well.", "Inevitably, then,", "And yet:". AI uses "Furthermore".
17. **Anaphora for the climax.** "We look at the asteroid ... We look at the void ... We look at the future".
18. **The ending calls back to the opening and flips it.** Dinosaurs at the start, "We do." at the end; Animal Farm's
    pigs as the last image.
19. **Claims his own ethos.** "this is not a polemical claim but a mathematical observation".
20. **Small human slips.** A pronoun that doesn't agree, a near-miss word. We don't fake errors, but we stop
    polishing every sentence to the same sheen. A draft where every sentence is 18-24 words is a machine's.

## What still made the TNA read as AI (and what to do instead)
| AI habit (seen in the TNA and v4.1) | Ray's move instead |
|---|---|
| Every sentence the same middle length; every paragraph four sentences | Lumpy: one long, one blunt; a one-line paragraph |
| Each paragraph opens with a topic sentence and ends on a moral | Open on a fact or the other side; end on a turn or a kicker |
| "X, not Y" again and again ("a maturity, not a sale"; "the floor, not a payment") | Use it once. Elsewhere, concede then "But" |
| Abstract nouns doing the work ("funding reliability", "certainty", "flexibility") | A concrete thing: the 2042 bond, the January cheque, the co-sponsor's meeting |
| No voice of the opposition | Say what a sceptic would say, in their words, then answer |
| Careful, symmetrical hedging | One flat verdict; if a risk exists, put a number on it and move on |
| Numbers written out or over-precise | Numerals, rounded: "about $10k", "1 in 3" |
| Metaphor applied evenly, as polish | One image that does work, placed once, called back once at the end |
| Every concept explained at the same length | Some concepts get one word ("pull to par"); the central one gets a sentence |

## How this transplants into 500 words
- Pitch: one turn and one image. No list.
- IPS: open on Laura's real problem (concrete), not "This policy governs". One sceptic's objection voiced and beaten.
  One short paragraph of one or two sentences. One wry line. A last line that calls back to the first and flips it.
- The Final Report carries the punny headings, the anaphora and the cited owners of the concepts.
- How to cut (Ray, 24 Oct): remove a whole idea that repeats, never squeeze a good sentence shorter.
