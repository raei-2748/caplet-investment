## Per entry

### Entry 1
- Investment Strategy 7/10, Client Knowledge 7/10, Portfolio Analysis 6/10, Articulation 5/10, Creativity & Presentation 6/10, Overall 7/10
- Top-50-of-2,300? yes
- The promise-and-growth split is clear enough to repeat to a friend after one read: Treasuries that mature before each cheque cover the ten payments, and world stocks grow the rest. The 2031 answer is direct, with a locked floor, a top that carries a stated confidence level, and a simple facility rule (floor plus at most half of the growth portfolio). ### Entry 2
- Investment Strategy 7/10, Client Knowledge 7/10, Portfolio Analysis 6/10, Articulation 4/10, Creativity & Presentation 8/10, Overall 5.5/10
- Top-50-of-2,300? maybe
- There is real energy and a clear core idea: the promise is bought in advance, the 2028 deposit builds a floor, and only the interest rides on stocks. The "serious case against all this" section shows the team weighed the other side, which I liked. But the writing is compressed in a way that annoyed me: "Sell low, by appointment", "Have your cake and invest the icing" and "Do not crush" are cute, and I had to guess at what they meant. Numbers arrive without explanation, such as "about a fifteenth of the portfolio by our sums", so I took them on trust rather than understanding them. The team reads as a pitch, not as a group that reasoned its way to the plan, so Articulation is thin.

### Entry 3
- Investment Strategy 7/10, Client Knowledge 7/10, Portfolio Analysis 4/10, Articulation 5/10, Creativity & Presentation 8/10, Overall 6.5/10
- Top-50-of-2,300? maybe
- The startup frame made this the easiest entry to picture: runway first, then growth capital, then money released in stages as the residency proves itself. ### Entry 4
- Investment Strategy 7.5/10, Client Knowledge 7.5/10, Portfolio Analysis 7/10, Articulation 6.5/10, Creativity & Presentation 8.5/10, Overall 8/10
- Top-50-of-2,300? yes
- The best mix of a clear story and checkable reasoning. It opens with the first plan that failed in the model, which shows the team learned something. The XR pill is a picture I could explain at dinner, and the "promise and hope" split gives a clear reason for keeping the two apart. ### Entry 5
- Investment Strategy 7/10, Client Knowledge 6/10, Portfolio Analysis 7.5/10, Articulation 4/10, Creativity & Presentation 5/10, Overall 5/10
- Top-50-of-2,300? maybe
- On paper this is the most rigorous entry: explicit assumptions, a named list of accepted risks (gap risk, cash-lock, the 2028 deposit, currency), a stated 95% confidence level for the 2031 minimum, and a clear flexibility rule for 2033. "Floor = Promise" is a good idea and the honest risk list earns respect. But the core is written in the language of a trading desk: a CPPI rule, a cushion, a sleeve, a multiplier. I could follow the pitch but not the mechanism. The "Why Laura" paragraph at the end is too short to bring her back into the picture, and the team says little about how it reached this plan, so Articulation is weak.
- Not understood: "CPPI"; how the floor "accrues at the short Treasury yield"; "stocks held equal three times the cushion"; "gap risk" and "cash-lock"; "sleeve"; what "the high end is the contribution that about half of 10,000 simulated paths would beat" means in practice.

## My overall view
All six give the same basic answer: pay the ten cheques from bonds bought at the start, put the rest in stocks, and make the 2031 range a locked floor plus a modelled top. What separated the best from the rest was whether I could explain the plan to a friend after one reading. Entry 4 let me do that, with a story, a picture I could repeat and odds stated in numbers that meant something to me; Entry 1 was the plainest and the easiest to repeat. Entries 2 and 6 had the same sound idea wrapped in clever lines and jargon I had to guess at, and Entry 5 was rigorous enough to impress a finance reader but lost me in its mechanics. To win, the best entry would need the Caplet XR logic and the honest risk section of Entry 4 and Entry 5, said in plain words, with each number explained once and some sign of how the team actually reasoned its way to the plan.
