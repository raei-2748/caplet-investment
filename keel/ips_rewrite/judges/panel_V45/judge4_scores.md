### Entry 2

### Entry 3
- Investment Strategy 7/10, Client Knowledge 7/10, Portfolio Analysis 8/10, Articulation 6/10, Creativity & Presentation 6/10, Overall 6.5/10
- Top-50-of-2,300? maybe
- This is a real, original idea: the number told to co-sponsors is the number the portfolio is built to protect, enforced by a pre-committed rule. It names its own weaknesses plainly (gap risk, cash-lock, the uncertain 2028 deposit, currency) and explains why they chose a multiplier of three, not five, which is the kind of choice a thinking team makes. It uses honest assumptions with numbers, and the 95% confidence on the minimum answers the case directly. The weakness is complexity: a CPPI rule rebalanced monthly is a lot to hand a client who wants a promise she can count on, and the "floor starts near 60% of the sleeve" detail is unexplained. It also reads a bit like a finance textbook, less like teenagers talking, and the "Floor = Promise" label is more of a slogan than the thinking behind it deserves. The 2031 top-of-range ("about half of 10,000 paths would beat") is a coin-flip top, which is thin for a range meant to avoid over-promising.
- Anything you did not understand on first reading: how the sleeve can start with a floor near 60% when the ladder is supposed to be funded first, and what "present value of Laura's announced minimum" means in practice.

## My overall view
What separated the best from the rest was evidence of changed minds and honest trade-offs: a team that built the aggressive plan, tested it, saw it fail, and then says plainly that the safe plan will usually end up poorer and why they accept that. Entries 2, 5 and 6 share that core, and the order among them is mostly about how much the writing performs; I rate the least performed highest. I rate Entry 3 for being the only genuinely independent line of thinking, though it is heavier than the client needs. Entries 1 and 4 are believable but thin; neither shows the numbers or the rejected alternatives. For Entry 6 to win outright it would need one concrete line saying how much the gift is expected to be (the range, in dollars, with the confidence), a plainer ending, and a little less wordplay. It must also be careful that three near-identical entries do not make the style look like a house formula.
