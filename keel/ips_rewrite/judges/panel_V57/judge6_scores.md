## My overall view
Entry 4 separated itself because it does what the other five only gesture at. It names a rule, states a confidence level for the range the co-sponsors will hear, lists what it accepts and why, and handles liquidity and flexibility as decisions rather than side notes. Its cost is density: a judge has to work to follow the mechanism, and a co-sponsor would not. Entry 1 is the clearest plan and the only one that gives Laura a real growth balance, but it leaves its own 2031 confidence unstated. The Caplet XR family (2, 3, 5, 6) understands Laura better than anyone on the list and argues its reinvestment risk honestly, but it puts under a tenth of the money at risk of growth, gives no range confidence and offers no test of how the range behaves across markets. To win, the best entry would need to keep Entry 4's stated confidence and tradeoffs, Entry 2's client reasoning and Entry 1's growth balance, in plain English a co-sponsor can follow.
