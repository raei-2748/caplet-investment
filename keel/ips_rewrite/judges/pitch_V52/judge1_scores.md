# Pitch judge 1

## Ranking (1 = best)
1. Pitch 2 - the only one that ties the strategy to Laura's real worry: "When she names a range in 2031, every dollar is already in bonds."
2. Pitch 3 - same plan as Pitch 2, said more directly ("we don't invest for them. We buy them"), but it drops the 2031 credibility point.
3. Pitch 1 - clear and memorable ("Stocks move the ceiling. Never the floor."), though it never says why the floor matters to co-sponsors.
4. Pitch 4 - the plainest central idea (buy the promise first, then grow the leftover), but "honest upside" and "guaranteed minimum" read like a sales line, and the 2028 deposit is missing.
5. Pitch 7 - a warm, human opening ("hopes for a gift"), but "repay it in 2032" is hard to follow and "it" is ambiguous.
6. Pitch 5 - accurate and tidy, but it reads as a list of mechanics, and I lost interest by the third sentence.
7. Pitch 6 - the 2031 framing is the right client point, but "stock-or-Treasury rule" is never explained, so I could not say what the strategy does.

### Pitch 2

### Pitch 7
- Substance 7/10, Client fit 7/10, Makes me read on 7/10, Sounds like a real person 8/10

There is also no mention of how big the gift could be or how sure she can be about it.

## Which one sounds most machine-written, and why (one paragraph)
Pitch 4 sounds the most machine-written to me. Its structure is a tidy three-part build ("a promise, so we buy the promise first", "a ladder ... held to the end", "a guaranteed minimum and an honest upside"), and the closing phrase "honest upside" is the kind of balanced flourish a generated summary tends to reach for. Pitch 1 has a similar slogan quality ("Stocks move the ceiling. Never the floor."), but it is short and specific enough to pass as a student's choice. Pitch 5 is the most formulaic in its sentence pattern, with three sentences that each open on a verb and a noun phrase, but that reads more like a careful student than a machine. None of the seven read as a brochure, so the real question is whether a human student would make these exact rhetorical moves, and Pitch 4 is where I would be least sure.
