### Entry 2

- Top-50-of-2,300? maybe

### Entry 4
- Investment Strategy 4/10, Client Knowledge 5/10, Portfolio Analysis 4/10, Articulation 5/10, Creativity & Presentation 5/10, Overall 4.5/10
- Top-50-of-2,300? no
- The "Lock, then Launch" split is a sound and readable idea and the ladder section is clear. The rest undermines it. A third of the growth money goes to QQQ, semiconductors and a Taiwan ETF on the stated conviction that AI will drive the next decade. That is a stock-picking story, not a diversified strategy, and Wharton says the goal is not simply to pick what will perform. The weak point is that nowhere does it say what happens if the satellites fall.

## My overall view
What separated the top two for me is that they argue from Laura's situation instead of from a template. Her 2028 money is uncertain, her word to co-sponsors is her currency, and the ladder has one real weak point (reinvestment) which they name and price. Those entries also tell me what they gave up by choosing the safe floor. The middle entries are sound but either over-engineered (the CPPI rule) or generic (an 80/20 mix). The last one picks a theme and calls it a strategy. I do distrust the XR branding and the slogan ending in the top two; they read a little like a brochure, and a judge who dislikes cleverness could mark them down. To win, the best entry would need to show a bit of the team itself: one thing they tried, got wrong or changed their mind about. It would also need to state its confidence in the 2031 range in plain numbers, and to drop the slogans in favour of one more plain sentence of reasoning. Note that Entries 1 and 3 share a pitch and most of their text, which I treated as two close versions of the same idea.
