## My overall view
The two best entries both answer the question Wharton asked: what the operating commitment is, how it is funded with high certainty, what the facility can responsibly give, and how the portfolio moves as the dates approach. They differ in what they show. Entry 1 shows its numbers and its failure modes; Entry 5 shows the one weak point of its own plan and prices it. The rest either sit a single growth fund with no assumptions (Entry 2), rest on a forecast with a concentrated growth sleeve (Entry 3), or are well ordered but do not show outcomes (Entry 4). What the best entry would need to win is a plain statement of the growth-risk balance and why its growth share is that size, the 2033 and 2031 amounts at a stated confidence level, the reinvestment gap given as a number, and a plainer description of its growth rule. Entries 5 and 6 are almost identical in the pitch and most of the IPS; I judged each as written and did not try to infer which team wrote which, or whether they share one. No entry says much about the team's own learning, so Articulation is low across the board and I did not let it move the ranking.
