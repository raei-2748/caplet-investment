# Pitch judge 1

## Ranking (1 = best)
1. Pitch 4: one plain sentence says the promise is bought first, then the leftover goes to growth, with a co-sponsor minimum and an honest upside.
2. Pitch 8: short and plain; "Caplet XR doesn't invest the promise" is a line I remember, and it covers the 2028 deposit and the gift.
3. Pitch 3: the only pitch that ties the strategy straight to Laura's 2031 promise to co-sponsors, and it names the flexibility she wants.
4. Pitch 1: clear structure with a useful risk line ("only a long fall in interest rates could shrink the floor").
5. Pitch 5: plain and correct, with the right dates, but the strongest idea (the floor, then stocks) is said less crisply.
6. Pitch 2: nearly the same as Pitch 1, with a punchier close ("Never the floor") that promises more than the strategy can guarantee.
7. Pitch 6: the capsule opening spends the first sentence on the name, not the strategy; the analogy is cute but distracting.
8. Pitch 7: "Ten Lanterns" reads like a brochure headline; the idea that the gift is sized from the surplus alone is good but gets lost.

## Which one sounds most machine-written, and why (one paragraph)
Pitch 7 sounds most machine-written. It opens with a headline ("Ten Lanterns:"), uses a stacked metaphor ("lit by a Treasury bond ... as each payment falls due"), and closes on a tidy, balanced line ("so no promise outruns the money") that reads like a generated summary of a strategy rather than a student explaining a plan. Pitch 2 is a close second, because "Stocks move the ceiling. Never the floor." has the same over-neat symmetry, and the pair of sentences reads like a template filled in twice.
