## My overall view
The entries mostly agree on the design: pay the promise with Treasuries bought up front, put the leftover into one world fund, and set the 2031 range from a protected floor. Three of them share almost the same text, so the separation comes from the details the case asks for and the entries leave out. The case asks the team to state how confident it is in the range and to name its assumptions. Entry 4 does both with figures and the others do one or neither. The second thing I weigh is whether the entry shows the trade-off behind a decision, which Wharton says judges want. Entry 6 does this best in plain words, and Entry 1 comes close. The best entry would need to keep the plain reasoning of Entry 6, state a confidence level for the range the way Entry 4 does, and explain the reinvestment gap with a number that a reader can check.
