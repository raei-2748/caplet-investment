## My overall view
What separated the best from the rest was whether I could say what Laura gets, and what could go wrong, after one read. The strongest entries put the ten payments on a bond ladder bought in 2027, then treated the growth fund as the part that can fall. Entries 5 and 6 did that most clearly, and both explained why the team buys the promise first: Laura's own income is stock-like, and a fixed-dollar debt forces a sale in a crash. The weakest, Entry 4, used a familiar idea but left the promise partly exposed to stocks. To win, the best entry would need to put its 2031 range and the confidence in that range in plain numbers inside the IPS rather than deferring them, and to show where the money for the floor comes from. Several entries also need one line less mechanism per rule, because a few of the rules cost me more than one read, and a non-finance judge will not give a second read.
