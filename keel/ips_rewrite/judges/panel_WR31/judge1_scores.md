## My overall view
The best entries answered the question I cared about in plain words: what happens to Laura's ten payments if the market goes wrong, and what she can promise co-sponsors without breaking her word. Entry 1 did that most cleanly and gave me one idea I could retell, which matters more to me than a list of rules. Entry 4 had the most honest numbers and risks, but I could not explain its mechanics to anyone afterwards, so it lost ground with me. Entries 1, 2 and 5 share the same pitch and most of the same sentences, so my ranking among them is a judgment about small edits, not three different strategies; a panel should treat them as one view. To win, the best entry would need a stated confidence level for the 2031 range in plain English, one clear line on what the team gives up, fewer technical terms per paragraph, and a pitch that matches the body. It would also need to show how the team learned, since Articulation was thin across the set.
