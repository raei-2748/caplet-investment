## My overall view
The best entries in this batch were the ones that admitted what the strategy gives up. Entries 6, 2 and 5 all concede that the rival does better on average and then argue the risk. That is the trade-off judges are asked to look for, and it is easy to follow when it is said in plain terms. Entry 6 did that best, with a clean comparison and a line I would repeat. The problem is that three of six entries read almost the same for most of their length, so tonight I had to remember which was which by one number. The lesser entries were either clear and forgettable (1) or original and vague about money (3), or rigorous and too dense for a non-specialist to follow (4). To win, the best entry would need one plain line that makes its choice obvious, a number for the 2031 confidence, the cost of its choice stated next to its benefit, and its own voice rather than a shared template.
