2. Entry 4 - the most memorable idea (runway first, then staged growth money), though the stock mix and the staging are hard to pin down.
3. Entry 5 - clear on the floor and the "bank note minus the bank" idea, but it reads like a clever version of Entry 3.
4. Entry 6 - the most thorough on Laura as a person (ability versus willingness to take risk), but dry and heavy going.
5. Entry 1 - sharp and quotable, but the wordplay and compression hide the mechanism from a non-specialist.
6. Entry 3 - strong numbers, but the floor and the 2028 deposit logic left me lost.

## My overall view
The three entries I would recommend tomorrow (2, 4 and 5) each make one simple promise and then stick to it: the payments are bought first and locked, and the growth money is kept apart from them. What separated them from the rest was that a non-specialist could repeat the plan after one reading. Entries 1 and 3 had sharp ideas and more clever compression, and I lost the mechanism in the wording, and Entry 6 had the most careful client reasoning but read like a policy and never gave me a reason to remember it. The best entry would need the clarity of Entry 2, the memorable framing of Entry 4, and the clean bank-note explanation of the floor from Entry 5, along with a stated confidence level for the 2031 range, a plain dollar range, and at least one sentence from the team about what it actually learned along the way.
