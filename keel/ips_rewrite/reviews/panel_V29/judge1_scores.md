## (a) Which entry I would advance and why
I would advance Entry A. Both entries share an excellent cash-flow-matched design with a clear thesis, sound correlation reasoning (the 2028 deposit as risky human capital) and an honest treatment of the range's asymmetric cost. A wins because it obeys its own rule: the coupon-reinvestment gap is closed in 2031, before the range is promised, and any shortfall lowers the quoted bottom, whereas B lets a risk it has identified "trim the gift" after the gift has been promised.

## (b) Sentences that are false, unclear or read machine-written

**Entry A**
- "Ten independent bets, each 99% safe, would miss a cheque one decade in ten" – arithmetic correct (1 − 0.99^10 ≈ 9.6%), but "Ten" is capitalised after a colon, and the "99% safe" figure appears from nowhere.
- "sized to regrow what's left by 2033 and backing the cheques until she names a range" – unclear: what is "what's left", and how does a floor sized for the gift "back the cheques"? The reader has to infer that it is a second layer behind the ladder.
- "Too high, and she breaks her word, which we judge three times worse." – an unexplained precise multiplier; reads as asserted, not derived.
- "To a pharmacist, XR means extended release – a dose let out on schedule, labelled do not crush." – charming but decorative; with the "do not crush" callback it reads as a crafted device rather than reasoning, and costs ~35 words.
- "the gap today's yields imply, plus a cushion, moves from spare cash and her half into the first cheque's fund" – the mechanism is right but "spare cash" and "her half" (of the stocks) are not defined at this point; dense.
- "floor and stocks are bought smaller together" – awkward phrasing; B's "shrink together" is clearer.
- "Every team will promise Laura her ten cheques. We would rather buy them." – a strong opening, but a judge may find the dig at "every team" presumptuous.

**Entry B**
- "Now the odd part:" – a signposting tic that reads machine-written.
- "Keep the seed; risk only the harvest." – aphoristic flourish; reads polished-by-machine and slightly misdescribes the design (the seed is the Treasury floor, the "harvest" is its expected interest, which is what A says plainly).
- "Ours shuts the first three." – a claim without support in the text (e.g. "guarantees nobody priced" is shut how?).
- "So in January 2033 the reserve changes once: any projected shortfall, plus a cushion, moves into the next fund to mature" – contradicts the rule "each promise is made safe no later than the day it becomes one", since the gift was promised in 2031; also "projected" in January 2033 is odd when the cheques start that month.
- "which we judge three times worse" – same unexplained multiplier as A.
- "a building promised only from money already in Treasuries" – loose: half the stocks were moved to Treasuries on naming, so true, but only if read with the earlier paragraph.
