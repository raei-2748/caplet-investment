## 2. Entry notes

**Entry 01**
- Remembered: "We therefore define risk not as volatility, but as the chance of missing a payment or overpromising to co-sponsors."
- Strength: it covers every required point in order: objective, risk/return, reserve, growth, glide path, 2031 range, flexibility, constraints.
- Weakness: it reads like a template. Client knowledge stops at "entrepreneur, irregular income", and nothing in it could only have been written for Laura.
- Advance: maybe (unlikely; it does not stand out).

**Entry 02**
- Remembered: "a crash in the gutter changes the facility, never the commitment."
- Strength: the four rules are clear and can be checked.
- Weakness: the comic-book device takes space that the analysis needed. A 15% single-country Taiwan ETF slice described as "deliberately small" is not small, and it puts the stocks in the same market as the facility's own costs. The IPS is 501 words, over the limit.
- Advance: no.

**Entry 03**
- Remembered: "Investors never fund a startup's full plan on day one; they fund milestones."
- Strength: an original framework. Staged tranches act as real options, so Laura never promises money she has not secured.
- Weakness: an analysis error. It adds 25% QQQ *because* "Laura's own career sits in technology and creative industries", which concentrates her risk with her own income instead of offsetting it. It also puts a tranche in 2034-35 that depends on the residency already running, which does not fit a facility contribution the co-sponsors must plan around.
- Advance: maybe.

**Entry 04**
- Remembered: "Inflation can make $50,000 buy less. It cannot make $50,000 cost more."
- Strength: the best client and risk analysis in the field. It shows Laura's 2028 deposit (advances, speaking, licensing) is correlated with the stock market. It names the exact conditions under which the plan fails ("yields falling more than two percentage points before the purchase while her 2028 deposit more than halves"). It builds the facility amount as a floor plus upside with an explicit asymmetric cost of overpromising, and it admits its own soft spot (coupon reinvestment) with a fix.
- Weakness: the pharmacist "XR / do not crush" device and the plan's brand name answer none of the five points. The text is dense: "IBTM", "iBond Treasury fund" and "capital-protected note with the bank taken out" each need rereading by a non-specialist. It names individual products where a framework was asked for.
- Advance: yes.

**Entry 05**
- Remembered: "never let Laura announce a number her portfolio cannot keep."
- Strength: the most original view of the client's world. Splitting the contribution into a committed lead gift and a conditional matching pledge turns market uncertainty into a fundraising tool.
- Weakness: the reserve is held in IEF (duration around 7 years) against payments of around 10 years until 2031, with only a 5% margin. That does not support the claim that payments are met "even in ... a large fall in interest rates". "97% in our simulations" is a weaker standard than matching, and the instructions ask for a framework, not a model output. "Fundraising research consistently finds" is asserted without detail.
- Advance: maybe.

**Entry 06**
- Remembered: "stock exposure = 3 × cushion".
- Strength: a real, explained decision rule that de-risks automatically.
- Weakness: a rule problem. In 2027 the cushion is about zero, so almost no stocks are held. After the 2028 deposit (cushion of roughly $150k) the rule asks for about $450k of stocks, more than the whole portfolio. That cannot be done without leverage, which is banned, and no cap is stated. CPPI's "cash-lock" risk is not mentioned, and client knowledge is one closing paragraph.
- Advance: maybe (lower end).

**Entry 07**
- Remembered: "Good markets will not raise it, and we say so plainly."
- Strength: the most internally consistent case for certainty. It treats the payments as a liability, reasons about the 2022 gilt crisis correctly, and takes the trade-off openly.
- Weakness: no growth at all, so W3 ("balance growth ...") and diversification are weak, and facility inflation is left fully unhedged. It is 502 words, over the 500 limit.
- Advance: maybe.

**Entry 08**
- Remembered: "measures what she owes before deciding what she can give."
- Strength: the clearest governance. It has an annual review, a 105% funding-ratio trigger, and duration that steps down as payments approach.
- Weakness: matching ETFs by duration is not holding to maturity. The 105% rule cannot be met in 2027, when the liability's PV is about $300k against a $300k deposit, and the text never admits this. Two sections are numbered "8". Client knowledge is one sentence.
- Advance: maybe.

**Entry 09**
- Remembered: "We are not quoting a model's probability; we are removing the market from the question."
- Strength: a clean, coherent framework. It has a matched ladder, a minimum locked with the 2028 deposit, one global fund left alone, a range from the floor to the floor plus a share, and a flexibility buffer. Each of the five points is answered.
- Weakness: it overclaims. "Every rung pays its known value whatever happens to ... interest rates" ignores coupon reinvestment and the fact that a fund's payout at maturity is not fixed, yet it says the only risks are default and execution. The client section is thin.
- Advance: maybe (upper end).

**Entry 10**
- Remembered: "We never sell ink to buy pencil."
- Strength: a clear glide path (a third of the growth portfolio moved each year from 2031) and stated rules.
- Weakness: it deliberately leaves rungs unbought in 2027 "to leave room to grow". That leaves the payments exposed to falling rates until 2028 and contradicts its own definition of certainty. It is the second graphic-novel entry.
- Advance: no.

**Entry 11**
- Remembered: "Data, not guesses."
- Strength: it states its assumptions and admits model risk.
- Weakness: several errors. The payments are not secured until 2031. The reserve holds 15% equities and is sized for 95% probability, which does not meet a "high degree of certainty". The facility amount is "the 25th percentile of its simulated 2033 value" at a date when the value is already known. Two annual 15-point moves take 60% equity to 30%, not "about 25%". The detailed calculations are exactly what the instructions say to leave out, and there is no client insight.
- Advance: no.

**Entry 12**
- Remembered: "a bad market can shrink the garden, never the house."
- Strength: the only entry that treats liquidity explicitly. It has plain, honest trade-offs ("We chose a smaller garden over a shaky foundation") and three clear rules.
- Weakness: it is the generic consensus plan in a house metaphor, with no original insight about Laura.
- Advance: maybe.

**Entry 13**
- Remembered: "Laura contributes the locked floor plus at most half of the remaining growth portfolio, capped at the top of her announced range."
- Strength: the most complete and correct version of the consensus plan. It has explicit 2031 and 2033 decision rules and stated assumptions.
- Weakness: it is interchangeable with 01, 09 and 12. The title-like slogan does no work, and nothing here is a new idea.
- Advance: maybe.

## 3. Sameness

Entries 01, 09, 12 and 13 (and, under metaphors, 02 and 10) are the same plan. They match the ten payments with a held-to-maturity
Treasury ladder, invest the rest in diversified stocks, move the bottom of the range into short Treasuries around 2031,
present the range as a locked floor plus upside, and hold part back for flexibility. They all define "high certainty" as "only a
US default could break it". 02 and 10 even share the same graphic-novel pencil/ink device. 07 and 08 share the
pension-fund liability-driven investing frame. Six or more entries call Laura "an entrepreneur who takes thoughtful risks".

## 4. Top 3

1. **Entry 04 (84).** It is the only entry with insight a judge could not have predicted. The 2028 deposit is correlated with the
   stock market, so buying the payments first is a hedge, not timidity. It states exactly where its certainty breaks. The
   sequencing argument (fixed-dollar withdrawals turn a crash into forced selling) explains why the payments are funded
   first. It sets the floor and the upside using an explicit asymmetric cost of overpromising. It names its own weak point and fixes it.
   It wins on substance despite style devices that do not earn their words.
2. **Entry 09 (70).** It is the cleanest, most disciplined statement of the consensus plan. The floor is set with the 2028 deposit, a single
   global fund is held untouched, and the line "removing the market from the question" defines certainty well. It sits above 13 for
   its clearer reasoning on certainty and a simpler framework.
3. **Entry 05 (69).** It has the best understanding of what the facility contribution is *for*: a lead gift that signals
   commitment, plus a conditional match. It is the most original reading of Laura's co-sponsor problem. Its reserve design is
   weaker than the others', which is the only thing keeping it from second place.

## 5. What the winning IPS would have to do

It would keep Entry 04's analytical spine (the correlation of the 2028 deposit with markets, a precise failure condition, the
asymmetric cost of overpromising, an admitted soft spot) but write it in plain sentences that a non-specialist reviewer reads once,
with no products named. It would add Entry 05's view of the facility contribution as a fundraising signal, and lay out
the decision rules for 2027, 2028, 2031 and 2033 as clearly as Entry 13. It would also name liquidity and flexibility as explicitly as
Entry 12. No entry joins a non-obvious client insight, a stated breaking point and plain, checkable rules in under 500 words
without decoration. That combination would stand out among 6,000 entries.
