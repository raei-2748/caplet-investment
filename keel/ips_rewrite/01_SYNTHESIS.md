## 1. The central idea (one sentence a judge could repeat)

**Every promise Laura makes is made safe on or before the day it becomes a promise.**

- The ten cheques are promises from the first page of the case, so they are bought first: Treasuries maturing before
  each January, in 2027, before a single share.
- The bottom of the 2031 range is bought in 2028, three years before she names it.
- The top becomes a promise only on the day she names it, so that is the day it moves into Treasuries.
- Until something is a promise, it's a hope, and hopes may carry risk: that is where the stocks live.

Why this and not the lock: 9 of 12 rivals lock stocks into bonds around 2031. None explains WHY all three dates fall
where they do. The birthday rule does (bank #34), and the diagnosis supplies the reason the 2031 date matters at all:
under a capped range, from 2031 the contribution keeps every fall and hands every rise to Laura (#3). A price trigger
is a guess about markets; a birthday is a fact about the promise. The builder who buys timber when the contract is
signed, not when timber looks cheap, is the picture.

XR fits it without strain: extended release means each dose is fixed when it's made and let out on schedule. X = ten
cheques. The R decision is in section 5.
