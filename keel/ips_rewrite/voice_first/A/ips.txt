Laura Gao has one promise and one hope, and most of the ways her money could go wrong begin with confusing the two. The promise is $50,000 every January from 2033 to 2042, paid from this portfolio alone "with a high degree of certainty". The hope is a gift to her residency's facility in Taiwan.

So we don't invest the promise. We buy it.

In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys all ten cheques – or nearly – as a ladder of Treasury bonds and Treasury funds that mature like bonds, each paying out before its own January. Any rung could be sold any day. None will be.

Only two things can break a cheque. One is a US government default. The other is a recession that arrives before we buy: it pushes yields down, which makes the ladder dearer, and it shrinks the advances, speaking and licensing behind her 2028 deposit, which has to fill the gap. Even then, in our model, after a two-point fall a deposit of about $125,000, not $150,000, still fills it.

Sell low, by appointment

There is a serious case against all this, and we made it first: our first plan put three-quarters of her money in stocks, until our own model sank it. Laura has been "willing to take thoughtful risks", and in our model, with world stocks at 7.5% a year, a 60/40 portfolio usually ends 2042 almost $200,000 ahead of ours.

But "usually" is doing a lot of work there: about one time in three, by the same model, the 60/40 lands below its own range or misses a cheque. Laura doesn't owe one ten-year bill; she owes ten one-year bills, and a crash in the wrong year forces a sale at the bottom – selling low, on a timetable printed years in advance. As for her taste for risk, she has already spent it on the big one: the residency.

Have your cake and invest the icing

Most of the 2028 deposit buys Treasuries maturing late in 2032 that grow back into all of it, and the interest they should earn, about a fifteenth of the portfolio by our sums, goes into one world index fund. A crash costs her icing, never cake. Banks sell this as a capital-protected note. Ours is the same note, minus the bank (and its fee).

A bird in the hand, half the bush

The range she names to co-sponsors in 2031 starts at the floor she has owned since 2028 and tops out at the floor plus half her stocks. The other half stays hers, to sell any day if a building priced in Taiwan dollars comes in dearer.

From 2033 the ten holdings are the operating reserve. Each turns to cash before its cheque, so the reserve runs itself down with nothing to rebalance. We call the plan Caplet XR, after the extended-release capsule that works only when swallowed whole.

Do not crush.
