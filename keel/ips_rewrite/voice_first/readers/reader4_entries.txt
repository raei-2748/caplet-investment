===== Entry 1 =====
PITCH:
Laura owes her residency $50,000 every January for ten years, and hopes to add a gift for the building. Caplet XR buys all ten cheques first, with Treasuries that mature in time. Her second deposit backs the gift with bonds, and only the interest rides on world stocks.

IPS:
Laura has one promise and one hope. The promise is $50,000 every January from 2033 to 2042, paid from this portfolio alone. The hope is a gift for the building. They need different kinds of money, so we keep them apart.

We started with three-quarters in stocks. Our model sank it: about one future in thirty missed a cheque. Laura doesn't owe one ten-year bill. She owes ten one-year bills, and a crash in the wrong year forces a sale at the bottom.

So we built Caplet XR. On a pill, XR means extended release: the same dose on schedule. Ours also means X for ten, R for redemption.

Her first $300,000 buys all ten cheques in January 2027, or nearly: a ladder of Treasury bonds and Treasury funds that mature like bonds, each before its January.

What does a high degree of certainty mean? Only two things break a cheque. The US government defaults. Or, in our model, her 2028 deposit can't fill the gap. Even if yields fall two points, about $125,000 still fills it. The ladder has one leak – early cash earns less if yields fall – and we price it and set the money aside in 2031, before she names her range.

Laura is willing to take thoughtful risks, but she has already taken the big one: the residency. Her income from advances, speaking and licensing shrinks in exactly the recessions that make bonds dear. So the cheques use money she already has. Her 2028 deposit plugs any gap in the ladder, then builds her gift.

Most of it buys a floor: Treasuries that grow back into all of it by late 2032. The interest they should earn (about a fifteenth of the portfolio, by our sums) goes into a world index fund. It's a bank's capital-protected note, minus the bank and its fee. In our model, with world stocks at 7.5%, a 60/40 portfolio usually leaves her almost $200,000 richer by 2042. But about one time in three, that portfolio lands below its own range or misses a cheque.

In 2031 Laura gives co-sponsors a range: low enough to keep her word, high enough that her lead gift draws bigger ones. The bottom is the floor she owns. The top adds half her stocks; the other half stays hers, to sell any day for a dearer building.

A capped range has a catch. Once it's named, a fall in stocks comes off the gift while a rise past the top goes to Laura. On the day she names it, the half she promised moves into Treasuries. In our model the gift reaches its top about 97 times in 100.

From 2033 the ten holdings are the operating reserve. Nothing is sold before it pays – you don't crush an XR pill – and each turns to cash before its cheque, so the reserve runs itself down. If anything changes, the cheques come first, then the floor, then the hope.

===== Entry 3 =====

Laura has one promise and one hope. Caplet XR doesn't invest the promise; it buys it. Her first $300,000 all but buys ten $50,000 cheques, in Treasuries due before each January. Her 2028 deposit finishes it, then a floor that grows back into itself. Only the interest chases the hope.

===== Entry 4 =====
PITCH:
Laura owes ten dated cheques and hopes for one gift. Her first deposit buys the cheques outright – ten Treasuries, one maturing before each January – and only her second deposit chases the gift. We call it Caplet XR: ten doses, one a year. You don't crush an XR pill.

IPS:
Laura owes ten cheques of $50,000, one every January from 2033 to 2042, and the first is six years away. She has taken thoughtful risks all her working life. Stocks beat bonds over six years more often than not. So our first plan put three-quarters of her money in the stock market, and on paper it looked like the obvious answer.

Then we ran it through our model, and about one future in thirty missed a cheque. The flaw was never the return. It was the calendar. Laura does not owe one ten-year bill; she owes ten one-year bills, and a crash in the wrong year forces a sale at the bottom. A patient investor can wait out a bad decade. A January cannot.

So in January 2027 her first $300,000 buys the ten cheques, or very nearly: a ladder of Treasury bonds, and Treasury funds that mature like bonds, one falling due just before each January it is for. Every holding can be sold any afternoon – but nothing is sold before it pays. It sits, it matures, it becomes the cheque. We call it Caplet XR, as in extended release.

Only two things break a cheque. The US government defaults. Or, in our model, yields fall before we finish buying, the ladder costs more than she has, and her 2028 deposit cannot fill the gap – though even after a two-point fall, about $125,000 of that $150,000 still fills it. Every cheque therefore rests on one borrower, on purpose.

Whatever the ladder does not need of the 2028 deposit is for the gift. Most of it buys Treasuries maturing in late 2032 – just enough of them to grow back into the whole sum by then. The rest, which is the interest they would otherwise have earned (about a fifteenth of the portfolio, by our sums), buys one world index fund: thousands of companies, dozens of countries. If the fund goes to nothing, she still has the whole sum in 2033. If it does well, she has more. A bank sells exactly this and calls it a capital-protected note; it arrives with a brochure and a fee. Ours arrives with neither.

In 2031 she names co-sponsors a range for that gift. The bottom is the floor she already owns. The top is the floor plus half of whatever the fund is worth that day. The other half stays hers, to sell if the building turns out dearer than planned (it will be priced in Taiwan dollars, which nobody can translate for her yet). Low enough to keep her word; high enough that a lead gift pulls in bigger ones.

From 2033 the ten holdings are the operating reserve, and it runs itself down: each turns to cash before its cheque, nothing is rebalanced, nothing is decided. If her life changes, the order is fixed – the cheques, then the floor, then the gift.

Ten doses, one a year. Nobody crushes the pill.
