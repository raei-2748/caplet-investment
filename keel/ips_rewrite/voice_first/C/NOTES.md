## Content items kept (brief's list) and cut
Kept, told at full size: 1 (promise and hope), 2 (first plan, ten one-year bills), 3 (ladder, sellable but never
sold), 4 (two things break a cheque, without its number), 6 (capacity, not willingness, folded into the steelman
and the 2028 aside), 7 (floor plus world fund = the bank's note minus the bank), 9 (range: bottom owned, top = floor
plus half, the other half hers), 11 (reserve runs itself down; cheques, then floor, then hope).

Cut, and why:
- **5, the coupon leak.** True and clever, but it is a second-order fix; telling it properly took about 40 words
  that the stakes needed more. Final Report.
- **8, the 60/40 price (about $200,000 richer, one time in three a miss).** The steelman slot went to our own
  first plan, which is a real confession and the same argument; two stock-versus-bond comparisons was one too many.
- **10, the capped range's catch and the 97-in-100 hit rate.** The best mechanism in the list, and the hardest
  cut. It took about 45 words; the range section now argues one thing (promise only what she owns), which is what
  "Money where her mouth is" says. Wharton does not expect the final range or its confidence in the IPS. If Caplet
  wants it back, the cheapest trade is the asymmetry paragraph's last two sentences.
- **From 4: the $125,000 break point and "one borrower, on purpose".** Several V39 panel judges could not tell
  what it was measured against; without room to explain it, it was better left for the Final Report. Diversification is still
  answered (one world index fund of thousands of companies; the cheques on the Treasury).
- **From 7: "about a fifteenth of the portfolio, by our sums" and "dozens of countries".** Cut for words; the
  first is the honest size of the stock share and is the first thing to restore if words free up.
