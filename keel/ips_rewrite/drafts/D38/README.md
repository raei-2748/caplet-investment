# A new name and a new central idea

Claude, Thu 5 Nov 2026. Keel's research, for you to rewrite in your own words. No trade changes. Every choice is yours.

**Roots is out.** Ahaan gave the case to other AIs and they also suggested "Roots", after Laura's memoir *Messy Roots*, so
it can't be what sets us apart. It's gone from every draft, and so is the tablet image: both were pictures laid over the

**The idea.** Every plan we've seen, ours included, treats Laura's portfolio as if it were the residency's only money,
but the case says the rest may come from co-sponsors, grants, program fees and her own business income (case p. 3).
Each of those can shrink in the downturns that hit share prices, and they come back slowly. Her portfolio is the one
source of money the residency has that can be built not to shrink. So the question that matters is how much more of that
risk the residency can carry, and the answer is very little.

That gives one test for every dollar: would the residency miss it in a recession? If it would, it sits in Treasuries by
the time anyone counts on it. The ten operating payments (counted on from 2027), her minimum facility contribution (from
2028, three years before co-sponsors are asked) and the top of her range (once she names it in 2031) all pass that test.
Shares hold only money nobody is counting on: the interest before 2031, then the half she keeps as flexibility.

**Why this is more than a label.** It changes the question, and the plan follows from the answer:
- It settles the puzzle a judge will see: Laura's living costs are covered outside the portfolio, so she could take more
  risk, yet our plan is mostly Treasuries. She could, but the residency would then be exposed to the same downturn twice.
- It says why her minimum is bought in 2028 and not in 2031: a crash in 2029-31 would shrink a minimum held in shares in
  the same years co-sponsors give less.
- It gives co-sponsors a reason in their own terms: her money is there in the year theirs is tight.

**The name: First In, Last Out.** Her money goes in first: the payments are bought in 2027 and her minimum in 2028, years
before anyone else is asked, and fundraising experiments find that money already in hand draws more giving. It's the last
to pull back: in a downturn, what she has committed doesn't shrink.

**Where it comes from** (for the Final Report; each source checked on 5 Nov unless marked):
- Robert Merton, "Optimal Investment Strategies for University Endowment Funds" (NBER Working Paper 3820, 1991; book
  chapter 1993). The common mistake is to manage an endowment "as if it were the only asset of the university"; once
  other income is counted, the best portfolio is "not efficient in the sense of the risk-return tradeoff". A plan that
  looks too safe for Laura on her own can be right for the residency.
- Stephen Dimmock, *Review of Economics and Statistics* 94(3), 2012: universities whose other income is more volatile
  hold more bonds. Institutions really invest this way.
- *Giving USA 2010* (checked 31 Oct): US giving fell 2.4% and 3.2% after inflation in 2008 and 2009, foundation giving
  7.6% in 2009, bequests 23.9%, and after past recessions giving took "three to five years" to recover. US private
  foundations must pay out at least 5% of their assets a year, so their grants fall when their assets do. Harvard slowed
  its Allston science building in February 2009 after its endowment fell about 30% (checked 31 Oct).
- List and Lucking-Reiley, *Journal of Political Economy* 2002 (checked 30 Oct): raising the money already in hand from
  10% to 67% of a campaign's goal gave a nearly sixfold rise in gifts.

**Where it's weak.** Total giving fell only a few percent in 2008-09, and List (*Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 2011)
says gifts kept coming "largely unabated" through good and bad times, so our claim is about timing, not size. The case
doesn't say who the co-sponsors are. And the half of her shares Laura keeps would be smallest in exactly the downturn
this idea is about, which is why it's her flexibility and never something anyone counts on.
