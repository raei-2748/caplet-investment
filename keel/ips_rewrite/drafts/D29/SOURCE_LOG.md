# V49 source log: every sentence, the human sentence it is built on (Sun 1 Nov 2026)

AI DRAFT (Claude, Sun 1 Nov 2026). Method: V48's sentence-borrowing approach continues — every sentence takes the
shape, rhythm and turn of a real human sentence and fills it with Caplet's meaning. V49 restructures several passages:
the numbered prescriptions (First/Second/Third) become one long dash-hinged build; the capped-range paragraph drops
the "warning label" framing; the rival section gains a participial list. New sources for the rewritten sentences sit
beside V48's writers who carry over.

## How to read this log

**What was borrowed.** From each source sentence we took its shape: its syntax, its rhythm, its turn (the place it
pivots) and, where it has one, its move (a question answered in four words, a concession opened with "But",
a participial cascade). We filled each shape with Caplet's own meaning. No source sentence is copied, and no
distinctive phrase is paraphrased closely: the IPS can't carry citations, and a judge who spotted a famous writer's
wording would have an academic-integrity problem with a Wharton entry. Where a few words do overlap, they are common
English (an idiom or a stock phrase), and the "Overlap" column names them so Caplet can see each one and decide.
Caplet rewrites in its own words before anything is submitted; these sources can go in the Final Report's Works Cited.

**How the originals were read.** Most sites block this environment's direct downloads, so every page went through a
web reader that returns short quotes (125 characters at most). The quotes below are as that reader returned them from
the page, on 1 Nov 2026. Before any of these quotes is printed in the Final Report, check it against the printed source.

**The writers (18, plus a pharmacy label).** V48's 15 writers carry over where their sentences still stand. New for
V49: James Baldwin (for the long dash-hinged build that replaced the numbered prescriptions), Annie Dillard (for the
gradual-risk sentence), Nassim Taleb (for the symmetry of the capped range), and John Maynard Keynes (for the
certainty sentence). Ahaan's three essays remain the tuning fork, not a source: no sentence is built on them, but
every borrowed shape had to sound like them once filled (pun headings, a long build and a blunt landing, a concession
and then "But", homely images in formal sentences, en dashes, British spelling).

## Read but not used, and why

Kept from V48 for the Final Report, where the room is bigger: more of Orwell's "Shooting an Elephant" ("He wears a
mask, and his face grows to fit it."), for the reflection on our first plan; Orwell, "Politics and the English
Language" ("The great enemy of clear language is insincerity."), for the voice section; Feynman's last line ("For a
successful technology, reality must take precedence over public relations, for nature cannot be fooled."), for
co-sponsor credibility; Galbraith ("There can be few fields of human endeavor in which history counts for so little
as in the world of finance."), for the capital-protected note's history; Taleb's river "four feet deep on average"
(*The Black Swan*, p. 161), for the rival's average; Schwed on losing money ("Like all of life's rich emotional
experiences, the full flavor of losing important money cannot be conveyed by literature."); Housel on "scared enough
to save for the short run and brave enough to invest for the long run", for the floor-and-stocks split.

New for V49 but not used: Seth Klarman, *Margin of Safety* (1991), on the distinction between price and value — strong
sentence shapes, but the IPS has no room for the distinction (the ladder buys at par, so price is value). Keynes ch. 12
on animal spirits — used for sentence 6; the longer passages on beauty contests and newspaper competitions kept for the
Final Report.
