# Blind voice A/B: V49 vs V48

**Winner:** Entry A, narrow margin

Entry A is closer to the essays' author. The margin is narrow because both drafts share most of their DNA — same opening question, same structure, same closing. The difference lives in about eight sentences, and the ones Entry A rewrites are, on balance, more Ahaan-like in their architecture.

**What Entry A does well:** The recession sentence is the best single sentence in either draft: 39 words that build through a dash-hinge, three consequences stacking before the reader gets to breathe, exactly the way Ahaan runs a clause chain in "a recession arrives before January 2027 and pushes yields down – making the ladder cost more at precisely the moment that Laura's advances, speaking and licensing shrink, and with them the 2028 deposit that was meant to top it up." That is the essays' move — the long carry that earns the short landing. The rival section's participial chain ("keeping our ladder, putting the 2028 deposit mostly in stocks, buying its bottom in January 2031 with whatever they fetch that morning") reads like Ahaan's long accusatory lists. And opening the capped-range paragraph with a balanced active clause ("Every fall... comes off the gift; every rise past the top goes to Laura") argues, where Entry B's version sets up a label.

**What Entry A does poorly:** "We know this rival because we built it first" is wordier and more explanatory than it needs to be. The "because" explains the connection where the reader has already made it — Ahaan would land that harder. "The order will not change" is slightly stiff next to a contraction; Ahaan uses contractions freely ("can't", "won't", "doesn't"). And "the strongest alternative takes one for her" is more formal than the essays' register. The word "alternative" is a committee word; "rival" (Entry B's choice) is a debater's word, and this author is a debater.

**What Entry B does well:** "Without a list of ways to fail, 'a high degree of certainty' is just an adjective" is a terrific sentence — it sounds like something the essays' author would write in a philosophy piece. The deflation to "just an adjective" is exactly his move (compare "It is revenge with a wig on"). "We know it well:" is leaner and more confident. Entry B uses contractions more naturally throughout ("can't", "won't"), which matches the essays' cerebral-casual register better on a word-by-word level.

**What Entry B does poorly:** The numbered risks ("First... Second... The third is too slow to notice") are structured like a listicle. Ahaan never numbers his arguments this way in the essays. He builds long chains or uses "But" pivots. The numbering reads as organised-by-an-outline rather than argued-by-a-mind. "A named range needs a warning label:" is a consultant's setup — it names what a thing is before showing what it does. Ahaan would show the catch first, then name it. And the rival section breaks into shorter sentences ("It keeps our ladder, puts the 2028 deposit mostly in stocks and buys its bottom...") where Entry A carries the reader through a participial build — closer to the essays' style.

**Sentence-by-sentence voice notes**

Entry A — most like the essays' author:
- "A recession arrives before January 2027 and pushes yields down – making the ladder cost more at precisely the moment that Laura's advances, speaking and licensing shrink, and with them the 2028 deposit that was meant to top it up." — This is Ahaan's signature move: a long sentence whose clauses stack consequence on consequence, building tension, with a dash-hinge that opens the floodgate. Compare his "To be truly, ultimately responsible for an action, you would need to be responsible for the mental state that produced it: your beliefs, desires and character in that moment."
- "Every fall in stocks before naming day comes off the gift; every rise past the top goes to Laura." — Balanced, active, argues through parallel action. Ahaan uses this exact structure: "Manufacturing more 'stuff' cannot manufacture happier people."
- "Laura takes thoughtful risks; the strongest alternative takes one for her – keeping our ladder, putting the 2028 deposit mostly in stocks, buying its bottom in January 2031 with whatever they fetch that morning." — The dash opens into a participial list that carries the reader forward. This is close to Ahaan's "a state designed and funded by billionaires does not deliver individual liberty. It serves merely to swap out liberal democratic 'tyranny' for corporate tyranny."

Entry A — least like the essays' author:
- "We know this rival because we built it first:" — The "because" over-explains. Ahaan doesn't justify his transitions; he states them and moves.
- "the order will not change" — Stiff. Ahaan would write "won't".

Entry B — most like the essays' author:
- "Without a list of ways to fail, 'a high degree of certainty' is just an adjective." — Pure Ahaan: take a phrase from the case, deflate it with "just". Compare "It is revenge with a wig on."
- "Sounds good, on average." — Wry, four words, the comma adds the eyebrow. Both drafts have this, but it's worth noting it works.
- "We know it well:" — Confident, lean, exactly how Ahaan transitions.

Entry B — least like the essays' author:
- "First, the US government defaults. Second, a recession arrives before January 2027; yields fall and the ladder costs more." — Numbering prescriptions is not how this author argues. He chains causes.
- "A named range needs a warning label:" — A consultant's intro sentence. Ahaan opens paragraphs with claims, not labels.

**The long-build test**

Entry A has 2 sentences over 30 words that build with dashes or participial phrases:
1. "A recession arrives before January 2027 and pushes yields down – making the ladder cost more at precisely the moment that Laura's advances, speaking and licensing shrink, and with them the 2028 deposit that was meant to top it up." (39 words, dash-hinged build with stacking consequences)
2. "Laura takes thoughtful risks; the strongest alternative takes one for her – keeping our ladder, putting the 2028 deposit mostly in stocks, buying its bottom in January 2031 with whatever they fetch that morning." (33 words, dash-hinged participial chain)

Entry B has 0 sentences over 30 words with clear dash-hinged or participial builds. Its longest constructions break into separate shorter sentences. The closest is "But when a recession arrives, her advances, speaking and licensing shrink too, and so does the 2028 deposit topping up the ladder" (22 words) — a build, but not long enough to carry the reader the way Ahaan's do.

This is the most significant structural difference between the two entries. The essays' author routinely writes 30-50 word sentences that carry the reader through a mechanism. Entry A has this; Entry B does not.
