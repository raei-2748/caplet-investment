## 1. Paraphrase
- **Pitch:** the first $300k buys Treasuries for the ten $50k payments; the 2028 deposit buys Treasuries plus world stocks for a gift.
- **P1:** fixed payments are a purchase, not an investment bet.
- **P2:** in 2027 the $300k buys a Treasury ladder covering all or nearly all ten.
- **P3:** three ways it fails: US default; a recession that raises the ladder's cost and shrinks the 2028 deposit at once; slow reinvestment losses. Fix: price the gap in 2031 and fund it before promising a gift.
- **P4:** most of the 2028 deposit buys Treasuries that grow back to the deposit by 2032 (the floor). The expected interest buys a world fund: a home-made capital-protected note.
- **P5:** the 2031 range runs from the floor to the floor plus half the stocks; the other half stays Laura's.
- **P6:** stocks move the gift. On naming day the promised half moves to Treasuries, and the model reaches the top 97 times in 100.
- **P7:** the first plan (75% stocks) failed its own model. A stock-heavy rival gives ~$15k more on average but $90k against $140k in the worst 5%, so the team pays for safety.
- **P8:** from 2033 the ladder is the reserve and runs down; the order is cheques, floor, hope.

**Read twice:**
- "Treasuries that repay it" (pitch): repay the deposit or the ladder? It also ignores the top-up.
- "give its building in Taiwan a gift": a gift to a building, with a distant "its".
- "a recession arrives before we buy": buy what? The ladder is bought in January 2027.
- "coupons and early repayments": Treasuries don't repay early.
- "take it from what she keeps before she names a gift": "what she keeps" isn't defined until P5. Does the fix cover risks one and two? Default gets no answer.
- "grow back into all of it": all $150k, or what's left after the top-up?
- "every fall in stocks comes off the gift" next to "the promised half moves into Treasuries": once it's locked, why does the model give 97 and not 100?
- "Modelled to name our $145,000 median bottom": a dangling modifier, and an owned floor shouldn't have a median. With "$140,000 after the 2031 set-aside", the bottom now has three definitions.
- **Used before explained:** "Caplet XR" and "the gift" (pitch), "what she keeps", "set-aside", "names" (the range or the gift?).

## 2. Summary
The first $300k buys Treasuries maturing before each payment. The 2028 money buys Treasuries that grow back to the deposit, with the interest in a global stock fund. In 2031 Laura promises the floor plus half the stocks, locks that half into bonds, and keeps the rest.

## 3. Three retellable ideas
1. "The cheques are a shopping problem": buy the payments. **Default.**
2. A DIY capital-protected note whose stocks "can't expire worthless". The structure is the default (bonds + stocks + lock); the framing is fresh.
3. "We'd pay the $15,000": the price of safety, measured against a rival. This is the most original move.

Judges may remember "Do not crush" before any idea.

## 5. Voice
It's one voice, not a collage, but it's a columnist's: clipped aphorisms, colon reveals again and again, and a pharmacy pun thread. Ahaan argues in long sentences with dashes, concessions and examples, then lands a short kicker. This draft is all kickers.
- **Most like him:** "The cheques are a shopping problem, not an investing one." / "Without a list of ways to fail, 'a high degree of certainty' is just an adjective." / "We'd pay the $15,000.".
- **Least like him:** "Sounds good, on average." (a filler tic); "Do not crush." (a pun where his kickers carry meaning); "Modelled to name our $145,000 median bottom, it leaves Laura..." (compressed past grammar).

## 6. Criteria and questions
- **Strategy:** clear thesis and a clean 2027/28/31/33 path. Misses: diversification is never argued, and there's no rule for a crash between 2028 and 2031.
- **Client:** strong on her recession-exposed income, "thoughtful risks" and Taiwan dollars. Never ties the range to her credibility with co-sponsors.
- **Analysis:** the failure-mode list and the worst-5% rival are good. Misses: no stated model assumptions, **inflation is absent** (the case asks for its effect on facility costs), and the bottom figures don't reconcile.
- **Articulation:** one line; fine for an IPS.
- **Presentation:** memorable, but compression hides the mechanics (the shortfall fix, 97/100); the gimmicks risk eye-rolls.
- **Questions:** the central idea is clear. Principles: only "cheques, floor, hope". Liquidity is never named. The allocation path is good, but stocks after the gift and leftovers after 2042 go unmentioned. Protection plus flexibility: yes.

## 7. Accuracy against the case
Nothing flatly contradicts it. (a) The case fixes the 2028 deposit at $150k. Shrinking it is a fair stress test, but the floor depends on it and the draft never says what happens then. (b) The pitch's "Treasuries that repay it" conflicts with P3/P4 (top-up first, then "most of" the deposit).
