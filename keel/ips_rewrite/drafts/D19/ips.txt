Laura has one promise and one hope. The promise is $50,000 every January from 2033 to 2042, paid from this portfolio alone. The hope is a gift for the building. They need different kinds of money, so we keep them apart.

We started with three-quarters in stocks. Our model sank it: about one future in thirty missed a cheque. Laura doesn't owe one ten-year bill. She owes ten one-year bills, and a crash in the wrong year forces a sale at the bottom.

So we built Caplet XR. On a pill, XR means extended release: the same dose on schedule. Ours also means X for ten, R for redemption.

Her first $300,000 buys all ten cheques in January 2027: a ladder of Treasury bonds and Treasury funds that mature like bonds, each before its January.

What does a high degree of certainty mean? In our model, only two things break a cheque. The US government defaults. Or her 2028 deposit can't fill the gap: after yields fall two points, it needs about $125,000 of its $150,000. The ladder has one leak – early cash earns less if yields fall – and we price it and set the money aside in 2031, before she names her range.

Laura is willing to take thoughtful risks, but she has already taken the big one: the residency. Her income from advances, speaking and licensing shrinks in exactly the recessions that make bonds dear. So the cheques use money she already has. Her 2028 deposit plugs any gap in the ladder, then builds her gift.

Most of it buys a floor: Treasuries that grow back into all of it by late 2032. The interest they should earn (about a fifteenth of everything she owns, by our sums) goes into a world index fund. It's a bank's capital-protected note, minus the bank and its fee. In our model, with world stocks at 7.5% a year, a 60/40 portfolio usually leaves her richer. But about one time in three it misses a cheque or lands below its own range.

In 2031 Laura gives co-sponsors a range: low enough to keep her word, high enough that her lead gift draws bigger ones. The bottom is the floor she owns. The top adds half her stocks; the other half stays hers, to sell any day for a dearer building.

A capped range has a catch. Once it's named, a fall in stocks comes off the gift while a rise past the top goes to Laura. On the day she names it, the half she promised moves into Treasuries. In our model the gift reaches its top about 97 times in 100. The rare miss is about $1,000.

From 2033 the ten holdings are the operating reserve. Nothing is sold before it pays – you don't crush an XR pill – and each turns to cash before its cheque, so the reserve runs itself down. If anything changes, the cheques come first, then the floor, then the hope.
