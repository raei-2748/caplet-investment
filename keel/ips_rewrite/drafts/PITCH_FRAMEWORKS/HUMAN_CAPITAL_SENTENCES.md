## 1. The insight
- Before we invest a dollar, Laura already owns a large stake in the stock market: her career. Book advances, speaking fees and licensing rise in good years and fall in bad ones, just as shares do.
- Laura's income from books, talks and licensing rises and falls with the economy, so most of her wealth is already exposed to the downturns that sink the stock market.
- Economists count future earnings as part of a person's wealth, and Laura's earnings move with the economy almost as closely as shares do.

## 2. The so-what (it flips the usual advice)
- The usual advice for someone with ten years before her first payment is to hold mostly shares. For Laura, that would double the one risk she already has too much of.
- In a recession her portfolio and her pay would fall in the same year, and that is the year she could least afford it.
- Our portfolio is the half of her wealth that stays still, because her career already supplies the half that moves with markets.

## 3. Naming the idea (human capital)
- Economists call this human capital. A tenured professor's salary is as safe as a bond, so she can hold more shares; a stockbroker's pay swings with the market, so he should hold more bonds. Laura's pay looks like the stockbroker's, so her portfolio should look like his.

## 4. The plan, following from it
- So none of her promises depends on shares. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasuries, one maturing just before each January cheque from 2033 to 2042, and a Treasury held to maturity pays what it promised whatever the market does.
- Her 2028 deposit comes from the riskiest part of her wealth, so we turn it into the safest thing we can buy: a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, which sets the minimum she can give to the building.
- Only what is left over, roughly the interest that minimum would earn, goes into one world index fund. If shares collapse she loses part of a bonus, and never a cheque or the minimum.

## 5. The 2031 range
- When Laura gives co-sponsors her range in 2031, every dollar she names is already in Treasuries, so a bad year in 2032 cannot shrink a figure she has said aloud.

## 6. Closing
- Laura has spent her career taking risks for her income; her portfolio's job is to keep those risks away from the people relying on her.
