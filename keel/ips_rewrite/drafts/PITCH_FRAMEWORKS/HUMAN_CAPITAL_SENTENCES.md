## 1. The insight
- Laura has "been willing to take thoughtful risks throughout her entrepreneurial career", and her 2028 contribution comes from that career: "publishing advances, speaking engagements, licensing, and other entrepreneurial ventures". Those earnings rise in good years and fall in bad ones, as shares do, so much of her wealth already moves with the market before we invest a dollar.
- Economists call future earnings human capital and count them as wealth. Laura's human capital comes from publishing advances, speaking engagements and licensing, and it behaves more like shares than like bonds.

## 2. The so-what
- The case asks for "an appropriate balance between pursuing growth and protecting the capital required for her goals". Laura's entrepreneurial career already does the pursuing, so her portfolio's first job is the protecting.
- In a downturn her publishing advances and speaking engagements would shrink in the same year as her shares, which is why we keep the operating commitment out of shares altogether.

## 3. Naming the idea
- A tenured professor's salary is as safe as a bond, so she can hold more shares; a stockbroker's earnings swing with the market, so he should hold more bonds. Laura's earnings from publishing, speaking and licensing look like the stockbroker's.

## 4. The plan
- All ten operating payments must be funded "with a high degree of certainty", so her first $300,000 buys ten Treasuries in January 2027, one maturing before each payment from 2033 to 2042. In 2033 those Treasuries become her operating reserve.
- Her 2028 contribution comes from the riskiest part of her wealth, so we turn it into the safest thing she can own: a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, which sets the lowest facility contribution she could make.
- Only what is left over, roughly the interest that Treasury fund would earn, goes into one world index fund. Unfavorable market outcomes can shrink the facility contribution above that lowest amount; they cannot touch the operating commitment.

## 5. Co-sponsors in 2031
- If Laura "promises more than she can ultimately contribute, she could damage her credibility", so every dollar in the credible range she gives co-sponsors in 2031 is already in Treasuries.

## 6. Closing
- Laura's entrepreneurial career is where she takes her thoughtful risks; her portfolio makes sure the residency's operating commitment and her credibility with co-sponsors never depend on them.
