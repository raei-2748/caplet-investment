In January 2033 Laura's portfolio writes the first of ten $50,000 cheques to her Taiwan residency – and nobody else may help.

We call it Caplet XR. To a pharmacist, XR means extended release: a dose let out on schedule, labelled do not crush. To us, X is ten and R is redemption – ten Treasuries, each redeemed in time for one January.

We take "a high degree of certainty" to mean by January 2028 every payment sits in its own Treasury bond or defined-maturity Treasury fund, maturing before its date. The funds are near-ironclad too – by their December close they hold nothing but cash.

Why every payment before a single share? Because Laura already owns plenty of stock-market risk – it just isn't in a brokerage account. Her 2028 deposit rests on book advances and speaking fees, and the slump that shrinks those would likely cut yields too, making bonds dearer as the deposit dwindles. So the $300,000 she already has buys the payments, latest-dated first, and any gap is the 2028 deposit's first job – a smaller deposit shrinks the facility gift long before it reaches a payment.

A bank would sell Laura a capital-protected note: its own IOU for her money, an option on the market, and a fee. We swap the IOU for a Treasury (ask Lehman's customers why), the option for the stocks themselves, and skip the fee. Most of the 2028 remainder buys a December 2032 Treasury fund sized to grow back to the whole – her floor, intact even if every share went to zero. The rest, its expected interest, buys one world index fund of 10,000 companies (picking winners is not on the menu). The catch is, a boom pays less than an option would.

In 2031 Laura names a range: the bottom is the floor's projected payout, rounded down – money she already holds – and the top adds half her stocks, valued that day. In 2033 the same rule sets the gift, capped at that top, so after 2031 only a two-year stock fall or a payment in need can pull it lower. The other half stays with Laura for overruns or a change of plan.

Five years rarely loses, says the sceptic, so stock the floor too. Rarely – but time makes a stock loss rarer, not smaller, and a number said aloud cannot be half-kept.

We refuse.

The soft spot is reinvestment: if yields fall for good, coupons earn less. So before the 2033 gift we price any gap and hold it back – from Laura's half first, the floor last. Stocks should beat Treasuries; no payment needs them to, and inflation cannot touch a fixed one.

Until 2033 nothing is sold early. Do not crush. For liquidity, something matures before every January; in 2033 the payment holdings become the operating reserve, one fewer each January, and we review the plan with Laura yearly.

Ten cheques, then – each bought before the first is written.
