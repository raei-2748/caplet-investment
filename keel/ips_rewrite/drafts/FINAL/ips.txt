We call the plan Caplet XR, after extended-release caplets, which release their dose on schedule and come labelled "do not crush". Our dose is Treasuries: before anyone relies on a sum Laura commits to, she owns the ones that will repay it. At current Treasury prices her ten operating payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and they are fixed, so in a balanced mix every gain or loss would fall on the last third. That third would swing like the shares of a heavily borrowed company.

In January 2027 her $300,000 buys a ladder of ten Treasury holdings, one repaying before each January from 2033 to 2042, and the 2028 deposit buys any it can't afford. Those ten rungs are the first meaning of XR, and crushing the caplet would mean selling one before it repays.

In 2028, what the ladder doesn't need buys more of the fund behind the 2033 payment, enough to return about all of it by December 2032, which sets the bottom of her range. Because it costs less than it returns, the difference, roughly its interest, goes into one world index fund, so the shares risk only that interest. It is bought in 2028, not 2031, because the creative residency in Taiwan can't depend on shares: its co-sponsors, grants and fees can shrink in the same recessions that hit shares, and her facility contribution, resting on the US government instead, must hold when they do.

The ladder also carries reinvestment risk: interest arrives years before the payment it serves, so money waits, earning less if rates fall and stay low. So in 2031, before she names a range, she sells up to half her shares for XR's second meaning, an extra reserve of Treasuries, enough to meet every payment even if waiting money earned nothing. If that half falls short, the 2028 holding's payout makes up the rest.

The bottom is what that holding will pay after this, less a margin because a fund's payout isn't fixed. Then she sells the other half for Treasury funds repaying before 2033; the top is the bottom plus what that half raised. Both ends are owned before anyone hears them, and in 2033 she gives what those Treasuries pay, up to the top.

Whatever of the first half isn't sold, and anything paid beyond the top, stays hers, liquid and promised to no one, so she keeps room as the project develops. In 2033 her operating reserve is already in place: the ladder, the extra reserve and the money waiting for them. The plan's biggest strength, and what we mean by a high degree of certainty, is that, barring a US default, no payment, and no figure once named, depends on a share price. Like the caplet it is named after, the plan works as long as nobody crushes it, and the tenth rung, with what was set aside for it, meets the last $50,000 in 2042.
