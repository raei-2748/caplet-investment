## PITCH

Caplet XR buys Laura's ten $50,000 Januaries first, with money she already has, in Treasuries held to their dates. From her 2028 deposit, a Treasury fund grows back to about what remains; its expected interest buys world stocks. Stocks can lift her gift. They cannot touch its floor.

## IPS

From 2033, Laura owes her residency $50,000 a year for ten years, and nobody else may pay a cent of it. So her first $300,000 goes to those ten Januaries before it buys a single share. Hence Caplet XR: X for ten, R for release, one Treasury holding timed to each January. To anyone who trades rates, a caplet is an option. There is none inside this one: the floor is a Treasury fund, the cap a promise.

Laura, a graphic novelist with a Wharton statistics degree, is building a place where artists, writers, designers, entrepreneurs and educators can live and work. With her living costs met elsewhere, she can afford "thoughtful risks". The payments cannot. We take "high degree of certainty" to mean each payment is owned in a Treasury holding that ends just before it falls due.

Why first? Her 2028 deposit will come from publishing, speaking and licensing, and a slump that squeezes that income could also make payment bonds dearer. So money she already has buys them, latest first. Any gap left in January is the 2028 deposit's first job.

Just enough of what remains buys a Treasury fund ending in 2032, sized to grow back to about that remainder: Laura's floor. Its expected interest buys one world index fund of about 10,000 companies. If stocks went to zero, she would still get about that remainder. A bank would sell this as a capital-protected note, bond plus option; we skipped the option. The catch is less upside in a boom. But nothing can expire worthless, and there is no bank to fail.

In 2031 Laura gives co-sponsors a window. The bottom is the floor's projected payout, rounded down with a margin: money Laura already holds, so they hear a commitment, not a forecast. The top adds half the stock fund's value that day. In 2033 the same rule sets her contribution, capped at that top, so only a two-year stock fall or a payment needing help can pull it lower. The other half stays hers, for flexibility as the project develops and for Taiwan cost overruns, where inflation bites; the payments are fixed in dollars, like Treasuries.

Moving floor money into stocks would raise the expected gift and could break a number Laura has said aloud. We refuse. Her credibility is worth more.

Until 2033 each holding is kept whole to its date; we trade only to invest the 2028 deposit and coupons, which wait in a short-term Treasury fund. Reinvestment is the soft spot: any gap is priced before the 2033 gift and paid from Laura's half first, the floor last. Liquidity comes from the calendar: something matures every January. In 2033 the payment Treasuries become the operating reserve, one fewer each year. Each January we review the plan with Laura.

Laura owes ten Januaries. From 2028 she owns all ten.
