## PITCH

Caplet XR puts Laura's first $300,000 into ten $50,000 payments, in Treasuries maturing before each January. Her gift is a capital-protected note with no derivative: a Treasury fund growing back to about what her 2028 deposit leaves, and a world index fund bought with its interest.

## IPS

From 2033 Laura owes her residency $50,000 a year for ten years, and nobody else may pay a cent of it. Caplet XR: X for ten, R for release, one Treasury holding timed to each of those Januaries. To a rates trader a caplet is an option; this one holds none: the floor is a Treasury fund, the cap a promise.

What we are trying to accomplish: ten payments funded with a high degree of certainty, then a facility contribution she can name in 2031 and never take back. Our thesis: her gift is a capital-protected note built without a derivative. After the 2028 deposit finishes any payment, a Treasury fund maturing in 2032 is sized to grow back to about what is left: her floor, even if stocks go to zero. Its expected interest buys a world index fund. Banks build this with an option; we used the index fund itself, giving up some boom upside, but nothing can expire worthless.

Why it fits Laura: a statistics graduate and founder planning a residency for artists, writers, designers, entrepreneurs and educators, she is "willing to take thoughtful risks" but wants "balance". Her living costs are paid elsewhere; the caution is the commitment's, not hers. So the payments are bought with the $300,000 she has, not the $150,000 she has yet to earn: the slump that could squeeze her publishing and speaking income could also make those bonds dearer later.

Growth, liquidity, funding reliability, risk and flexibility: we take "high degree of certainty" to mean every payment is owned by January 2028 in a Treasury bond or fund maturing before it. The payments rely on one issuer, the safest in dollars; the index fund diversifies. The funds sell any day, a liquidity no odd-lot bond offers. The risk left: reinvested coupons earn less if yields fall for good. Inflation cannot raise fixed payments; facility costs are the index fund's job.

As needs change: every Treasury holding is kept whole to its date, its interest reinvested in Treasuries. A coupon gap draws on the stocks first, the floor last. We review the plan with Laura each January. In 2033 the payment holdings become the operating reserve, all Treasuries, shrinking by one each January.

Protecting the payments while supporting the contribution: in 2031 Laura offers co-sponsors a window, not a number. The bottom is the floor's projected payout, rounded down with a margin: money she already holds, a commitment, not a forecast. The top adds half the index fund that day. In 2033 the same rule sets the gift; it misses the top only if stocks fall between 2031 and 2033 or a payment needs help first. The other half stays hers, for flexibility as the project develops. Moving floor money into stocks would raise the expected gift. But it would turn her bottom into a forecast. We refuse.

Laura owes ten Januaries. From 2028 she owns all ten.
