Laura makes two commitments: ten $50,000 operating payments for her creative residency in Taiwan from 2033, and a credible range for her facility contribution, named in 2031. Caplet XR pays for each in Treasuries before anyone relies on it. Fixed in dollars and payable only from her portfolio, the payments are, in effect, a debt; inflation can raise what the building costs, never what she owes. Each of the ten payments gets its own Treasury holding, kept until its redemption, hence the X and R. Should her $300,000 in 2027 fall short of all ten, the 2028 deposit buys the rest.

A balanced mix would fit her record of thoughtful risks and probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, leaving a third for the facility and flexibility. Every gain or loss would land on that third, levered like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, and her 2031 range would rest on it.

In 2028 she buys an extra stake in the Treasury fund behind the 2033 payment; it costs less than what's left of the deposit, but its interest makes up the difference by December 2032. What the stake returns, less a margin because a fund's payout isn't fixed, is the bottom of her range. Her shares are bought with the difference, in a world index fund of thousands of companies, so they risk only the interest the stake will earn. Buying the bottom this early keeps it in Treasuries through years when a slump could also shrink co-sponsors' giving.

Interest and redemptions arrive before the payments they serve, and a long spell of low rates on that waiting money would leave the payments short. In 2031, before naming the range, she buys extra Treasuries as cover, enough to meet the payments even at zero interest. Half her shares are held back to pay for it, and whatever that half can't pay comes from the extra stake, lowering the bottom before she names the range.

Naming the range creates, in effect, a second debt. She gives what the Treasuries behind it pay, capped at the top. Before she speaks, she sells the other half of her shares for Treasury funds paying out before 2033, and the top is the bottom plus what those shares fetched. Left in shares, a named top could fall short and cost her credibility. Our test for a high degree of certainty is that once the cover is bought, no market can put a payment or a named figure out of reach, barring a US default.

Her financial flexibility, kept liquid because the project will change as it develops, is the shares the cover didn't need and anything paid beyond the top. In 2033 she sets aside her operating reserve without a trade: it is everything held for the payments, cover included. When the tenth holding is redeemed for the 2042 payment, the debt is repaid.
