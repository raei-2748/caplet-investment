- **P4.** Laura's income behaves like a stock, because it dries up in the same downturns that make the ladder dearer to buy. If her 2028 deposit is smaller, the building gift shrinks. A cheque funded by a Treasury already bought is not hurt by that.
- **P5.** The 2028 deposit first completes any missing rung. Most of the rest buys Treasuries maturing in late 2032, which is the floor. The other half stays with Laura as liquid flexibility in case the building costs more in Taiwan dollars.
- **P7.** The team could have put the floor in stocks for a bigger gift. They didn't, because a number told to co-sponsors can't be quietly cut if markets fall.
- **P8.** The weak spot is reinvesting the ladder's interest. If yields fall and stay low, the ladder comes up short. Before naming the range, the team prices that shortfall at that day's yields and sets it aside from Laura's stocks. Anything the stocks can't cover lowers the whole range.
- **P9.** From 2033 the ladder is the operating reserve and pays out one bond per cheque. The priority order is cheques, then floor, then gift.
- **P10.** "Do not crush": I think it means do not break the ladder up or sell it early, but the text does not say.

## Sentences whose logic does not hold or contradicts another

1. **P3 vs P5 (and P4).** P3: the $300k "buys a ladder ... one rung falling due before each cheque". P5: the 2028 deposit "first finishes any rung the first $300,000 couldn't buy". So the ladder may not be complete in 2027. Then P4's closing claim ("A cheque funded by a Treasury already bought does not [shrink]") holds only for rungs already bought; if the 2028 deposit is smaller and rungs are missing, the cheques are exposed. The IPS never says how likely a shortfall is or what happens first. The case's own "high degree of certainty" test is at stake here.
2. **P3 vs P8.** P3: "A Treasury held to maturity pays exactly what it promises ... That, to us, is what 'a high degree of certainty' means." P8 then admits the ladder can "fall short" if yields fall. Both are true (the bond pays its face value, but the reinvested coupons are the gap), but P3 reads as unqualified, and the definition of certainty is left less than certain. The case also asks teams to say how they evaluated certainty and what assumptions they used; the only assumption given is 7.5% on stocks, which the cheques "never depend on".
3. **P6 vs P9.** P6 makes the floor the *bottom of the gift range*. P9 ranks "the floor, then the gift" as if they were separate. Either "floor" and "gift" mean different things in P9 or it is a repeat.
4. **P6 vs P8.** P6 says the stocks Laura keeps are her *flexibility* (for a Taiwan-dollar overrun). P8 spends them first on the reinvestment shortfall, and then lowers the range if they run out. So the flexibility she was promised is partly pledged elsewhere, and P6 does not say so.
5. **P8, "lowers the whole range".** If the floor is "money she already owns" and is held in Treasuries for the gift, covering a shortfall out of it is the only way to lower the bottom. The text does not say that the floor can be used for the ladder, which would weaken "a number said aloud ... cannot be quietly halved" in P7.
6. **P5, "about a fifteenth of her money"** is a rough size, so the stock fund looks tiny compared with the 7.5% line a few words earlier. The stated 7.5% has no source ("We assume"), though Book 10 asks every number to say where it came from.
7. **Case requirements the text does not meet.** The case asks for a dollar range with a stated confidence, the size of the operating reserve, and the facility contribution's justification. The IPS gives the *method* of the range but no dollars and no confidence, and no reserve size. A judge scoring against the case would mark these missing.
8. **P2, "ten holdings".** The IPS also holds floor Treasuries and the fund, so "ten holdings" is only true of the ladder. Minor, but it is a literal claim.

## Sentences that read as machine-written, and why

- **"We don't invest toward these payments. We buy them."** (P3, and again in the pitch as "so we don't invest for them. We buy them.") Setup/knock-down pair of short sentences, a very common LLM cadence. Having it in both the pitch and the IPS makes it more noticeable.
- **"Laura's career already behaves a lot like a stock."** Tidy analogy delivered as a thesis line; "already behaves a lot like" is a hedge-plus-flourish pattern.
- **"A cheque funded by a Treasury already bought does not."** Elliptical punchline ("does not" what?). It reads as a crafted mic-drop, not as how a student would explain it.
- **"That is her floor."** and **"That, to us, is what 'a high degree of certainty' means."** Short declarative "label" sentences placed after a build-up, repeating the same beat.
- **"The order never changes: the cheques, then the floor, then the gift."** A neat rule-of-three that sounds authoritative but carries no checkable content (see LOST above).
- **"Do not crush."** One-line callback closer. It is the kind of ending an AI is fond of, and it depends on the cute-metaphor opener in P2.
- **The spaced en dash used as an em dash** appears many times (P1, P2, P3, P4) and is itself a common machine tell; plus the British spellings "cheques" and "dearer" in a US competition about a Texan client may be intentional (Sydney team), but together with the polish they give the prose an unusually even, performed tone.
- **P2 as a whole.** Pharmacist, capsule, "do not crush" is a polished invention that the actual plan hardly uses. It reads like a branded story attached to the plan, not something a student arrived at while thinking about the case.

## Verdict

The ideas land in P1, P3 and P7 and the plan is believable. The weak points are P5's bank-note comparison, P9's order sentence, and the lack of a dollar range with a confidence level in P6, the one thing the case asked for by name.

**Single worst sentence:** "A bank would call this a capital-protected note, except a bank would spend that interest on an option, which is worth nothing if the market stands still." (P5) It uses two pieces of jargon, sets up an "except" that never completes, and gives the judge no payoff for working it out. (Runner-up: "The order never changes: the cheques, then the floor, then the gift.", where floor and gift are used as if they were different things.)
