# Blind comparison: every Bible expectation

Strict counts (pitch, IPS): A 50 and 495; B 50 and 500. Both avoid every standing ban (banned instruments, rhetorical opening, "our model", statistics, citations, team history, subheadings, medicine imagery, "floor").

## Hard-rule breaches

**A**
- Book 12 (the first paragraph carries strategy, not the case), partly breached: it opens "Laura makes two commitments: ten $50,000 operating payments ... and a credible range for her facility contribution, named in 2031."
- Books 10-12 and Commandment IV (plain on one reading): "In 2028 she buys an extra stake in the Treasury fund behind the 2033 payment; it costs less than what's left of the deposit, but its interest makes up the difference by December 2032." It explains the range's bottom and is the hardest sentence in either draft.
- I83 (the case's words), a near-breach. "Debt" becomes the running label and stands in for the operating commitment at the close ("the debt is repaid"). "Cover" turns into a noun label, and "the building" stands in for the facility.
- I80, a borderline slogan flip: "inflation can raise what the building costs, never what she owes."

**B**
- I82 (every claim holds on the page): "Falling shares alone, before 2031, lower only the top." The next paragraph pays for the reinvestment cover from the half of her shares she kept, and "If that falls short, the 2032 fund covers the rest and her range starts lower." So falling shares can lower the bottom too.
- I82, minor: "Whenever Laura commits to a sum, she buys Treasuries" doesn't fit buying the bottom in 2028, three years before the range is named. The wording is at fault, not the plan.
- I80, borderline: "markets can change how large a range Laura names, but never whether she meets a payment" is a mirrored line, even though it is also the strength Ray asked for.

## Sentences a plain reader would stumble on

**A:** in the pitch, "half those shares become the top's Treasuries before she speaks"; "hence the X and R"; the 2028 stake sentence; "so they risk only the interest the stake will earn"; "Half her shares are held back"; "is the shares the cover didn't need and anything paid beyond the top".

**B:** in the pitch, "topped up if needed" (topped up by what?) and "buys a minimum facility contribution"; the jump from the opening rule to the pension sentence; "dollar Treasuries"; "the Treasuries moved to cover its shortfall".

## Verdict

Hand the students **B**. The margin is **clear** but not a landslide. B meets the rulings that weigh most now: Book 12's opening, plain sense on one reading, I83's case words and I86's named strength. A has the sharper idea and tighter logic, but its opening and its mechanism paragraph would have to be rewritten, not just edited.

What B must still fix:
1. The falling-shares claim must agree with para 5: the bottom falls only if the kept half can't pay for the cover.
2. The rule must convey that Treasuries are bought no later than the commitment.
3. The opening needs a link: the plan prices each sum at what Treasuries charge, which is the practice the pension sentence rejects.
4. The pitch must say what tops up the $300,000, that the Treasuries pay the minimum, and why this serves Laura.
5. It must say what happens to anything paid above the top. A's cap-and-keep rule would do it.
6. The strength line can keep its claim but shouldn't read as a mirrored slogan.
7. Optional: A's two-thirds leverage argument is the best answer to "why not a balanced mix".
8. At 500/500, every addition costs a whole idea, never a squeezed sentence.
