| B1 | Her ten operating payments are already bought when she meets co-sponsors, so their money can go to the building, where donors prefer to give. In a field test of 40,000 mailed appeals, saying a lead donor had already covered overhead raised total giving about 75% more than calling the same gift seed money. | Turns the dullest choice (buying the payments first) into the best fundraising fact; it is what the case means by a contribution that shows the residency is "financially viable". | Co-sponsor communication and the draft fundraising text | Gneezy, Keenan & Gneezy, *Science* 346 (2014), "Avoiding overhead aversion in charity" |
| B2 | What Laura keeps back is never promised to the building, because money a project knows it can call on tends to get spent (a soft budget). | Turns financial flexibility, our weakest answer, into a reason. | Financial flexibility | Kornai, Maskin & Roland, *JEL* (2003); Andreoni & Payne (2011) on crowd-out |
