## The strategy in three sentences
Whenever Laura commits to a sum, she buys the Treasuries that will pay it, on the day she commits: the ten operating
payments in 2027, a minimum facility contribution in 2028, and both ends of the range she gives co-sponsors in 2031.
Shares only ever hold money nobody is counting on: roughly the interest on that minimum. So markets can change how
big a range she names, but never whether she meets a payment or a number she has already named.

## What is new compared with V57
- No opening paragraph retelling the case. The first sentence is the strategy, and the first paragraph says why
  (pricing a fixed payment at share returns counts growth that hasn't happened; US public pensions do it and took on
  more risk).
- The biggest strength is stated outright (Ray's question on 5 Nov).
- No tablets or medicine. Caplet XR stays: X for the ten holdings, R for redemption. The name opens in paragraph 1,
  and the IPS ends on the tenth redemption in 2042.
- New reasons: why the minimum is bought in 2028 rather than 2031 (Laura could afford more risk, but the residency
  couldn't, because its co-sponsors, grants and fees can shrink in the same recessions); why only half her shares go
  into the range (the half she keeps covers the ladder's one weakness, then stays her financial flexibility); why the
  bottom is rounded down; inflation (fixed-dollar payments, so dollar Treasuries match them).
