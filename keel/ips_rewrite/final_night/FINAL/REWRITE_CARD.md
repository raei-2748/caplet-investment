# Rewrite card: putting the final IPS in your own words

Keep `pitch.txt` and `ips.txt` open beside this. For each sentence: **Keep** = the idea it must still carry.
**Locked** = words that stay as they are, because a fact check or our October trading notes and TNA depend on them.
**Free** = where you can cut or reword to make room for your own words.

**Limits:** both texts are already full (50 and 500 words by the strict count, which splits hyphens and dashes:
`python3 keel/office/tools/textcheck.py ips.txt --limit 500`). Every word you add needs one cut.
**Never write:** "floor", "cheque", "promise(s)" as a label, "risk-free", "guaranteed", "immunised",
"duration-matched", "just before January", STRIPS, TIPS, CPPI. No model numbers (the range, odds) in the IPS:
they go in the Final Report.

## Pitch
1. *Laura buys Treasuries for each sum as she commits to it.* **Keep:** the one rule (buy the Treasuries the day she
   commits). **Free:** wording, but keep the timing ("as she commits").
2. *Her $300,000, topped up if needed, covers ten $50,000 operating payments.* **Locked:** "topped up if needed"
   (on the 4 Nov prices the ladder costs about $600 more than $300,000). **Free:** order of the words.
3. *In 2028 she buys a minimum facility contribution and puts roughly its interest into world shares.*
   **Locked:** "minimum" (never a fixed sum), "roughly". **Free:** "world shares" could be "a world share fund".
4. *Both ends of her 2031 range are owned before she names it.* **Keep:** both ends bought before co-sponsors hear it.
   This is the strongest idea; don't cut it.

## IPS ¶1 – the rule and why
- *Whenever Laura commits to a sum, she buys Treasuries that will repay it in time.* **Keep:** the rule, first.
- *Pricing a fixed sum at what shares usually earn … growth that hasn't happened yet.* **Keep:** why the rule is
  right. **Free:** say it your way; this is the best place for your voice.
- *American public pensions are allowed to price their promises that way, and they have taken on more risk to keep
  up.* **Locked:** no time span (no "two decades": unverified). **Free:** "to keep up" could become "to keep up with
  it" if you find two words.
- *We call the plan Caplet XR: X for its ten Treasury holdings, R for redemption.* **Locked:** X = ten, R = redemption.
  No tablets or medicine (Ray, 5 Nov).

## ¶2 – the ten payments
- *In January 2027 … one maturing before each January from 2033 to 2042.* **Locked:** "before each January".
- *Each holding, kept to its end, pays out what its Treasuries repay, whatever its price does meanwhile.*
  **Locked:** "pays out what its Treasuries repay" (the funds pay their net asset value, not a promised sum).
  Never "pays exactly what it promised" for all ten.
- *The payments aren't adjusted for inflation … a high degree of certainty means that no operating payment depends on
  a share price.* **Keep:** both halves (inflation, and our definition of certainty, which the case asks for).

## ¶3 – the 2028 deposit
- *Her 2028 deposit first covers any shortfall in the ladder.* **Locked:** "any shortfall" (conditional).
- *Most of the rest buys more of her first holding, a fund ending in late 2032, …* **Locked:** "a fund ending in late
  2032" (it introduces the fund; it ends 15 Dec 2032 and also pays the first January).
- *Roughly the interest they will earn goes into one world index fund … the one place the plan is allowed to be
  wrong.* **Locked:** "allowed to be wrong" (from our VT reflection). **Free:** "holding thousands of companies".
- *Buying the minimum in 2028, not 2031, gives up some growth.* **Keep:** the cost, said plainly.
- *Laura's living costs are paid from outside the portfolio … the one source that won't.* **Locked:** "can shrink"
  (never "will" or "do": the case doesn't say they move with markets). The judges' favourite sentence; keep the idea.

## ¶4 – the 2031 range
- *Its bottom is what those extra units will pay, rounded down because a fund's final payout isn't fixed.*
  **Locked:** the whole reason clause.
- *Its top adds half of what her shares are then worth, moved into Treasuries before she names it.* **Locked:** "half",
  "before she names it".
- *With both ends bought … as sure of landing inside it as of Treasuries.* **Keep.** No "guaranteed".
- *Falling shares alone, before 2031, lower only the top, and rising shares lift it.* **Locked:** "alone" (falling
  shares plus low rates can lower the bottom, under ¶5).

## ¶5 – the one weakness
- *The ladder's weakness is money that waits … if rates fall and stay low.* **Keep:** say the weakness plainly.
- *Before she names her range, we assume that money earns nothing … to cover it.* **Locked:** "earns nothing",
  "the half of her shares she kept" (not "her other half").
- *If that falls short, the 2032 fund covers the rest and her range starts lower.* **Locked.** It keeps the
  bottom honest.
- *Whatever that half isn't needed for … the financial flexibility she would lose by committing everything.*
  **Keep:** the case's words "financial flexibility".

## ¶6 – the strength and the close
- *The plan's strength is that markets can change how large a range Laura names, but never whether she meets a
  payment or a number already named.* **Keep.** "never" is allowed (true by design); "not" is your call.
- *From 2033 the ladder and the Treasuries moved to cover its shortfall become her operating reserve, and no holding
  is sold early.* **Locked:** "no holding is sold early" (never "nothing is ever sold").
- *One holding is redeemed before each January, until the tenth redemption pays the last $50,000 in 2042.*
  **Keep:** the ending on the tenth redemption (it closes the Caplet XR circle).
