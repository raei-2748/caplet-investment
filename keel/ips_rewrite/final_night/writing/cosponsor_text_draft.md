# Co-sponsor text: draft for the Final Report (Writing 1.2, Fri 6 Nov 2026)

The case asks each team to "draft part of Laura's fundraising materials describing this potential contribution for
prospective co-sponsors". Below are two versions in Laura's voice, for Caplet to rewrite in its own words. Both follow
the final IPS (`../FINAL/ips.txt`): every operating payment is already paid for, and both ends of the range are held
in Treasuries before she says it. The dollar figures are a model estimate from 4 Nov 2026 (`../FACTS.md`, N4-N5). The
real range is set in 2031 and must replace them.

## Version A: one paragraph (about 120 words)

> I am putting my own money into the Taiwan residency in two ways. First, I will pay $50,000 a year towards its running
> costs for ten years, from 2033 to 2042. By 2028 that money is set aside in US Treasuries, one maturing before each
> January, so it does not depend on the stock market. Second, I expect to contribute between [$140,000] and
> [$160,000] towards the building in 2033. I hold both of those amounts in Treasuries before I name them, so a bad
> year in the markets should only lower the top of this range. What I keep back stays unpromised. I would rather tell you a
> smaller number I will meet than a bigger one I might not.

## Version B: with the range and how sure I am (about 250 words)

> **My commitment to the residency**
>
> I am backing the residency with my own money before asking anyone else to.
>
> **Running costs.** I will pay $50,000 at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042, ten payments in all. These
> payments are not left to the markets. By 2028 I hold Treasury bonds and Treasury funds that mature before each
> January, one for each payment. So my ten payments are already paid for.
>
> **The building.** In 2033 I expect to contribute **between [$140,000] and [$160,000]** towards the facility.
>
>
> **How sure I am.** I hold the bottom of that range in Treasuries from 2028. Before I name the top, I move the money
> for it out of shares and into Treasuries too. So both ends are already owned when you read this. My contribution
> should land inside the range. Falling share prices before 2031 can lower only the top. Rising prices can lift it.
>
> **What I am not promising.** I keep part of this money back, liquid and unpromised, so that the residency and I can
> both adapt as the project grows. I would rather name a range I can meet than one that sounds bigger.
>
> **What would change it.** If interest rates fall and stay low, the money waiting between payments earns less. I
> cover that first, before naming the range, so it can make the range smaller, not the payments.

## Notes for Caplet and the reviewers
- **Placeholders.** The range in both versions must be rerun on the final design and dated in Caplet's own note, not in Laura's voice (Review, 6 Nov). [$140,000] and [$160,000] are rounded from the 4 Nov model (bottom $140,000; median top $163,000,
  rounded down to $160,000). Both are MODEL numbers on an older design (no naming-day lock). Rerun before printing.
- **Case words used:** operating payments, running costs ("operating expenses" in the case), facility, co-sponsors,
  range. No coined labels (no "floor", "cheques", "promises" as a noun).
- **Deliberately left out:** any claim that funders prefer to give to buildings (unsourced; bank B1 flags it), any
  probability ("99%"), and "guaranteed", "risk-free" or "ironclad".

## Claims the Fact-Checker should confirm
1. "In 2027 I bought US Treasuries that mature before each January, one for each payment": true of the plan. Five of
   the ten are iBond Treasury funds, which pay their net asset value, not a fixed sum. Is "Treasuries" fine for a
   co-sponsor audience, or does it need "Treasury bonds and Treasury funds"?
2. "a bad year in the markets cannot make me break this number" (A) and "it can only make the range smaller, never
   break it" (B): true by design (IPS ¶5-6). Check that it isn't stronger than the TNA's "near-ironclad".
3. "My contribution should land inside the range": consistent with the IPS's "as sure … as of Treasuries"; the funds'
   payout isn't fixed, which is why the bottom is rounded down.
4. The [$140,000]-[$160,000] figures: MODEL, 4 Nov, old design; need a rerun on the final design.
5. "my share of the first ten years' running costs is already paid for": the ten payments cover only Laura's
   $50,000 a year, not all running costs. The wording says "my share"; confirm it reads that way.
