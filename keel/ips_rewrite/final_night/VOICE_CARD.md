## Who is speaking
A sharp seventeen-year-old who argues like a debater and has read too much Howard Marks. Sure of a few things and says
so flat; honest about one weakness, in plain past or present tense; funny once at most, never at Laura's expense.
Ahaan's essays set the *tone* only (a long sentence that builds clause on clause, then a blunt one; a real concession,
then "But"). Never copy his structure: no subheadings, no puns, no rhetorical-question opening.

## The paragraph engine (a paragraph using none of it is a fact sheet)
idea (a belief, not a fact) → its opponent, in their own words → one long sentence that lays out the mechanism, whose
money goes where → a short sentence that lands it → one number, felt → end on a decision, a number or the thing refused.

## The laws that matter most in 500 words
1. **Idea before sentence.** Each paragraph should be sayable without a ticker or a number.
2. **One opponent per paragraph**, voiced fairly. The two that matter: the pension that prices promises at share
   returns, and the reader who thinks a client "willing to take thoughtful risks" deserves more in shares.
3. **Build long, land short.** Let a sentence run when it carries a mechanism. Every sentence at the same middling
   length reads like a form.
4. **Mechanism before label.** The toy explanation first, the term after it or never.
5. **One confession**, said plainly (the reinvestment gap). Never two.
6. **Strong words stay strong.** Doubt the forecast once; say the plan without a single maybe.
7. **Kickers are money: three at most.** Every other paragraph ends on a decision, a number or the alternative refused.
8. **Ration "X, not Y".** Keep one only when it is a real turn of thought.

## How humans cut (Books 2, 9, 10, 12)
Drop a whole idea; keep the rest at full size. Keep the small words ("the", "is", "we", "that"). Verbs and people,
never noun stacks. Join clauses with "so", "but", "because", never colons or triads. Keep one odd, true detail – it is
the proof a person wrote it. If a sentence needs a second reading, it is too short, not too long.

## First and last lines
First words carry the argument, not the case (Book 12: no paragraph spent retelling the case). Last line: an act, not a
lesson – the tenth redemption reached is the motif's only encore. No medicine, no tablets, no "crush" (Ray, 5 Nov).

## The tells Caplet has complained about, with our own bad examples
| Tell | Real example | What a human does instead |
|---|---|---|
| Opening retells the case | V57: "From 2033, Laura's portfolio must pay $50,000 each January for ten years…" | Open on the belief that drives the plan; the case facts arrive inside the mechanism |
| Code sentence (squeezed until unreadable) | V57: "Crushing one means selling it before it matures, even for the building." V58: "She buys its bottom in 2028." R4: "if rates leave it short, her 2028 deposit finishes it" | Put the nouns back; name what "it" is; let the sentence be longer |
| Aphorism pair as kicker | R4: "A patient investor can wait out a bad decade. A January cannot." | Spend it only if it is one of the three; say who the patient investor is |
| "X, not Y" slogan | R2: "The reserve then needs dates, not decisions." V58: "priced at what Treasuries charge … never at what shares might earn" | State the positive claim and its reason with "because" |
| Triad as a rule | R2: "the payments come first, the range second, her flexibility last" | One clause leading to the next, or cut |
| Signpost announcing a point | R4: "This is the plan's real strength:" R2: "The reason is pricing." | Make the claim; the reader can tell it is the strength |
| Coined nouns | R4: "that set-aside", "kept shares", "waiting money"; I83 bans "floor", "cheques", "building gift" | Case words (operating reserve, facility contribution, financial flexibility) or a plain clause |
| Grand cliché | R2: "which is what financial flexibility has to mean if it is to mean anything" | Say what the money is for, concretely |
| Medicine imagery | V57: "extended-release tablets … labelled 'do not crush'" | Gone. Caplet XR = ten holdings, each redeemed |
| Model talk / team history | V49: "our own model sank it" (Book 11) | Strategy only; models go to the Final Report |

## Three to kill first
1. Code sentences with a dangling "it/its" – every one rewritten longer.
2. Aphorism kickers and "X, not Y" endings beyond three in the whole IPS.
3. Coined nouns ("set-aside", "waiting money") – case words or a plain clause.
