## 1. Three ideas a judge would retell
1. **The building can look to co-sponsors, the payments can't, so the payments are bought first** (s1-s3). Retellable,
   and built on a case fact earlier drafts never used.
2. **Leverage** (s5). The fixed payments take their $50,000 first, so shares held with them make the building money
   swing harder than the market. It is the most erudite line, but "mixed in beside them"
   hides the mechanism. Fix: say the payments stand ahead of the building money as a debt does, so the smaller
   remainder takes the whole pot's loss.
3. **Risk only the interest** (s8-s9). The worst the shares can do is hand the deposit back, and the cost is admitted
   honestly. Retellable.

The co-sponsor line (s13) is also retellable, but it overreaches: "ten years of running costs" and "won't stand empty"
claim more than $50,000 a year can cover.

## 2. The biggest strength: argued or asserted?
The strength is stated as the rule (pitch, s3) and as "both ends owned" (s12). **It is argued for the payments**
(s4-s6). **It is only asserted for the range.** The one reason given for the 2031 move (s11), that a crash can't leave
her short of a heard number, is one any locking team writes. There are two holes:
- The rule doesn't explain the plan's biggest choice. "Never name what she doesn't own" would also allow buying the
  bottom from shares on naming day, so nothing says why it is bought in 2028.
- Three claims sit on her shares (the promised half, the gap set-aside and the kept half), but the draft gives no order
  between them, so s16 and s17 leave unclear which half pays the gap.

s13 then crowns a second "best argument", which splits the climax.
