Laura will stake her word on this portfolio twice: on ten operating payments of $50,000, one each January from 2033, and in 2031 on a credible range for her facility contribution. Caplet XR is built so that both times she already owns the money before she gives her word. The payments rest on ten Treasury holdings, each redeemed, or repaid by the US government, in the year before the January it pays for, and the plan is named for those ten redemptions (X is the Roman ten).

In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys the ten holdings: iBonds funds for the 2033 to 2037 payments and Treasury bonds for 2038 to 2042. At this autumn's yields that is about what all ten cost, so the payments need no more than Treasuries already pay, and shares could add nothing to them except the chance of falling short. We take a high degree of certainty to mean that no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or an early sale, so each could fail only if the US government missed a payment of its own.

Shares nearly always beat Treasuries over ten years, and the plan gives that up for two reasons. Because the payments are fixed, they take their $50,000 first whatever markets do, so any loss lands on the money left for the building; with shares mixed in beside the payments, that money would swing harder than the share market itself. And her 2028 deposit comes from her work as an author and speaker, which can shrink in the same recession that cuts share prices.

When that deposit arrives, it first tops up the ten holdings if falling yields made them cost more than $300,000. Most of the rest buys an iBonds fund maturing in late 2032, at a price that grows back to roughly the whole deposit by then, and only the slice left over, about the interest that fund will earn, goes into a world index fund. A bank would sell Laura this shape as a capital-protected note and spend the interest on an option; built by hand, she owns the shares herself.

In 2031 the bottom of her range is what the 2032 fund will pay, rounded down because a fund's payout is not fixed. For the top, she moves half of her shares into Treasuries before naming the range, so a crash in 2032 can't leave her short of a figure co-sponsors have heard. With both ends owned on the day she says them, she can give the range with near-complete confidence. Her strongest argument to co-sponsors is the payments themselves: a new residency's worst risk is a building that can't pay to run, and Laura's ten years are already bought.

The weak point is the interest the holdings pay along the way, which must be reinvested until its payment is due; if rates fall and stay low, it comes up short. So before she names her range, we work out that gap as if the waiting money earned nothing, and move that much from her shares into Treasuries. If her shares can't cover it, the range is smaller.

The other half of her shares is never mentioned to co-sponsors, since every overrun would become a claim on it; it is her flexibility if building costs in Taiwan outrun the figure she named. From 2033 nothing changes because markets move. Each January one holding is redeemed and pays that year's $50,000, so the operating reserve shrinks by one holding a year while her kept shares stay invested, until in January 2042 the tenth redemption pays the last operating payment.
