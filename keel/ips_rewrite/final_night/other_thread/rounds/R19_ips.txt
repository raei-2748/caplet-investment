Caplet XR buys every amount Laura commits to with Treasuries before she commits to it, and only money nobody has been promised goes into shares. Her ten operating payments of $50,000 come first, because co-sponsors may help pay for the facility, never for those. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each maturing in the year before the January it pays for, and the plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption).

Fixed payments are made in full whatever markets do, so every loss falls on what is left over. A balanced mix of shares and bonds behind them would make that leftover swing harder than the share market, just as a company's shares grow riskier as it borrows. So we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or a sale before maturity. Every payment rests on one borrower, the US government, on purpose, since a second borrower would only add a second way to miss a January.

The 2028 deposit first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, if bond prices rose before January 2027. Most of the rest buys more of the iBonds fund behind the first payment, whose Treasuries all mature in late 2032. What that extra holding pays out, rounded down, since a fund's payout isn't fixed, is the bottom of the range she will name in 2031. Waiting until 2031 to buy it would leave the bottom at the mercy of shares in the same years that co-sponsors give less. The slice left over, about that fund's interest, buys a world index fund.

The plan's weak point is the interest its holdings pay along the way. It waits in a short-term Treasury fund until the January it was meant for, and if rates fall and stay low, it earns less than the bonds' prices assumed.

So before naming her range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus that half. From the other half she sets aside enough Treasuries to cover the interest shortfall, assuming the waiting money earns nothing. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, and afterwards shares move neither end. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost is known.

What remains of the other half stays out of the range as her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day and the only part of her money that can grow with building costs in Taiwan. From 2033 the ten holdings and the money set aside are her operating reserve. Nothing in the plan changes because markets move, and each January's $50,000 comes from the holding redeemed before it. The tenth redemption, a Treasury bond maturing in November 2041, pays the last operating payment in January 2042.
