Caplet XR buys every amount Laura commits to with Treasuries before she commits to it, and lets only money nobody has been promised own shares. Her ten operating payments of $50,000 come first, because co-sponsors may help pay for the facility but never for those. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each maturing in the year before the January it pays for, and the plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption).

Fixed payments are made in full whatever markets do, so every loss falls on what is left over. A balanced mix of shares and bonds behind them would make that leftover swing harder than the share market itself, just as a company's shares grow riskier as it borrows. We therefore read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or a sale before maturity. Every payment rests on one borrower, the US government, on purpose, since a second borrower would only add a second way to miss a January.

The 2028 deposit first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, if bond prices rose before January 2027. Most of the rest buys more of the iBonds fund behind the first payment, whose Treasuries all mature in late 2032, and that extra payout is the bottom of the range she will name in 2031. Keeping the deposit in shares until then would probably mean a bigger range, but a bad market would shrink it in the same years that co-sponsors, and Laura's own income, can shrink too. The slice left over, about that fund's interest, goes into a world index fund of thousands of companies.

The holdings pay interest along the way, which waits in a short-term Treasury fund until the January it was meant for, and earns less if rates fall and stay low.

Before naming her range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus that half. From the other half she sets aside enough Treasuries to cover the interest shortfall, assuming the waiting money earns nothing at all. Only if that half runs out does the top come down. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, and afterwards shares move neither end. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost is known.

What remains of the other half stays out of the range as her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day and the only part of her money that can grow with building costs in Taiwan. From 2033 the ten holdings and the money set aside are her operating reserve, and each January's $50,000 comes from the holding redeemed before it. The tenth redemption, a Treasury bond maturing in November 2041, pays the last operating payment in January 2042.
