Co-sponsors can help Laura pay for the facility, but her ten operating payments of $50,000 must come from this portfolio alone, so Caplet XR buys them first. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each maturing in the year before the January it pays for, and the plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption). From then on it keeps one rule: Laura never states an amount she doesn't already hold the money for.

Fixed payments work like a debt the portfolio owes. They are paid in full whatever markets do, so every loss falls on what is left over, and with the usual balanced mix of shares and bonds behind them, that leftover would swing harder than the share market itself. So no shares sit behind a payment, and we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or selling anything before it matures.

The 2028 deposit first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, which happens if bond prices rise before January 2027. Most of the rest buys more of the iBonds fund behind the first payment, a fund of Treasuries that all mature in late 2032, so that one fund does two jobs: its extra payout is the bottom of the range she will name in 2031. Leaving the deposit in shares until then would probably mean a bigger range, but in a bad market the bottom would shrink in the same years that co-sponsors give less. Only the slice left over, about the interest that fund will earn, goes into a world index fund.

The holdings pay interest along the way, and that interest waits in a short-term Treasury fund until the January it was meant for. If rates fall and stay low, the waiting money earns less than the bonds' prices assumed. That is the plan's weak point, and 2031 deals with it.

Before naming her range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top of her range is the bottom plus that half. She covers the interest shortfall from the other half, worked out as if the waiting money earned nothing at all; if that half can't cover it, the top comes down first. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, but once named the range no longer depends on shares, since a top left in shares would take every fall while every rise above it went to Laura. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost and the co-sponsors' pledges are known.

Whatever remains of the other half stays outside the range. Offered, it could become a claim for every cost overrun; kept back, it is her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day, and the only part of her money that can grow with building costs in Taiwan. From 2033 the ten holdings and the money set aside are her operating reserve, and each January's $50,000 comes from the holding redeemed before it. The tenth redemption, a Treasury bond maturing in November 2041, pays the last operating payment in January 2042.
