Co-sponsors may help pay for the facility, but never for Laura's ten operating payments of $50,000, so Caplet XR buys those first. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each maturing in the year before the January it pays for, and the plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption). Its one rule from then on: Laura never states an amount she doesn't already hold the money for.

Fixed payments work like a debt the portfolio owes. They are paid in full whatever markets do, so every loss falls on what is left over, and with the usual balanced mix of shares and bonds behind them, that leftover would swing harder than the share market itself. We therefore keep shares away from the payments and read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or selling anything before it matures.

The 2028 deposit first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, which happens if bond prices rise before January 2027. Most of the rest buys more of the iBonds fund behind the first payment, a fund of Treasuries that all mature in late 2032, so that one fund does two jobs: its extra payout is the bottom of the range she will name in 2031. Leaving the deposit in shares until then would probably mean a bigger range, but in a bad market the bottom would shrink in the same years that co-sponsors give less. Only the slice left over, about that fund's interest, goes into a world index fund.

The holdings pay interest along the way, which waits in a short-term Treasury fund until the January it was meant for, and if rates fall and stay low, that waiting money earns less than the bonds' prices assumed.

Before naming her range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top of her range is the bottom plus that half. She covers the interest shortfall from the other half, worked out as if the waiting money earned nothing at all; if that half can't cover it, the top comes down first. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, and once she has named the range, neither end depends on shares. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost and the co-sponsors' pledges are known.

Whatever remains of the other half is never offered, because it could become a claim for every cost overrun. It is her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day, and the only part of her money that can grow with building costs in Taiwan. From 2033 the ten holdings and the money set aside are her operating reserve, and each January's $50,000 comes from the holding redeemed before it. The tenth redemption, a Treasury bond maturing in November 2041, pays the last operating payment in January 2042.
