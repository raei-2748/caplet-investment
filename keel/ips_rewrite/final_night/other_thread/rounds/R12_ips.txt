Co-sponsors can help Laura pay for the facility, but her ten operating payments of $50,000 must come from this portfolio alone, so Caplet XR buys them first. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each maturing in the year before the January it pays for, and the plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption). From then on it keeps one rule: Laura never states an amount she doesn't already hold the money for.

Fixed payments work like a debt the portfolio owes. They are paid in full whatever markets do, so every loss falls on whatever is left over, and with shares held beside the payments, that leftover would swing harder than the share market itself. So no shares sit behind a payment, and we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or selling anything before it matures, because each payment's cash arrives from its own holding.

The 2028 deposit first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, which happens if bond prices rise before January 2027. Most of the rest buys an iBonds fund, a fund of Treasuries that all mature in late 2032, priced to grow back to about what she put in, and its payout is the bottom of the range she will name in 2031. Leaving the deposit in shares until then would probably mean a bigger range, but in a bad market the bottom would shrink in the same years that co-sponsors give less. Only the slice left over, about the interest that fund will earn, goes into a world index fund. Treasuries alone would leave her a larger sure sum, but shares are the only part of her money that can grow with building costs in Taiwan.

The holdings pay interest along the way, and that interest waits in a short-term Treasury fund until the January it was meant for. If rates fall and stay low, the waiting money earns less than the bonds' prices assumed. So before naming her range, Laura moves enough of her shares into Treasuries to cover the shortfall, worked out as if the waiting money earned nothing at all.

Then she moves half of the remaining shares into Treasuries, and the top of her range is the bottom plus that half. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, but after she names the range neither end depends on shares, since a named top left in shares would take every fall while every rise above it went to Laura. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost and the co-sponsors' pledges are known.

The other half of her shares stays outside the range. If it were offered, every cost overrun would become a claim on it; kept back, it is her financial flexibility as the project develops. From 2033 the ten holdings and the money set aside are her operating reserve, and each January's $50,000 comes from the holding redeemed before it. The tenth redemption, a Treasury bond maturing in November 2041, pays the last operating payment in January 2042.
