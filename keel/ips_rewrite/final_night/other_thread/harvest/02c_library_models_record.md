### 1A. The three TNA entries, verbatim

**Trading Note 1: B-T-4.750-15022037.** Note: "Future funding: we use the 4.750% Treasury maturing 15 Feb 2037 for
Laura's 1 Jan 2038 payment because it is the latest WInS bond available beforehand. The 10.5-month gap creates
reinvestment-rate risk on the proceeds held in a short-term Treasury ETF."
Reflection: "We know Treasury bonds (including this one – maturing 2037) won't create wealth, but they will remove
uncertainty, through duration-matching. In other words, we take Laura's requested 'high degree of certainty' to mean a
near-ironclad guarantee, and we provide exactly that. Every year the bonds sit untouched is a good year – one less year
of Laura's money being exposed to anything the restless markets might decide to do. Notably – Laura's payments are
fixed in nominal dollars, so a nominal Treasury matches the liability perfectly, meaning we don't need TIPS, which would
hedge against a nonexistent inflation risk."

**Trading Note 2: IBTM.** Note: "Future funding and risk management: IBTM does two jobs in Laura's plan. It supports her
first $50,000 payment and provides a floor for her facility contribution, giving co-sponsors a minimum figure in 2031.
It holds 2032 Treasuries, so the timing fits, although the final payout is not fixed."
Reflection: "Some years, WInS lists no Treasuries maturing – so we purchased iShares iBonds (like IBTM for 2032) instead.
Defined-maturity, investment-grade, liquid, cheap and transparent about yields, these behave a lot like bonds when held
to their end – so we'll simply ignore price noise in the interim. Together, our T-Bonds and iBonds ensure each of
Laura's payments rests on a dedicated instrument (not merely a forecast). The catch is, their terminal payout is a net
asset value, as opposed to a promise of par – but in return we gain a level of liquidity which no odd-lot bond offers."

**Trading Note 3: VT.** Note: "Growth: Vanguard Total World Stock ETF holds about 10,000 stocks at a 0.06% fee, so one
cheap fund diversifies Laura's stocks. They only set how far her gift rises above her promised minimum. A fall can
shrink the extra, and a rise before 2031 lifts the top of the range co-sponsors hear."
Reflection: "VT is where we allow ourselves to be wrong – because it's the only investment not standing between Laura
and a payment deadline. This ETF owns thousands of companies, diversified across countries and sectors, which means we
can avoid paying the price for the vain illusion that we can pick winners, instead buying the entire market's growth
premium at an expense ratio of just 0.06%, for a forecast return of about 7.5% a year (as per JPMorgan). The T-Bonds and
iBonds already cover the operating commitments, so VT answers a different question – how generous can the facility
contribution be?"

### Case details past drafts ignored that a judge would expect used (client knowledge)
1. **Living costs sit outside the portfolio and nothing flows in or out before 2033 (p2 L61-65).** It means no
   liquidity need before 2033 (so every holding can be held to its end) and it sets her capacity for loss by goal:
   a fall in the kept shares never touches her day-to-day life; a missed payment can't be made good from outside
   (p3 L91-92). Zero drafts use it.
2. **"Laura understands that investing involves uncertainty" and "willing to take thoughtful risks" (p2 L68-71).**
   The case says she is not risk-averse; her career already carries her risk. Never write that she underestimates
   risk (V50 did, `keel/rooms/archive.md:96`, A28) and never infer risk appetite from her degree.
3. **The asymmetry (p3 L84-86 against L91-92).** Outside money may cover the facility and any support beyond her
   commitment; it may not cover the payments. That is the case's own reason for where risk can sit.
4. **"Meaningful" is half of the co-sponsor test (p3 L110-112).** Drafts argue "credible" only.
5. **"Reliable support for its early operations" (p3 L83).** The payments are exactly that support.
6. **The residency itself (p2 L76-80):** small, community-oriented, people who "temporarily live, work, teach, and
   collaborate". Never described in any draft; the ten payments keep that place running for its first decade.
7. **"How they evaluated" and "the assumptions" (p3 L98-99; p4 L146-149).** Both drafts define certainty but never
   say how it was tested or what it assumes (US Treasuries pay; the 2028 deposit arrives; flows at the start of
   each year; no outside money; inflation lands on the facility, not the payments).
8. **"State how confident" (p3 L117-118).** Neither draft gives the basis of confidence, even in words. The Final
   Report gives the level; the IPS can still say what the confidence rests on.
9. **"Favorable" outcomes (p3 L118-119; p4 L148).** Neither draft says what a good market does.
10. **The reserve's "size" (p3 L96).** Neither draft says, even in words, what the reserve is sized to do.
11. **"Other entrepreneurial ventures" (p2 L45) and "continued business income" (p3 L86).** Her business is both a
    source of the deposit and a possible source of facility money; drafts cut both.
12. **"She will contribute" (p2 L43-44).** The case treats the deposit as given. Doubt it once at most, as the team's
    own stress case (`keel/library/insight_v1/phase_E/E5_premortem.md:364-392`, PM-07 tripwire).
13. **The statistics degree (p1 L16).** A reader of ranges and confidence; at most once, never as a reason for risk.
14. **"With an asset management firm" (p2 L43).** Costs exist; the plan can say which money pays them (idea 23).
