## 1. The submitted TNA (three entries, verbatim)

**Note 1, 4.750% T-bond 15 Feb 2037.** Note: "Future funding: we use the 4.750% Treasury maturing 15 Feb 2037 for
Laura's 1 Jan 2038 payment because it is the latest WInS bond available beforehand. The 10.5-month gap creates
reinvestment-rate risk on the proceeds held in a short-term Treasury ETF."
Reflection: "We know Treasury bonds (including this one – maturing 2037) won't create wealth, but they will remove
uncertainty, through duration-matching. In other words, we take Laura's requested 'high degree of certainty' to mean a
near-ironclad guarantee, and we provide exactly that. Every year the bonds sit untouched is a good year – one less year
of Laura's money being exposed to anything the restless markets might decide to do. Notably – Laura's payments are fixed
in nominal dollars, so a nominal Treasury matches the liability perfectly, meaning we don't need TIPS, which would hedge
against a nonexistent inflation risk."

**Note 2, IBTM.** Note: "Future funding and risk management: IBTM does two jobs in Laura's plan. It supports her first
$50,000 payment and provides a floor for her facility contribution, giving co-sponsors a minimum figure in 2031. It
holds 2032 Treasuries, so the timing fits, although the final payout is not fixed."
Reflection: "Some years, WInS lists no Treasuries maturing – so we purchased iShares iBonds (like IBTM for 2032) instead.
Defined-maturity, investment-grade, liquid, cheap and transparent about yields, these behave a lot like bonds when held
to their end – so we'll simply ignore price noise in the interim. Together, our T-Bonds and iBonds ensure each of
Laura's payments rests on a dedicated instrument (not merely a forecast). The catch is, their terminal payout is a net
asset value, as opposed to a promise of par – but in return we gain a level of liquidity which no odd-lot bond offers."

**Note 3, VT.** Note: "Growth: Vanguard Total World Stock ETF holds about 10,000 stocks at a 0.06% fee, so one cheap fund
diversifies Laura's stocks. They only set how far her gift rises above her promised minimum. A fall can shrink the
extra, and a rise before 2031 lifts the top of the range co-sponsors hear."
Reflection: "VT is where we allow ourselves to be wrong – because it's the only investment not standing between Laura
and a payment deadline. This ETF owns thousands of companies, diversified across countries and sectors, which means we
can avoid paying the price for the vain illusion that we can pick winners, instead buying the entire market's growth
premium at an expense ratio of just 0.06%, for a forecast return of about 7.5% a year (as per JPMorgan). The T-Bonds and
iBonds already cover the operating commitments, so VT answers a different question – how generous can the facility
contribution be?"

## 5. What the IPS may and may not say because of this record

**May say (AGREES or grows from the record):**
1. Each of the ten payments has its own Treasury holding, held until it ends, before its January.
2. A Treasury **bond** held to maturity pays the amount it promised; the funds pay their net asset value when they close
   in December, which is near the amount but not fixed (keep "near"/"not fixed" somewhere if the funds' payout is
   described).
3. "High degree of certainty" read as no payment depending on shares (reflection 3 + reflection 1's near-ironclad).
4. Payments are fixed in dollars, so nominal Treasuries match them and inflation-linked bonds aren't needed.
5. The shares are the only place the plan is allowed to be wrong; they decide only how far the facility contribution
   rises above its minimum; a rise before 2031 lifts the top of the range; a fall shrinks the extra.
6. The 2032 fund backs the first payment and sets the minimum co-sponsors hear in 2031.
7. If yields fall before the bonds are bought, the 2028 deposit tops up the ladder first and the facility gets less.
8. Reinvesting coupons and early maturities is the ladder's named weakness.
9. The bonds are not traded as markets move; changes happen on planned dates.
10. One cheap world fund holding thousands of companies, rather than picking winners.
11. NEW-but-compatible (no locked words either way): the 2031 range's top is moved into Treasuries before it is named;
    the other half of the shares stays unpromised; diversifying the residency's money; pricing each commitment at what
    Treasuries charge; the 2028 deposit's sources "can" shrink in the same downturns.
