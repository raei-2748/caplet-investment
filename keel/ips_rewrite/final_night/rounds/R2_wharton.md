## Scores: 39 / 50 (would be ~41 inside 500 words; as submitted it is excluded, see X8)

**1. Investment Strategy – 9/10.** The thesis is one reason a judge can repeat: money leaves shares the day someone
starts counting on it, because a fixed sum has one honest price (what Treasuries charge). Every dated move follows
from it – 2027 buys the payments, 2028 buys the least she could give, 2031 buys the top before it is said, 2033-42
runs on redemptions – so the plan reads as one argument, not a list. Diversification is argued at the level of the
residency's money ("the part of the residency's money that doesn't shrink with the rest"), which is the most
original answer to "appropriate diversification" I have seen in the drafts. Lost a point because the share fund
between 2028 and 2031 has no stated behaviour (does it ever rebalance? what if it doubles or halves?).

**2. Client Knowledge – 8/10.** Strong case facts used to decide things: fixed nominal payments → Treasuries; the
2028 money from "book advances, speaking fees and licensing" → keep shares small; the credibility risk → buy the
range before saying it; "flexibility" defined as money "promised to no one". Missing: the case's "willing to take
thoughtful risks" is never answered head-on. A judge who notices only the interest goes into shares will ask
whether the plan listened to that line; the draft's answer (her fortunes already ride on the economy) is there but
reads as a defence, not as the thoughtful risk itself.

**3. Portfolio Analysis – 8/10.** Concepts are used, not named: pricing a liability at the Treasury rate (and the
public-pension counter-example), cash's reinvestment risk, the capital-protected-note shape, dividend smoothing as
the logic of the range, the ladder's own reinvestment weakness with a rule and a priority order. Certainty is
defined (C1). Weaker on C3: unfavourable outcomes are handled for *rates*, but a share crash before 2031 and a boom
are never followed through to the range and to her flexibility.

**4. Competition Experience – 7/10.** As far as an IPS can: it shows choices weighed and refused (cash, a bank's
note, selling a rung early). No team history, correctly (Book 11).

**5. Creativity and Presentation – 7/10.** Original thinking is real (pricing rule applied three times; range
owned at both ends; residency-level diversification). Mostly a human voice. But several sentences need a second
read on a cold pass: "Cash only looks safer: for fixed dollars on fixed dates, it is a bet that interest rates
won't fall"; "its bonds pay interest along the way, and if rates fall and stay low, that interest earns less while
it waits for its January". The XR line sits mid-paragraph and interrupts the ladder explanation.
