# R5, blind judge: the contrarian (V7)

Read only `rounds/blind/R5_pack.txt` (pitch + IPS, 545 words in the pack) and `keel/WHARTON_WORD.md`. Persona: an
equity-minded industry judge who walks in suspecting a Treasury-heavy plan is too timid for a client the case calls
"willing to take thoughtful risks". Scored only on Wharton's five criteria.

## Scores

| Criterion | /10 | Why |

**Top 50: maybe, leaning yes.** It is clearer and more original than a typical Treasury-ladder plan, mainly because of
the "bought before anyone hears it" range. What holds it back from a firm yes is that it argues only one side of
Wharton's "balance growth, risk … and financial flexibility".

## The strongest attack, and whether the page answers it

**"You commit her minimum facility contribution in 2028, three years before any co-sponsor needs a number, and by doing
so you turn the whole facility contribution into a Treasury outcome."** The page's rule is "whenever Laura commits to a
sum, she buys the Treasuries", but nobody makes her commit to a minimum in 2028. The plan chooses to. The case only
requires a range in 2031. Between 2028 and 2031 the facility money is exactly the "money nobody else is counting on"
that the page says belongs in shares. By the page's own logic, that money could sit in shares until 2031 and be bought
into Treasuries on the day she names the range.

The page's defence for keeping shares small is "Laura's own income can rise and fall with the economy". But by 2028
that income has already been deposited, and after 2028 the portfolio needs nothing more from her career. So the
double-bet argument explains why the 2028 *deposit* is uncertain before it arrives. It doesn't explain why the money
should stay out of shares once it's in hand.

**Answered on the page? No, only half.** The pension line justifies Treasuries for sums already promised. Nothing on the
page says why the minimum is promised as early as 2028. To me, it reads as a timid choice dressed as a rule.

## The one thing that would change my mind

One sentence giving the reason the minimum is bought in 2028 and not in 2031, and naming its cost. For example: the
2028 deposit is the last money Laura adds, so it is the one moment the minimum can be locked against falling rates, and
what that costs her is said plainly. If the page showed it had weighed the alternative (shares until 2031) and chosen
early certainty for a stated reason, my Client Knowledge score would go to 8 and Portfolio Analysis to 8, total 37,
and my verdict to yes.
