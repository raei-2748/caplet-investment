## Paragraph by paragraph, after one read, in my words

- **Pitch.** Once someone relies on money, it comes out of shares. Treasuries pay the ten $50k payments. The 2028 money
  buys the minimum facility gift and puts only its interest into world shares, and the 2031 range is fully covered
  before she says it. *Understood, but s1 and s3 each made me pause.*
- **P1.** Value a fixed payment at the cost of the Treasuries that pay it, not at the return shares might earn, because
  that return isn't earned yet. US public pensions did the second and took more risk. So each commitment is bought in
  Treasuries the day she makes it. *Correct. Still a riddle for one sentence.*
- **P2.** The $300k (Jan 2027) buys a ladder that matures before each payment, 2033-2042, and the 2028 money tops it up
  if rates leave it short. Held to maturity, so "high certainty" means no payment rests on shares. Shares can wait out a
  bad decade; a payment date can't. Caplet XR = ten holdings, redemption. Nothing is sold early. *Clear. "A January
  cannot." landed at once.*
- **P3.** Most of the 2028 money buys a late-2032 Treasury fund, the minimum facility contribution. The rest (about its
  interest) goes into a world index fund. That fund is where the plan can be wrong, and it's small because her income
  already rises and falls with the economy. *Clear. The appositive problem from R2 is fixed.*
- **P4.** 2031 range: bottom = the 2032 fund's payout, rounded down. Top = plus half her shares' value, moved to
  Treasuries before she announces it, because co-sponsors will plan around it. The other half stays hers. *Clear.*
- **P5.** Weakness: the bonds' interest must be reinvested until each payment date; low rates mean less. So before 2031
  they assume zero reinvestment and cover that from her kept shares, or she names a lower range. *Clear.*
- **P6.** Strength: by 2033 nothing anyone relies on depends on shares. The ladder plus "that set-aside" is the reserve,
  and one holding pays out each January to 2042. *Correct apart from "that set-aside".*

## Sentences I had to stop on (5 of 30: pitch 3, IPS 27)

1. **Pitch, s1** – "Laura's money leaves shares the day somebody starts counting on it." Better than R2 ("Laura's money"
   anchors it), but "somebody" is still vague and "leaves shares" is still abstract. I only got it from what follows.
2. **Pitch, s3** – "both ends of her 2031 range are bought before anyone hears it." On the pitch alone (no IPS yet), you
   can't buy the end of a range. The IPS's P4 explains it; the pitch, read first, doesn't.
3. **IPS P1, s1** – same opening line; same stop.
4. **IPS P1, s3** – "...have spent two decades taking on more risk to keep up with it." "It" is still unclear (the
   assumed return? the saving?). Unchanged since R2.
5. **IPS P6, s2** – "The ladder and that set-aside become her operating reserve." "Set-aside" is never named before; I
   had to think back to P5's "move that much ... into Treasuries". Unchanged since R2.

## The strategy in three sentences, as I'd retell it
Anything Laura commits to is put into Treasuries the day she commits to it, so shares never back a number someone relies
on. The $300k buys a ladder that pays the ten $50k operating payments, and the 2028 $150k buys a Treasury fund for the
minimum facility contribution, with only its interest in a world share fund. Before naming the 2031 range she moves half
her shares into Treasuries, so both ends are owned, and keeps the other half for herself.
