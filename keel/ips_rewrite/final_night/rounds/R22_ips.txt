Before Laura commits to any amount, Caplet XR makes sure she already holds it in Treasuries, and only money she has promised to no one goes into shares. Her ten operating payments of $50,000 come first, because co-sponsors may help pay for the facility but cannot be relied on for those. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each paying out in the year before the January it pays for, and with the interest they earn along the way they are sized to return the full $500,000. The plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption).

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would be the wrong tool here. The payments must be made in full whatever markets do, so every loss would fall on the money left for the facility, which would then swing harder than the share market itself, just as a company's shares grow riskier as it borrows. So shares sit only where a loss costs no payment, and we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment rests on a share price, a forecast or a sale before maturity.

The 2028 deposit first buys whatever part of the ten the first $300,000 couldn't afford, which happens only if bond prices rise before January 2027. Most of the rest buys more of the Treasury fund that pays the first January, which holds bonds maturing during 2032 and pays out that December. Its extra payout becomes the bottom of the range she will name in 2031, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed. Securing the bottom in 2028, rather than paying for it from shares in 2031, means a slump before 2031 can lower only the top. What is left, about the interest those Treasuries will earn by 2032, goes into a world index fund, so even if every share were lost she would still get back about her deposit.

The plan's weak point is time. Every dollar that arrives before its January, mostly interest, waits in a short-term Treasury fund, and if rates fall and stay low it earns less than the ladder was sized on.

In 2031, before naming her range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus that half. From the other half she sets aside enough Treasuries to cover that gap, as if the waiting money earned nothing, and if that half falls short, the difference comes off the top, then the bottom, before she names a number. Owning so few shares will probably leave her less to give than a bolder plan would. But a named top left in shares would take every fall while any rise above it came back to her, and a number she later cut would cost her the co-sponsors' trust. So, barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost is known.

The payments are fixed in dollars, which is why Treasuries suit them. Building costs in Taiwan are not, so whatever remains of her kept shares stays outside the range as her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day and the one part of her money with a chance to keep pace. From 2033 her operating reserve is everything still waiting for a payment, and it changes only by paying out, one holding each January, until the tenth redemption makes the last operating payment in January 2042.
