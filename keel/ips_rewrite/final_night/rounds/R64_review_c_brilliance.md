## 2. The biggest strength
Every number Laura says aloud is owned before she says it, and losses fall only on money nobody was promised.
- **Stated:** yes, first sentence.
- **Argued:** half. "Before anyone counts on it" would let her buy the bottom from shares in 2031, since nobody counts on it in 2028. R64 buys it in 2028 without saying why, so rule and plan split at the plan's biggest choice.
- **Priced:** half. A balanced mix is "probably" richer, and next to all-Treasuries the cost is capped at the interest. Missing: what the locks give up (share growth on the deposit from 2028 to 2031, then on the promised half), and that this cost falls on the top and her kept money in good markets, never on a payment or the bottom.

## 4. Missing ideas, ranked
1. **Ability over willingness (~25 words).** She is willing, but the payments take nearly all of the first $300,000. Her only cushion is a deposit she hasn't earned yet, from work that can dry up in the same slump that cuts co-sponsors' giving. So her shares come only from interest. With "a bet on good times", it also explains 2028. *Displaces* the credibility clause (11), "since new buildings throw up surprises" (6) and the 4 spare.
2. **A cheaper reason for 2028 (~10).** The stake is the payments' last line behind the cover ("comes out of the bottom" implies it), so it can't sit in shares. *Displaces* the credibility clause.
3. **The leverage payoff (~10):** Caplet XR's facility money swings less than shares. *Displaces* "pay out that December", which repeats "December 2032", plus the credibility clause.
4. **Price the locks in words (~18),** and say that cover the payments never need goes back to her. *Competes with 1.*
5. **Inflation (~20).** Ordinary Treasuries match a fixed-dollar promise exactly. Inflation and the Taiwan dollar fall on the building, and shares are the only part that can grow with it, which is the honest reason to hold any. *Fuse into the Taiwan-dollar clause.*
6. **Argued back from FR #9 (~15).** Her share of the running costs is bought before she asks anyone, the part funders least like to pay. Never say "all running costs". *Displaces* the credibility clause.
7. **Flexibility's real reason (net +5).** Kept money is worth most because nobody can count on it. *Replaces* "surprises".

Only one of 1 to 6 fits. Pick 1: it answers growth-minded readers and closes the rule-plan gap.

## 5. Packaging that should go
- **The credibility clause:** the judges wrote it in the case, and "could only disappoint" already does the thinking.
- **"Since new buildings throw up surprises":** folksy, and it turns the kept money into the building's cushion, the opposite of "unpromised".
- **Keep the rest.** The borrowed-company line is an argument, not decoration. The X and R line is well placed and paid off at the end.

## 6. Where cleverness costs clarity
- **"Big enough to get that money back":** "that money" can read as the 2033 payment. It must be clear that the rest of the deposit returns in full and only its interest is risked.
- **"No payment ever waits on shares, interest rates…"** follows straight after rates and shares were shown reaching the payments through the cover, so it reads as a contradiction. It should say these are covered, not that they can't reach.
- **The pitch.** "Half the shares bought with the rest" is a garden path, and "her 2028 deposit comes back as the bottom" overstates it. The pitch also leaves out the opening rule, the IPS's best idea.
