Before Laura commits to any amount, Caplet XR makes sure she already holds it in Treasuries, and only money she has promised to no one goes into shares. Her ten operating payments of $50,000 come first, because co-sponsors may help pay for the facility but cannot be relied on for those. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each repaid in the year before the January it pays for, and with the interest they pay along the way they return the $500,000 she owes. The plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption).

The usual balanced mix of shares and bonds would be the wrong tool. The payments must be made in full whatever markets do, so every loss would fall on the money left for the facility, which would then swing harder than the share market itself, just as a company's shares grow riskier as it borrows. Shares therefore sit only where a loss costs no payment, and we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment rests on a share price, a forecast or a sale before maturity. No borrower in dollars is safer than the US government, and another borrower's extra interest would only pay for a second way to miss a January.

The 2028 deposit first buys whatever part of the ten the first $300,000 couldn't afford, which happens if bond prices rise before January 2027. Most of the rest buys more of the Treasury fund that pays the first January, which holds bonds maturing during 2032. Its extra payout becomes the bottom of the range she will name in 2031, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed. Waiting until 2031 would leave the bottom at the mercy of markets in the same years that co-sponsors may give less. Only what is left, about the interest those Treasuries will earn by 2032, goes into a world index fund, so even if every share were lost, she would still have about her deposit back.

The plan's weak point is time. Every dollar that arrives before its January, mostly interest, waits in a short-term Treasury fund, and if rates fall and stay low, it earns less than the ladder was sized on. So before she names her range, Laura sets aside enough Treasuries to cover that, assuming the waiting money earns nothing at all. The cover comes from the half of her shares she will keep, and if that isn't enough, from the range itself, top first, so the range she names is what is left and no payment ever waits on shares.

She then moves the other half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus that half. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, but once she has said a number she holds it in Treasuries, because a top left in shares would take every fall while every rise above it went back to her. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost is known.

Whatever remains of her kept shares stays out of the range as her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day and the part of her money with a chance to keep pace with building costs in Taiwan. From 2033 the operating reserve is everything waiting for a payment, and it changes only by paying out, one holding each year, until the tenth redemption makes the last operating payment in January 2042.
