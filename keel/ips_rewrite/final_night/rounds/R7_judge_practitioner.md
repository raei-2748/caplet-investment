## After one read
**The strategy:** every sum Laura commits to is bought in Treasuries the day she commits to it. In 2027 the $300,000
buys a ten-holding Treasury ladder for the operating payments. In 2028 the deposit first fills any ladder gap, then
buys a minimum facility contribution in the late-2032 Treasury fund, with only about that fund's interest in world
shares. In 2031 half the shares move to Treasuries before she names the range. Any reinvestment shortfall is covered
from the kept half first, then from the 2032 fund, which lowers the range. From 2033 the reserve simply runs off, one
redemption a year.

**Its biggest strength:** "markets can change how large a range Laura names, but never whether she meets a payment
or a number already named." Market risk is confined to the size of a future promise and never touches a past one.

**Length:** pitch 50 words, IPS 493 (wc). Inside the limits.

## Strongest technical claim
> "markets can change how large a range Laura names, but never whether she meets a payment or a number already named."

It is precise about what is hedged (past commitments) and what is not (the size of future ones). That is exactly the
distinction an asset-liability manager draws.

## Weakest technical claims
1. > "at today's rates, a small one"

   This is still a claim about current ladder pricing, though softer than R6's "as they do today". It needs to match
   the team's latest pricing (Fact-Checker).
2. > "never whether she meets a payment" / "nothing in it is ever sold"

   It holds only on US Treasury credit, which the IPS never names. With "never" on the page, one clause on the single
   credit the plan accepts is now worth more than it was.
3. > "for us a high degree of certainty means that no operating payment depends on a share price"

   It is still narrower than the closing sentence, which actually states the stronger and correct test. The
   definition could borrow it.
4. > "American public pensions, which are allowed to do exactly this, have spent two decades taking on more risk"

   Still stated as settled cause and effect, with nothing on the page to support it.

## What I'd retell to a colleague tomorrow
"Market risk only ever changes the size of the next number she names, never a number she's already said. They bought
each commitment in Treasuries the day it was made, the pledge range included."
