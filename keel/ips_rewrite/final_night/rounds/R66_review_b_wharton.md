## 3. Flags

Contradictions
1. Shares: "up to half ... are sold", "the other half is sold", then "including unsold shares". It squares only if the unused part of the first half stays invested; a first reader sees every share sold, then shares left. Say which shares survive 2031.
2. "or less if the building needs less, but at least the bottom": as written she gives the bottom even when the building needs less. Say what the bottom is a floor on.
3. Pitch vs IPS: the pitch's 2028 Treasuries "return it by 2033 as the bottom", but in the IPS the deposit first finishes the ladder and the 2031 top-up can cut the bottom. The pitch must not fix the bottom in 2028, and "it" is ambiguous.

Facts that look wrong or thin
4. The Taiwan-dollar claim (professor's doubt).
5. "Barring a US default": payments 2033-37 sit in funds whose payout "is not fixed" (locked Note 2). The range absorbs it; the definition names default only.
6. "two thirds of her $450,000" holds on the last close on file (23 Oct) only.

Wharton's don'ts
7. Para 3 pins one security by role and date ("the Treasury fund behind the 2033 payment ... December 2032 payout"): borderline "describing individual investments". Otherwise clean.
8. The WInS book holds shares and partial rungs bought with the first $300,000; the IPS says that money "buys all it can" of the ladder. The Final Report must explain the scaling.

Clarity: "Buying the bottom in 2028 means a slump ... can't touch the fund behind it" misparses on first read; the pitch's "the top, which is in Treasuries" puts a dollar figure inside a bond.
