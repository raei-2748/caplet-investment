## Flags

1. **Contradiction.** "Every holding lends only to the US government" vs the world index fund. A fixed version scopes it to what funds the payments and the bottom.
2. **Overclaim by order.** P3 rests certainty on US credit alone; P5 reveals a short-rate gap. The definition must name the gap as a second condition and the order that covers it (shares, then top, then bottom).
3. **Pitch.** "Her first $300,000 buys Treasuries for her ten $50,000 operating payments" is flat where the IPS hedges with the 2028 top-up (about 8bp headroom at the 23 Oct close). "half her shares" appears with no shares introduced. Zero word margin.
4. **Locked TNA tension.** Note 3: stocks "only set how far her gift rises above her promised minimum"; here half the shares first backstop the payments' gap. It survives (the minimum is named only in 2031, after the gap), but a fixed version must show the gap draws on the upside.
5. **Flexibility can vanish.** If the gap takes the first half and the building costs at least the top, she commits everything left, the case's own warning. A fixed version says what she keeps whatever the building costs.
6. **Wharton's don'ts.** None breached. "about two thirds of her $450,000" is one rounded figure; "the fund behind the 2033 payment" is near an individual investment but framed by role.
7. **Readability.** The 2031 mechanism, which scores most, sits in appositive chains ("the top, the bottom plus that half"; "What she doesn't give, with any shares the gap didn't need, is ..."). All three judges re-read P5.
