## Strongest technical claim
> "The payments aren't adjusted for inflation, so dollar Treasuries match them exactly"

It answers in one clause why the plan uses nominal Treasuries. Joint first: the closing strength sentence.

## Weakest technical claims
1. > "Falling shares before 2031 can lower only the top, and rising shares lift it."

   Not quite true under the plan's own rules. The kept half of the shares is the first cover for the reinvestment
   shortfall, so falling shares combined with low rates can push that cover onto the 2032 fund and lower the bottom
   too. ("If that falls short, the 2032 fund covers the rest and her range starts lower.") The sentence is true for
   falling shares *alone*. A reader who joins the two paragraphs will see the contradiction. Restoring "on their own",
   or similar, fixes it.
2. > "for us a high degree of certainty means that no operating payment depends on a share price"

   Still narrower than the closing sentence, and the one credit the reserve rests on (the US Treasury) is still never
   named, while "never" in the last paragraph relies on it.
3. > "American public pensions are allowed to price their promises that way, and they have taken on more risk to keep up."

   Clearer than before, but still causal and unsupported on the page.

## What I'd retell to a colleague tomorrow
