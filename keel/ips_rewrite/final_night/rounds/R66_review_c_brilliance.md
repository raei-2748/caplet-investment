# R66 review (c): is it as brilliant as possible?
AI-generated review (Claude, 6 Nov 2026). Not submission text.

**Verdict: close, not yet.** R66 argues rather than describes, and almost no packaging is left. It falls short three
ways: it prices caution only against the riskier alternative, reverses one endorsed idea, and packs its two cleverest
mechanisms so tightly that readers must rebuild them.

## 1. Dinner ideas, and whether each is fully argued
- **A balanced fund is a geared bet on the building** (para 2). The best paragraph: a concession, a size and an exact
  mapping. It never says why gearing hurts *this* client: a swinging leftover makes any 2031 number a forecast, and
  its worst case is a missed payment. Check "far harder than the share market": the verdict file gives about 1.8
  times the share market, but FR_IDEAS row 28 says "about twice the mix".
- **The range is bought before she names it** (para 5, pitch). Argued from the lopsided capped promise, but
  unpriced. "A rise would add nothing she'd promised" hides that the rise would have been hers.
- **Her 2028 deposit returns as the bottom; only its interest buys shares.** The pitch says it more clearly.
- **Waiting money earns nothing** (para 4). Fully argued.
- **The slump that cuts co-sponsors' giving can't reach the bottom** (para 3). The endorsed "diversify the residency"
  idea, buried in a subordinate clause and never offered as the diversification answer.

## 2. The biggest strength
- **Stated:** yes, in the opening sentence. Each commitment becomes a matched Treasury holding before it becomes a
  promise, so nothing anyone counts on rests on a forecast.
- **Argued:** in three unjoined pieces (paras 2, 3 and 5). The design answers the case's question of how confident
  she can be in the range from her holdings, but the draft leaves that answer to inference.
- **Priced:** only against a 60/40. Unsaid:
  - all-Treasuries would give a higher certain sum;
  - the lock gives up two years of share growth on the promised half;
  - buying the bottom in 2028 gives up some expected gift.

  A better version must say that, against the safest alternative, the most the plan can cost her is the interest on
  her 2028 deposit. That limit turns caution into strength.

## 3. Used versus only mentioned
- **Used:** residual leverage; coupon reinvestment as the held Treasury's weak point; a capped promise's asymmetry;
  ability against willingness; income, giving and shares falling together; certainty by ownership; defined benefit
  against defined contribution (built in, rightly unnamed).
- **Only mentioned:** diversification (a company count); inflation ("fixed in dollars" is never drawn out); flexibility
  (pointed the wrong way); the reserve's composition; liquidity (absent).
- **Endorsed ideas:** buying the range first is in, diversifying the residency is a clause, prepaid operating costs
  are absent, and unpromised spare money is reversed.

## 4. Missing ideas, ranked
About 25 words can be freed: 5 spare, "or less if the building needs less" (6), "of thousands of companies" (4), and
half of para 4's 16-word "every bond, even inside the funds" clause.

1. **One forecast, placed on purpose** (~15). Must convey that the one market assumption (shares out-earn
   Treasuries) sets only the range's height, never whether a payment or named number is met. This states the strength and the
   case's return assumption, and it echoes the submitted third reflection ("where we allow ourselves to be wrong").
   It displaces the para 4 trim and the hedge.
2. **Spare money stays hers, unpromised** (net 0). Para 6 gives flexibility "for whatever the residency needs", but
   the case's flexibility is Laura's.
3. **The cost has a limit** (~12). Must convey that, against all-Treasuries, only the interest is at risk. It answers the hardest judge
   question unprompted, displacing the spare words and the company count.
4. **The reserve changes only by redemptions.** This is the case's "if at all": nothing is sold, liquidity comes from
   maturities, and the unused set-aside returns to her. It fits only folded into the reserve sentence; otherwise it
   costs about 15 words, pushing out 1 or 3.
5. **Prepaid operating costs** (~12). Argue it back from the Final Report list, conveying only that her share of
   ten years' running costs is already bought. That is the case's "financially viable" signal.
6. **Inflation for the payments** (~6, inside "fixed in dollars"). Required, not distinctive.
7. **Why half** (~8). The one unexplained number.

## 5. Packaging that should go
Almost none. Keep the borrowed company: it maps exactly. The company count stands in for an argument. The name is
handled plainly, and "all ten redemptions" pays it off.

## 6. Where cleverness costs clarity
- **"Costs less than its payout, and the difference buys":** two sums in one clause. It must say the deposit comes
  back whole and the shares are its interest.
- **"Extra Treasuries that assumption calls for":** it must say that assuming zero reveals a shortfall, which is
  bought now.
- **"Half", "the other half", "the same day" (paras 4-5):** referents to chase.
- **"Can't touch the fund", then "the whole range moves down":** these read as a contradiction. Only the set-aside
  lowers the bottom, and only before she names the range.
- **"Land on the range":** add "before she names it".
- **Pitch:** "Shares ... set the top" overstates (only half do, net of the set-aside). It gives three moves and no
  reason.
