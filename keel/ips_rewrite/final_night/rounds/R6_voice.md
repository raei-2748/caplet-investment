## The Writer's question: is the pitch's run of "buys" a tic?
**Yes.** It is five, not four: "buys the Treasuries", "buys ten $50,000 operating payments", "buys her minimum facility
contribution", "its interest buys world shares", "is bought before anyone hears it". A repeated verb can be a deliberate
drumbeat, but here its meaning drifts. Laura buys Treasuries, yet the pitch also has her "buying" payments, a
contribution and a range, and "its interest buys world shares" makes the interest the buyer. A listener hears the rhythm
before the meaning, which is the "rhythm the reader can hear coming" warning in the guide (s9). The move: keep "buy"
for the one literal act (buying Treasuries) and let the other clauses say what the money *does*.
Two smaller pitch points: "That deposit" points back to the 2028 deposit, but the nearest noun is "$300,000", so the
reader stalls. And the pitch still never says "Caplet XR".

## Sentences not clear on one reading (6)
1. "Whenever Laura commits to a sum, she buys the Treasuries that will pay it that day." – "that day" can attach to
   "pay" (the Treasuries pay on the day she commits) as easily as to "buys". This is the thesis, and the pitch's version
   ("the day she commits to it") is clearer.
2. "If falling rates make it cost more than $300,000, as they do today, her 2028 deposit makes up the difference." –
   "as they do today" can mean rates are falling today or the ladder costs more today. "it" = the ladder, but only just.
3. "Held to its end, each one pays out what its Treasuries repay, whatever markets do" – "each one" and "its
   Treasuries" make the reader rebuild which holding has which bonds. The idea is simple; the sentence is not.
4. "Most of the remaining 2028 deposit buys more of the late-2032 fund that pays her first January, and that extra sets
   her minimum facility contribution." – "the late-2032 fund" arrives with "the" though it has never been named, and
   "that extra" is a new coined noun.
5. "Only what's left, roughly the interest it will earn, goes into…" – "it" = the extra holding? the whole fund?
6. "The ladder's weakness is that its bonds pay interest, and two repay months early, so some money waits for its
   January, and if rates fall and stay low it earns less than planned." – four clauses joined by "and/so/and", a
   personified "its January", and an "it" at the end. The confession is right; the sentence buries it.
