# R20 cold read (reviewer D): one reading, sentence by sentence

Cold read by Claude, 5 Nov 2026. Read only: the Laura Gao case (txt), `R20_pitch.txt`, `R20_ips.txt`.
The reader has the case and nothing else from the team. No replacement sentences are offered.

Marks: CLEAR = understood on one reading. SLOWED = had to reread or guess (mild or moderate). LOST = could not
tell, or two readings were possible.

**Tally:** pitch 2 SLOWED. Plan (22 sentences): 6 CLEAR, 11 SLOWED, 5 LOST.

---

## Pitch

**P1.** "Caplet XR buys Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments first, with Treasuries maturing before each January, because co-sponsors can't fund them."
- Means: the plan's first step is to pay for the ten $50k residency payments by buying US government bonds that come due just before each payment, because outside sponsors aren't allowed to cover those payments.
- **SLOWED (mild).**
- Cause: "buys ... payments": you buy bonds, not payments, so the sentence has to be unscrambled. "them": the payments or the Treasuries? "each January": no years given (a case reader supplies 2033-2042). "Caplet XR" is a name with no meaning yet.

**P2.** "Her 2028 deposit buys Treasuries for the least she'll give the facility, and a small slice of world shares decides how much more she can promise in 2031."
- Means: the $150k added in 2028 buys government bonds that lock in her minimum gift to the building, and a small stock investment sets how far above that minimum she can go when she talks to sponsors in 2031.
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: "a small slice": a slice of what? Of the 2028 deposit, presumably. "decides": shares don't decide anything. "promise": the case says Laura wants to give a range "rather than promising one exact amount", so promising the top reads against the case. "how much more": more than the least, which takes a moment to connect.

## Plan

### Paragraph 1

**S1.** "Caplet XR buys every amount Laura commits to with Treasuries before she commits to it, and only money nobody has been promised goes into shares."
- Means: before Laura promises any sum, the plan has already bought government bonds that will pay it. Only money that isn't promised to anyone goes into stocks.
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: "buys every amount ... with Treasuries" reverses the object (you buy Treasuries with money, not amounts with Treasuries). "Caplet XR" is used before the name is explained (S3). "money nobody has been promised" is an awkward phrase. The whole line reads like a thesis slogan. It also overstates, since the plan's own weak point (S13-S14) is only covered in 2031.

**S2.** "Her ten operating payments of $50,000 come first, because co-sponsors may help pay for the facility, never for those."
- Means: the ten payments take priority, because sponsors may help with the building but the case doesn't let them help with the payments.
- **CLEAR.**
- Note: this repeats the pitch's reason almost word for word. "never for those" is clipped but the pronoun is clear.

**S3.** "In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each maturing in the year before the January it pays for, and the plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption)."
- Means: in January 2027 the $300k buys ten government bond investments. Each comes due in the calendar year before the January payment it funds (for example, late 2032 for January 2033). The name XR means "ten repayments".
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: "holdings": bonds or funds? (S9 later shows at least one is a fund.) "in the year before the January it pays for" needs a worked example in the reader's head. "redemptions" is a finance word; a lay adult may not know that a bond "redeems" when it repays. The naming aside breaks into the middle of the mechanics. Unanswered jump: ten payments total $500,000, and nothing says how $300,000 can cover them (interest earned on the bonds before they mature is never mentioned here).

### Paragraph 2

**S4.** "Fixed payments are made in full whatever markets do, so every loss falls on what is left over."
- Means: the $50k payments never shrink, so when investments lose money, the loss comes out of whatever money is beyond what the payments need.
- **SLOWED (mild).**
- Cause: "what is left over": left over after what? It is also unclear whether this describes Caplet's plan or any portfolio that has to make fixed payments. The next sentence shows it is the general case.

**S5.** "A balanced mix of shares and bonds behind them would make that leftover swing harder than the share market, just as a company's shares grow riskier as it borrows."
- Means (best guess): if a normal stock-and-bond mix stood behind the payments, the leftover money would rise and fall more sharply than the stock market itself, the way a company that borrows heavily has riskier shares.
- **LOST.**
- Cause: "them" is two clauses away from "Fixed payments" and could be the payments or the losses. The claim goes against intuition, because a balanced mix is usually safer than shares. Here the reader is told it is riskier than shares, and the only support is a borrowing (leverage) analogy that a normal adult can't check on one reading. This is the plan's main argument for its approach, and it is the hardest sentence to follow.

**S6.** "So we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or a sale before maturity."
- Means: so the team takes the case's "high degree of certainty" to mean near-total certainty. No payment relies on stock prices, on predictions, or on selling a bond before it repays.
- **CLEAR.**
- Notes: "So" only works if S5 landed, and for most readers it won't have. "we" appears here for the first time; until now the subject was "Caplet XR" or "the plan". **Contradiction:** S13-S14 then say the payments partly rely on what parked interest earns, which is a rate forecast.

**S7.** "Every payment rests on one borrower, the US government, on purpose, since a second borrower would only add a second way to miss a January."
- Means: all payments rely on the US government repaying, and this is deliberate, because adding a second issuer adds a second chance of a missed payment.
- **SLOWED (mild).**
- Cause: "on purpose" is wedged between commas and stalls the sentence. "miss a January" is shorthand for "miss a January payment". The reader must also see that buying a bond means lending to a "borrower". The idea goes against the usual "diversify" advice, so the reader stops to accept it.

### Paragraph 3

**S8.** "The 2028 deposit first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, if bond prices rose before January 2027."
- Means: if bonds got more expensive before January 2027 and the $300k couldn't buy all ten holdings, the 2028 money first buys the missing ones.
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: the condition comes at the end, so the reader reaches "couldn't afford" before learning why. **Contradiction with S3**, which said flatly that the $300,000 "buys ten Treasury holdings". "rose before January 2027" uses the past tense for an event that, as written in November 2026, is still in the future. It isn't said which holding would be the one left unbought. The years run backwards: S3 is set in January 2027, S8 is set in 2028, and the condition takes the reader back before January 2027.

**S9.** "Most of the rest buys more of the iBonds fund behind the first payment, whose Treasuries all mature in late 2032."
- Means: most of the remaining 2028 money buys more units of the same bond fund that pays the January 2033 payment, and that fund's bonds all repay in late 2032.
- **LOST.**
- Cause: "iBonds" is never explained, and a US reader will likely confuse it with Series I savings bonds ("I bonds"), a different product. This is the first sign that one of the "ten Treasury holdings" is a fund. "the first payment" could briefly be read as the first deposit. The reader can't tell why money goes into the first payment's fund: is the first payment short? The purpose only comes in S10.

**S10.** "The bottom of the range she will name in 2031 is what that extra holding pays out, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed."
- Means: the low end of the range she tells sponsors in 2031 equals what those extra fund units repay in late 2032, trimmed a little because a fund doesn't promise an exact sum the way a single bond does.
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: this is the plan's first mention of "the range"; a case reader knows it, but it arrives unannounced. "that extra holding": until now "holding" meant one of the ten, so is this an eleventh holding or part of the first? "rounded down": to what? No dollar figure appears, and a reader holding the case is waiting for one.

**S11.** "Waiting until 2031 to buy the bottom would leave the bottom at the mercy of shares in the same years that co-sponsors give less."
- Means: if the minimum were bought in 2031 instead, its size would depend on how stocks did until then, and the years when stocks fall are the same years sponsors give less.
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: "buy the bottom" is market slang for buying at the lowest price, which gives a second reading for anyone who has heard it. "the same years that co-sponsors give less" states a link between bad markets and smaller donations without saying so; the reader has to supply it. The case doesn't say this. The sentence is a what-if dropped between two sentences about the 2028 purchase, so the years go 2028, then 2031, then back to 2028 (S12).

**S12.** "The slice left over, about that fund's interest, buys a world index fund."
- Means (best guess): what remains of the 2028 money, roughly equal to the interest the iBonds fund will earn, goes into a global stock index fund.
- **LOST.**
- Cause: "about" can mean "approximately" or "concerning". "that fund" could be the iBonds fund or the world index fund named in the same sentence. Why the leftover should match a fund's interest is never explained. The amount is never given. The pitch called this "a small slice of world shares" and this sentence is the only place it is sized, so the idea that decides the top of the range is the hardest to read.

### Paragraph 4

**S13.** "The plan's weak point is the interest its holdings pay along the way."
- Means: the plan's risk sits in the interest the bonds pay before they mature.
- **SLOWED (mild).**
- Cause: a reader expects interest to be good news, so calling it the weak point is puzzling until S14. "along the way" is vague: along the way to what? The opening reads like an announcement.

**S14.** "It waits in a short-term Treasury fund until the January it was meant for, and if rates fall and stay low, it earns less than the bonds' prices assumed."
- Means (best guess): the interest is parked in a short-term government fund until the payment it is supposed to help fund. If interest rates fall and stay low, that parked money earns less than the bond prices assumed, which leaves a gap.
- **LOST.**
- Cause: "It" (twice) could be the interest, the weak point, or the plan; the nearest nouns are "holdings" and "the way". "the January it was meant for" brings in a new idea without saying so: interest is "meant for" a payment, so the payments depend partly on interest and not only on each holding repaying. "than the bonds' prices assumed": prices don't assume anything; this is a pricing idea (reinvestment at the bond's yield) that a lay adult won't know. **Contradictions:** with S6 ("no payment depends on ... a forecast") and with S21 ("Market moves change nothing in the plan"), because a lasting fall in rates is a market move that creates a shortfall.

### Paragraph 5

**S15.** "Before naming her range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus that half."
- Means: in 2031, before she talks to sponsors, Laura sells half her stock fund and buys government bonds. The top of the range is the minimum plus the value of that half.
- **CLEAR.**
- Notes: the year (2031) is left for the reader to infer. "that half" means its dollar value, which comes through on one read.

**S16.** "From the other half she sets aside enough Treasuries to cover that interest shortfall, assuming the waiting money earns nothing."
- Means: from the remaining half of the stocks, she buys enough government bonds to cover the interest gap in S14, sized as if the parked interest earned zero.
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: "that interest shortfall" points back two paragraphs, and S14 never used the word "shortfall" ("earns less"). "the waiting money" is a new name for the parked interest. It isn't said what happens if the other half is too small to cover the gap. Timing: the risk exists from 2027, but the cover is bought only in 2031, which sits uneasily with S1 and S6.

**S17.** "A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, and afterwards shares move neither end."
- Means: stock performance up to 2031 sets the top. After 2031 both ends of the range are in bonds, so stocks no longer affect either.
- **CLEAR.**
- Note: the balanced "raises ... lowers ... neither end" rhythm sounds slogan-like.

**S18.** "Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where it lands is her choice in 2033, once the facility's cost is known."
- Means: unless the US government fails to pay, her gift will be inside the range, and she picks the exact amount in 2033 once she knows what the building costs.
- **CLEAR.**
- Note: this is the plan's only answer to the case's "state how confident". It is a condition, not a number, and a reader may notice that. "her choice" also makes the reader wonder whether "will land in the range" is a forecast or a decision.

### Paragraph 6

**S19.** "What remains of the other half stays out of the range as her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day and the only part of her money that can grow with building costs in Taiwan."
- Means: whatever is left of the second half of the stocks, after the shortfall cover, isn't counted in the range. It is her spare money, it can be sold on any market day, and it is the only part of her money that can keep pace with rising construction costs.
- **SLOWED (mild).**
- Cause: "What remains of the other half" is the third step in a chain of halves (S15 → S16 → S19), so the reader has to rebuild it. The reason it is "the only part that can grow" is left to the reader: everything else is fixed bond repayments. Long list after the comma. No size is given.

**S20.** "From 2033 the ten holdings and the money set aside are her operating reserve."
- Means: from 2033 the ten bond investments plus the bonds bought in 2031 to cover the interest gap make up the operating reserve the case asks for.
- **SLOWED (moderate).**
- Cause: "the money set aside" could be the 2031 shortfall cover (S16), the extra iBonds units for the range's bottom (S9-S10), or the parked interest in the short-term fund (S14); all three have been described as money set aside or waiting. Also, by January 2033 the first holding has already repaid (late 2032), so "the ten holdings" is slightly off. The case asks the team to recommend the reserve's size, and no size is given.

**S21.** "Market moves change nothing in the plan, and each January's $50,000 comes from the holding redeemed before it."
- Means (two readings): either (a) markets can't affect the payments, or (b) the plan never trades in response to markets. Each payment comes from the holding that repaid just before it.
- **LOST.**
- Cause: two readings of "change nothing in the plan". **Contradictions:** S14 says falling rates create a shortfall and S17 says a good or bad market moves the top, so market moves do change things. "comes from the holding redeemed before it" leaves out the interest that S14 said is "meant for" each January. It reads as a slogan.

**S22.** "The tenth redemption, a Treasury bond maturing in November 2041, pays the last operating payment in January 2042."
- Means: the last bond repays in November 2041 and that money makes the final $50k payment in January 2042.
- **CLEAR.**
- Notes: this is the first time a holding is called "a Treasury bond" rather than a holding or fund, so the reader learns only at the end that the ten are a mix of funds and bonds. The plan ends on a dated detail rather than on Laura or the range, which feels like a closing echo of the name and not like a conclusion.

## Cross-cutting notes

**Terms used before they are explained (or never explained):** "Caplet XR" (S1; explained S3). "Treasuries" (never; most adults will cope). "redemption/redeemed" (S3, only via the name aside). "iBonds" (S9, never; confusable with US savings I bonds). "the range" (S10, assumed from the case). "interest shortfall" (S16, named two paragraphs after the risk). "operating reserve" (S20, case term, fine).

**Shifting names for the same things:** "Treasuries", "Treasury holdings", "holding", "iBonds fund", "extra holding", "short-term Treasury fund", "Treasury bond". Also "slice" (pitch, S12), "shares", "world index fund". And "half", "other half", "what remains of the other half". The reader can't tell how many separate investments exist.

**Pronouns whose noun is unclear:** P1 "them". S5 "them". S12 "that fund". S14 "It" (twice). S16 "that interest shortfall". S20 "the money set aside".

**Years out of order:** S3 (Jan 2027) → S8 (2028, then back to "before January 2027"). Paragraph 3 goes 2028 (S9) → 2031 (S10, S11) → 2028 (S12). Paragraph 4 covers the whole 2027-2042 span, then paragraph 5 returns to 2031, then 2033 (S18), then flexibility with no date (S19), then 2033 again (S20).

**Sentences that seem to contradict each other:**
1. S3 ("$300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings") vs S8 ("any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford").
2. S6 ("no payment depends on ... a forecast", "near-ironclad") vs S13-S14 (payments partly depend on what parked interest earns).
3. S21 ("Market moves change nothing in the plan") vs S14 (falling rates) and S17 (markets move the top).
4. S1 (every committed amount bought "before she commits to it") vs S16 (the interest gap is covered only in 2031).
5. Pitch ("how much more she can promise in 2031") vs the case ("Rather than promising one exact amount").

**Lines that sound machine-written or like slogans:** S1 (the balanced rule stated as a thesis), S4 ("every loss falls on what is left over"), S7 ("a second way to miss a January"), S17 ("raises the top ... lowers it ... move neither end"), S21 ("Market moves change nothing in the plan"). The naming aside in S3 ("X for ten, R for redemption") reads as branding.

**What a reader holding the case looks for and doesn't find:** a dollar range, a confidence figure beyond "barring a US default", the operating reserve's size, the size of the stock slice, and how $300,000 covers $500,000 of payments.

## Finish

**1. What the plan does, as I'd tell a friend.** They put Laura's first $300k into ten US government bond investments, each one repaying just before one of her ten $50k residency payments from 2033 to 2042, so those payments never depend on the stock market. Her 2028 $150k buys more government bonds that repay in late 2032; that's the guaranteed minimum she can give toward the building, and a small leftover goes into a global stock fund. In 2031 she turns half that stock fund into bonds to set the top of the range she tells sponsors, uses part of the other half to cover a risk about reinvesting interest, and keeps the rest as spare money.

**2. Single biggest strength.** The bottom of the range is already bought in 2028, three years before she names it in 2031, so the low end she tells sponsors is something she owns, not a forecast. Together with one government bond per payment, that makes "high certainty" something a judge can check rather than take on trust.

**3. The one thing I still don't understand.** How the interest fits in. S3 and S21 say each payment comes from the holding that repays before it, but S14 says the interest is "meant for" a January and a fall in rates leaves a shortfall. So do the ten holdings pay $50,000 each on their own, or do they need the interest too? And how big is the gap the 2031 Treasuries must cover?

**4. Person or machine.** Machine, or a person writing under a machine's heavy editing. The deciding line is S7, "since a second borrower would only add a second way to miss a January": a polished, aphoristic turn with a compressed metaphor ("miss a January"). The same habit shows in "buys ... payments", "buy the bottom" and "Market moves change nothing in the plan". A student explaining this aloud would not talk like that.

**5. The one sentence I'd most want rewritten.** S14: "It waits in a short-term Treasury fund until the January it was meant for, and if rates fall and stay low, it earns less than the bonds' prices assumed." It is the plan's admitted weak point, and the 2031 fix (S16) and the reserve (S20) both depend on it. On one reading its subject is unclear, it slips in a new mechanism, and it contradicts S6 and S21.
