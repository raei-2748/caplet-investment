# R8, blind judge: the contrarian (V7)

Read only `rounds/blind/R8_pack.txt` (547 words in the pack) and `keel/WHARTON_WORD.md`. Same persona: an
equity-minded industry judge who suspects a Treasury-heavy plan is too timid for a client "willing to take
thoughtful risks". Scored only on Wharton's five criteria.

## After one read

- **The strategy:** whenever Laura commits to a sum, she buys the Treasuries that will repay it. That means the ten
  payments in 2027, a minimum facility contribution in 2028 and both ends of the 2031 range. Shares get only about the
  interest on the minimum, and half of them are kept back, promised to no one.
- **Its biggest strength:** "markets can change how large a range Laura names, but never whether she meets a payment
  or a number already named." Still the line I'd repeat.

## Is "thoughtful risks" engaged now? Yes, and well.

"Laura's living costs are paid from outside the portfolio, so she could afford more risk, but the residency couldn't:
its co-sponsors, grants and program fees can shrink in the same recessions that hit shares, so her part is most useful
as the one source that won't."

This is the argument I'd been waiting for. It concedes the client *could* take more risk (her willingness and her
personal ability are both real), then moves the question to who actually bears the risk: the residency, whose other
income is equity-like. It doesn't quote "thoughtful risks", but it engages exactly that point and wins it. It also
quietly fixes the old weak defence (her own income being cyclical, which mattered only before 2028), and it gives
the first creative answer on this page to Wharton's "appropriate diversification": she diversifies *the residency's*
funding, not just her own account. An equity judge can disagree with the caution and still respect that reasoning.

## Did the why-2028 answer survive? Partly.

R7 gave a timing reason: "it is the last money Laura adds, so a crash in 2029 or 2030 can shrink the top of her range
but never its bottom." R8 drops it. What's left is "gives up some growth" plus the residency argument, which explains
*why Treasuries* but not quite *why already in 2028* rather than at the 2031 naming. The timing logic now survives only
in "A crash on its own lowers only the top", which a reader has to connect back. A small regression against a larger
gain.
