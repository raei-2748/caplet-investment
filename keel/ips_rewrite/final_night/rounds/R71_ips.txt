Caplet XR keeps every sum Laura commits to in Treasuries before anyone counts on it, starting with her ten $50,000 operating payments. They're fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. We repay it with ten Treasury holdings, the X in the name, each kept until its redemption, the R, and all owed by the US government, which issues the dollars it owes. Her first $300,000 buys all it can, and the 2028 deposit buys any it can't. Five are funds of Treasuries maturing in one year, trading a fixed payout for being easier to sell than a small lot of bonds.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get only what they left. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market.

In 2028 Laura buys an extra stake in the fund behind the 2033 payment, sized to repay about what's left of that deposit by December 2032. Since that payout isn't fixed, the credible range she gives co-sponsors starts a little under it. The stake costs less than it repays, so she can put the difference into a world index fund of thousands of companies without shrinking what the stake hands back. Buying it in 2028 means the bottom is held in Treasuries, not shares, through years when a slump could also shrink co-sponsors' giving.

A risk survives holding to redemption. Interest, maturing holdings and the bonds inside each fund all pay out before the payment they serve, and years of near-zero rates would leave the payments short. So in 2031, before naming her range, Laura buys Treasuries to cover what the payments would lack if waiting money earned nothing. Up to half her shares pay for them, then, if needed, part of the stake behind the bottom, which lowers the whole range. No payment depends on how shares do.

Naming the range creates a second debt, so it is matched the same way. That day the other half of her shares is sold for Treasuries, setting the top at the bottom plus what it fetched. Co-sponsors hear only figures she already owns, so no later fall can cost her credibility. Barring a US default and with both deposits made, markets and rates can change the range but not a payment, and that is the high degree of certainty she asked for.

She contributes the top, or less if the building in Taiwan needs less, which is why it's a range. The project will change as it develops, so anything else she holds, unsold shares included, stays outside every commitment and can be sold any day. Her operating reserve in 2033 is the Treasuries still held for the payments, and it empties one redemption at a time.
