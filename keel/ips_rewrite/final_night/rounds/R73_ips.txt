Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments are fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so Caplet XR treats them as a debt and buys the Treasuries that repay it before anyone counts on them. There are ten holdings, each kept until its redemption, which gives the plan its X and its R. Her first $300,000 should buy all ten; if not, the 2028 deposit buys the rest. Five, the 2033 one included, are funds of bonds maturing in one calendar year, each paying out what its bonds return, not a fixed sum.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would fit her record of thoughtful risks and probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get only what they left. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that levered leftover would swing far harder than the share market.

In 2028 she buys an extra stake in the 2033 fund. It costs less than what's left of the second deposit but, with its interest, hands back about all of it by December 2032; her range's bottom sits a little under that. The money the stake didn't need buys a world index fund, so her shares risk only interest the Treasuries haven't paid yet. Bought in 2028, the bottom is held in Treasuries through years when a slump could also shrink co-sponsors' giving.

Interest and redemptions arrive before the payments they serve, so if rates on that waiting money fell and stayed low, the payments would fall short. In 2031, before naming the range, she buys extra Treasuries as cover, enough to meet the payments even if waiting money earned nothing. Up to half her shares pay for it; if they can't, the extra stake does and the range she names is lower, so no payment waits on a share price.

Naming the range creates a second debt: the bottom for certain, and up to the top if building costs in Taiwan run high. Before she names it, she pays for the top the same way, selling the other half of her shares for Treasuries that pay out before 2033, so the top is the bottom plus what they fetched and a rise before then lifts it. Left in shares, a named top could fall short and cost her credibility, while any rise past it would never reach the building. A high degree of certainty, to us, means every payment and every named figure survives any market, barring a US default or a missed deposit.

As the project develops, she can sell any day whatever the payments and the range don't need, unsold shares included, since none is committed. She needs no trade to set aside her operating reserve in 2033: the payment holdings and the 2031 cover already form it, and their redemptions pay the debt down a year at a time until it is gone in 2042.
