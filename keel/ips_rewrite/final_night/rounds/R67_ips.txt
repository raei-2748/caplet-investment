Caplet XR keeps every sum Laura commits to in Treasuries before anyone counts on it, starting with her ten $50,000 operating payments from 2033. They're fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. We repay it with ten Treasury holdings, each held until it pays out; her first $300,000 buys all it can and the 2028 deposit buys the rest. Five are funds that hold one year's Treasuries and pay out that December, and they sell more easily than a small lot of bonds. The X and R stand for those ten and their redemption.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get what was left over. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market.

In 2028 Laura buys an extra stake in the fund behind the 2033 payment, sized so its December 2032 payout matches what's left of that deposit. She'll offer co-sponsors that payout, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed, as the bottom of her credible range. The stake costs less than it pays out, and the difference buys a world index fund of thousands of companies. Bought in 2028, the bottom doesn't rest on shares in years when a slump could also shrink co-sponsors' giving and her earnings.

Interest arrives years before the payment it's meant for, and every bond, even inside the funds, repays weeks or months early, and a lasting fall in short-term rates would leave the payments short. So the plan assumes the waiting money earns nothing. In 2031 she sells up to half her shares and buys enough Treasuries to fill the gap that assumption leaves. When that half isn't enough, the stake behind the bottom pays the rest, and the whole range moves down before she names it.

Once co-sponsors hear the top, a fall in shares could put it out of reach and cost her credibility, while a rise would add nothing she'd promised. So she sells the other half for Treasuries the same day, and the top is the bottom plus what they fetch. Barring a US default and with both deposits made, falling markets and rates land on the range, not the payments, which is what we mean by a high degree of certainty.

Her operating reserve in 2033 needs no trade: it is the holdings, cash and extra Treasuries held for the payments. She contributes the top, or less if the building needs less, but never below the bottom she named. Everything else, including what the kept half didn't need, stays in shares and Treasuries she can sell any day, uncommitted so she has room as the project develops. Once the final redemption meets the final payment in 2042, nothing she holds is spoken for.
