Laura makes two commitments: ten $50,000 operating payments for her Taiwan residency from 2033, and a credible range for her facility contribution, named in 2031. Caplet XR pays for each in Treasuries before anyone relies on it. The payments are fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt; inflation can raise what the building costs, never what she owes. Each payment gets its own Treasury holding, kept until its redemption, and ten holdings and ten redemptions are the X and the R in the name. Her first $300,000 should buy all ten; if not, the 2028 deposit buys the rest.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would fit her record of thoughtful risks and probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, leaving a third for the facility. Every gain or loss in the mix would land on that third, levered like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, and her 2031 range would rest on it.

In 2028 she buys an extra stake in the Treasury fund behind the 2033 payment, costing less than what's left of the 2028 deposit but returning, with its interest, about that whole amount by December 2032. The bottom of her range starts a little under that sum. Her shares are bought with the difference, in a world index fund of thousands of companies, so they risk only the interest the stake will earn, never the deposit.

Interest and redemptions reach Laura before the payments they serve, and a long spell of low rates on that waiting money would leave the payments short. In 2031, before naming the range, she buys extra Treasuries as cover, enough to meet the payments even at zero interest. Half her shares are held back to pay for it; a deep enough fall in rates could also take part of the extra stake and lower the bottom.

Naming the range creates a second debt. She will give the facility whatever the Treasuries behind the range pay, never less than the bottom and never more than the top. Before she speaks, she sells the other half of her shares for Treasury funds that pay out before 2033, and the top is the bottom plus what those shares fetched. Left in shares, a named top could fall short and cost her credibility. Our test for a high degree of certainty is that once the cover is bought, no market can touch a payment or a named figure, barring a US default.

Her financial flexibility is the shares the cover didn't need, which nobody is told to count on, because the project will change as it develops. In 2033 she sets aside her operating reserve without a trade: it is everything already held for the payments, cover included. When the last holding is redeemed for the 2042 payment, the debt is repaid and anything left is hers.
