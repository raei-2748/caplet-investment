Caplet XR keeps every sum Laura commits to in Treasuries before anyone counts on it, starting with her ten $50,000 operating payments from 2033. They're fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. We repay it with ten Treasury holdings, each held until it pays out. Her first $300,000 buys all it can, and the 2028 deposit buys the rest. The X and R in the name stand for those ten and their redemption.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get what was left over. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market. Matching the debt first removes that leverage.

In 2028 Laura buys an extra stake in the Treasury fund behind the 2033 payment, sized so its December 2032 payout matches what's left of that deposit. She'll offer co-sponsors that payout, rounded down because a fund promises no fixed sum, as the bottom of her credible range. The stake costs less than its payout, and the difference buys a world index fund of thousands of companies, which can grow with the building's cost in Taiwan dollars. She has long taken thoughtful risks, but the payments leave her first $300,000 no room for them. Buying the bottom in 2028 means a slump before 2031, which could also cut co-sponsors' giving and her earnings, can't touch the fund behind it.

Interest arrives years before the payment it's meant for, and every bond, even inside the funds, repays weeks or months before the payment it serves. That money waits at short-term rates, and years of near-zero rates would leave the payments short. So the plan assumes the waiting money earns nothing. In 2031, before she names a range, up to half her shares are sold for the extra Treasuries that assumption calls for. When that half isn't enough, the stake behind the bottom pays the rest, and the whole range moves down.

Once co-sponsors hear the top, a fall in shares could put it out of reach and cost her credibility, while a rise would add nothing she'd promised. So the other half is sold for Treasuries the same day, and the top is the bottom plus what it fetched. Barring a US default and with both deposits made, falling markets and rates land on the range, not the payments, which is what we mean by a high degree of certainty.

In 2033 she gives the top, or less if the building needs less, but at least the bottom. Anything left after the gift, including unsold shares, is flexibility for whatever the residency needs as it develops. The operating reserve is everything still held for the payments, and by January 2042 all ten redemptions have met them.
