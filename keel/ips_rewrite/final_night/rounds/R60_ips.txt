Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments work like a debt: fixed in dollars and payable only from her portfolio. Caplet XR, X for ten and R for redemption, meets them with ten Treasury holdings, each redeemed in the year before its payment, and lets her tell co-sponsors only figures she already holds in Treasuries.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at today's Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, so in that mix her facility contribution would be whatever is left after a fixed claim. Like the shares of a company that has borrowed heavily, it would swing far harder than the market beneath it, and a named figure that fell short could cost her co-sponsors' trust.

The ten holdings are bought in January 2027 with her first $300,000, and if they cost more, the gap is the first claim on the 2028 deposit. The last five are Treasury bonds. The first five are funds that each own Treasuries maturing in one calendar year and pay out that December, since some of those years have no listed Treasury of their own. All of it is lent to the US government, because no other borrower is safer and adding one would only add ways to miss a payment.

The 2028 deposit buys more of the fund behind the 2033 payment, enough to pay the whole deposit back in December 2032, and only that extra part's payout, rounded down, counts as the bottom of the range she names in 2031. Bought this early, the bottom barely moves in a slump before 2031, just when co-sponsors' giving and her own earnings could shrink too. What is left buys a world index fund, the plan's thoughtful risk: if every share were lost, the deposit would still come back almost in full.

Money for the payments arrives early, interest years ahead and each holding's repayment months before its January, and waits at short-term rates. If those rates fell and stayed low, the payments could come up short. So in 2031, before naming a range, Laura uses up to half her shares to buy Treasuries that would cover that shortfall even if the waiting money earned nothing, and if that half is not enough, money meant for the range makes up the rest and the range comes out lower. The other half of her shares is sold for Treasuries too, and the top of the range is the bottom plus what it fetched, so strong markets until then raise the top and weak ones lower it. With that done, barring a US default and with both deposits made, no payment waits on a share price, on interest rates or on an early sale, which is how we define a high degree of certainty.

After 2031 share markets can't move the range. She will give no less than the bottom and no more than the top, choosing where in 2033 as the building's cost in Taiwan becomes clearer. Everything else stays hers, unpromised: shares she can sell any day, and whatever the cover and the range turn out not to need, because the project will need things nobody can name in 2031.

The operating reserve she sets aside in 2033 is everything still held for the payments, the cover included, so it needs no trade. It loses one holding a year, and in January 2042 the tenth redemption and the interest saved for it meet the last payment.
