Caplet XR has one rule: before anyone relies on a sum Laura names, she owns the Treasuries that will repay it. A balanced mix would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices her ten $50,000 operating payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, so every gain or loss would land on the last third, swinging it like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, and her 2031 range would rest on that third.

In January 2027 her $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, one repaying before each January from 2033 to 2042, topped up if needed from the 2028 deposit. X stands for the ten, and R for redemption, the day each one is repaid. The payments are fixed in dollars, so inflation can raise what the building costs but never what she owes.

Most of what's left of the 2028 deposit buys more of the fund behind the 2033 payment. It costs less than it will pay out in late 2032, and roughly what it will earn goes instead into one world index fund of thousands of companies, the one place the plan is allowed to be wrong. Laura could afford the risk, since her living costs are met outside the portfolio, but the residency couldn't: its co-sponsors, grants and fees can shrink in the same recessions that hit shares, so her part helps most as the one that won't.

The ladder's one weakness is money that waits. Interest and early repayments can sit for months or years before the payment they serve, and earn less if rates fall and stay low. So in 2031, before she names a range, she buys extra Treasuries, enough to meet every payment even if that money earned nothing, and pays for them with half her shares. Whatever that half can't cover comes out of the 2028 stake, and her range starts lower.

The bottom of her range is what that stake will pay, less a margin because a fund's payout isn't fixed. On the same day she sells the other half of her shares for Treasury funds repaying before 2033, and the top is the bottom plus what they fetched. Both ends are owned before anyone hears them, and in 2033 she gives what those Treasuries pay, up to the top. Falling shares can lower the range before she names it; after that, only a US default could put it out of reach.

Whatever the first half of her shares isn't needed for, and anything paid beyond the top, stays hers, liquid and promised to no one, so she keeps room as the project develops. In 2033 her operating reserve needs no trade, since it is the ladder and the extra Treasuries. By a high degree of certainty we mean that no payment and no figure she has named depends on a share price. The tenth redemption, with the interest kept for it, meets the last $50,000 in 2042.
