**Verdict: not yet.** It has one brilliant idea (paragraph 2's leverage argument) and honest mechanics. But only the
payments get a real argument. The ends of the range, the two numbers Laura says aloud, rest on the case's own words or
the obvious reason, and the strength is never priced.

## 1. Ideas a judge would retell
- **A "balanced" mix is a leveraged bet with the facility money** (P2). Fully argued: it has a mechanism, a concession
  and a consequence. Gap: once the leverage is gone, the draft still keeps most of the facility money in Treasuries,
  and nothing explains why.
- **Losing every share costs only the bottom's interest** (P3, pitch). The worst case is argued. What it costs on
  average is never said.
- **Both ends owned before she names them** (P4). Stated, but the only reason is "could fall out of reach", which any
  plan that locks its range would give.
- **One borrower on purpose** (P2). Argued, but it rests on an unstated premise: a US default would hit every other
  dollar borrower too.
- **Where the contribution lands depends on the building's needs, not on markets** (P5). This is the most distinctive
  idea in the draft, and it is buried in a subordinate clause.

## 2. The biggest strength
**Stated:** yes, in the first sentence. **Argued:** for the payments only. For the bottom, the reason given is the
case's signalling sentence reworded. And the opening rule ("by the time anyone relies on it") would allow the bottom to
be bought from shares in 2031. Nothing says why it is bought in 2028, the plan's biggest choice. **Priced:** half. P2
concedes she would probably end richer, and P3 bounds the worst case. Missing: the average cost (share growth given up
on most of the 2028 deposit for five years, and on the promised half for two), when it is paid (mostly in strong
markets), and what it buys (a bottom that 2028-30 markets can't move).

5. **Why zero:** it is the floor that waiting cash can't fall below, so the set-aside is a bound, not a guess (+8).

## 5. Packaging that should go
- P3's signalling clause presents the case's own sentence as reasoning, and "willing to take thoughtful risks" only
  echoes the case.
- The X/R sentence is a decoder for now. The R earns its place by doing work: its second sense, keeping a promise, is
  the thesis, and the tenth redemption is the natural ending. Five of the ten holdings are funds, so "redemption" is
  exact only for the bonds.
- **Pitch:** it lists trades and never mentions the range or the strength. It should lead with the strength (nothing she
  names is left unowned), keep its last line, and drop "using her 2028 deposit for any shortfall".

## 6. Where cleverness costs clarity
- "If bond prices rise first" makes the reader reason backwards. The point is that the ladder might cost more than
  $300,000.
- P4 tells naming day out of order: the top is defined before the gap is paid, and it's unclear what "first" comes
  before.
- "Whatever markets did" holds for shares. It doesn't hold for the payments' cost, which moves with rates.
