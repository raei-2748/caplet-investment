## G1. The true 2028 timing reason, at about zero net words (asked for by the Chief of Staff; Contrarian's #1)
**The idea.** Buy the minimum in 2028 because co-sponsors are approached in 2031. If the minimum sat in shares until
then, a crash in 2029 or 2030 would shrink it in the very years co-sponsors' own budgets are shrinking, just before
she asks them. Buying it in 2028 means that a crash before 2031 can only lower the top of her range. That is the R7
claim with the non sequitur removed. The old "it is the last money she adds" was never the reason.
**True?** Yes. The bottom is bought in 2028, so a share crash can only reach the half of the shares that sets the
top. Two side effects are also true: it locks 2028's rates on that money, and the residency logic in ¶3 already
explains why this matters.
**Word-neutral route.** Delete ¶4's last sentence ("A crash on its own lowers only the top; a boom raises it." 13
words; the Contrarian stumbled on it). Then extend ¶3's "Buying the minimum in 2028, not 2031, gives up some growth"
with a clause carrying the reason (about +16). Net about +3, which the Writer can find.
**Record.** Agrees with the IBTM note ("giving co-sponsors a minimum figure in 2031") and VT note ("a rise before 2031
lifts the top of the range co-sponsors hear"). No contradiction (Archive C7, C9).

## G2. Make "a high degree of certainty" a two-part test with a pedigree, at zero net words (Creativity and Portfolio Analysis)
**The idea.** In corporate finance an asset is riskless for a date only if it meets two conditions: it cannot default,
and nothing has to be reinvested before that date (Damodaran, Corporate Finance lecture packet 1, pp. 108-109,
Spring 2026; cited in the Final Report, not the IPS). Our plan meets the first by construction: every holding is a
dollar Treasury, and the payments are dollar payments. The ladder's one weakness is exactly the second condition,
and the shortfall rule exists to pay for it. So the certainty definition and the weakness paragraph become one idea
told in two places, instead of a definition and a separate confession.
**Why it lifts the page.** It replaces our home-made definition ("no payment depends on a share price") with a
standard test a finance judge recognises, applied honestly. We pass one condition and pay for the other. The
weakness paragraph then reads as rigour rather than as an admission, and a judge can retell it. No team that simply
"buys Treasuries" will have framed it this way.
**Word-neutral route.** In ¶2, the two conditions replace "Held to its end, each pays out … however its price moves
before then" plus the definition clause. In ¶5, the opener can then point back ("the second test") instead of
re-explaining.
**Cautions.** Never write "can't default" or "risk-free" flat (FACTS never-say list); "is a Treasury" carries the
first condition. The funds still pay their net asset value, so keep R8's "rounded down because a fund's final payout
isn't fixed".
**Record.** It grows from Note 1 ("reinvestment-rate risk") and reflection 1 ("a nominal Treasury matches the
liability perfectly"). Archive C3: GROWS-FROM.
