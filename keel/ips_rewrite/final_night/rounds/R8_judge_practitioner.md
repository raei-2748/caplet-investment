## After one read
**The strategy:** every sum Laura commits to is bought in Treasuries when she commits to it. In 2027 the $300,000
buys a ten-holding Treasury ladder, one maturity before each January from 2033 to 2042. In 2028 the deposit tops up
the ladder, then buys extra units of the late-2032 fund as her minimum facility contribution, with only about their
interest in world shares. In 2031 half the shares move to Treasuries before she names the range. Any reinvestment
shortfall is covered from the other half first, then from the 2032 fund. From 2033 the reserve runs off by
redemption, with nothing sold.

**Its biggest strength:** "markets can change how large a range Laura names, but never whether she meets a payment
or a number already named." The new risk argument underneath it: Laura could afford more risk, but the residency
couldn't, so her contribution should be the one source of its money that doesn't shrink in a recession.

## Strongest technical claim
> "The payments aren't adjusted for inflation, so dollar Treasuries match them exactly"

In one clause it answers why the plan uses nominal Treasuries, not inflation-linked bonds or stocks "for inflation".
Most teams get this wrong. Close second: "Laura's living costs are paid from outside the portfolio, so she could
afford more risk, but the residency couldn't".

## Weakest technical claims
1. > "Laura's living costs are paid from outside the portfolio"

   This is a factual claim about the client. If the case says it, it is excellent. If it is inferred, it is the one
   sentence a judge holding the case will check. Fact-Checker to verify against the case text.
2. > "for us a high degree of certainty means that no operating payment depends on a share price"

   It is still narrower than the closing sentence, which states the real test, and the reserve's one credit (the US
   Treasury) is still never named. "Never" in the last paragraph rests on it.
3. > "American public pensions, which are allowed to do that, have taken on more risk to keep up with it"

   Softer than before, but still causal and unsupported on the page.
4. > "A crash on its own lowers only the top"

   Correct, since a crash combined with low rates can also lower the bottom via the shortfall rule, but "on its own"
   is doing a lot of quiet work. Acceptable.
