# R3 cold-reader review

Source: `rounds/blind/R3_pack.txt`. Read once, plus the context given in the request (Laura, $300,000 in 2027, $150,000 in 2028, $50,000 each January 2033 to 2042, a range to co-sponsors in 2031).

## 1. One-sentence paraphrase of each paragraph

Paragraphs are numbered as they appear in the file. Line 1 counts as paragraph 1 because it sits above the `---` break.

1. (Line 1, the opening) Money is moved out of shares as soon as someone begins relying on it, and Laura's $300,000 goes into ten Treasuries, one maturing each year before 2033 to 2042, with her 2028 money and her 2031 range set up in advance.
2. (Line 4) Fixed payments should be priced at the Treasury rate rather than at expected share returns, because public pensions that used the higher rate took on risk to meet it, so each commitment is bought in Treasuries the day it is made.
3. (Line 6) The first commitment, called Caplet XR, is a ladder of Treasuries and fixed-date Treasury funds bought in January 2027 that pay out each January from 2033 to 2042, and none is sold early.
4. (Line 8) The 2028 deposit tops up the ladder if needed, most of the rest buys a 2032 Treasury fund, and the leftover goes into a world index fund, which is the only risky part and is limited to the interest it earns, since her income also depends on the economy.
5. (Line 10) The second commitment is the 2031 range given to co-sponsors, whose bottom is the 2032 fund's expected payout and whose top adds half her shares moved into Treasuries first, so both ends are funded before she names them.
6. (Line 12) The ladder's weakness is that falling interest rates would reduce the interest its bonds earn while waiting, so before naming the range she moves enough of her kept shares into Treasuries to cover that gap, and lowers the range if she can't.
7. (Line 14) From January 2033 the ladder and that set-aside form her operating reserve, paying the residency each year and shrinking by one holding each year until the final $50,000 in 2042.

## 2. Sentences I had to read twice or still don't understand

- Line 1: "Her 2028 deposit buys the least she could give the facility; the most she'll name in 2031 is bought before co-sponsors hear it." Confusing: "the least she could give the facility" and "the most she'll name" are not clear, and I could not tell what "facility" refers to.
- Line 4: "A fixed payment has one honest price, which is what Treasuries charge to deliver it on its date." Treasuries do not charge anything, so "what Treasuries charge" needs rewording. I read it twice.
- Line 4: "Price it at what shares usually earn and it looks cheaper, but the difference is a return nobody has earned yet." I understood the idea only after reading it twice.
- Line 4: "...whose rules let them do exactly this, have spent two decades taking on more risk to keep up with it." "Do exactly this" points back to pricing at expected share returns, but the sentence is three clauses away from that idea, so the reference is hard to track.
- Line 6: "Cash only looks safer: for fixed dollars on fixed dates, it is a bet that interest rates won't fall." Why holding cash is a bet on rates was unclear until I reread it.
- Line 6: "...and none is sold before its redemption, even for the building." "The building" has not been mentioned anywhere before this point, so the phrase came out of nowhere.
- Line 8: "Her 2028 deposit tops up the ladder if rates have moved against it." "Moved against it" is vague: I had to work out that it means rates have moved in a way that hurts the ladder.
- Line 8: "...and we keep it to the interest because Laura's fortunes already ride on the economy." The reason is not obvious: the sentence links her income to the economy, but it does not say why that means the fund gets only the interest.
- Line 8: "Her contribution is worth most as the part of the residency's money that doesn't shrink with the rest." "The rest" has no clear referent, and I had to reread it to work out what would shrink.
- Line 10: "Its top adds half her shares, moved into Treasuries before she names it, because a number said aloud gets planned around." I could not tell what "adds half her shares" means, whether it is half the value or half the shares, until I reread the whole paragraph.
- Line 10: "Companies raise dividends only to levels they expect to keep for the same reason..." "The same reason" points back to a reason that was not stated in this paragraph.
- Line 10: "Its bottom is what the late-2032 fund will pay, rounded down, which she has owned since 2028." Unclear whether "which" refers to the fund or to the bottom of the range.
- Line 12: "So before Laura names her range, we price the gap as if every waiting dollar earned nothing and move that much from her kept shares into Treasuries." "Price the gap" is not explained.

## 3. Words or terms used before they are explained

- **Shares**: used in line 1 with no definition; I assumed it meant stocks, which is only confirmed later.
- **Treasuries / Treasury bonds / Treasury funds**: used in line 1 before any explanation of what they are.
- **Facility**: used in line 1 and again in line 8 but never defined, and I could not tell whether it means the residency's building or the residency itself.
- **The residency and the building**: "the residency's grants" (line 8) and "the building" (line 6) appear without being introduced in the pack. The request context supplied the residency, but the pack itself does not.
- **Co-sponsors**: used in line 1 and never explained.
- **Maturity / held to maturity**: used in line 6 without a definition.
- **Operating commitment / operating payment / operating reserve**: "operating" is used in line 6 before it is explained; the meaning is only clear by the end of the document.
- **Interest rates**: used in line 4 and line 6 as though known.
- **Index fund / world index fund**: used in line 8 without explanation.
- **Book advances, speaking fees, licensing, grants, program fees**: a list of income sources in line 8 that is not explained; "book advances" is especially opaque to a non-expert.
- **Liquid**: used in line 10 without explanation.
- **Dividends**: used in line 10 without explanation.
- **Redemption**: defined in line 6 as "the bond market's word for the day a bond is repaid". It is used at line 6 only after that definition, so this one is fine, but "redeemed" in line 14 is used without restating it.
- **Credible range**: the phrase is used in line 10 as a term; the reason it is a range rather than a single number is explained only by the phrase "a number said aloud gets planned around".

## 4. The plan and its main strength (two sentences)

The plan is to lock Laura's money into a ladder of Treasury bonds and funds that pay the residency $50,000 each January from 2033 to 2042, to add a small index fund for growth, and to give co-sponsors a 2031 range whose both ends are already funded. Its main strength is that the required payments are bought and held to maturity in Treasuries, so market falls cannot reduce the residency's core funding.

## 5. Least like a real person talking

"A Treasury held to maturity repays what it promised whatever markets do, so we define a high degree of certainty as no operating payment depending on a share price." It reads like a definition written into a rulebook, with "we define" stating a term rather than explaining a thought. A person would more likely say what they mean by "certain" in plain words.

## Notes

- Lines 1 and 4 open with almost the same sentence ("money leaves shares..."), so the reader meets the same idea twice in the first few lines.
- The pack uses "facility," "residency," and "building" for what may be one thing. Choosing one term would remove a source of confusion.
