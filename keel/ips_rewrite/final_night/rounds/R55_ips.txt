Laura's ten operating payments work like a debt: $50,000 each January from 2033 to 2042, fixed in dollars and payable only from her portfolio. Caplet XR meets them with Treasuries bought years ahead, and lets her tell co-sponsors only figures she already holds in Treasuries.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042, above all in strong markets, and that is the price of this plan. But at today's Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, so in that mix the facility money is whatever is left after a fixed claim. Like the shares of a company that has borrowed heavily, it would swing far harder than the market beneath it, and it is the very number she has to name in 2031.

So in January 2027 her first $300,000 buys a Treasury bond or fund for each payment, repaid in the year before it falls due, with the 2028 deposit topping up any shortfall. All ten lend only to the US government, since other borrowers would add ways to miss a payment without removing that one. Each payment is met by a holding that is simply redeemed, never sold early: the R in Caplet XR, and the X is the ten.

Laura has been willing to take thoughtful risks throughout her career, and that career still pays her living costs and funds her 2028 deposit, through earnings that can shrink in the same slump that sinks shares. So her portfolio doesn't double that bet. Most of the deposit buys an extra holding in the fund behind the 2033 payment, and that holding's payout, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed, sets the bottom of the range she names in 2031. Only the rest, about what the extra holding will earn by 2032, goes into a world index fund. If every share were lost, she would still get back about all she put into the two.

Some money arrives early, interest along the way and holdings repaid months before their January, and waits at short-term rates. If those rates fall and stay low, the payments could come up short. So before she names a range in 2031, she sells half of her shares for Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus what that half fetched, because a top left in shares would gear her promise just as the balanced mix geared the facility money. From the other half she sells only enough to buy Treasuries that would cover the shortfall even if the waiting money earned nothing. If that half can't pay for them, money meant for the range does, and she names a lower range, cutting the top before the bottom. Either way, no payment waits on a share price.

From then on, barring a US default, no market can move her outside the range. She will give at least the bottom and at most the top, and the building's cost in Taiwan by 2033 decides where. What she doesn't give, any cover the shortfall never needed and the shares she kept are her financial flexibility, which comes after the payments and her promise.

In 2033 she sets aside the operating reserve without a trade, because it is the Treasuries still waiting for a payment, enough for all ten. It shrinks by one redemption a year until the tenth, with the interest saved for it, meets the last payment.
