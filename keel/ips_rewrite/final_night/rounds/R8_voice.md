## Overall
The best round yet for voice. The thesis now reads one way, the pitch's tic is gone, Caplet XR is planted in paragraph 1

## Must-change (1)
- **"move enough of her other half into Treasuries"** – in ordinary English "her other half" means her partner. A judge
  will smile at the wrong moment. Say "the half of her shares she kept" or similar.

## Sentences not clear on one reading (6 in the IPS, 1 in the pitch)
1. "Pricing a fixed sum at what shares usually earn makes it look cheaper by counting a return nobody has earned yet,
   and American public pensions, which are allowed to do that, have taken on more risk to keep up with it." – the
   central idea is squeezed back into one sentence. R6/R7 gave the mechanism its own sentence ("because future growth is
   expected to cover part of it"); this round folds it into "by counting", and the pensions become a subordinate clause
   ending on "it". This is the one idea that most needs room.
2. "Held to its end, each pays out what its Treasuries repay, however its price moves before then." – three "its" and an
   "each"; unchanged in substance for three rounds. Name the holding once.
3. "Only about the interest they will earn goes into one world index fund…" – "Only about" trips the reader; it sounds
   like a hedge stuck to the front of the sentence.
4. "A crash on its own lowers only the top; a boom raises it." – "on its own" is unclear (a crash without falling rates?
   a crash alone, as opposed to what?), and "it" = the top, just.
5. "The ladder's weakness is that some of its money, its interest and its early repayments, waits months for its January
   and earns less than planned if rates fall and stay low." – far better than R7, but four "its" and the
   comma-appositive make the reader parse twice.
6. "move enough of her other half into Treasuries" – see must-change; it reads wrong before it reads right.
7. **Pitch:** "with about its interest in world shares" – "about its" is hard to say aloud, and "its" could be the
   deposit's interest or the minimum's.
