**Verdict.** Not yet. It needs to finish its best idea, not add more. P2 is genuinely distinctive: a balanced mix makes the building money geared equity. But the argument is used only against the rival and never turned on the plan itself, so 2027, 2028 and 2031 read as three rules, not one idea. Inflation is missing.

## 1. Ideas a judge would retell
1. **"A balanced portfolio is a geared bet on the building"** (P2). Half argued. Two things are missing:
   - the consequence: a residual that swings this hard can't credibly be named two years early, which is exactly what the case asks her to do;
   - the mirror: once the debt is matched with Treasuries, the gearing cancels. P4's "losing every share" line is that mirror, but nothing connects it to P2.
2. **"She names only numbers she already holds in Treasuries"** (P1, pitch). Argued for the payments, asserted for the range. Nothing says why the top must be in Treasuries *before* she names it.
3. **"Even a Treasury ladder isn't riskless: interest arrives early and waits"** (P5). The mechanism is clear. Why zero is the right test is not: it's the one rate waiting cash can't fall below, so the test can't prove too hopeful.
4. **"The minimum is bought in 2028 because the slump that would shrink it also shrinks co-sponsors and her earnings"** (P4). Argued in one clause; what it gives up goes unsaid.
5. **"The range is her building decision, not a market forecast"** (P6). It quietly answers "why a range if both ends are owned?", but never says the width carries no market risk, so "how confident" is answered only by implication.

## 2. The biggest strength
Nothing Laura says aloud depends on a forecast. Each commitment is held in Treasuries before she makes it, and risk sits only on money nobody is promised. Certainty by construction, not probability, survives a wrong model.
- **Stated:** yes.
- **Argued:** for the payments only, not for the range.
- **Priced:** once, for the plan as a whole ("probably leave her richer"). The real costs sit in two decisions that go unpriced: the growth given up on most of the 2028 deposit, and on the promised half from 2031. A better version names both, in words, as the price of a number that can't fall.

## 4. Missing ideas, ranked (496/500, gate 497)
1. **The named range is a second debt.**
   - Once said aloud, the range binds like the payments do (breaking it costs credibility; beating it earns nothing). So it gets the same treatment, and leaving it in shares would recreate P2's geared position. One principle then explains every dated move. FR_IDEAS #11/#14, argued back in.
   - Cost: about 14 words.
   - Displaces: P3's "since some of those years have no listed Treasury" (a constraint, not a choice; the Final Report can explain it) and "the plan's thoughtful risk".
2. **Inflation.**
   - Fixed dollars mean ordinary Treasuries match the payments exactly. Inflation lands on the building and on what each payment buys in Taiwan. That is the honest reason her kept money stays in shares: they can *grow with* costs, never "protect against" them.
   - Cost: about 12 words.
   - Displaces: P6's "because the project will need things nobody can name in 2031", which this makes specific.
3. **The price of owning early** (section 2). This also answers growth-minded judges.
   - Cost: about 10 words.
   - Displaces: "and pay out that December".
4. **Diversification on purpose.**
   - One borrower for every payment, by design, because a second borrower is only a second way to miss a January. What counts is diversifying the residency's money, which P4 half-says.
   - Cost: about 12 words, or the Final Report.
5. **Why zero** (about 8 words). **Ability before willingness** (about 14 words; P3 already shows it).
