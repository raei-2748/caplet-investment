Before Laura commits to any amount, Caplet XR makes sure she already holds it in Treasuries, and only money she has promised to no one goes into shares. Her ten operating payments of $50,000 come first, because co-sponsors may help with the facility but cannot be relied on for those. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each redeemed in the year before the January it pays for, and their interest makes up the rest of the $500,000. The plan is named for those ten redemptions (X for ten, R for redemption).

In the usual mix of shares and bonds, payments that are owed whatever markets do would take most of her money, so every loss would fall on the thin slice left for the facility. That slice would swing harder than the share market, just as a company's shares grow riskier the more it borrows. Caplet XR keeps shares only where a loss costs no payment, and we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment rests on a share price, and nothing bought for a payment is sold before it matures.

The 2028 deposit first completes the ten if bond prices rose before January 2027 and the first $300,000 fell short. Most of what remains buys more of the fund behind the 2033 payment, which pays out just before her facility contribution is due. That extra payout, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed, is the bottom of the range she names in 2031. The rest, about the interest the extra holding will earn by 2032, goes into a world index fund spread across thousands of companies. It is a thoughtful risk of the kind she has taken all her career, because the extra holding alone pays back about her whole deposit, so losing every share would cost her only that interest.

The plan's weak point is the interest and other money that arrives before the January it is meant for, which waits in a short-term Treasury fund and may earn too little if rates fall and stay low. In 2031, before naming her range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries maturing before 2033, and the top is the bottom plus that half. With the half she keeps, she buys enough Treasuries to fill that gap even if the waiting money earned nothing, and if it can't, she names a lower range, so no payment ever waits on shares. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it, and the bottom falls only if low rates need more than all her shares.

Owning so few shares will probably leave her less to give. But a top left in shares could fall below what she had told co-sponsors and cost her their confidence, while a rise would only add to what she keeps. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, at a point she chooses in 2033.

Inflation can't touch payments fixed in dollars, but it can raise building costs in Taiwan. So whatever is left of the half she kept stays outside the range as her financial flexibility, sellable on any trading day and the only money she has that isn't fixed in dollars. From 2033 her operating reserve is everything still waiting for a payment, and it changes only by making one each January, the last in January 2042 from the tenth redemption.
