Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments work like a debt: fixed in dollars and payable only from her portfolio. Caplet XR, X for ten and R for redemption, meets them with ten Treasury holdings, each redeemed in the year before its payment, and lets her tell co-sponsors only figures she already holds in Treasuries.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at today's Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, so in that mix her facility contribution would be whatever is left after a fixed claim. Like the shares of a company that has borrowed heavily, it would swing far harder than the market beneath it.

The ten holdings are bought in January 2027 with her first $300,000, and if they cost more, the gap is the first claim on the 2028 deposit. The last five are Treasury bonds; the first five are funds that each own Treasuries maturing in one calendar year and pay out that December, since some of those years have no listed Treasury.

Most of the 2028 deposit buys enough more of the fund behind the 2033 payment to repay the whole deposit in December 2032, and that extra payout, rounded down, is the bottom of the range she names in 2031. Bought this early, the bottom barely moves in a slump before 2031, when co-sponsors and her own earnings could shrink too. What is left buys a world index fund, the plan's thoughtful risk, so losing every share would still return almost all the deposit.

Money for the payments arrives early, interest years ahead and each holding's repayment months before its January, and waits at short-term rates. If those rates fell and stayed low, the payments could come up short. So in 2031, before naming a range, up to half her shares buy Treasuries that would cover that shortfall even if the waiting money earned nothing; if that half is not enough, the range she names is smaller. The other half is sold for Treasuries too, and the top is the bottom plus what it fetched, so strong markets until then raise the top and weak ones lower it. That done, barring a US default and with both deposits made, no payment waits on shares, interest rates or an early sale, which is how we define a high degree of certainty.

She will give no less than the bottom and no more than the top, choosing where in 2033 as the building's cost in Taiwan becomes clearer. Everything else stays hers and unpromised, including shares she can sell any day, because the project will need things nobody can name in 2031.

The operating reserve she sets aside in 2033 is everything still held for the payments, the cover included, so it needs no trade. It loses one holding a year, and in January 2042 the tenth redemption and the interest saved for it meet the last payment.
