# R74 review (c): is it as brilliant as possible?
AI-generated review (Claude, 6 Nov 2026). Not submission text.

**Verdict: close, not yet.** R74 is ideas, not packaging; what it lacks is argument. Its most original thought sits in a
subordinate clause, and the leverage paragraph never draws its conclusion. Of Ray's four endorsed ideas, one is in (the
range as a debt), two are half in (diversifying the residency; spare money unpromised, but with no reason given) and
one is missing (prepaid running costs).

## 1. Ideas a judge would retell
1. **A balanced mix makes the building money riskier than the share market** (P2). The best erudition, unnamed.
   Missing link: the payments must be met in full, so every dollar a mix loses comes out of the last third. The
   paragraph ends on the critique, not on the conclusion.
2. **Naming the range creates a second debt** (P5). Argued, but undersold. "Up to the top if the building costs more"
   reverses the case's premise. The case wants a range because the portfolio is uncertain; here the portfolio is
   settled first, and only the building's need decides where she lands. That answers "why a range?", hidden in a
   clause.
3. **The 2033 reserve is set aside without a trade** (P6). Fully argued.
4. **She could lose every share and still get the remaining deposit back** (P3). The idea is there, but buried in
   "interest the stake hasn't paid yet".
5. **Waiting money priced at zero** (P4). Explained, but never offered as the answer to the case's "how they
   evaluated that level of certainty", which it is.
6. **Her money holds when co-sponsors' giving shrinks** (P3). The cause is given, the payoff is not: hers is the part
   of the residency's money that doesn't fall in the same slump.

## 2. The biggest strength
Risk sits only where nobody relies on it. Each commitment is owned before it is made, so markets can change only what
hasn't been promised yet.
- **Stated:** yes, in the pitch and P1.
- **Argued:** well against growth (P2). Ownership as the test of certainty is only asserted ("strictly"). No judge is
  told why a worst-case bound beats a percentage.
- **Priced:** half. "Probably leave her richer" is honest, but it never says who pays: the contribution and her spare
  money, never a payment. The price against all-Treasuries (at worst, the interest) is buried in P3. The cost of naming
  (no gain on the promised half afterwards) is unstated. The cover's price is clear.

- **Only mentioned:** diversification (one borrower behind the payments is never defended), certainty, flexibility

## 5. Packaging that should go
- **The XR gloss** ("gives the plan its X and its R") puts a puzzle inside the debt definition. Let the plan explain
  the name: the tenth redemption retires the debt. The ending closes on "debt" and never touches the name.
- **"Strictly"** is self-praise.
- **The pitch** spends 15 of its 50 words on where each end comes from. Those words should carry the reason: nothing
  she says aloud rests on a market.

## 6. Cleverness that costs clarity
- **"Risk only interest the stake hasn't paid yet"** needs present value to parse. It should say that losing every
  share still leaves the whole remaining deposit by 2032.
- **Three halves** ("up to half", "the other half", "unsold shares"): readers can't tell which shares she keeps.
- **"Paid first":** a date, or a priority?
- **"Once the cover is bought ... barring a short deposit":** the deposit settles in 2028, before the cover exists. Say
  what each remaining risk threatens, and when.
- **The pitch's "the top from half its shares":** the top is the bottom *plus* that sale.
