Laura makes two commitments, ten $50,000 operating payments from 2033 and a credible range for her facility contribution in 2031, and Caplet XR buys each one in Treasuries before anyone relies on it. The payments are fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. Ten Treasury holdings repay it, one per payment, each kept until its redemption, which gives the plan its X and its R. Her first $300,000 should buy all ten; if not, the 2028 deposit buys the rest.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would fit her record of thoughtful risks and probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000. Paid first, they would leave the facility the last third, which would swing like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, far harder than the share market.

In 2028 she buys an extra stake in the Treasury fund behind the 2033 payment, costing less than what's left of the second deposit but returning, with its interest, about that whole amount by December 2032. The bottom of her 2031 range is set a little under that sum. What the stake didn't cost buys a world index fund of thousands of companies, so her shares risk only interest the stake hasn't paid yet. Held from 2028, the bottom sits in Treasuries through years when a slump could also shrink co-sponsors' giving.

Interest and redemptions reach Laura before the payments they serve, and a long spell of low rates on that waiting money would leave the payments short. In 2031, before naming the range, she buys extra Treasuries as cover, enough to meet the payments even at zero interest. Up to half her shares pay for it; if that half falls short, part of the extra stake makes up the difference and the bottom she names is lower.

Naming the range creates a second debt. She owes the facility the bottom whatever happens, and up to the top if the building in Taiwan costs more. So before she speaks, she sells the other half of her shares for Treasury funds that pay out before 2033, and the top is the bottom plus what the shares fetched. A rise in shares before 2031 lifts the top. Left in shares, a named top could fall short and cost her credibility. We read a high degree of certainty strictly: once the cover is bought, no market can touch a payment or a named figure, barring a US default or a short deposit.

As the project develops, she can sell her unsold shares, and anything else the payments and range don't need, any day, since none is committed. In 2033 she sets aside her operating reserve without a trade: it is just the payment holdings and the 2031 cover. From then on, redemptions meet the payments one January at a time, and the debt is gone by 2042.
