## Sentences not clear on one reading (7)
1. "Whenever Laura commits to a sum, she buys the Treasuries that will pay it that day." – unchanged; "that day" can
   attach to "pay". This is the thesis.
2. "Held to its end, each pays out what its Treasuries repay, whatever markets do" – unchanged; "each" and "its
   Treasuries" send the reader back to work out which holding owns which bonds.
3. "Most of the rest buys more of the late-2032 fund that pays her first January, and that extra sets her minimum
   facility contribution." – "the late-2032 fund" has still never been introduced, and "that extra" is still a coined
   noun. It comes back in para 4 as "Its bottom is that extra in the 2032 fund".
4. "Only what's left, roughly the interest it will earn…" – "it" = the extra holding or the whole fund?
5. "But it is the last money Laura adds, so a crash in 2029 or 2030 can shrink the top of her range but never its
   bottom." – new. The concession is good, but "it" is ambiguous (the deposit? the minimum?) and the "so" skips a step:
   the bottom is safe because it sits in Treasuries, not because it is the last money she adds. A reader feels the logic
   jump.
6. "The ladder's weakness is that its bonds pay interest, and two repay months early, so some money waits for its
   January, and if rates fall and stay low it earns less than planned." – unchanged; four clauses on "and/so/and", a
   personified "its January", and a final "it".
7. "From 2033 the ladder and that set-aside become her operating reserve" – "that set-aside" now has no antecedent (para
   5 moves money "into Treasuries" but never calls it a set-aside). And since the case defines the operating reserve *as*
   the set-aside, "the ladder and that set-aside become her operating reserve" reads circular. Name the money: the
   Treasuries moved to cover the shortfall.
