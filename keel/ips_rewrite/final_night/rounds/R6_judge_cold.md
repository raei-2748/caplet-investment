## The strategy and its biggest strength, as I understood them
**Strategy.** Every amount Laura commits to is bought in Treasuries on the day she commits: the ten $50k payments with
her first $300k (2027), the minimum facility contribution with most of her 2028 deposit, and both ends of the 2031 range
by moving half her shares into Treasuries before she names it. Only money nobody is relying on (roughly the interest on
the 2028 Treasuries) sits in a world share fund.
**Biggest strength (as the draft states it).** By 2033 nothing a resident or a co-sponsor relies on depends on a share
price, so she can be as sure of landing inside her range as she is of Treasuries.

## Paragraph by paragraph, after one read, in my words

- **Pitch.** She buys Treasuries for each amount the day she commits to it. The $300k (topped up in 2028 if needed) buys
  the ten payments; the 2028 money buys the minimum facility gift, its interest goes into shares, and the range is covered
  before she says it. *Clear except s3's start (below).*
- **P1.** Value a fixed payment at the Treasury cost. Valuing it at share returns makes it look cheaper only because it
  counts growth that hasn't happened; US pensions did that and took on more risk. *Clear now. The pension sentence works
  this round: "it" follows "that growth".*
- **P2.** The $300k buys the ladder, one holding maturing before each payment 2033-2042. If it costs more than $300k, the
  2028 money covers the difference. Held to the end, each pays out regardless of markets, so "high certainty" = no payment
  depends on shares. Caplet XR = ten holdings, redemption. *Clear, with one stop.*
- **P3.** Most of the rest of the 2028 money buys more of the late-2032 fund; that extra is the minimum facility gift. The
  rest (about its interest) goes into a world fund, kept small because her income moves with the economy. *Understood,
  with the same stop as R5.*
- **P4.** 2031 range: bottom = that extra, rounded down; top = plus half her shares' value, moved into Treasuries first.
  So she's as sure of landing in the range as she is of Treasuries. Other half stays hers. *Clear. The new
  "as confident ... as she is of Treasuries" sentence is the clearest statement of the payoff in any round.*
- **P5.** Weakness: some money waits for its January (bond interest, and bonds that repay early), and low rates mean it
  earns less. Before 2031 they assume it earns nothing and cover that from her shares, then from the 2032 fund, so the
  range starts lower. *Understood, with one stop.*
- **P6.** By 2033 nothing anyone relies on depends on shares. The ladder plus the money set aside for that shortfall is the
  reserve, paying one January at a time to 2042. *Clear. The "set-aside" stop is fixed.*

counting on. Her $300k buys a ladder for the ten $50k payments, and her 2028 $150k tops that up if needed, then adds to the
