Under Caplet XR, every amount Laura commits to is held in Treasuries before anyone relies on it. The R stands for redemption in both senses, a bond repaid and a promise kept, and the X for the ten operating payments of $50,000 that come first, because she cannot count on co-sponsors to fund them. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys one Treasury holding for each payment, redeemed in the year before it falls due, and their interest makes up the rest of the $500,000.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave more for the facility. But the payments, owed whatever markets do, would take most of her money, so every loss would fall on the thin slice left over, and that slice would swing harder than the share market, as a company's shares do when it borrows. With every payment held to maturity in Treasuries, the money for the facility carries no such gearing, and a high degree of certainty means that no payment rests on a share price or on selling a bond before it matures.

Laura has taken thoughtful risks all her career, but her first $300,000 is spoken for, so her room for risk arrives with the 2028 deposit. That deposit first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, if bond prices rose before January 2027. Most of the rest buys more of the fund that pays the 2033 payment, and that extra payout, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed, becomes the bottom of the range she names in 2031. Only what is left, about the interest the extra holding will earn by 2032, goes into a world index fund, so even if every share were lost, the extra holding alone would pay back about what she put into both. Securing the bottom three years early gives up that money's share growth, so that a slump before 2031, when co-sponsors may also give less, falls on the top.

In 2031, before naming a range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries maturing before 2033, and the top is the bottom plus that half. A top left in shares could fall below what she had told co-sponsors and cost her their trust, while a rise would only add to what she keeps.

What could still fall short is the money that arrives before its payment, mostly interest, which waits in a short-term Treasury fund and may earn less than the payments need if rates fall and stay low. So the other half of her shares first buys enough Treasuries to cover that shortfall, priced as if the waiting money earned nothing, the one rate it cannot fall below. If the other half can't pay for it all, the top comes down, and the bottom only if the top runs out, so no payment ever waits on shares. A good market before 2031 raises the top and a bad one lowers it. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where in it will be her decision in 2033, not the market's.

Payments fixed in dollars are untouched by inflation, but the facility's costs in Taiwan are not, so she commits only half her shares. Whatever remains of the other half is her financial flexibility, promised to no one and sellable on any trading day. From 2033 her operating reserve is everything still waiting for a payment, and it changes only as each January's payment leaves it, until the tenth redemption pays the last in January 2042.
