## 2. The biggest strength: stated, argued, priced?
The strength is that no number anyone relies on is a forecast. Every number is a holding she owns, so the confidence
the case asks for (lines 117-118) comes from ownership, not from odds.
- **Stated:** yes, in P1's first sentence.
- **Argued:** for the payments, yes. For the range, half: the bottom has no reason. The draft never draws the
  contrast that turns timidity into strength: a forecast range inherits the forecast's overconfidence, and an owned
  range can't. So "Barring a US default" reads as an assertion.
- **Priced:** once, in the wrong currency. "Richer by 2042" prices the payments. The facility side's price is never
  stated: only interest is risked, the promised half loses two years of growth, and the zero-rate cover can eat the
  kept half. Nor is it said why the price is worth paying: the case makes a broken number costlier than a modest one
  (lines 114-115), and Treasuries already pay what the payments need, so their certainty is nearly free.
