# R09 cold read (reviewer D)

5 Nov 2026. I came to this as a first-time reader. I had read the client case, `R09_pitch.txt` and `R09_ips.txt`, and nothing else from the team.

Marks: **CLEAR** = understood on one reading. **SLOWED** = I had to reread or guess. **LOST** = I could not tell what it meant, or two readings were possible.

## Pitch

**P1.** "Under Caplet XR, Laura never names a number she doesn't already own."
- Means: the plan is called Caplet XR, and its rule is that Laura only promises an amount she already has the money for.
- **SLOWED.** "Caplet XR" is the first word and nothing says it is the plan's name (it could be a fund or a product). "Names a number": to whom, and for what? The case's 2031 co-sponsor range is the likely meaning, but the pitch never mentions co-sponsors. "Own a number": you own money or bonds, not numbers, so I had to translate the metaphor. It reads as a motto, not as something a person would say.

**P2.** "Her first $300,000 buys Treasuries maturing before each of her ten $50,000 operating payments."
- Means: in 2027 the $300,000 buys US government bonds, timed so one comes due just before each $50,000 payment.
- **CLEAR.** Minor: "maturing before each of" doesn't strictly say one bond per payment, but that is the natural reading.

**P3.** "Her 2028 deposit buys Treasuries for her minimum facility contribution and puts only their interest into world shares, which can raise it."
- Means: the 2028 $150,000 buys bonds to cover the smallest amount she will give to the building, and an amount equal to those bonds' interest goes into a global stock fund that could make the gift bigger.
- **LOST.**
  - "Puts only their interest into world shares" has two readings: the interest goes into shares as it is paid over the years, or an amount equal to the expected interest goes in on day one. The plan (paragraph 3) means the second.
  - "Which can raise it": "which" could be the world shares or the interest, and "it" could be the minimum contribution, the deposit or the interest.
  - "Minimum facility contribution": no minimum has been explained, so it is the minimum of what?
  - Here the 2028 purchase is "Treasuries", but in the plan it is "an iBonds fund". I can't tell whether these are the same thing.

## Plan

### Paragraph 1

**S1.** "The building Laura plans can look to co-sponsors for help, but its ten operating payments of $50,000 cannot, so Caplet XR pays for those first."
- Means: co-sponsors can help pay for the building, but the ten $50,000 running-cost payments can't come from them, so the plan pays for those first.
- **SLOWED.**
  - The building is the one doing the "looking".
  - "Its ten operating payments": "its" means the building's, but in the case these are Laura's payments.
  - "Cannot" only makes sense once I filled back in "cannot look to co-sponsors".
  - "The building" is a third name for the place: the case says residency and facility, and the plan never uses the word "residency" at all.
  - "Caplet XR" is still unexplained.

**S2.** "In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys ten Treasury holdings, each redeemed, or repaid by the US government, in the year before the January it pays for."
- Means: in January 2027 the $300,000 buys ten government bonds. The government repays each one in the year before the January payment it is meant to fund.
- **SLOWED.**
  - "Each redeemed, or repaid by the US government": on first reading, "redeemed or repaid" looked like two alternatives, not "redeemed, which means repaid".
  - "In the year before the January it pays for": I had to reread it to work out that "it" is the holding, and that a holding repaid in 2032 pays January 2033.
  - "Treasury holdings" is another new name (the pitch said "Treasuries").
  - This sentence contradicts S18 (see there).

**S3.** "The plan is named for those ten redemptions (X is the Roman ten), and it holds to one rule from then on: Laura never names a number she does not already own."
- Means: the X in the name is the Roman numeral ten, for the ten repayments, and from 2027 on the plan has one rule: Laura never promises an amount she doesn't already hold.
- **SLOWED.**
  - "Named for those ten redemptions" only explains the X. The R is never explained (I guessed "redemption", but the text doesn't say), and "Caplet" is never explained.
  - "From then on": from when? January 2027, presumably.
  - "Names a number" still doesn't say to whom. That only becomes clear in paragraph 4 ("before naming the range").
  - This is the pitch's motto again, word for word, and the second time it reads as a slogan.

### Paragraph 2

**S4.** "Shares nearly always beat Treasuries over ten years, but the payments have no use for the extra, since at this autumn's yields $300,000 is about what the ten holdings cost."
- Means: stocks usually earn more than government bonds over ten years, but the payments don't need the extra growth, because right now $300,000 is roughly what the ten bonds cost.
- **SLOWED.**
  - "The payments have no use for the extra": I had to stop to see the link. The deposit already buys the payments, so extra return does nothing for them.
  - "This autumn's yields": which autumn? "Yields" is never explained, and a reader who doesn't know that a bond's price depends on its yield won't see why yields set the cost.
  - "About": more or less than $300,000? It matters, because S7 is about it being more.
  - "Shares nearly always beat Treasuries over ten years" has no source, and a first reader asks "says who?".

**S5.** "Worse, the payments are fixed, so they take their $50,000 first whatever markets do and every loss lands on the money left for the building; with shares mixed in beside them, that money would swing harder than the share market itself."
- Means: also, the payments are fixed and come out first no matter what, so any loss falls on whatever is left for the building. If shares were mixed in with the payment money, that leftover would rise and fall more than the stock market itself.
- **LOST.**
  - "Worse": worse than what? S4 gave a reason, not a problem, so the step isn't signposted.
  - "With shares mixed in beside them": "them" could be the payments or the ten holdings. Mixed in where: inside the $300,000, or across the whole portfolio?
  - "Would swing harder than the share market itself" is the sentence's main claim, and it gives no reason. On one reading I could not work out why the leftover would swing more than the market. (The fixed payments act like borrowed money, but the reader has to supply that.) I can repeat the claim, but I could not explain it.

**S6.** "So we take a high degree of certainty to mean that no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or an early sale, and a payment could fail only if the US government missed one of its own."
- Means: the team's definition of "high certainty" is that no payment relies on a stock price, a prediction or having to sell something early. The only way a payment fails is if the US government misses one of its own debt payments.
- **CLEAR.** Minor: "an early sale" leaves the reader to work out a sale of what (a bond before it comes due). "Missed one of its own" took half a beat to read "one" as "a payment".

### Paragraph 3

**S7.** "When the 2028 deposit arrives, it first tops up the ten holdings if falling yields made them cost more than $300,000."
- Means: when the $150,000 arrives in 2028, part of it first covers any extra cost of the ten bonds, if falling rates made them cost more than $300,000.
- **LOST.**
  - S2 said the $300,000 already bought all ten holdings in January 2027. So how can bonds she already owns "cost more" in 2028? There are two readings: (a) some holdings couldn't be bought in 2027 because the price had gone above $300,000, and 2028 buys the missing ones; (b) money is added to holdings she already owns. Only (a) makes sense, but nothing says so.
  - "Falling yields made them cost more" assumes the reader knows that bond prices rise when yields fall.
  - The price rise it refers to happens before the January 2027 purchase, so the timeline runs backwards here.

**S8.** "Most of the rest buys an iBonds fund maturing in late 2032, priced to grow back to roughly the whole deposit by then, and the slice left over, about the interest that fund will earn, goes into a world index fund."
- Means: most of what's left buys a bond fund that ends in late 2032, bought so that it grows to about the full $150,000 by then. The small remainder, roughly what that fund will earn in interest, goes into a global stock index fund.
- **SLOWED.**
  - "iBonds fund" is never explained. A general reader may confuse it with US savings "I bonds", and a fund that "matures" is unusual to non-specialists.
  - The pitch called this purchase "Treasuries". Is an iBonds fund Treasuries? The plan doesn't say.
  - "Grow back" suggests something was lost first.
  - "The whole deposit": the whole $150,000, even after part of it went on the top-up in S7?
  - "The interest that fund will earn" is the first of three different "interests" (see S14).
  - Two side-clauses packed into one long sentence.

**S9.** "Treasuries alone would leave her a larger sure sum, but shares are the only part of her money that can grow with building costs in Taiwan, and the worst they can do is hand back her deposit."
- Means: putting all of the 2028 money into government bonds would give a bigger guaranteed amount, but stocks are the only part that can keep up with rising building costs in Taiwan, and at worst they leave her with her deposit back.
- **LOST.**
  - "The worst they can do": grammatically "they" is the shares, but the worst shares can do is fall to zero, not "hand back her deposit". The reading that works is that the fund and the shares together can at worst return about the deposit, but then "they" isn't the shares. That leaves two readings, and one of them is false.
  - "A larger sure sum": larger than what? I worked out that it means larger than the roughly $150,000 the fund grows to.
  - "Hand back her deposit" doesn't fit S10's "a fund's payout is not fixed".

### Paragraph 4

**S10.** "In 2031 the bottom of her range is what the 2032 fund will pay, rounded down, since a fund's payout is not fixed."
- Means: when she approaches co-sponsors in 2031, the low end of her range is the expected payout of the 2032 bond fund, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't guaranteed.
- **CLEAR.** This is the first time the plan says "range", and I only understood it because the case explains the 2031 range. It also sits uneasily with the motto: if the payout isn't fixed, she doesn't quite "own" that number.

**S11.** "For the top, she moves half of her shares into Treasuries before naming the range, so a crash in 2032 can't leave her short of a figure co-sponsors have heard."
- Means: to set the high end, she sells half her stocks and buys government bonds before announcing the range, so a 2032 crash can't push her below the figure co-sponsors heard.
- **LOST.**
  - "For the top": the sentence says what she does but never says what the top number is. There are two readings: (a) top = bottom + the value of the half she moved; (b) top = bottom + all her shares, with the moved half as a cushion. S12 points to (a), but nothing states it.
  - "A crash in 2032": why only 2032, when the gap between naming the range (2031) and giving (2033) is longer than that?
  - "Half of her shares": S16 then moves more out of the shares, so "half" no longer means half.

**S12.** "With both ends owned on the day she says them, she can give the range with near-complete confidence."
- Means: because the low and high figures are both already backed by bonds when she announces them, she can be almost certain the gift lands in the range.
- **CLEAR.** But "near-complete confidence" is not a number, and the case asks teams to state how confident they are. It also raised a question I couldn't answer: if she already owns the top on the day she names it, what makes the gift land anywhere below the top? Why is it a range at all? (S16 partly answers this, but only later.) "Both ends owned" is the motto again.

**S13.** "Her best argument to co-sponsors is the payments: ten years of running costs are already bought, so the building they help pay for won't stand empty."
- Means: the strongest thing she can tell co-sponsors is that ten years of running costs are already paid for, so the building they help fund will actually run.
- **CLEAR.** This is the most human line in the plan. "They" is clearly the co-sponsors.

### Paragraph 5

**S14.** "The weak point is interest."
- Means: the plan's weak spot has something to do with interest.
- **SLOWED.** Which interest? The pitch's "their interest" was the 2028 bonds' interest, and S8's "the interest that fund will earn" was the 2032 fund's interest. Only the next sentence shows that this is the interest paid by the ten 2027 holdings, so I had to read on to know. It is a short set-up line that reads as a device.

**S15.** "The holdings pay it along the way, and it must be reinvested until its payment is due, so a lasting fall in rates leaves it short."
- Means: the ten bonds pay interest before they come due. That interest has to be reinvested until the payment it is meant for, and if rates drop and stay down, it won't grow enough.
- **LOST.**
  - There are four pronouns ("it", "it", "its", "it"), and their nouns shift: "pay it" (the interest), "it must be reinvested" (the interest), "its payment" (the interest's payment? the holding's? which payment?), "leaves it short" (the interest? the payment? the plan?).
  - A key fact arrives late. S2 led me to think each holding pays its $50,000 on its own. Only here do I learn that the payments depend on reinvesting interest. That means S6's definition of "high certainty" has an exception it never mentioned.
  - "Rates" here and "yields" in S4 and S7: same thing? The plan doesn't say.

**S16.** "Before she names her range, we work out that gap as if the waiting money earned nothing and move that much from her shares into Treasuries; if her shares can't cover it, the range is smaller."
- Means: in 2031, before she announces the range, the team works out the shortfall assuming the reinvested interest earns nothing, and moves that amount from stocks into bonds. If the stocks aren't worth enough, she announces a smaller range.
- **SLOWED.**
  - "That gap" is a new word for S15's "short", and "the waiting money" is a new label for the reinvested interest, used only once.
  - "The range is smaller": which end moves? A lower top, a lower bottom, or both?
  - "Her shares": which half? The half already moved for the top in S11, or the other half that S17 keeps?
  - This is a 2031 step, but S12 has already said she gives the range with near-complete confidence. I only learn about a step before the range is named after the range has been named.

### Paragraph 6

**S17.** "The other half of her shares is never mentioned to co-sponsors, because every overrun would become a claim on it, and it stays her flexibility if the building costs more."
- Means: the remaining half of the stocks stays out of her talks with co-sponsors, because otherwise every cost overrun would be charged to it. It stays as her cushion if the building costs more.
- **SLOWED.**
  - "Overrun" comes before "if the building costs more" explains it, later in the same sentence.
  - "It stays her flexibility" uses "flexibility" as a name for money. It is the case's word, but still a jump.
  - "The other half": after S16 moves the gap out of her shares, is it still half?
  - On first reading, "never mentioned to co-sponsors" can sound like hiding money from the people she is asking for money. That is a credibility worry in a plan built on credibility.

**S18.** "From 2033 the ten holdings are her operating reserve, and nothing changes because markets move: each January one is redeemed and pays that year's $50,000, until in January 2042 the tenth redemption pays the last."
- Means: from 2033 the ten bonds are her operating reserve and are not traded when markets move. Each January one is repaid and covers that year's $50,000, until the tenth covers the last payment in January 2042.
- **SLOWED.**
  - S2 said each holding is redeemed "in the year before the January it pays for". This sentence says "each January one is redeemed". Which is it?
  - "Nothing changes because markets move" can first be read as "nothing changes, because markets move" (a cause) before the intended "market moves don't change anything".
  - If the first holding is repaid in 2032 (as S2 says), then at the start of 2033 the reserve is nine holdings plus cash, not "the ten holdings".
  - It ends neatly, echoing "XR" and "redemptions".

## Patterns across sentences

**Terms used before they are explained**
- "Caplet XR": pitch P1 and plan S1. S3 explains the X, but never the R or "Caplet".
- "Minimum facility contribution" (P3): never defined in the pitch. In the plan it becomes "the bottom of her range" (S10).
- "iBonds fund" (S8): never explained.
- "Yields" (S4, S7): never explained, and "rates" (S15) may or may not be the same thing.
- "Overrun" (S17), "that gap" and "the waiting money" (S16).
- The fact that interest must be reinvested, which S2 to S6 depend on, only appears at S15.

**One thing, many names**
- The 2027 bonds: Treasuries / ten Treasury holdings / the ten holdings / the holdings / redemptions / operating reserve.
- The 2028 bond purchase: "Treasuries for her minimum facility contribution" (pitch) / an iBonds fund maturing in late 2032 / that fund / the 2032 fund / a fund.
- The stocks: world shares / shares / a world index fund / her shares.
- The place: facility (pitch) / the building (plan). The case's own word, "residency", appears nowhere.
- Interest: three different interests (the 2028 bonds' in P3, the 2032 fund's in S8, the 2027 holdings' in S14 and S15).

**Pronouns whose noun is unclear**
- P3 "which can raise it".
- S1 "its" (the building's payments).
- S5 "beside them".
- S9 "they" (the shares, which can't "hand back her deposit").
- S15 "it" four times.
- S16 "it" (the gap, or the range?).
- S18 "one" is fine.

**Years out of order**
- S7 (2028) refers to a price rise that happens before the January 2027 purchase, which S2 described as already done.
- Paragraph 4 runs 2031 → 2032 → 2033 onwards. Paragraph 5 then goes back to a 2031 step ("Before she names her range") after S12 has already named the range.
- S2 and S18 disagree on when each holding is redeemed (the year before, or each January).
- "This autumn" (S4) is a relative date: autumn 2026 for an American reader, spring in Sydney.
- Otherwise the order 2027 → 2028 → 2031 → 2033–2042 holds.

**Lines that sound machine-written or like a slogan**
1. "Laura never names a number she doesn't already own." It appears in the pitch (P1), again in S3, and echoes in S12 ("both ends owned"). It is the clearest slogan.
2. "(X is the Roman ten)": reads like a branding aside.
3. "The weak point is interest.": a set-up line.
4. "the worst they can do is hand back her deposit": a reassuring line to end on, and false if "they" means the shares.
5. "until in January 2042 the tenth redemption pays the last.": a neat ending that circles back to the name.
6. The habit of glossing in mid-sentence, such as "each redeemed, or repaid by the US government," and "the slice left over, about the interest that fund will earn,". It packs two ideas into one sentence, and it causes most of the SLOWED marks.

These lines sound like a person: "every loss lands on the money left for the building"; "every overrun would become a claim on it"; "so the building they help pay for won't stand empty".

**What a first reader would still ask**
- The plan gives no dollar figures for the range or the gift, and no number for confidence.
- It never says plainly what she actually hands over in 2033.

## Verdict

**1. What the plan does, as I'd explain it to a friend.** Laura's first $300,000 buys ten US government bonds, one coming due just before each of her ten $50,000 yearly payments from 2033 to 2042, so those payments are safe whatever the stock market does. Her second deposit in 2028 mostly buys a bond fund that should grow back to about $150,000 by late 2032, which is the least she can give towards the building, and a small slice goes into a global stock fund that could make the gift bigger. In 2031, before telling co-sponsors her range, she moves half the stocks into bonds (plus enough to cover any shortfall in bond interest) so she never promises more than she has, and quietly keeps the other half for cost overruns.

**2. Biggest strength.** The payment side is airtight and easy to picture: ten government bonds, one per payment, bought on day one. Its definition of "high certainty" is one a reader can test ("no payment depends on a share price, a forecast or an early sale"). It also gives the best line for co-sponsors: the building they help pay for won't stand empty.

**3. The one thing I still don't understand.** How the top of the range is set, and what decides where in the range the 2033 gift lands. The plan says she already owns both ends when she names them, so the gift should simply be the top unless the interest gap eats into it. In that case I can't tell whether the range is a real range or a floor and ceiling she expects to hit.

**4. Person or machine.** Mostly a person: a sharp writer packing too much into each sentence. The motto pulls parts of it towards branding copy. The deciding line was S13: "ten years of running costs are already bought, so the building they help pay for won't stand empty." It is a concrete, ordinary image, the kind a person reaches for. The line that pulls hardest the other way is "Laura never names a number she does not already own", which appears twice and reads as a tagline.
