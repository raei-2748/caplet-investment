Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments work like a debt: fixed in dollars, owed whatever markets do, and payable only from her portfolio. Caplet XR meets them with Treasuries bought years ahead, and lets her tell co-sponsors only figures she already holds in Treasuries.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042, especially in strong markets, and that is what this plan gives up. But at today's Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, so in that mix her facility contribution would be whatever is left after a fixed claim. Like the shares of a company that has borrowed heavily, it would swing far harder than the market beneath it.

So in January 2027 her first $300,000 buys a Treasury bond or fund for each payment, repaid in the year before it falls due, with the 2028 deposit topping up any shortfall. We take a high degree of certainty to mean that, barring a US default, no payment depends on a share price or on selling anything early. Each is met by a holding that is simply redeemed, and those redemptions are the R in Caplet XR, the ten holdings its X. All ten lend only to the US government, since other borrowers would add ways to miss a payment while leaving that risk in place.

Laura has been willing to take thoughtful risks throughout her career, and her earnings from it, which pay her living costs and fund the 2028 deposit, can shrink in the same slump that sinks shares. So her portfolio holds few shares. Most of the deposit buys an extra holding in the fund behind the 2033 payment, and she counts only its payout, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed, as the bottom of the range she names in 2031. A world index fund gets about what that extra holding will earn by 2032, so if every share were lost, she would still get back about all she put into the extra holding and the shares.

The ladder counts on the cash it pays out early, its interest and the holdings repaid months before their January, earning short-term rates until each payment. If those rates fall and stay low, the payments could come up short. So before naming a range in 2031, Laura sells half of her shares for Treasuries, and the top of the range is the bottom plus what that half fetched. Leaving the top in shares would gear the range just as the balanced mix geared her contribution. From the other half she sells only enough to buy Treasuries covering the shortfall as if the waiting cash earned nothing, and if that half can't pay for them, money meant for the range does, so she names a lower one.

After that, no market can move her outside the range. She will give at least the bottom and at most the top, and the building's cost in Taiwan by 2033 decides where. Anything she doesn't give stays hers, with any cover the shortfall never needed and the shares she kept, sellable any day. That is her financial flexibility, left unpromised because the project will need things no one can name in 2031.

In 2033 she sets aside the operating reserve without a trade, because it is the Treasuries still waiting for a payment, enough for all ten. It shrinks by one redemption a year until the tenth and the interest saved for it meet the last payment.
