Read only `rounds/blind/R6_pack.txt` (546 words in the pack) and `keel/WHARTON_WORD.md`. Same persona as R5: an
equity-minded industry judge who suspects a Treasury-heavy plan is too timid for a client "willing to take
thoughtful risks". Scored only on Wharton's five criteria.

## After one read

- **The strategy:** every sum Laura commits to is bought in Treasuries on the day she commits to it. That means the
  ten payments in 2027, a minimum facility contribution in 2028 and both ends of the 2031 range. Shares get only
  roughly the interest on the minimum, and half of them are kept back, promised to no one.
- **Its biggest strength:** by 2033 nothing a resident or a co-sponsor relies on depends on a share price, so the range
  Laura names in 2031 is as reliable as the Treasuries behind it. The page now says this outright: "she can be as
  confident of landing inside it as she is of Treasuries."

## Is my R5 attack answered? No.

The R5 attack: nothing in the case makes Laura commit to a minimum in 2028, since co-sponsors need a number only in
2031. Between 2028 and 2031 the facility money is exactly the "money nobody else is counting on" that this page's
own logic would put in shares.

R6 still gives no reason for 2028. The only defence of keeping shares small is still "her income can rise and fall
with the economy too", and by 2028 that income has already been deposited. The new confidence sentence makes the
range's *credibility* stronger, but it doesn't say why the bottom is locked three years before it is said, or what
locking it early costs her. So the page still reads as if early certainty was assumed rather than chosen.
