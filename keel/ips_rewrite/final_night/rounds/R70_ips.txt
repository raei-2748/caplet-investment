Caplet XR keeps every sum Laura commits to in Treasuries before anyone counts on it, starting with her ten $50,000 operating payments. They're fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. We repay it with ten Treasury holdings, the X in the name, each kept until its redemption, the R. Her first $300,000 buys all it can, and the 2028 deposit buys any it can't. Five are funds of one year's Treasuries, which pay out what their holdings are worth, not a fixed sum.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get only what they left. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market, and she'd have to put a range on it in 2031.

In 2028 Laura buys an extra stake in the fund behind the 2033 payment, sized so its December 2032 payout matches what's left of that deposit. Since that payout isn't fixed, the credible range she gives co-sponsors starts a little under it. Because the stake costs less than it pays out, she can put the difference into a world index fund of thousands of companies and still get that sum back. Buying it in 2028 means the bottom is held in Treasuries, not shares, through years when a slump could also shrink co-sponsors' giving.

A Treasury held to redemption still carries a risk. Its interest, its repayment and the bonds inside each fund all pay out before the payment they're meant for. Years of near-zero rates on that waiting money would leave the payments short, so the plan assumes every waiting dollar earns nothing. In 2031, before she names a range, she sells up to half her shares for the extra Treasuries that assumption calls for. When that half falls short, the stake behind the bottom pays the rest and the whole range moves down, so no payment depends on how shares do.

The other half sets the top. She sells it for Treasuries the same day, and the top is the bottom plus what it fetched, so no later fall in shares can cost her credibility. A rise before then lifts the top. Barring a US default and with both deposits made, falling markets and rates land on the range, not the payments.

In 2033 her operating reserve needs setting aside only on paper, since the Treasuries held for the payments already make it up. She contributes the top, or less if the building in Taiwan needs less, which buildings seldom do. Since the project will change as it develops, anything else she holds, unsold shares included, stays out of every promise and can be sold any day. Once the final redemption meets the final payment in 2042, nothing she holds is spoken for.
