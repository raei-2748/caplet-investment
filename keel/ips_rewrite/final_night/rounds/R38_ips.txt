Under Caplet XR, every amount Laura commits to is held in Treasuries before anyone relies on it. The R stands for redemption, a bond repaid and a promise kept, and the X for the ten operating payments of $50,000 that come first, because she cannot count on co-sponsors to fund them. In January 2027 her first $300,000 buys one Treasury holding for each payment, redeemed in the year before it falls due, and their interest makes up the rest of the $500,000.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But the payments, fixed in dollars whatever markets do, would take most of her money, so every loss would fall on the thin slice for the facility, which would swing harder than the share market, as a company's shares do when it borrows. Matching each payment with a Treasury held to maturity removes that gearing, and we read a high degree of certainty as near-ironclad: no payment rests on a share price or on selling a bond before it matures.

Laura has taken thoughtful risks all her career, but her first $300,000 is spoken for, so her room for risk arrives with the 2028 deposit. It first buys any holding the first $300,000 couldn't afford, then an extra holding in the fund behind the 2033 payment. That extra holding's payout, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed, is the bottom of the range she names in 2031. Only the rest, about the interest it will earn by 2032, goes into a world index fund, so if every share were lost, the extra holding alone would pay back about what she put into both.

In 2031, before naming a range, Laura moves half of her shares into Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus that half. A top left in shares could prove out of reach, while a rise above it would only add to what she keeps.

Money that arrives before its payment, mostly interest, earns short-term rates while it waits, and if those rates fall and stay low, it may earn less than needed. So the other half of her shares first buys enough Treasuries to cover that shortfall, priced as if the waiting money earned nothing. If the other half can't pay for it all, the range comes down, top first, so no payment ever waits on shares. Barring a US default, her contribution will land in the range, and where in it is her decision in 2033, once she sees what inflation has done to building costs in Taiwan.

Whatever remains of the other half is her financial flexibility, kept out of the range because committing everything would limit it as the project develops, and sellable on any trading day. The operating reserve she sets aside in 2033 is everything still waiting for a payment, and it runs down one payment each January until the tenth redemption pays the last in 2042.
