Articulation is low because the IPS carries no team story (team ruling); the Final Report must carry it.

**PM.** Remembers: "before she names a range, she buys extra Treasuries, enough to meet every payment even if that money earned nothing". Doubts: "The ladder's one weakness is money that waits." Five rungs are funds, and the IPS itself says "a fund's payout isn't fixed"; nothing says the 2031 purchase covers that. She also sees wrong-way timing: falling rates make that purchase dearer just as recessions shrink the shares paying for it. The text handles the outcome without saying the two tend to coincide.

**Growth.** Remembers: "the one place the plan is allowed to be wrong". Doubts: "co-sponsors, grants and fees can shrink in the same recessions that hit shares". Plausible but asserted, yet it carries the whole cost the IPS concedes. Shares are a sliver, all sold in 2031; nothing grows after, and the money "promised to no one" has no stated home. For a client "willing to take thoughtful risks", that reads as a token risk.

**Professor.** Remembers: "every gain or loss would land on the last third, which would swing like a heavily borrowed company's shares": surplus risk explained without jargon. Doubts: "The rest, about what that stake will earn by late 2032, goes into one world index fund". Its most original idea (the 2028 money comes back whole even if every share goes to zero) is never stated. The certainty definition is true by construction; how it was evaluated belongs in the Final Report.

## Flags
1. **Contradiction.** "Locking in that bottom three years early" vs "comes out of the 2028 stake, and her range starts lower": the bottom isn't locked until 2031. A fixed version must say the stake is bought in 2028 but becomes a promise only in 2031, after the reinvestment purchase. That also eases a tension with TNA Note 3 ("stocks only set how far her gift rises above her promised minimum").
2. "Three years early": readers will count 2028 to 2033 (five). "One weakness" overclaims (see the PM).
3. **Unsaid consequence.** The funds bought in 2031 pay back more than they cost, so the gift almost always lands at the top. A judge who notices asks why it is a range. A fixed version must say which end she expects and what alone could push it lower.
4. **Pitch vs IPS.** The pitch says "topped up from the 2028 deposit" as a certainty, but the IPS makes it conditional ("any it can't afford"). "Buys Laura's commitments" and "half sold for its top" are hard to parse cold, and "its" could mean two things.
5. One holding, three names: "the fund behind the 2033 payment", "that stake", "the 2028 stake".
6. Wharton's don'ts: none broken. "About two thirds of her $450,000" matches the ladder cost on file (23 Oct, ~$297.6k).
7. TNA: no contradiction found.
