## The strategy and its biggest strength, as I understood them
**Strategy.** Whenever Laura commits to an amount, she buys Treasuries that repay it in time. Her $300k buys a ladder for the
ten $50k payments; her 2028 deposit fills any gap, then buys extra units of the late-2032 fund as the minimum facility
contribution, with only about their interest in a world share fund. Before naming the 2031 range she moves half her shares
into Treasuries, so both ends are owned; the other half stays hers as her financial flexibility.
**Biggest strength (as stated).** Markets can change how large a range she names, but never whether she meets a payment or
a number already named.

- **P5.** Weakness: interest and early repayments wait months for their January and earn less if rates stay low. Before 2031

- **P6.** Strength: markets change the range's size, never whether she meets a payment or a named number. From 2033 the

relying on. Her $300k buys a ladder for the ten $50k payments; her 2028 $150k fills any gap and buys the minimum facility
contribution in a late-2032 Treasury fund, with roughly its interest in a world share fund, because the residency needs her
part to be the money that doesn't shrink in a recession. Before naming the 2031 range she moves half her shares into
Treasuries, so markets can change how big the range is but never whether she meets it, and the other half is her financial
flexibility.
