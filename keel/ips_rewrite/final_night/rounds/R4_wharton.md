## Scores: 40 / 50 (R2: 39, and R2 was over the limit)

**1. Investment Strategy – 8/10.** The thesis is still one repeatable reason ("money leaves shares on the day
somebody starts counting on it", priced at "the price of Treasuries that will pay it on its date"), and every dated
move follows from it: 2027 ladder, 2028 deposit, 2031 range, 2033-42 redemptions. Planning across the three phases
is disciplined and dated. **Lost ground on diversification:** R2's residency-level argument ("the part of the
residency's money that doesn't shrink with the rest") is gone, and what is left is "one world index fund holding
thousands of companies". A judge looking for "appropriate diversification" finds a single line about the share fund.
Nothing says why holding almost nothing but Treasuries, all from one issuer, is the *appropriate* diversification
for dollar payments on fixed dates. That reasoning exists in the plan but not on the page.

**2. Client Knowledge – 8/10.** Fixed nominal payments lead to Treasuries; the 2028 money's source ("book advances,
speaking fees and licensing") leads to a small share fund; the 2031 credibility risk leads to buying before naming;
flexibility is "promised to no one". "Willing to take thoughtful risks" is still only answered indirectly ("the one
place the plan is allowed to be wrong"). Better than R2 because that sentence now reads as a choice, not a defence.

**3. Portfolio Analysis – 8/10.** Concepts are used, not named: liability cost at the Treasury price and the
public-pension counter-example; time does not rescue a dated payment ("A patient investor can wait out a bad decade.
A January cannot." – the best line in the draft); the ladder's reinvestment weakness with a rule and an outcome.
Certainty is defined (C1). C3 is now implicitly covered: because the top is "half of what her shares are then
worth", a bad share market lowers the top and a good one raises it, while the bottom doesn't move. But a judge has to
work that out; the draft never says it.

**4. Competition Experience – 7/10.** Choices refused and why (no early sales, shares kept small). No team history,
correctly. About as much as an IPS can carry.

**5. Creativity and Presentation – 9/10.** The original thinking is clear and retellable: one pricing rule applied
on three dates, and a range owned at both ends before it is said. The voice is plainer than R2 and mostly clear on
one reading. One sentence still needs a second pass: "its bonds pay interest before their January arrives, and if
rates fall and stay low, that waiting money earns less than we planned". The close lands on a date, not a slogan.

## The "final range" question (Chief of Staff's test)
**Clean, X3 not breached.** The IPS gives a *rule* for each end of the range (bottom = the late-2032 fund's payout
rounded down; top = plus half the shares' value, moved into Treasuries first), with no dollar figures and no
confidence level. That is "determining a responsible facility contribution" (S3) as a framework, which is what the
instructions ask the IPS to establish. A final range would be "$X to $Y with Z% confidence"; that stays in the Final
Report. Two small cautions, neither a breach: (a) "both ends of Laura's range are bought" in the pitch and the body
is a claim about the mechanism, so it is fine as long as no figure follows; (b) the IPS no longer says what she
actually *contributes* in 2033 (R2 had "paid from the 2032 fund and the shares she moved"). A judge can infer it,
but S3 is cleaner if the 2033 decision is said in one clause.

## The single change that would lift the total most
Answer diversification and liquidity in the plan's own logic, in one or two sentences, probably in the 2028
paragraph. Say why the money that has a date is spread across dates rather than across asset types: ten
maturities, one issuer, on purpose, because the bills are in dollars. Then say the only money that needs to be
liquid is the kept half, since no payment ever needs a sale. Bring back R2's residency-level point if it fits:
her money is the part of the residency's funding that doesn't shrink in a recession. That moves criterion 1 and
Q3/M3/S5 together. Room for it: the reinvestment-weakness explanation can lose its hardest clause, and "This is the
plan's real strength" can carry one fewer idea.
