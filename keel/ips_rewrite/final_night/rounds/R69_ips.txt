Caplet XR keeps every sum Laura commits to in Treasuries before anyone counts on it, starting with her ten $50,000 operating payments. They're fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. We repay the debt with ten Treasury holdings, the X in the name, each kept until its redemption, the R. Her first $300,000 buys all it can, and the 2028 deposit buys the rest. Five are funds, each holding one year's Treasuries, since no Treasury she may buy matures in those years.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get only what they left. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market, and it's the money she'd have to put a range on in 2031.

In 2028 Laura buys an extra stake in the fund behind the 2033 payment, sized so its December 2032 payout matches what's left of that deposit. Since that payout isn't fixed, the credible range she gives co-sponsors starts a little under it. Because the stake costs less than it pays out, she can put the difference into a world index fund of thousands of companies and still get that sum back. Bought in 2028, the stake keeps the bottom clear of any share slump that could also shrink co-sponsors' giving and her earnings.

A Treasury held to redemption still carries a risk. Its interest and early repayment wait in a short-term Treasury fund, though the holdings were priced as if that money earns today's rates, and those rates can fall to nothing for years. So the plan assumes it earns nothing. In 2031, before she names a range, she sells up to half her shares for the extra Treasuries that assumption calls for. If that half falls short, the stake behind the bottom pays the rest, and the whole range moves down before anyone hears it.

The other half sets the top. She sells it for Treasuries that same day, so both ends are owned before she says a word and no later fall can cost her credibility. A rise before then lifts the top. Barring a US default and with both deposits made, falling markets and rates land on the range, not the payments, which is what we mean by a high degree of certainty.

In 2033 her operating reserve needs setting aside only on paper, since the Treasuries held for the payments already make it up. She contributes the top, or less if the building in Taiwan needs less, which buildings seldom do. Since the project will change as it develops, everything beyond her contribution stays out of every promise and can be sold any day. Once the final redemption meets the final payment in 2042, nothing she holds is spoken for.
