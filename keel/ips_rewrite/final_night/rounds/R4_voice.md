## Overall
Much more human than V57/V58: the case summary is gone, the first sentence carries the strategy, and most sentences
read first time. The voice is now plain, but a little flat in the middle (paragraphs 3-5 are mechanism with no opponent)
and three code sentences remain.

## Sentences a smart adult won't get first time
1. "Cost it at what shares usually earn and it looks cheaper, but the saving is a return nobody has earned yet" – "the
   saving" is a finance step the reader has to reconstruct (lower cost because of a higher discount rate). The mechanism
   needs one more clause before the punch.
2. "if rates leave it short, her 2028 deposit finishes it" – two "it"s; "leave it short" is trader shorthand. Name the
   ladder, and say why rates could make $300,000 not enough.
3. "Its top adds half of what her shares are then worth" – "then" carries too much; the reader has to work out that the
   shares are valued in 2031.
4. "we work out the gap as if every waiting dollar earned nothing" – "the gap" and "waiting dollar" are both coined;
   a cold reader won't know which gap. Say what money is waiting and for what.
5. "The first is the operating commitment." – "the first" what? It points back to "each sum" three sentences earlier.

- **Kickers** (count: four, one over budget): "A patient investor can wait out a bad decade. A January cannot.";
  "So both ends of Laura's range are bought before anyone hears it."; "None is sold early, even for the facility.";
  "That fund is the one place the plan is allowed to be wrong" (Ray's own TNA line – keep this one). Cut or flatten one.
- **Signpost**: "This is the plan's real strength:" – announces instead of argues. Also makes the closing paragraph do
  two jobs (strength claim + 2033 mechanics).
- **Coined nouns**: "that set-aside", "kept shares", "waiting money", "waiting dollar".
- **Repetition of the slogan**: the pitch's first line and the IPS's first line are the same sentence. Fine once; reads
  as a tagline when the judge meets it twice in a minute.
- **Personification**: "money leaves shares", "A January cannot" – one is a voice; two is a style tic.

- Para 4 (the range): no "why a range at all" voice, and no felt number; it ends on a plain fact, which is good.
- Para 5 (weakness): good confession, plain grammar. Keep it this flat.
- The pensions sentence in para 1 is the best opponent in the draft.

## Caplet XR full circle (added 00:05, at the Chief of Staff's request; R4 files unchanged since 23:36)
- **The payoff works.** "until the tenth redemption pays the last $50,000 in 2042" is an act, not a lesson, and the
  last-line callback is the motif's only encore. No medicine anywhere. Good.
- **The setup is buried, so it half-reads as decoration.** The name arrives in the middle of paragraph 2, squeezed
  between the "A January cannot." kicker and the "None is sold early" refusal. A reader meets it as a label stuck onto
  the ladder, not as the plan's frame. Book 11 asks for the motif in the introduction *and* the conclusion. Here it is
  in the middle and the conclusion.
- **The pitch never names it**, so the judge meets "Caplet XR" for the first time partway through the IPS.
- **Move, not words:** give the name its own moment near the opening, where the ten Treasuries first appear, so
  "ten redemptions" is planted early and "the tenth redemption" pays it off. Deleting the name today wouldn't change
  the argument, which is the test the card uses for decoration. If it moves to where the ladder is introduced, the
  name *is* the ladder.
- Small: "R for redemption, the bond market's word for the day a bond is repaid" is clear on one reading. Keep it.
