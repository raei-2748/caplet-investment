## Flags

1. **Contradiction:** "a fund promises no fixed sum" against "never what a payment gets". A fix must say what absorbs a short fund payout (over-saved money, then the bottom) or drop "never".
2. **Antecedent:** in "sized to pay about that amount back", the nearest amount is "the 2033 payment", so a judge can read the bottom as $50,000. It must be clear the stake repays about what was left of the deposit.
3. **Opening vs paragraph 4:** "ten Treasury holdings that repay it", yet under its own zero-rate assumption they don't until the 2031 sale or the bottom fills the gap. The opener promises more than paragraph 4 delivers.
4. **TNA 3:** shares "only set how far her gift rises above her promised minimum"; VT is "the only investment not standing between Laura and a payment deadline". The IPS sends up to half the shares to the payments' gap. It survives because the bottom backs the gap; show the shares only spare the bottom.
5. **Whose assumption:** "Laura assumes" hands the team's planning assumption to the client.
6. **Pitch:** no shares, no flexibility, the top's source unexplained, so it reads all-Treasury; "repay it first" is ambiguous; "names it ... its top" is a pronoun chain. No slack at 50.
7. **WInS:** the book holds VT and a bottom-sized IBTM from the $300,000 (the scaled post-2028 plan); the IPS buys shares in 2028. The Final Report must explain this.
8. **Wharton's don'ts:** none breached: no tickers, citations, charts or model statistics. "Two thirds" holds on the 23 Oct close (about $297,600 of $450,000).
