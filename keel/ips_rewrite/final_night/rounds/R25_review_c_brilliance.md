# R25 review (c): is it as brilliant as possible?
AI-generated review (Claude, 5 Nov 2026). Not submission text.

**Verdict: not yet.** Three ideas are Caplet's own, but R25 says what the plan does far better than why. It never
faces growth against protection for a client "willing to take thoughtful risks" ("growth", "willing",
"diversification" and "inflation" never appear). About 30 strict words of packaging can pay for most fixes.

## 1. Dinner-table ideas
- **A "balanced" mix is the reckless choice for the building money** (para 2). The best idea here, buried mid-sentence,
  and missing its step: the payments take most of her money, so the facility money is a thin slice that any loss hits
  magnified. It argues the facility half but concludes the payment half (a deep enough fall reaches a payment).
- **Only the interest is risked; lose every share and her deposit returns** (para 3). Fully argued, and unnamed.
- **A capped promise left in shares takes every fall and gives away every rise** (para 5). Argued, but misplaced (2).
- **Waiting money priced at zero** (para 4). Reads as pessimism; its force is that zero is the one rate
  waiting cash cannot fall below: a bound, not a forecast.

## 2. The biggest strength
Every number Laura says aloud is owned before she says it, so certainty is a fact co-sponsors can check, not a
model's opinion, and the interest still buys upside.
- **Stated:** yes, in the first sentence.
- **Argued:** for the payments and the top, not the bottom. Buying it in 2028, not from shares in 2031, is the
  biggest choice; only its consequence appears. FR_IDEAS #22 assumed "the IPS keeps the reason"; R25 doesn't.
- **Priced:** half. The downside is priced (at worst, the interest). "Probably less to give" has no size, payer or
  timing, and the "But" after it defends the 2031 move, not the small share slice. A better version must say what 2028
  gives up (share growth, lost mainly in the years shares do best), that the 2031 move costs only about two years'
  growth on the promised half, and what each buys.

## 3. Concepts used versus mentioned
- **Used and critiqued:** leverage against the balanced mix, the only alternative critiqued.
- **Used, uncritiqued:** matching per payment; principal protection; reinvestment; the zero test; cap asymmetry; liquidity.
- **Mentioned only:**
  - "Near-ironclad": defined, but no evaluation or assumptions; the 2028 deposit arriving is one, unnamed.
  - "US default": named, never weighed.
  - "Financial flexibility": no argument that unpromised money is worth most because nobody can count on it.
  - "Half": no reason given (cold readers asked).
  - Diversification: only implied, though Wharton scores it by name.

## 4. Missing ideas, ranked (strict words; what each displaces)
1. **The 2028 reason, at residency level** (~20). A bottom built from 2028-30 shares would shrink in the slump that
   cuts co-sponsors' and grant-makers' giving. Bought in 2028, hers is the residency's one source of money that doesn't
   fall with the others: diversification where it counts. Displaces para 1's case restatement (14).
2. **Answer "willing to take thoughtful risks"** (~15). Para 1 says interest makes up the $500,000 but stops short of the
   inference: shares could add nothing the payments need, only a way to miss one, so her appetite gets the only money
   she can afford to lose. Displaces the cost sentence (11), rebuilt as item 5.
3. **Riskless means a matched date, proved by the exception** (~10). The one money without its own date, waiting cash,
   is the one weak point. Joins para 2 to para 4 and makes zero a bound. Displaces the XR gloss (14).
4. **Certainty by its named breaks** (~20). A payment fails only on a US default, or if most of the 2028 deposit never
   arrives after the first $300,000 fell short. Answers "how they evaluated" and "assumptions"; fits only if 6 or 7 goes.
5. **The cost paired with its own reason** (about +5 net; see 2).
6. **Inflation, payment side** (~12). A fixed $50,000 can buy less but never cost more, so plain Treasuries match it.
   Required.
7. **One borrower by design** (~18). Payments that must all be met are safest on one issuer whose debts stand or fall
   together. Misread before.
8. **FR #13 argued back** (about 0 net, if 1 and 2 find words elsewhere). Running costs come from money in hand, while a
   building can be shared: a principle in place of para 1's restated rule.

## 5. Packaging to cut

- "near-", a hedge on a definition.
- Para 1's case restatement (14): the judges' own rule, told back.
