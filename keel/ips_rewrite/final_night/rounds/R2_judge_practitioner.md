**Top 50?** On substance, **maybe leaning yes** — it is one of the few I'd expect to price the liability correctly
and to say why. **As submitted at 632 words, no:** it fails the format rule.

## Strongest technical claim
> "A fixed payment has one honest price, which is what Treasuries charge to deliver it on its date. Price it at what
> shares usually earn and it looks cheaper, but the difference is a return nobody has earned yet"

This is the liability-pricing point every LDI desk lives by, put in plain words. Runner-up: "Cash only looks safer:
for fixed dollars on fixed dates, it is a bet that interest rates won't fall."

## Weakest technical claims
1. > "A Treasury held to maturity repays what it promised whatever markets do, so we define a high degree of certainty
   > as no operating payment depending on a share price."

   True of the bonds, but the ladder is half Treasury *funds*, whose closing payout is a NAV, not a promised sum, and
   the IPS itself admits two paragraphs later that the bond rungs depend on reinvested coupons. The definition of
   certainty should cover rates as well as shares, or it reads as stronger than the plan.
2. > "the smallest facility contribution she could make"

   Loose: the smallest she *could* make is zero. It means the minimum the plan commits to.
3. > "American public pensions ... have spent two decades taking on more risk to keep up with it"

   Directionally supported by the research, but stated as settled cause and effect. Fine in the Final Report with a
   citation; a touch strong unsupported in the IPS.
4. Not addressed: the whole reserve rests on a single credit, the US Treasury. A practitioner would want one clause
   saying that is the one concentration the plan accepts on purpose.

## What I'd retell to a colleague tomorrow
"A high-school team priced their client's promises at the Treasury curve, not expected returns, and then applied the
same rule to her fundraising range: both ends bought before she says it. Same discipline as a pension buyout, applied
to a pledge."

## The one change that would move my score most
Cut 130+ words to get under 500 (the capital-protected-note paragraph and the pension aside can each lose a sentence
without losing an idea), and in doing so make the certainty definition honest about rates: "no operating payment
depends on a share price or on where rates go after we buy". That keeps the strongest idea and removes the one claim a
fixed-income reader would circle.
