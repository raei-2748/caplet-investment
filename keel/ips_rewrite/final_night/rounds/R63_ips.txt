Caplet XR has Laura buy each promise before she makes it. Her ten $50,000 operating payments from 2033 are fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, which makes them a debt. X is for the ten Treasury holdings her first $300,000 buys in January 2027 to repay it, and R for their redemption, one in the year before each payment.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and in such a mix the facility would get only what was left over. Like the shares of a company that has borrowed heavily, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market under it.

Five of the ten are funds that each hold one calendar year's Treasuries and pay out that December, because the competition's platform has no individual Treasury for some of those years. The 2028 deposit first covers anything the ten cost above $300,000, then buys enough more of the fund behind the 2033 payment to return about $150,000 in December 2032. She'll round that down and call it the bottom of her range, since a fund's payout isn't fixed. The rest goes into a world index fund of thousands of companies.

Interest arrives years before the payments it's meant for, and each holding repays weeks or months before its January, so years of near-zero rates, as after 2008, would leave the ten short. In 2031, before naming a range, Laura assumes the waiting money will earn nothing, which is gloomy enough that nobody has to forecast rates, and sells up to half her shares to buy Treasuries covering the gap. If that half falls short, the rest comes out of the bottom, moving the whole range down.

The range is a promise too. Co-sponsors will plan around its top, and missing it would cost her the credibility the residency depends on, so she sells the other half for Treasuries as well, and the top is the bottom plus what they fetch. From then on, barring a US default, no payment waits on shares, interest rates or an early sale, which is what we mean by a high degree of certainty.

Resting every payment on the US government looks undiversified until you count the residency's other money. Co-sponsors' giving and Laura's book and speaking income can both shrink in a recession, and Treasury repayments can't.

In 2033 she'll give at least the bottom, and how close to the top depends on what inflation and the Taiwan dollar have done to the building's cost. What she doesn't give, and any shares the cover left, stay hers and unpromised, for costs nobody can list in 2031. Her operating reserve is what's left of the ten holdings and the cover, and it shrinks one holding a year until the last payment, in January 2042, settles the debt she paid for in 2027.
