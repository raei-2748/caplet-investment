Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments work like a debt: fixed in dollars and payable only from her portfolio. Caplet XR, X for ten and R for redemption, meets them with ten Treasury holdings, each redeemed in the year before its payment, and lets her tell co-sponsors only figures she already holds in Treasuries.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at today's Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, so in that mix her facility contribution would be whatever is left after a fixed claim. Like the shares of a company that has borrowed heavily, it would swing far harder than the market beneath it, and a named figure that fell short would cost her co-sponsors' trust.

So in January 2027 her first $300,000 buys the ten holdings, and if they cost more, the 2028 deposit first buys what is missing. Five are Treasury bonds; five are funds holding only Treasuries that mature in one year, paying out when they do. We call it a high degree of certainty when, barring a US default, no payment waits on a share price or an early sale. All ten lend only to the US government, because a default there would shake every other borrower too.

Most of the 2028 deposit buys more of the fund behind the 2033 payment, and only that extra part's payout, rounded down, counts as the bottom of the range she names in 2031. Her one thoughtful risk is a world index fund sized at about what that extra part will earn by 2032, so losing it all would cost only that interest.

Interest and holdings repaid months early sit at short-term rates until each payment, so if those rates fall and stay low, the payments could come up short. Before naming a range in 2031, Laura sells half her shares for Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus what that half fetched. Until then, strong markets raise the top and weak ones lower it; once it is named, shares behind it would take every fall and hand any rise above it back to her. From the other half she sells enough to buy Treasuries covering that shortfall even if the waiting money earned nothing; if that costs more than the half, she names a lower range.

From then on, share markets can't move the range. She will give at least the bottom and at most the top, and where between is her call in 2033, once the building's cost in Taiwan is known. What she doesn't give stays hers, with the shares she kept, and none of it is promised, because the project will need things nobody can name in 2031. The operating reserve she sets aside in 2033 is the money still waiting in the holdings, so it needs no trade. In January 2042 the tenth redemption and the interest saved for it meet the last payment.
