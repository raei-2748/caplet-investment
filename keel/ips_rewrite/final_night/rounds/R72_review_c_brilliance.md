# R72 review (c): is it as brilliant as possible?
AI-generated review (Claude, 6 Nov 2026). Not submission text.

**Verdict.** Close, and almost all intellect, not packaging. One rule (every sum she commits to is owned before
anyone counts on it) drives six paragraphs, and four finance ideas are used unnamed: leverage, hand-built principal
protection, reinvestment risk, a capped payoff. What holds it back: three of its best arguments stop one step early.

## 1. Ideas a judge would retell
1. **A "balanced" mix is a geared bet with the building money (P2).** Half argued. It never says why the swing
   matters here: a geared leftover can't back a credible 2031 range without a forecast, and a deep fall reaches a
   payment. Nor does it say what Caplet XR's leftover carries instead.
2. **Naming the range is a second debt; a named top left in shares can only disappoint (P5).** Most distinctive, best
   argued, but the cap it relies on ("up to the top") only arrives in P6.
3. **Shares bought with interest the Treasuries haven't paid yet (P3).** Mechanism clear; consequence missing:
   whatever shares do, the deposit comes back, so against all-Treasuries the most they can cost her is that interest.
4. **The ladder's one weak point, waiting interest, priced at zero (P4).** Fully argued, even why money waits.
5. **Certainty defined by ownership, with two named assumptions (P5, last sentence).** Fully argued; the quotable line.
6. **Implied, not said (P6):** once named, the range's width reflects the building's needs, not markets.

Quiet strength: "commits to" (P1) and "none of it is committed" (P6) are one test, seen twice.

## 2. The biggest strength
Answer to Ray: co-sponsors can check the plan's promises rather than believe them. Every figure Laura owes or names
is a holding, so confidence needs no model; uncertainty reduces to one borrower and one assumption (the 2028
deposit); no market move triggers anything.
- **Stated:** yes (P1, P5).
- **Argued:** against a balanced mix and for the range. Implicit: the case's warning that overpromising costs her
  credibility.
- **Priced:** one way only ("probably richer"). Unpriced: the ceiling against all-Treasuries (the interest); growth
  given up by selling the promised shares before 2033; expected contribution given up by buying the bottom in 2028.

Regret capped on the safe side and admitted on the growth side: the draft makes only the second half of that claim.

## 5. Packaging that should go
- Little is left. The R is glossed, not used: the two short X and R sentences read as a footnote. Fold them so
  redemption carries item 4.
- "Their cover" and "the stake" are labels; describe the holdings.
- Pitch: "Before she speaks" is staging, and the 2028 mechanics crowd out the payoff (nothing she owes or names
  depends on a market).

## 6. Where cleverness costs clarity
- **P3 opening:** "with its interest, about what's left of the second deposit" stacks three qualifiers; "its" is
  ambiguous.
- **P4:** up to half her shares pay for the gap, then "no payment depends on shares". Say share prices.
- **P5-P6:** "the other half" and "unsold shares" never say what she keeps. Say what the gap didn't need stays hers.
- **P1 against P5:** funds "pay what those bonds fetch", yet "no market can change a payment". Say why the payout is
  nearly fixed, or that the zero-rate set-aside covers it.
- **P6:** "anything outside the range" mixes a figure with holdings; it means money behind nothing promised.
- **Pitch:** "half become Treasuries": half of what?
