Caplet XR keeps every sum Laura commits to in Treasuries before anyone counts on it, starting with her ten $50,000 operating payments. They're fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. We repay the debt with ten Treasury holdings, the X in the name, each kept until its redemption, the R. Her first $300,000 buys all it can and the 2028 deposit buys the rest, so co-sponsors will know her share of the Taiwan residency's operating costs is bought before they're asked. Five are funds that each hold one year's Treasuries, because a fund sells more easily than a small lot of bonds.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get what was left over. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market.

In 2028 Laura buys an extra stake in the fund behind the 2033 payment, sized so its December 2032 payout matches what's left of that deposit. The bottom of the credible range she names in 2031 is a little below that payout, which isn't fixed. Because the stake costs less than it pays out, she can put the difference into a world index fund of thousands of companies and still get about the whole deposit back. Bought in 2028, the bottom is in Treasuries before any slump that could shrink co-sponsors' giving and her earnings, so her part needn't fall with theirs.

A Treasury held to redemption still carries one risk: its interest and repayment, even inside the funds, arrive before the payment they're meant for. That money waits in a short-term Treasury fund, where rates could fall and stay low, so the plan assumes it earns nothing. In 2031 she sells up to half her shares, the half kept back for this, and buys enough Treasuries to fill the gap that assumption leaves. When that half isn't enough, the stake behind the bottom pays the rest, so the whole range moves down.

So that a named top can't fall out of reach and cost her credibility, she sells the other half for Treasuries that same day, and the top is the bottom plus what it fetched. Barring a US default and with both deposits made, falling markets and rates land on the range, not the payments, which is what we mean by a high degree of certainty.

Her operating reserve in 2033 needs no trade: it is the Treasuries held for the payments. She contributes the top, or less if the building needs less. Everything else, including any shares the gap didn't need, can be sold any day and is left uncommitted, so she has room as the project develops. Once the final redemption meets the final payment in 2042, nothing she holds is spoken for.
