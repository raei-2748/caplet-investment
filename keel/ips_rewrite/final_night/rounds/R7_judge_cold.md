## The strategy and its biggest strength, as I understood them
**Strategy.** Every amount Laura commits to is bought in Treasuries on the day she commits: the ten $50k payments with her
first $300k (2027), the minimum facility contribution with most of her 2028 deposit (bought then, not in 2031, so a later
crash can't touch it), and both ends of the 2031 range by moving half her shares into Treasuries first. Only roughly the
interest on the 2028 Treasuries sits in a world share fund.
**Biggest strength (as stated).** Markets can change how big a range she names, but never whether she meets a payment or a
number she has already named.

## Paragraph by paragraph, after one read, in my words

- **Pitch.** Unchanged from R6. She buys Treasuries for each amount the day she commits; the $300k (topped up in 2028)
  buys the payments; the 2028 money buys the minimum gift with its interest in shares; the range is covered first.
- **P1.** Price a fixed payment at the Treasury cost; pricing it at share returns counts growth not yet earned, and US
  pensions that do this have taken more risk. *Clear.*
- **P2.** The $300k buys a ladder, one holding maturing before each payment 2033-2042; held to the end, each pays out
  regardless of markets, so "high certainty" = no payment depends on shares. Caplet XR. *Clear, and shorter than R6.*
- **P3.** The 2028 money first fills any gap in the ladder (small today), then mostly buys more of the late-2032 fund (the
  minimum facility gift), and the leftover goes into the world fund. Buying the minimum in 2028 rather than 2031 costs
  some growth, but protects the bottom of the range from a 2029-30 crash. *Understood; two stops. The new trade-off
  sentence is good: it's the first time a draft admits what a choice costs.*
- **P4.** 2031 range: bottom = that extra, rounded down; top = plus half her shares, moved into Treasuries first. So she's
  as sure of landing in it as of Treasuries. *Clear.*
- **P5.** Weakness: some money waits for its January and earns less if rates stay low. Before 2031 they assume it earns
  nothing and cover that from the half she kept, then from the 2032 fund (lower range). What's not needed stays hers.
  *Understood; one stop.*
- **P6.** Strength: markets change the size of the range, never whether she meets a payment or a named number. From 2033
  the ladder plus "that set-aside" is the reserve; nothing is sold; one holding per January to 2042. *Clear except
  "that set-aside".*

## The strategy in three sentences, as I'd retell it
Whenever Laura commits to an amount, she buys the Treasuries that pay it that day, so shares only hold money nobody is
counting on. Her $300k buys a ladder for the ten $50k payments; her 2028 $150k fills any gap, then buys the minimum facility
contribution in a late-2032 Treasury fund (giving up some growth so a later crash can't touch it), with roughly its interest
in a world share fund. Before naming the 2031 range she moves half her shares into Treasuries, so markets can change how big
the range is but never whether she meets it.
