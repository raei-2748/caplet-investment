Under Caplet XR, Laura owns the Treasuries that will repay a sum before anyone relies on it. At current Treasury prices her ten $50,000 operating payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000. In a balanced mix every gain or loss would land on the last third, since the payments don't move, and it would swing like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, with her 2031 range resting on it.

In January 2027 her $300,000 buys a ladder of ten Treasury holdings, one repaying before each January from 2033 to 2042, and the 2028 deposit buys any it can't afford. X is for the ten, R for redemption. Five are funds of one year's Treasuries, which pay out what they hold rather than a fixed sum. The payments are fixed in dollars, so inflation can raise what the facility costs but never what she owes.

Most of the remaining 2028 deposit buys a further stake in the fund behind the 2033 payment, which sets the bottom of her range. The rest, about what the stake will earn by late 2032, goes into one world index fund of thousands of companies, so losing every share would cost her only that interest. Buying the bottom in 2028 gives up growth Laura could afford to chase, as her living costs are met elsewhere. The creative residency in Taiwan couldn't: its co-sponsors, grants and fees can shrink in the same recessions that hit shares, and her part must hold when they do.

The ladder's other weakness is money that waits. Interest arrives years before the payment it serves and some holdings repay months early, so money sits in a short-term Treasury fund, earning less if rates fall and stay low. So in 2031, before she names a range, she buys extra Treasuries, enough to meet every payment even if every waiting dollar, inside the funds too, earned nothing, and pays for them with half her shares. Whatever that half can't cover comes out of what the stake pays in late 2032, and her range starts lower.

The bottom is what the stake will pay, less a margin because a fund's payout isn't fixed. Then she sells the other half of her shares and puts the money in Treasury funds repaying before 2033; the top is the bottom plus that money. Both ends are owned before anyone hears them, and in 2033 she gives what those Treasuries pay, up to the top.

Whatever the first half isn't needed for, and anything paid beyond the top, stays hers, liquid and promised to no one, so she keeps room as the project develops. In 2033 her operating reserve needs no trade: it is the ladder and the extra Treasuries. The plan's biggest strength, and what we mean by a high degree of certainty, is that no payment or named figure depends on a share price. The tenth redemption and the interest kept for it meet the last $50,000 in 2042.
