Caplet XR puts every sum Laura commits to into Treasuries before anyone counts on it: her ten $50,000 operating payments, then both ends of the credible range she names in 2031. The payments are fixed in dollars and can come only from her portfolio, so we treat them as a debt. Ten Treasury holdings, the X in the name, repay it. Each is kept until its redemption, the R. Her first $300,000 buys all it can, and the 2028 deposit buys any it can't. Five are funds of bonds maturing in one calendar year, which pay what those bonds fetch rather than a fixed sum but sell more easily than a small lot of bonds.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would fit her record of thoughtful risks and probably leave her richer by 2042. But at current Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, and the facility would get only what they left. Like the shares of a heavily borrowed company, that leftover would swing far harder than the share market.

In 2028 she buys more of the fund behind the 2033 payment, enough to hand back, with its interest, about what's left of the second deposit by December 2032. Her range's bottom sits a little under that. The stake costs less than it hands back, and the difference buys a world index fund of thousands of companies, so her shares are bought with interest the Treasuries haven't paid yet. Buying the stake in 2028 holds the bottom in Treasuries, not shares, through years when a slump could also shrink co-sponsors' giving.

Interest and redemptions arrive before the payments they serve, and years of near-zero rates on that waiting money would leave the payments short. In 2031 she closes that gap before naming her range, buying enough Treasuries to meet the payments even if waiting money earned nothing. Up to half her shares pay for them, and any shortfall comes from the stake behind the bottom, lowering the whole range, so no payment depends on shares.

Naming the range creates a second debt, paid for the same way. Before she names it, the other half of her shares is sold for Treasuries that pay out before 2033, and the top is the bottom plus what the sale fetched. Left in shares, a named top could fall short but never pay co-sponsors more. A high degree of certainty means that, barring a US default and with both deposits made, no market can change a payment or a named figure.

Once she names it, co-sponsors can plan on the bottom, and she contributes up to the top as the building in Taiwan needs it. Her operating reserve in 2033 needs no trade: the payment Treasuries and their cover make it up, and each payment draws it down. As the project develops, she can sell anything outside the range, unsold shares included, any day, since none of it is committed.
