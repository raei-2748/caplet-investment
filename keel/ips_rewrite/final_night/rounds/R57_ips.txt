Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments work like a debt: fixed in dollars and payable only from her portfolio. Caplet XR meets them with Treasuries bought years ahead, and lets her tell co-sponsors only figures she already holds in Treasuries.

A balanced mix of shares and bonds would probably leave her richer by 2042. But at today's Treasury prices the payments cost about two thirds of her $450,000, so in that mix her facility contribution would be whatever is left after a fixed claim. Like the shares of a company that has borrowed heavily, it would swing far harder than the market beneath it.

So in January 2027 her first $300,000 buys a Treasury bond or fund for each payment, redeemed in the year before it falls due. Those redemptions are the R in Caplet XR, and the ten holdings its X. We take a high degree of certainty to mean that, barring a US default, no payment depends on a share price or on selling anything early. All ten lend only to the US government, since other borrowers would add ways to miss a payment while leaving that risk in place.

Laura's thoughtful risks have been in her career, whose earnings can shrink in the same slump that sinks shares. So her portfolio holds few shares. Most of the 2028 deposit buys an extra holding in the fund behind the 2033 payment, and she counts only its payout, rounded down because a fund's payout isn't fixed, as the bottom of the range she names in 2031. Her shares are a world index fund sized at about what that extra holding will earn by 2032, so losing them all would cost only that interest.

The ladder also counts on its early cash, interest and holdings repaid months before January, earning short-term rates until each payment. If those rates fall and stay low, the payments could come up short. Before naming a range in 2031, Laura sells half of her shares for Treasuries, and the top is the bottom plus what that half fetched, because a top left in shares could fall out of reach. From the other half she sells only enough to buy Treasuries covering the shortfall as if the waiting cash earned nothing. If that half can't pay for them, money meant for the range does, and she names a lower one.

After that, no market can move her outside the range. She will give at least the bottom and at most the top, and the building's cost in Taiwan by 2033 decides where. What she doesn't give, and the shares she kept stay hers, sellable any day, as financial flexibility, unpromised because the project will need things nobody can name in 2031.

The operating reserve she sets aside in 2033 is the Treasuries still waiting for a payment, so it needs no trade. It shrinks by one redemption a year until the tenth and the interest saved for it meet the last payment.
