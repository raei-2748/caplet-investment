## The three points the Chief of Staff asked about
1. **"Both ends of Laura's range are bought before anyone hears it" (pitch, P4) against the IBTM note's "although the
   final payout is not fixed": NEW-but-compatible, and it holds.** "Bought" says she owns the money. It doesn't say the
   amount is fixed. The bottom is written as "that 2032 fund's payout, rounded down", and rounding down is exactly how
   the IBTM note's "minimum figure in 2031" is kept honest. P5's "If they can't cover it, she names a lower range" also
   allows the range to move before it is named. What would break it: dropping "rounded down", or turning "bought" into
   "fixed", "guaranteed" or "locked to the dollar" (C10, C12). Keep "rounded down" in the same sentence as the bottom.
2. **"One maturing before each January" (P2) and "Treasuries maturing before each January" (pitch) against the Feb-2037
   and May-2038 bonds: AGREES.** Those bonds mature 10.5 and 7.5 months early, and that is still "before". The record
   only rules out "just before" or "in January" (C3 strain), and R4 uses neither.
3. **Claims the TNA reflections contradict: none.** Reflection 1: bonds untouched, no TIPS, high certainty = near-ironclad.
   R4 says "None is sold early" and "no operating payment depends on a share price", so it agrees, and R4 makes no
   duration claim either way (A34). Reflection 2 (NAV, not par): see strain 2 below. Reflection 3 ("the one place the
   plan is allowed to be wrong"): R4 states it almost verbatim.

## Strains, highest first
1. **P3: "it stays small because Laura's fortunes already ride on the economy."** STRAINS the case, not the TNA. The
   case lists her income sources and never ties them to markets (C5: "can" is the most we may say). "Already ride on"
   states it as fact. The very next sentence correctly uses "can dry up". *Fixed in R5* ("Laura's own income can rise
   and fall with the economy").
2. **P2: "A Treasury held to maturity repays what it promised whatever markets do."** This comes straight after "Treasury
   bonds and Treasury funds", so a reader can apply it to all ten holdings. Only a bond promises an amount. The five
   funds pay "a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par" (reflection 2), and their "payout is not fixed" (IBTM,
   IBTO, IBTP, IBTQ notes). Fix: "A Treasury bond held to maturity ...". (R5 made this worse, not better: "Each holding
   pays out ... whatever markets do". See `R5_archive.md` strain 2.)
3. **Opening line, pitch and P1: "Laura's money leaves shares the day somebody starts counting on it."** Read literally,
   her money sits in shares until someone counts on it. Her first $300,000 never goes into shares: it buys the ladder
   in January 2027. A32 says the IPS must never suggest her first $300,000 bought shares. This doesn't say that, but it
   implies a path the record doesn't have. Low to medium. R5's opening ("Whenever Laura commits to a sum, she buys the
   Treasuries...") avoids it.

## Low
- P3 "a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, which sets the smallest facility contribution" and P4 "that 2032 fund's
  payout": the IBTM note says the same fund also pays the first $50,000 ("does two jobs"). Read alone, "that fund's
  payout, rounded down" could take in the 2033 payment's share as well. R5's "more of the late-2032 fund that pays
  her first January ... that extra" is the record's own picture (C7).
- P6 "until the tenth redemption pays the last $50,000": the Nov-2041 note says "reinvested coupons funding part of
  each". Fine while P5's reinvestment sentence stays.
