## A. The five criteria as observable descriptors

The IPS is one of three deliverables judged together ("Evaluators will consider the three deliverables together").
Criterion 4 (competition experience) belongs mostly to the Final Report and TNA, so the IPS is marked on what it can
show there, and the total is read with that in mind.

### 1. Investment Strategy
> "Presents a clear and creative investment thesis; demonstrates disciplined planning across Laura's changing time
> horizons and cash-flow needs; uses appropriate diversification; and maintains consistency with the team's IPS."

| Mark | What the page shows |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | One thesis a judge can repeat after one read, and it is *a reason*, not a product list. Every move in the plan (2027, 2028, 2031, 2033-2042) follows from that one reason, so the plan reads as one argument. The plan has a dated shape across all three phases (build to 2032, decide 2031-33, pay out 2033-42). Diversification is argued for what each part of the money has to do, not just asserted ("thousands of companies"), and the judge can see why the plan holds so few kinds of asset. Nothing contradicts the trades or TNA. |
| 7-8 | Clear thesis and a dated plan, but the reason behind it is partly implicit, or one phase is thin (usually the payout years or 2031). Diversification mentioned, not argued. |
| 5-6 | A competent ladder-plus-shares plan described rather than argued; the "creative" part is a name or image laid over it. |
| ≤4 | Muddled thesis, or a plan that changes with markets, or a contradiction with the trades. |

### 2. Client Knowledge and Objectives
> "Tailors the strategy to Laura's circumstances, priorities, risk considerations, and residency goals; demonstrates
> a thoughtful understanding of the client; and presents recommendations that can earn her confidence."

| Mark | What the page shows |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Uses facts only this case has, and *uses* them to decide something: the payments are fixed nominal (so nominal Treasuries fit); the 2028 deposit comes from "publishing advances, speaking engagements, licensing" (so it is risky income); "willing to take thoughtful risks" is answered, not ignored; "committing all remaining assets could limit her financial flexibility"; the 2031 credibility risk ("If Laura promises more than she can ultimately contribute"). Uses the case's own words. A judge feels the plan could only be for Laura. |
| 7-8 | Tailored on the payments and the range, but her risk appetite or the source of the 2028 money is unused. |
| 5-6 | Generic "conservative client" plan with Laura's name on it. |
| ≤4 | Gets a case fact wrong, or relies on co-sponsors/outside money for the payments. |

### 3. Portfolio Analysis
> "Demonstrates understanding and effective use of investment concepts and tools; integrates quantitative and
> qualitative analysis; and uses reasonable assumptions and projections to evaluate funding reliability, the facility
> contribution, and financial flexibility under varying market outcomes."

| Mark | What the page shows |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Investment concepts are *used*, not named: each one explained in a clause and doing work in the argument (e.g. why a dated Treasury fits a dated payment; why time does not make a share loss smaller; what the plan's real weakness is and its rule). States its definition of a high degree of certainty and what it rests on. Says what happens in a bad market and in a good one to each of the three goals (payments, contribution, flexibility). Assumptions are visible in words, not tables. |
| 7-8 | Sound concepts, one or two outcomes shown, but the bad case or the plan's weakness is missing or vague. |
| 5-6 | Concepts named without being used, or claims of certainty with no stated basis. |
| ≤4 | Wrong concepts (calling cash-flow matching "immunisation", calling a static plan "CPPI"), "risk-free"/"guaranteed" for shares, or model numbers with no meaning. |

Note: Wharton does not want detailed calculations in the IPS, so a 9-10 here is reached through *reasoning about
outcomes*, not through numbers. Over-numbered pages lose here as well as under "not expected".

### 4. Articulation of Competition Experience
> "Demonstrates teamwork, communication, and learning; clearly explains the team's research and decision-making
> process; and reflects meaningfully on the team's growth and response to challenges."

| Mark | What the page shows |
|---|---|
| 8-10 | The IPS reads as the product of a decision process: it says what the plan refuses and why, so a judge sees choices were weighed. Consistent with the TNA's reasoning. (Book 11 bans team history in the IPS, so this criterion is mostly earned in the Final Report; do not reward history paragraphs.) |
| 6-7 | Decisions stated without the alternative weighed. |
| ≤5 | Team history or "we first thought..." filling the IPS, or a page that contradicts the TNA. |

### 5. Creativity and Presentation
> "Presents a compelling, well-organized narrative in an authentic team voice; uses data effectively to support
> conclusions; demonstrates original thinking and meaningful reflection; and communicates Laura's potential facility
> contribution and investment uncertainty clearly and credibly to prospective co-sponsors."

| Mark | What the page shows |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Original *thinking*: at least one idea a judge has not met in other IPSs and could retell (Ray, I84: creativity is in the strategy and reasoning, not in metaphor). Reads as people talking, every sentence clear on one reading. Organised as one narrative, not a list of sections. Few numbers, each one doing a job. Explains how the 2031 range is made credible and what uncertainty it carries, in words a co-sponsor would believe. |
| 7-8 | Clear and well organised; the original idea is there but buried or under-explained. |
| 5-6 | Competent but generic; or the voice is compressed and machine-like (sentences that need a second read). |
| ≤4 | Slogans, kickers, imagery doing the work of argument; or sentences a judge cannot follow. |

## B. Must-answer lines (each ticked with a quote from the round)

The five questions Wharton tells teams to consider (IPS instructions, p.1):
- **Q1** What is the central idea behind your investment strategy?
- **Q2** What principles will guide your investment decisions?
- **Q3** How does your strategy balance growth, risk, liquidity, funding reliability, and financial flexibility? (all five named or clearly handled)
- **Q4** How will the portfolio's asset allocation and composition change as future funding needs approach and payments are made? (before 2033 *and* 2033-2042)
- **Q5** How will your strategy protect the operating commitment while preserving flexibility for a responsible facility contribution?

The five "should make it clear" points (p.2):
- **M1** What your team is trying to accomplish for the client.
- **M2** Why your strategy is appropriate for the client and their financial goals.
- **M3** How your strategy balances growth, liquidity, funding reliability, risk, and financial flexibility.
- **M4** How your strategy will guide portfolio construction and investment decisions as the client's needs change.
- **M5** How your approach will protect the required operating payments while supporting a responsible facility contribution.

The "establish your strategic approach" list (p.2), each needs at least a sentence:
- **S1** preparing for the operating commitment;
- **S2** managing the operating reserve's asset composition over time (the reserve's makeup from 2033 as payments are made);
- **S3** determining a responsible facility contribution (the *rule* for it, not the number);
- **S4** preserving appropriate financial flexibility;
- **S5** risk and return; liquidity; portfolio construction and diversification; planned changes as funding needs approach.

From the case (lines 91-99, 114-119), which the IPS should at least set the frame for:
- **C1** A stated meaning of "a high degree of certainty" and what it rests on (the case asks teams to "define" it).
- **C2** No reliance on co-sponsors, grants or outside money for the ten payments.
- **C3** How favourable and unfavourable markets affect the contribution (the principle; the numbers are Final Report).
- **C4** The 2031 range must "protect the portfolio's ability to fund the ten-year operating commitment".

The pitch (≤50 words):
- **P1** States the central idea.
- **P2** Says how it supports Laura's goals and future funding needs.

## C. Must-avoid lines (each checked clean, or quoted)

From the IPS instructions:
- **X1** A final operating-reserve calculation (a reserve dollar figure presented as the answer).
- **X2** Detailed portfolio projections (percentiles, probabilities, projected values).
- **X3** A final facility-contribution range (dollar range).
- **X4** The draft communication to co-sponsors (fundraising text in Laura's voice).
- **X5** Describing individual investments (tickers, fund names, specific bonds) instead of strategy.
- **X6** Detailed financial calculations.
- **X7** Graphics, charts, links, footnotes, formal citations, or named sources ("Gneezy (2014)", "Damodaran says").
- **X8** Over limit: pitch > 50 or IPS > 500 words.

From Wharton's advice and rules:
- **X9** WInS performance or P&L mentioned, or added to projections.
- **X10** Anything outside the approved universe (STRIPS, TIPS, options, derivatives, margin, shorting).
- **X11** A strategy that changes "simply because markets move" (the guide: planned adjustments yes, market-timing no).
- **X12** Reliance on outside funding for the operating payments (case line 92).

## D. What a round file contains (`rounds/RN_wharton.md`)
1. Scores: five criteria /10, total /50, one paragraph of reasons each.
2. Table of Q1-Q5, M1-M5, S1-S5, C1-C4, P1-P2: answered / partly / no, with the quote.
3. Table of X1-X12: clean / breach, with the quote.
4. **The single change that would lift the total most**, said as an idea, not as a sentence for the page (only the
   Writer writes prose).
