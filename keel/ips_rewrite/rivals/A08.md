# A08
STRATEGY NAME: Foundation First, Then the Garden
## PITCH
Think of Laura's residency as a house. We pour the foundation first: Treasury bonds timed to land when each of her ten $50,000 payments is due. Only money beyond that grows in a global stock garden, so a bad market can shrink the garden, never the house.
## IPS
Laura is building in two layers. The ten $50,000 operating payments (2033 to 2042) are the foundation, and it cannot wobble. Everything else, including her facility contribution, is the garden: it can grow, and it can have a bad season. Our strategy keeps them from ever getting mixed up.

The foundation

Laura's first $300,000 goes into U.S. Treasury notes and bonds chosen so one matures just before each payment, 2033 through 2042, like ten beams that each carry one load. The dates and dollar amounts are fixed, so we match fixed dollars with fixed dollars instead of hoping for returns. We hold each to maturity, so price swings do not matter. We use ordinary coupon Treasuries, not TIPS or STRIPS.

Our definition of a "high degree of certainty": the payments are covered by bonds already owned, so the only realistic way to miss one is a U.S. Treasury default. If $300,000 cannot quite buy every beam, the 2028 contribution finishes the foundation before any stock is bought. Coupons before 2033 get reinvested at unknown rates, so we keep a small cushion.

The garden

From 2028, every dollar beyond the foundation goes into low-cost index funds spread across U.S. and international stocks. The garden's job is to outgrow inflation, which will not change the $50,000 payments but will make the facility pricier. Her living costs are covered elsewhere and the payments are already safe, so this is where she can afford real stock risk, and nowhere else.

Seasons

2027 to 2030: the foundation is built and the garden grows. We rebalance only if stocks drift five points from target, never by selling the foundation.

2031: Laura talks to co-sponsors, so we harvest. About half the garden moves into short Treasuries maturing in 2033; the rest stays in stocks. The harvested half is the bottom of her range and the stock half is the top, so the low number is one we are very confident in and the high number needs good markets. We would rather she promise less than lose credibility. The final range and confidence level come in the Final Report.

2033 onward: the foundation becomes the operating reserve. It stays in Treasuries and never holds stocks. Each payment comes from the bond that just matured.

Facility contribution and flexibility

Laura contributes from the garden only, never the reserve. We also suggest holding part of the garden back, because committing everything could limit her flexibility while the project develops.

Liquidity and risk

Treasuries and broad index funds are easy to sell, so nothing is locked up. The trade-off is real: if stocks fall hard just before 2033, the facility contribution shrinks and co-sponsors may need to cover more. The payments do not change. We chose a smaller garden over a shaky foundation.

Three rules

1. Foundation first, always.
2. Only money the foundation does not need takes stock risk.
3. Review every January; change the plan only if Laura's goals change, not because of a headline.
