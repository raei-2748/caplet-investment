# H05
ARCHETYPE: Treasury bond ladder for the ten payments plus a core-satellite growth sleeve (S&P 500 core; satellites in tech/AI, semiconductors and Taiwan).
COUNTRY / LEVEL: Singapore (top independent school, members in a fintech CCA; strong maths)
STRATEGY NAME: Lock & Launch
## PITCH
Lock first, then launch. Laura's 2027 deposit buys a ladder of U.S. Treasury notes maturing just before each of her ten $50,000 payments, so the operating commitment is funded on day one. Her 2028 deposit powers a core-satellite growth engine, anchored in the S&P 500, for the residency's facility.
## IPS
Central idea
Laura's goals contain two very different kinds of money. The ten $50,000 payments are fixed, dated and promised: they need certainty, not returns. The facility contribution is flexible and has six years to grow: it needs returns, not certainty. Mixing them in one portfolio forces a compromise that serves neither, so we separate them from the start. Lock, then Launch.

Lock: the Treasury ladder (2027)
In January 2027 we use Laura's first deposit to buy ten individual U.S. Treasury notes and bonds, one maturing shortly before each payment from 2033 to 2042. Each rung is sized so that its face value delivers $50,000 when the payment is due. Because Treasuries held to maturity repay face value whatever happens to interest rates or market prices, the ladder's outcome is known the day we buy it. At current yields of roughly 4.5-5%, the ladder costs close to Laura's $300,000. If it costs slightly more, the first part of the 2028 deposit completes the longest rungs; if less, the leftover joins the growth engine. Coupons received before the payments are reinvested in short-term Treasuries (SGOV) as a cushion.

In 2033 the ladder simply becomes the operating reserve. Its composition does not need to change: each year a rung matures and funds that year's payment, and the reserve winds down to zero in 2042. We define high certainty as every payment backed by U.S. government obligations held to maturity, with no forced sale of any risky asset. The remaining risks, a U.S. default and lower rates on reinvested coupons, are very small.

Launch: the core-satellite growth engine (2028-2033)
The 2028 deposit, after completing the ladder, is invested for growth:
- Core, 70%: VOO (S&P 500), low-cost and broad.
- Satellites, 30%: QQQ (10%), SMH semiconductors (10%) and EWT Taiwan (10%).
The satellites reflect our conviction that AI and semiconductors will drive the next decade, and that Taiwan, home to TSMC and to Laura's residency, sits at the centre of it. Satellites are capped at 30%; any satellite above 15% is trimmed back into the core each January.

From 2031: protect the range
Laura approaches co-sponsors in 2031, two years before the gift. At that point we move half of the growth engine into Treasury notes maturing in late 2032. That locked half becomes the floor of the range she communicates; the top adds the expected value of the remaining equity. A bad market can lower the top, never the floor, so Laura never promises money she might not have.

Facility contribution and flexibility
In 2033 Laura contributes the locked floor plus part of the equity half, keeping at least 25% of the growth engine invested as her flexibility fund. Because the payments were funded in 2027, every dollar of the facility contribution is true surplus: nothing she gives can endanger the operating commitment.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: the strongest structure of the eight: payments locked at purchase, a clean 2031 floor-plus-upside range, and a facility gift that cannot touch the reserve.
Blind spot: QQQ + SMH + EWT is one concentrated bet on semiconductors and Taiwan, the same place the residency lives, so a Taiwan shock hits gift and project together; the ladder's cost versus $300,000 is waved through, and "rung sized by face value" ignores that coupons also pay.
