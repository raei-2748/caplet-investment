# A06
STRATEGY NAME: Three Acts, One Promise
## PITCH
Three Acts, One Promise: first, Treasury bonds fully fund Laura's ten $50,000 payments. Second, surplus capital grows in low-cost global equities. Third, gains are progressively locked in before co-sponsor talks in 2031. Only surplus beyond the secured promise is ever offered to the residency facility.
## IPS
**Central idea.** Like a good story, Laura's plan has structure. Act One secures the promise, Act Two earns the upside, and Act Three protects what was earned before it is shared. The ten operating payments are non-negotiable, so they are funded first and kept separate. Growth is pursued only with money not needed for that promise.

**Objective.** Fund all ten $50,000 payments (2033-2042) with a high degree of certainty, then offer the residency a responsible facility contribution from genuine surplus while keeping Laura flexible.

**Defining certainty.** A payment is certain when a U.S. Treasury bond, held to maturity and maturing shortly before the payment date, covers it, rather than a forecast of market returns. Equities never count toward the operating reserve. Because the payments are fixed and not inflation-adjusted, matching them with fixed Treasury cash flows removes market, interest-rate and reinvestment risk, leaving only U.S. government default risk.

**Act One: Secure (2027-2028).** The 2027 contribution first buys individual Treasury bonds whose maturities and coupons match the ten payments. If that falls slightly short, the 2028 contribution completes the ladder. No TIPS, STRIPS, derivatives, leverage or shorting are used. Held to maturity, interim price swings do not matter.

**Act Two: Grow (2028-2031).** Everything beyond the ladder goes into broad, low-cost, diversified equity ETFs: U.S. large companies as the core, alongside international developed and emerging markets. This sleeve is Laura's growth engine and the only source of facility money. Her entrepreneurial income and willingness to take thoughtful risks justify it, but it stays structurally separate so it cannot threaten the payments. We rebalance annually, never in reaction to headlines.

**Act Three: Protect (2029-2033).** Because Laura names a range to co-sponsors in 2031 and credibility matters, the growth sleeve gradually moves into short-term Treasuries. The shift is scheduled by date, not market opinion, so equities are a small share by 2031. The communicated range is therefore built mainly from secured assets plus a conservative share of equity upside. Favourable markets raise the contribution; unfavourable markets narrow it without touching the payments.

**Operating reserve after 2033.** At the start of 2033 the ladder becomes the operating reserve. It stays entirely in Treasuries and shortens naturally as bonds mature and payments are made. No new risk is added during the payment years.

**Facility contribution and flexibility.** The facility draws only on surplus above the reserve. Laura contributes a deliberate majority of that surplus, not all of it, retaining a portion for cost overruns, delays and opportunities as the residency develops. If markets disappoint, the contribution shrinks first; the payments never do.

**Liquidity.** Treasuries and large ETFs are highly liquid, so cash is available quickly if plans change.

**Inflation and assumptions.** Inflation does not alter the fixed payments but may raise facility costs, so equity exposure offers some protection. We assume moderate, not best-case, equity returns and test weaker markets and lower interest rates.

**Discipline.** Every decision faces one question: does it protect the promise before it pursues the upside?
