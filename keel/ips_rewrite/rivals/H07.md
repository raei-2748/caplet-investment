## PITCH
Data, not guesses. Using 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations, we place Laura's portfolio on the efficient frontier at the highest Sharpe ratio her goals allow, then size a Treasury-led operating reserve that funds all ten $50,000 payments in at least 95% of simulated futures, freeing the rest for the residency.
## IPS
Central idea
Laura studied Statistics at Wharton, so we built her strategy the way she might: with data. Every decision below comes from a Monte Carlo model of 10,000 simulated futures using expected returns, volatilities and correlations for each asset class.

Capital market assumptions (nominal, annual)
- U.S. equity (VTI): 7.0% expected return, 16% volatility
- International equity (VXUS): 7.5%, 18%
- Intermediate Treasuries (IEF): 4.2%, 6%
- Long Treasuries (TLT): 4.6%, 12%
- Treasury bills (SGOV): 3.5%, 1%
Correlations: U.S./international equity 0.85, equity/Treasuries -0.1. Inflation 2.5% affects facility costs, not the fixed payments.

Phase 1, 2027-2030: the tangency portfolio
We computed the efficient frontier from these inputs and chose the maximum-Sharpe portfolio subject to a 60% equity cap: 35% VTI, 20% VXUS, 35% IEF, 10% TLT. Expected return 5.9%, volatility 9.1%, Sharpe ratio 0.26. No other mix in our model offers more return per unit of risk. We rebalance quarterly to these weights.

Phase 2, 2031-2032: de-risking
Each year we move 15% of the portfolio from equity into Treasury notes maturing between 2033 and 2042, so the 2033 portfolio is about 25% equity. In our simulations this cuts the standard deviation of the 2033 portfolio value from about $70,000 to about $40,000, which narrows the range Laura can communicate.

Phase 3, 2033-2042: the operating reserve
We size the reserve as the smallest amount that funds all ten payments in at least 95% of the 10,000 simulated paths. It holds 85% Treasury notes matched to the payment dates and 15% U.S. equity (VTI), falling to zero equity by 2038. In the current model this requires about $445,000. A fully matched Treasury reserve would be safer but has no upside; the equity slice lets the reserve earn more than its cost.

Defining high certainty
We define a high degree of certainty as at least a 95% simulated probability that every payment is met. We also stress-test the reserve against a 2008-style shock (equities -40%, yields -150bp) and a 1970s-style inflation shock.

Facility contribution
The facility pool is everything above the reserve. Laura contributes the 25th percentile of its simulated 2033 value minus a 15% flexibility buffer, so in three futures out of four she keeps more flexibility than planned, not less.

Co-sponsor range
In 2031 we rerun the model from the actual portfolio value. The range Laura communicates is the 10th to 90th percentile of the simulated facility contribution, giving 80% confidence that her 2033 contribution falls inside it.

Model risk
The model assumes lognormal returns and stable correlations, which understate crashes. We therefore cap equity at 60%, use conservative return assumptions and re-estimate every input each January.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: explicit, internally consistent assumptions, a stated confidence level for both the payments and the 2031 range, and honest model-risk caveats; judges see real analysis.
Slip / blind spot: 95% means a 1-in-20 chance of breaking a fixed promise, and the reserve keeps equity to "earn" when a matched ladder would be cheaper and certain; the 2033 gift is set at a simulated percentile of a value that is already known by then; mean-variance optimises the asset mix while ignoring the liability, and the numbers-heavy text cuts against the IPS brief.
