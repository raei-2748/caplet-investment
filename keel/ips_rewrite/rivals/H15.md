# H15
ARCHETYPE: Safe-withdrawal-rate team; applies the 4% rule / Trinity study thinking to the $50k payments with a ~60/40 total-return portfolio
COUNTRY / LEVEL: US (suburban Ohio high school); personal-finance fans who read Bogleheads; clear, numerate, a bit formulaic
STRATEGY NAME: The Sustainable Ten
## PITCH
Laura's residency needs income she can count on for ten years. We adapt proven safe-withdrawal research to a low-cost 60/40 global portfolio, sizing the operating reserve so $50,000 a year survives even the worst historical markets, and contributing to the facility only what keeps that withdrawal rate safe.
## IPS
Our goal
Laura must withdraw $50,000 at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042, and she wants to contribute to her residency's facility without putting those payments at risk. This is a classic withdrawal problem, the same one retirees face, and decades of research tell us how to solve it.

Central idea: a sustainable withdrawal rate
William Bengen's 1994 study and the 1998 Trinity study found that a balanced portfolio could support an inflation-adjusted 4% withdrawal for 30 years in nearly every historical period. Laura's situation differs in two important ways. First, her horizon is only ten years, so she can safely spend down principal. Second, her payments are fixed at $50,000, not inflation-adjusted, which makes them easier to sustain. We ran the same historical method on rolling ten-year periods since 1926. A 60/40 portfolio supported a fixed withdrawal of about 9% of its starting value in every period we tested, including those beginning in 1929, 1966 and 2000.

Portfolio construction
- Total-return portfolio: 40% US stocks (VTI), 20% international stocks (VXUS), 40% US bonds (BND).
- Low cost: all three funds charge under 0.10%.
- Rebalanced once a year to target, which forces us to buy low and sell high.
- Both contributions follow the same mix. We do not try to time markets.

Operating reserve (2033)
Using the 9% rate, Laura's operating reserve should be about $555,000 ($50,000 / 0.09). That is the amount we believe sustains all ten payments with a high degree of certainty. To reduce sequence-of-returns risk, the danger that a crash early in the payout years does lasting damage, we use a "bond tent": the reserve moves to 40/60 stocks/bonds in 2033 and keeps one to two years of payments in short-term Treasuries (SHY). As payments are made, the reserve becomes steadily more conservative, and by 2040 it is mostly bonds and cash.

Defining high certainty
We define high certainty as at least a 95% success rate in both historical backtests and Monte Carlo simulation. Assumptions: stocks 7% a year, bonds 4%, inflation 2.5%.

Facility contribution
Our projections, $300,000 in 2027 and $150,000 in 2028 at about 6% a year, suggest a 2033 portfolio near $625,000. After the reserve, that leaves around $70,000 for the facility in an average outcome. If markets do better, the reserve stays the same and the facility gets the surplus; if markets do worse, the reserve is filled first and the facility contribution shrinks, possibly to zero. That order of priorities is what protects the payments.

Flexibility
Because the reserve is sized for the worst historical periods, average markets will leave it with a surplus by 2042. That surplus is Laura's flexibility: money she can use for overruns or new projects after the payments are complete.

Decision rules
1. Fill the reserve first, at a 9% withdrawal rate.
2. Keep the 60/40 mix until 2033, then shift to the bond tent.
3. Rebalance annually; never sell in a panic.
4. In 2031, Laura can share our latest projection with co-sponsors.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: grounded, well-known research translated thoughtfully to a 10-year fixed-nominal horizon, sequence-risk awareness (bond tent), clear priority order and transparent arithmetic.
Blind spot: a 95% backtest success rate on a 60/40 reserve is not a "high degree of certainty" for fixed dated payments and needs far more capital than a matched Treasury ladder, leaving a small facility contribution; it effectively forgets the 2031 co-sponsor range.
