# H18
ARCHETYPE: "Anchor and Sail" / lighthouse style team: safe anchor for the payments, sail for growth, nautical metaphor
COUNTRY / LEVEL: UK (sixth-form economics society, Year 12-13); strong, AI-assisted, heavily edited
STRATEGY NAME: Anchor and Sail

## PITCH
Every voyage needs an anchor and a sail. Our anchor, high-quality US Treasuries sized to Laura's ten $50,000 payments, holds firm in any storm. Our sail, a diversified global equity portfolio, catches the wind for her Taiwan residency. As 2033 nears, we reef the sail, never cut the anchor.

## IPS
1. Our objective
Ten fixed payments of $50,000 from 2033 to 2042 must be met with high certainty; the facility contribution should be as large as responsibly possible. We separate the portfolio into two parts with different jobs, so neither goal compromises the other.

2. The Anchor (operating commitment)
The Anchor holds enough US Treasuries to fund all ten payments, measured at today's interest rates, plus a 10% margin. It is built in 2027 from the first contribution and topped up in 2028 if needed. We use a mix of short and intermediate Treasury ETFs (SHY, IEF) and long Treasuries (TLT) weighted so the Anchor's interest-rate sensitivity is close to that of the payments; if rates rise, the Anchor and the cost of the payments both fall.

We define "high degree of certainty" as at least 95% probability, in our modelling, that the Anchor alone pays every instalment, with no help from equities or outside funding. We test this against historical interest-rate shocks and a sustained 2% fall in yields.

3. The Sail (growth and the facility)
Everything above the Anchor is the Sail: 70% global equities (VT), 20% US large-cap (VOO) and 10% short-term Treasuries (SGOV) for dry powder. Global diversification matters because Laura's future lies outside the US. Expected equity return in our model is about 6-7% a year, with a realistic chance of losing a third in a bad year.

4. Reefing the sail: how the portfolio changes
2027-2030: full sail. The Sail stays fully invested; we rebalance annually back to target weights.
2031-2032: reefing. Each year we move a third of the Sail's gains into short Treasuries, so most of the facility contribution is no longer exposed to a crash just before 2033.
2033 onwards: the Anchor becomes the operating reserve. Each January, the reserve pays $50,000 and shortens its duration, moving from long bonds to short bonds to cash as the final payments approach, so the money for the next payment is never at the mercy of rates.

5. The lighthouse: the 2031 range
In 2031 Laura will see the coast but not the harbour. Our range will be set from the reefed part of the Sail, which is largely secure, up to a level the remaining equities reach in roughly three out of four scenarios. She should never promise the top of the range.

6. Flexibility
After the facility contribution Laura should retain a meaningful portion of the Sail, which we will size in the Final Report, so the residency can adapt without her going back to co-sponsors.

7. Discipline
We will not change the Anchor's size in response to market news, will not time the market, and will rebalance only on a set annual date. The Anchor is never used to rescue the Sail.

Why it fits Laura
She is comfortable with entrepreneurial risk, but her promise to the residency is not hers to gamble. Anchor and Sail lets her be bold and reliable at the same time.

## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: very readable, confident structure; a numerical certainty definition (95%) and a stress test; explicit glide path and an annual discipline rule; the duration-matching line shows real fixed-income knowledge.
Blind spot / slip: the Anchor is bond FUNDS with no maturity, so "certainty" is a modelled 95% rather than locked cash flows, and the 10% margin plus duration match must be rebalanced continually; the 2031 range floor rests on equity gains being "reefed", so in a bad market the floor itself is small and undefined.
