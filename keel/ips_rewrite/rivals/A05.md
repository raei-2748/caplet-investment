# A05
STRATEGY NAME: Promise First
## PITCH
Promise First: fund Laura's ten $50,000 payments first, using a Treasury ladder that matures as each falls due. Her 2028 contribution grows in diversified global equities, then shifts toward Treasuries approaching 2031. Facility money is only what remains, so promises to co-sponsors stay credible.
## IPS
**Objective.** Fund Laura's ten fixed $50,000 operating payments (2033-2042) with a high degree of certainty, then contribute as much to the residency facility as she can responsibly afford while staying flexible.

**Central idea: promise first, then growth.** The payments are a fixed, dated liability, so they are matched with assets whose cash arrives on the same dates. Only money beyond that obligation is exposed to market risk. Growth is pursued with surplus capital, never with money Laura has already committed.

**Operating reserve sleeve.** Her 2027 contribution buys US Treasury notes and bonds maturing at or just before each payment date, held to maturity. If yields fall and the ladder costs more than that contribution, the 2028 contribution completes it before anything else is bought. Coupons are reinvested in short Treasuries. We define "high certainty" as every payment funded in every scenario we test, including a decade of falling rates and a severe equity crash. Payments rest on government principal and interest, not market prices; the residual risk is the US government itself. Individual TIPS and STRIPS are not used.

**Growth sleeve.** Whatever remains of the 2028 contribution is invested for the facility in low-cost, broadly diversified global equity ETFs plus some Treasuries. Diversification avoids dependence on any single company or theme. Laura is an entrepreneur with outside income and no withdrawals before 2033, so this sleeve can carry real equity risk for several years and needs no cash drag.

**Glide path to 2031.** In 2031 Laura must tell co-sponsors what she can give. Equity exposure therefore falls in steps from 2029, and gains are locked into short Treasuries by rules, not forecasts. By 2031 a meaningful share of the sleeve is a known amount; that becomes the low end of the range she communicates, and remaining equity exposure supports the high end. We commit only to a range defensible in bad markets and state our confidence plainly.

**Facility contribution.** In 2033 the reserve is set aside first and left untouched. Laura then contributes from the growth sleeve only, holding back part of the surplus so the project can absorb overruns and opportunities. In weak markets the facility contribution shrinks; the operating payments never do.

**Reserve after 2033.** The ladder shortens naturally as payments are made. Holding Treasuries to maturity, rather than selling early, keeps later payments free from price swings. Any surplus the ladder releases joins the facility pool.

**Trade-offs.** Risk and return: risk lives only in surplus money. Liquidity: Treasuries are liquid in an emergency, though we would not plan on selling. Reliability: maximized by matching. Flexibility: preserved by a retained surplus and a modest contribution cap.

**Discipline.** We rebalance on set dates, not headlines, and review annually, after rate moves of one percentage point or more, or if Laura's circumstances change. We use no margin, shorting, derivatives or crypto, and our WInS portfolio mirrors this structure so demonstrated decisions match stated strategy.
