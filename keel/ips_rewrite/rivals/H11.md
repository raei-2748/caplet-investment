# H11
ARCHETYPE: Goals-based "two pots" team; a Promise Pot for the ten payments and a Possibility Pot for the facility, built on mental accounting and behavioural finance
COUNTRY / LEVEL: Australia (selective high school, Sydney); economics students, reflective and warm; edited AI drafts heavily
STRATEGY NAME: Two Pots, Two Jobs
## PITCH
Two pots, two jobs. The Promise Pot holds US Treasuries maturing as each of Laura's ten $50,000 payments falls due, untouched for anything else. The Possibility Pot invests globally for growth and becomes her facility contribution, so a bad market can shrink her dream but never break her promise.
## IPS
Our goal
Laura's money has two very different jobs: keep a ten-year promise to the residency, and help build something new. One portfolio would force every decision to trade one against the other, so we separate them.

Why two pots
Richard Thaler showed that people naturally sort money into "mental accounts". Economists usually call this a bias. Goals-based investing, developed by researchers including Harry Markowitz and Meir Statman, turns it into a tool: give each goal its own portfolio, matched to its own deadline and its own tolerance for failure. Kahneman and Tversky's work on loss aversion explains why this matters for Laura. If the payments and the facility share one pot, a crash in 2032 feels like a threat to everything, and the temptation is to sell at the worst time. With two pots, a crash only touches the dream, never the promise.

The Promise Pot (the ten payments)
- Job: deliver $50,000 at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042, with a high degree of certainty.
- Holdings: individual US Treasury notes and bonds, one rung maturing just before each payment, held to maturity.
- Funding: filled first, from the 2027 deposit; any shortfall is the first call on the 2028 deposit.
- Certainty: no payment depends on a market price, a return forecast or outside money; only a US government default could break it.
- In 2033 the Promise Pot simply becomes the operating reserve. Its make-up does not change; it gets smaller each year as rungs mature and are paid out, and ends in 2042.

The Possibility Pot (the facility)
- Job: grow money for the facility while giving co-sponsors a number they can trust.
- Holdings 2027-2030: about 75% global shares (VT) and 25% intermediate Treasuries (IEF), rebalanced yearly. Laura has taken thoughtful risks her whole career, and this pot can afford to.
- Glide path from 2031: we move a set share of the pot into Treasuries maturing at the start of 2033 each year, so most of it is locked by early 2033.

Talking to co-sponsors in 2031
The bottom of Laura's range is the amount already locked in Treasuries plus what the remaining shares would be worth after a severe fall. The top assumes ordinary growth. Because the floor is owned, not forecast, we expect to state high confidence that her contribution lands inside it. Bad markets lower the top, never the floor, and never the payments.

Keeping flexibility: a "Breathing Room" share
Committing every dollar would leave Laura no room if costs rise. We recommend she pledges most of the Possibility Pot and keeps a fixed share back in short Treasuries. This is not a separate fund; it is a rule that protects her from her own generosity.

How we will make decisions
1. The Promise Pot is funded before anything else and never sold.
2. Each pot is judged only against its own job.
3. Risk falls in the Possibility Pot as 2033 approaches.

Assumptions: Treasuries earn their yield at purchase; global shares earn moderate long-run returns with real crashes possible; inflation does not affect the fixed payments but may raise facility costs, which the Breathing Room share helps absorb.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: crystal-clear, memorable structure with genuine behavioural-finance reasoning that maps neatly onto the case (promise vs dream, owned floor in 2031).
Blind spot: the pots framing does a lot of the work; key sizes (Breathing Room share, glide-path pace, confidence level) stay vague, and it runs a little over 500 words.
