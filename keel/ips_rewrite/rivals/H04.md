# H04
ARCHETYPE: Endowment-model team; Yale-style diversification across US/intl/EM stocks, REITs, gold and Treasuries, with a spending-rule mindset for the payments.
COUNTRY / LEVEL: United States (New England boarding/prep school, investing elective taught by a former endowment analyst)
STRATEGY NAME: The Laura Gao Endowment
## PITCH
We manage Laura's money like a small university endowment: diversified across U.S., international and emerging-market stocks, real estate, gold and Treasuries, rebalanced by rule. A Treasury operating reserve, built gradually from 2030, secures her ten payments; a smoothed spending rule sets a facility contribution she can keep.
## IPS
Central idea
The best long-term investors we studied in our investing elective are university endowments. Yale's model, built by David Swensen, rests on four ideas: own many different engines of return, keep a strong bias toward equity-like assets over long horizons, rebalance with discipline, and spend by rule rather than by mood. Laura is, in effect, creating a small institution: a residency that needs reliable funding for ten years. We think she should invest like one.

Phase 1: the diversified core (2027-2030)
Target allocation, rebalanced whenever any asset moves five points from target:
- U.S. stocks (VTI) 30%
- Developed international stocks (VEA) 15%
- Emerging-market stocks (VWO) 10%
- Real estate (VNQ) 10%
- Gold (GLD) 5%
- U.S. Treasuries (IEF, TLT) 25%
- Inflation-protected Treasuries (TIPS) 5%
No asset class exceeds 30%, and each responds differently to growth, inflation, recession and crisis. Yale uses private equity and hedge funds; we cannot, so we rely on breadth, low-cost public ETFs and rebalancing instead. The 2028 contribution is used to rebalance toward whatever is most underweight.

Phase 2: building the operating reserve (2030-2033)
An endowment separates money it must spend from money it can invest for the long run. Starting in January 2030, we move one quarter of the reserve target each year out of the core and into individual Treasury notes maturing just before each payment from 2033 to 2042. Moving gradually means we are never forced to convert everything in one bad year. By January 2033 the reserve holds Treasuries whose maturities and interest equal the ten $50,000 payments, plus a 5% buffer in short-term Treasuries (SGOV). After that its composition does not change: each note matures, the payment is made, and the reserve winds down to zero in 2042.

What high certainty means
Because the reserve is matched with U.S. Treasuries held to maturity, the payments do not depend on markets after 2033. The real risk is the build-up period, which is why we start in 2030 and spread it over four years. Our test: if the core fell 35% in 2029, as it did in 2008, could the reserve still be completed by 2033? Our projections in the Final Report must answer yes.

Facility contribution: a spending rule
Endowments do not spend whatever their portfolio is worth on a lucky day; they smooth. We apply the same mindset. In 2033 Laura contributes no more than 80% of the core, measured on its average value over 2031-2033 rather than its January value, so one strong or weak year cannot inflate or crush the gift. The remaining 20% stays invested in the diversified core as her flexibility fund. The same smoothing gives her a calm way to talk to co-sponsors in 2031: the range is anchored to a three-year average, not a single market day.

Governance
Rebalance annually and at five-point bands. Every fund costs less than 0.15% a year. Leave the strategy alone when markets are exciting.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: real investing literacy (Swensen's principles, honest about lacking private assets), a clever gradual reserve build and a spending-rule idea that gives the 2031 range a story.
Slip / blind spot: names TIPS (individual TIPS are banned); EM, REITs and gold add volatility a six-year horizon cannot absorb, and averaging 2031-2033 values lets Laura give more than she has after a 2032 crash, the opposite of protecting her.
