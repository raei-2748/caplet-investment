# H09
ARCHETYPE: Liability-matching purist; every dollar in Treasuries matched to the payments, facility = the residual, no equities
COUNTRY / LEVEL: UK (sixth form, London); one member reads FT Alphaville daily; precise, dry, very strong on bond mechanics
STRATEGY NAME: Match First
## PITCH
Laura's ten $50,000 payments are a debt with a date on it, so we treat them as one. Both contributions buy US Treasuries maturing just before each payment, 2033 to 2042. No stocks, no forecasts, no hope required. Whatever the matched ladder does not need becomes her facility contribution.
## IPS
Purpose
Laura has one obligation that cannot fail, ten fixed $50,000 payments from 2033 to 2042, and one ambition that can flex, the facility. We match the obligation first, with certainty, and fund the ambition from what remains.

Central idea: match, don't forecast
A fixed nominal payment on a known date is, financially, a bond Laura has already sold. The cleanest way to meet it is to own a bond that pays the same amount on the same date. We will buy individual US Treasury notes and bonds maturing shortly before each payment, sized so that principal plus coupons cover each $50,000. Pension funds call this liability-driven investing. Its best-known failure, the 2022 UK gilt crisis, came from leverage and derivatives; an unlevered ladder held to maturity faces no margin call and no forced sale.

Why no equities
Laura is willing to take thoughtful risk, but nothing in her goals requires it. Over seven to sixteen years, stocks are not reliably safe: the S&P 500 returned less than nothing over 2000-2009. Equities in our plan would either sit beside the payments, adding risk to the one thing that cannot fail, or sit in the facility money, making Laura's 2031 promise to co-sponsors less credible. We accept a smaller expected facility contribution in exchange for one she can announce and keep.

Implementation
- 2027: the $300,000 buys the ladder, longest maturities first, because distant payments are cheapest per dollar today and most exposed if yields fall.
- 2028: the $150,000 first completes any unbought rungs; the remainder buys Treasuries maturing at the start of 2033.
- Coupons received before 2033 are reinvested in Treasury bills maturing by 2033.
- No rebalancing and no rate views. Held to maturity, interim price swings do not touch the funding.

Operating reserve and certainty
In 2033 the operating reserve is simply the ladder already in place. Its composition never changes: each rung matures into the payment it was bought for, and the reserve runs down to zero in 2042. We define a high degree of certainty as every payment funded by US government cash flows held to maturity: no asset sale, no return assumption, no outside money. Residual risks, US default and coupon reinvestment, are remote. We stress-tested a fall in yields before the 2028 purchase: the extra cost comes out of the facility, never the payments.

Facility contribution and 2031
The facility contribution is the residual: the 2033 value of the short Treasuries plus surplus coupons. Because nearly everything is fixed by 2028, Laura can quote co-sponsors a tight range at very high confidence in 2031. Good markets will not raise it, and we say so plainly. We recommend she commits most, not all, of the residual, keeping a modest share in Treasury bills as a buffer for overruns.

Assumptions
Expected return equals Treasury yields at purchase. Inflation cannot erode fixed nominal payments, but it will raise the facility's cost; we leave that risk visible rather than hedge it with assets that could endanger the payments.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: rigorous, honest cash-flow matching with a clear certainty definition, a narrow and credible 2031 range, and confident British prose that judges will find refreshing.
Blind spot: "stocks have no place" ignores Laura's stated appetite for thoughtful risk and leaves a small, flat facility contribution; it also glosses over coupon-reinvestment risk and the chance the 2027 money cannot buy the whole ladder.
