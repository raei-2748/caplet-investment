# H24
ARCHETYPE: Real-options / staged-commitment team: the facility contribution treated like a venture investment with stage gates; keeps optionality, commits in tranches; startup-world language
COUNTRY / LEVEL: Israel (Tel Aviv-area high school, entrepreneurship programme, grade 11-12); strong, AI-assisted, heavily edited
STRATEGY NAME: Staged Commitment

## PITCH
Investors never fund a startup's full plan on day one; they fund milestones. We apply that to Laura's residency. Ten $50,000 payments are locked in US Treasuries from day one. The facility contribution is released in stages, each unlocked by market and project milestones, so Laura keeps her options open.

## IPS
The problem, as founders see it
Laura is an entrepreneur launching a new venture in 2033. Like any founder, she faces two kinds of money: committed burn she must never miss, the ten $50,000 operating payments from 2033 to 2042, and growth capital, her contribution to the facility. Startups that commit everything before they know the real costs run out of runway. Our strategy keeps Laura's options alive until information arrives.

Runway first: the operating commitment
The payments are Laura's runway, and runway is not speculated with. In 2027 we use the first contribution to buy US Treasuries maturing just before each payment date, held to maturity: ten payments, ten maturities. Held to maturity, their payout is fixed whatever markets do, so the commitment depends only on the US government. That is our standard of high certainty: no payment relies on a return assumption. If the 2027 money is not enough, the 2028 contribution completes the runway first.

Growth capital: the surplus
Everything after the runway is invested for growth: 75% VT and 25% QQQ, because innovation-driven companies have led global returns and Laura's own career sits in technology and creative industries. We rebalance once a year and assume about 7% a year with deep drawdowns possible.

Stage gates: how the facility contribution is committed
We treat the facility contribution as three tranches, each with a gate:
- Gate 1 (2031, co-sponsor pitch): Laura announces a range. The bottom is a "seed tranche" we move into short Treasuries in 2031, so it is locked. The top is the seed tranche plus later tranches that depend on markets.
- Gate 2 (start of 2033, launch): the seed tranche is paid. A second tranche is paid if the growth portfolio is above its target and co-sponsors have committed their share.
- Gate 3 (2034-2035, after launch): a final tranche is released only if the residency is operating on plan and the portfolio has recovered from any downturn.
Each gate is a real option: Laura has the right, not the obligation, to invest more. In bad markets she simply does not exercise it, and nothing she has promised is broken.

How the portfolio evolves
2027-2030: runway locked, growth capital fully invested.
2031-2032: seed tranche de-risked into Treasuries; the rest stays invested.
2033 onward: the runway becomes the operating reserve and shortens by one maturity every year. Remaining growth capital stays invested as Laura's flexibility fund and the source of later tranches.

Why this beats a single number
A single 2033 commitment forces Laura to bet on one moment in the market. Staging spreads that risk across time and links money to evidence about the project, which is how co-sponsors already think. It protects her credibility because every promise is sized to a tranche already secured.

Why it fits Laura
Laura has always pursued unconventional opportunities with a strategic mindset. This is her own playbook: secure the base, test the idea, scale what works.

## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: a fresh, fully coherent frame (runway = held-to-maturity ladder, tranches = real options), keeps the floor locked, gives flexibility a mechanism rather than a slogan, and speaks a language many judges like.
Blind spot / slip: the case asks for the facility contribution in 2033 and for co-sponsors' range then, so post-2033 tranches (Gate 3) stretch the brief; conditioning a tranche on co-sponsors' commitments mixes outside funding into the plan; the 25% QQQ tilt adds concentration for a story reason; "runway" wording for payments may confuse judges.
