STRATEGY NAME: Four Seasons
## PITCH
Markets have four seasons, and no one forecasts them reliably. Our risk-parity core spreads risk equally across global stocks, Treasuries, gold and commodities, so no single season can sink Laura's plan. From 2028 a Treasury ladder progressively takes over the ten $50,000 payments, while the balanced core funds her facility.
## IPS
Our objective
Laura needs two outcomes with very different tolerances: ten $50,000 payments from 2033 to 2042 that must not fail, and a facility contribution that should be as large as is responsible. We do not believe anyone can reliably predict which economic environment will arrive between now and 2033. Our strategy is therefore built to survive all of them.

Central idea: balance risk, not dollars
A traditional 60/40 portfolio looks diversified in dollars, but around 90% of its risk comes from stocks. Following the all-weather research on economic regimes, we see four "seasons": rising growth, falling growth, rising inflation and falling inflation. Each asset thrives in some and suffers in others. Risk parity weights each asset by its volatility so that each contributes a similar share of total risk.

Core portfolio (2027-2032)
Approximately 25% global equities (VT), 40% long Treasuries (TLT), 15% intermediate Treasuries (IEF), 10% gold (GLD) and 10% broad commodities (PDBC). We rebalance quarterly to equal risk contributions using one-year volatility. Leverage is not permitted, so our version is unlevered: we accept a lower expected return, roughly 5% a year, in exchange for much smaller drawdowns. 2022, when stocks and bonds fell together, is our main stress test: gold and commodities cushioned the core when the usual 60/40 relationship broke.

Building the operating reserve
A balanced core is excellent for growth with controlled risk, but it is not a promise. Fixed, dated payments need a fixed, dated asset. Starting with the 2028 deposit, we buy individual US Treasury notes and bonds that mature just before each payment date, longest maturities first. Each year until 2033 we transfer additional money from the core to complete the ladder, so that by the start of 2033 every payment is fully matched. This ladder is the operating reserve. Its composition does not change after 2033: each rung is held to maturity and paid out, and the reserve shrinks to zero by 2042.

High degree of certainty
We define it as each payment matched by a Treasury held to maturity, with no dependence on market prices or outside money. Before the ladder is complete, the risk is that falling yields make remaining rungs more expensive; our long Treasury holding in the core rises in value in exactly that case, partly hedging the cost.

Facility contribution and 2031
After the reserve is set aside, the core is the source of the facility contribution. Because risk parity targets a stable volatility, around 7-8% a year, we can estimate a statistical range for 2033 from 2031 values. Laura should communicate the band we expect with about 90% confidence. Favourable outcomes move her toward the top; a 2022-style shock moves her toward the bottom, but never touches the ladder. We recommend she commits about 80% of the remaining core and keeps 20% invested as flexibility for overruns.

Decision rules
1. The ladder is completed by 2033, whatever markets do.
2. The core stays risk-balanced; no forecasts.
3. Facility pledges are based on the 90% band, not the average.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: intellectually serious diversification argument with a real stress test (2022), and a correct recognition that dated payments need a matched Treasury ladder, with an elegant partial hedge argument.
Blind spot: the payments stay exposed to rate and core-drawdown risk until the ladder is finished in 2033, so "high certainty" is not secured early; the unlevered core is heavy in TLT and commodity futures ETFs, and a volatility-based 90% band understates tail risk.
