# A03
STRATEGY NAME: Fund the Promise, Then Grow
## PITCH
Fund the promise first, then grow the surplus. Treasury bonds matched to Laura's ten $50,000 payments, 2033-2042, held to maturity, make the residency's operating commitment insulated from market swings. Only the surplus goes into diversified equity ETFs, setting her facility contribution as a credible range.
## IPS
Objective. Laura's portfolio has two jobs, in priority order: fund ten fixed $50,000 operating payments from 2033 to 2042 with a high degree of certainty, then support the largest responsible contribution to the Taiwan residency's facility. Growth serves only the second job.

Central idea. Match each obligation with assets that mature when it falls due, and place market risk only in the surplus. A fixed, dated promise should be funded with fixed, dated assets, not with forecasts.

Principles. (1) Fund the liability before chasing return. (2) Hold the operating reserve to maturity; never trade it. (3) Take equity risk only with money that has years to recover. (4) Diversify: broad equity ETFs form the core, and no single stock exceeds 5% of the surplus. (5) Use only permitted instruments: stocks, ETFs and government/Treasury bonds. No margin, shorting, derivatives, TIPS or STRIPS.

Operating reserve. The 2027 deposit buys individual Treasury bonds that mature each year from 2033 to 2042, one per payment, each sized to $50,000 and maturing before its payment date. The 2028 deposit completes the ladder if the first deposit falls short, and otherwise flows to the surplus. Priced at current yields, the ten payments cost close to the first deposit. Held to maturity, nominal Treasuries pay fixed amounts, so the payments do not depend on equity markets or on interest-rate moves.

Reserve composition. It does not change. Bonds stay until maturity, no equity is added, each maturity funds that year's payment, and any amount above the remaining payments is released to the surplus.

Growth surplus. The remainder goes to a global equity ETF core with a Treasury bond buffer. Equity weight is 80% through 2030, 60% in 2031 and 40% from 2032. The step-down is planned, not reactive: as 2033 approaches, a late market fall would shrink the gift, so growth risk is retired on schedule.

Facility contribution. In 2031 Laura communicates a range rather than one figure. The low end is the amount the surplus should deliver in a poor-markets case; the high end is the median case. We state the confidence level in the Final Report; the low end never draws on the reserve. In 2033 she contributes from the surplus after the reserve, but keeps at least one-third uncommitted, because facility costs are still unknown.

Risk. The main risks are an equity drawdown in the surplus and a market fall close to 2033. Both reduce the facility contribution, not the operating commitment, because the reserve sits outside the growth sleeve. Payments are fixed nominal amounts under the case, so inflation affects facility costs rather than the payments; equity is the portfolio's inflation defense.

Liquidity. Treasuries and large ETFs trade daily. Laura's living costs sit outside the portfolio, so no forced sales are expected.

Review. Each January we test the strategy against four triggers: a change in Laura's commitments, a surplus below the glide path, a change in facility timing, and a rule breach. The team proposes changes; Laura approves them.
