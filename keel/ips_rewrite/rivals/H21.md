# H21
ARCHETYPE: Behavioural "seed money" team: fundraising research (leadership gifts, matching, signalling) shapes how the 2031 range is framed to co-sponsors; conservative-to-moderate portfolio
COUNTRY / LEVEL: US (public magnet high school, juniors and seniors; one member interned at a nonprofit development office); strong, AI-assisted, heavily edited
STRATEGY NAME: Lead Gift Strategy

## PITCH
Laura's money does two jobs: it guarantees ten $50,000 operating payments, and it acts as the lead gift that convinces others to give. Research shows donors follow a committed leader. So we protect the payments first, then grow a contribution she can announce as a firm floor plus a match.

## IPS
What we are trying to accomplish
Laura's portfolio will not build the residency alone. Its facility contribution matters as much for what it signals as for what it pays. Fundraising research consistently finds that announced "seed" or "leadership" gifts raise how much others give, because they show the project is real and that the founder has skin in the game. Matching offers increase participation too. But a lead gift that later shrinks destroys trust. So our strategy is built around one rule: never let Laura announce a number her portfolio cannot keep.

Protecting the operating commitment
The ten $50,000 payments come first. From 2027 we hold a reserve sleeve of US Treasuries sized to their present value with a 5% margin, using intermediate Treasury ETFs (IEF) in the early years and individual Treasury notes maturing before each payment from 2031 on, so each payment has its own bond by the time the reserve is formally set aside in 2033. We define high certainty as at least a 97% probability, in our simulations, that every payment is met from the reserve alone, even in a recession or a large fall in interest rates.

The growth sleeve
Everything else is invested moderately, not aggressively: 65% stocks (VTI and VXUS for broad US and international exposure, plus SCHD for steadier dividend payers) and 35% short and intermediate bonds (BND, SHY). We chose moderate rather than all-equity because the cost of a shortfall is not just money: a shrunken gift in 2033 would damage Laura's reputation with partners she has courted for two years. We rebalance once a year.

How the portfolio changes over time
2027-2030: reserve plus moderate growth.
2031: the reserve is converted to matched Treasury maturities; the growth sleeve begins moving 20% a year into short Treasuries.
2033: the reserve pays out one maturity per year and gets shorter each year; the growth sleeve funds the facility contribution.

Framing the 2031 range
In 2031 we will split Laura's announced contribution into two parts that match what donors respond to:
- A committed lead gift: the amount already moved into Treasuries, which she can promise with near-certainty.
- A matching pledge: further dollars Laura will add only if co-sponsors give, capped by what the growth sleeve can provide in most outcomes.
In a bad market, Laura still keeps her lead gift; only the match shrinks, and the match was always conditional. In a good market, the match is filled and co-sponsors feel their gift doubled. This turns market uncertainty into a reason to give rather than a reason to hesitate.

Flexibility
Laura should not contribute her whole surplus. We recommend she keep a portion invested after 2033 so she can respond to overruns or new opportunities without breaking a public promise.

Why this is right for Laura
Laura is a storyteller whose work changed how people saw a crisis. The residency's first story to co-sponsors will be her own commitment. Our strategy makes sure that story is true.
