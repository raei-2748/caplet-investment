# H12
ARCHETYPE: Storytelling team built on Laura's graphic novels; the strategy told in chapters and panels, comic-book metaphor throughout
COUNTRY / LEVEL: US (arts magnet high school, California); one member is an illustrator, another does the numbers; vivid voice
STRATEGY NAME: Ink Before Colour
## PITCH
Laura's next chapter needs a strong ending. We plan it like a graphic novel: we ink the ten $50,000 payments in US Treasuries that mature panel by panel from 2033 to 2042, then a growth fund draws the facility contribution, pencilled as a range in 2031 and inked in 2033.
## IPS
Chapter 1: The Setup
Every graphic novelist inks the outlines before adding colour, because the outlines hold the page together. Laura's outlines are her ten $50,000 payments from 2033 to 2042: fixed, dated, and promised. Her colour is the facility, the part of the residency that can be bolder. Our strategy follows that order.

Chapter 2: Ink the Payments
We buy individual US Treasury notes and bonds that mature just before each payment date, one "panel" per year, sized so principal and coupons equal $50,000. They are bought first, with the 2027 deposit; if rates leave a gap, the 2028 deposit finishes the row. Held to maturity, these bonds do not care what markets do in between. We define "high certainty" as payments that depend on no market forecast, no asset sale and no outside money; only a US government default could erase the ink.

Chapter 3: The Colour
Everything not needed for the payments goes into a growth sleeve: roughly 60% US stocks (VTI), 25% international stocks (VXUS) and 15% Taiwan (EWT). The Taiwan slice is deliberately small: it ties Laura's money to the place her residency will live, but we cap it so one market cannot decide her facility. We rebalance annually.

Chapter 4: The Gutter (2028-2031)
In comics the gutter is the white space between panels, where the reader imagines what happens. For Laura, that is the uncertain years between the deposits and 2031. We do not try to predict the gutter. We diversify, rebalance, and accept that the colour sleeve may rise or fall. Because the payments are already inked, a crash in the gutter changes the facility, never the commitment.

Chapter 5: Pencils (2031)
Artists sketch in pencil before committing to ink. In 2031 Laura pencils her contribution as a range. From 2031 we move a growing share of the colour sleeve into Treasuries maturing in early 2033. The bottom of the range is the value already locked in plus the rest after a severe market fall; the top assumes normal growth. Co-sponsors see a range drawn from money Laura already owns, not hopes.

Chapter 6: Ink (2033)
In 2033 the operating reserve is the Treasury ladder we inked in Chapter 2. Its composition does not change: each panel matures into its payment, and the reserve shrinks to zero by 2042. The facility contribution comes from the colour sleeve. Like a printed page, we leave a "bleed", a margin beyond the cut, by holding back part of the sleeve in short Treasuries for overruns and new ideas. Laura keeps flexibility without weakening her pledge.

Chapter 7: Our Rules
1. Ink first: payments are funded before growth.
2. No panel is ever sold early.
3. Risk in the colour sleeve falls as 2033 approaches.
4. Taiwan exposure stays a small slice.

Epilogue: Assumptions
Treasuries earn their purchase yield. Stocks earn moderate long-run returns with real crashes possible. Inflation does not change Laura's fixed payments, but it may raise facility costs, which is why we keep the bleed.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: memorable, client-centred voice that turns comic craft (ink, gutter, pencils, bleed) into real strategy logic, with a sound ladder-plus-growth structure judges can follow.
Blind spot: the 15% EWT slice adds concentrated single-country and currency risk for sentimental reasons; the metaphor sometimes stands in for specifics (glide-path pace, bleed size, confidence level).
