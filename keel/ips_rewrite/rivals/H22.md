ARCHETYPE: "Messy Roots" garden metaphor team: roots, branches, seeds, growth, pruning, tied to Laura's graphic memoir title
COUNTRY / LEVEL: Canada (Toronto-area high school investment club, grades 11-12); strong, AI-assisted, heavily edited, a little over the word limit
STRATEGY NAME: Deep Roots, Open Branches

## PITCH
Laura's memoir is called Messy Roots; her portfolio's roots will be anything but messy. Deep roots of US Treasuries, timed to her ten $50,000 payments, hold the residency steady. Open branches of global stocks grow her facility contribution. Each season we prune, so growth never weakens the roots.

## IPS
Our goal
Laura is planting something in Taiwan that she hopes will outlive the portfolio itself. Our job is to make sure the residency has roots that cannot be pulled up, ten payments of $50,000 from 2033 to 2042, while giving her the best responsible chance of a generous facility contribution.

The roots: the operating commitment
Before anything grows, the roots go in. In 2027 we invest enough of Laura's first contribution in US Treasury bonds to fund all ten payments: a laddered set of Treasuries maturing before each payment date, held until they mature. Because they are held to maturity, their value at each payment date is known today; interest-rate changes in between do not matter. Our definition of high certainty is that every payment is matched to a Treasury that repays at least $50,000 before it is due, so meeting the commitment relies on the US government and nothing else. If the first contribution cannot fully fund the roots, the 2028 contribution completes them before anything is planted elsewhere.

The trunk: a protected middle
Between the roots and the branches we keep a smaller layer of short-term Treasuries (SGOV, SHY), about 10% of the growth money. It gives Laura liquidity and lets us rebalance without selling stocks at bad prices.

The branches: the facility contribution
The rest is planted for growth in broad, low-cost index funds: VT for global stocks, with some VOO for the US market. Diversification is how a garden survives a bad season: one crop can fail without the garden failing. Over six years we expect stocks to grow around 6-7% a year on average but accept that they can fall 30% or more in a single year.

Pruning: how the portfolio changes
2027-2030: growing season. We rebalance once a year back to target.
2031-2032: harvest preparation. We prune a share of the branches each year into short Treasuries, so most of the facility contribution is picked before a late storm.
2033-2042: the roots become the operating reserve. Each year one Treasury matures and pays one instalment, so the reserve shrinks safely on its own and never needs to sell anything in a falling market.

Seeds: what Laura gives in 2033
The facility contribution comes only from the branches and trunk. We recommend Laura contributes the harvested portion and keeps a seed fund invested for later needs, because new projects always need more water than planned.

The 2031 range
The bottom of the range is what has already been harvested into Treasuries; the top is what the remaining branches can reasonably add by 2033. We will state our confidence level in the Final Report and explain how good and bad markets change both ends.

Inflation
The payments are fixed dollars, so the roots match them exactly. The facility's construction costs will rise with inflation, which is why the branches are invested in stocks, the asset most likely to grow faster than prices.

Why it fits Laura
Messy Roots is about growing up between two worlds and finding where you belong. This strategy gives Laura what she gave her readers: deep roots in something stable, and room to grow toward something new.
