# H16
ARCHETYPE: Tactical / momentum team; strategic core plus a tactical overlay shifting between stocks and bonds on a 200-day moving average and Fed-cycle signals
COUNTRY / LEVEL: India (CBSE senior secondary, Mumbai); quant-minded, coding club members; formal, dense prose
STRATEGY NAME: Core and Compass
## PITCH
Laura's payments deserve certainty; her facility deserves opportunity. A strategic core of US Treasuries locks in the ten $50,000 payments, while a rules-based Compass sleeve rotates between equities and Treasuries using the 200-day moving average and the Federal Reserve's rate cycle, capturing growth and stepping aside in deep bear markets.
## IPS
1. Objective
Laura's portfolio must (a) fund ten $50,000 payments from 2033 to 2042 with a high degree of certainty and (b) generate a responsible facility contribution in 2033 that she can communicate credibly from 2031. These goals have different risk tolerances. Hence we separate them into a Core and a Compass.

2. The Core: protecting the commitment
The Core consists of individual US Treasury notes and bonds maturing just before each payment date, sized so that principal plus coupons equal $50,000 per year. It is purchased first, from the 2027 contribution, with any shortfall completed from the 2028 contribution. The Core is held to maturity and is never subject to tactical signals. In 2033 it becomes the operating reserve; its composition remains unchanged, each bond maturing into its payment, and the reserve declines to zero by 2042. We define high certainty as every payment being funded by a US government security held to maturity, independent of market prices, forecasts or outside sources.

3. The Compass: growing the facility contribution
All remaining capital forms the Compass sleeve. Its strategic allocation is 70% equities (VOO 50%, VXUS 20%) and 30% intermediate Treasuries (IEF). Two disciplined rules adjust it:
- Trend rule: at each month-end, if the S&P 500 closes below its 200-day moving average, the equity portion moves to short-term Treasuries (SHY); it returns when the index closes back above. Research on trend-following (Faber, 2007; Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, 2012) shows such rules historically avoided much of the 2000-2002 and 2008 drawdowns.
- Rate-cycle rule: when the Federal Reserve begins cutting rates after a hiking cycle, the Treasury portion extends from IEF towards TLT to benefit from falling yields; when hikes resume, it returns to IEF.
Signals are reviewed monthly only, to limit whipsaw and transaction costs. We accept that trend rules lag sharp V-shaped recoveries, as in 2020; the objective is avoiding prolonged bear markets, not perfect timing.

4. Glide path and the 2031 range
From 2031 the overlay is switched off. Each year the Compass moves a fixed share into Treasuries maturing in early 2033, so that most of the facility money is locked in before it is needed. In 2031 Laura can therefore communicate a range: the lower bound is the value already locked plus the remaining equity after a severe decline; the upper bound assumes average returns. We will state our confidence level based on simulation.

5. Facility contribution and flexibility
After the operating reserve is established, the Compass funds the facility. We recommend Laura commits about 80% of it and retains 20% invested, preserving flexibility for cost overruns and future ventures without weakening the payments.

6. Return and risk assumptions
Equities 7% and intermediate Treasuries 4% per year over the long term; the trend rule is expected to reduce maximum drawdown substantially and to add modestly to risk-adjusted returns. Inflation does not affect the fixed nominal payments but may raise facility costs.

7. Decision rules
(i) Core first, never traded. (ii) Compass signals are mechanical, monthly and documented. (iii) No overlay after 2031. (iv) No leverage, short selling or derivatives.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: protects the payments properly with a held-to-maturity Core, and gives the overlay clear, documented, evidence-backed rules plus a sensible switch-off before 2031.
Blind spot: tactical alpha assumptions are optimistic (trend rules have whipsawed often since 2010), and the rate-cycle rule is a forecast in disguise; frequent switching sits awkwardly with a frozen strategy and adds complexity a judge may see as unearned.
