# H02
ARCHETYPE: CFA-Institute-curriculum team; IPS built on return objective, risk tolerance (ability vs willingness) and the constraints (time horizon, liquidity, legal, taxes, unique circumstances), with a required return and a strategic asset allocation with ranges.
COUNTRY / LEVEL: India (urban private school, two members studying CFA Level I material, very structured)
STRATEGY NAME: none (titled "Investment Policy Statement for Ms. Laura Gao")
## PITCH
A disciplined, CFA-style policy for Laura: a required return of about 4.4%, a moderate-risk strategic allocation built to meet it, and risk that steps down as 2033 approaches. A maturity-matched Treasury reserve secures all ten $50,000 payments; the diversified surplus funds a responsible facility contribution and lasting flexibility.
## IPS
1. Client Overview
Laura Gao, author, entrepreneur and Wharton alumna, will invest $300,000 in January 2027 and $150,000 in January 2028. From 2033 she must make ten fixed payments of $50,000 to her Taiwan creative residency and contribute toward its facility. Her living expenses are covered outside the portfolio.

2. Return Objective
The operating reserve needed in January 2033 is the present value of ten $50,000 payments, approximately $422,000 at a 4% discount rate. For a meaningful facility contribution of around $150,000, the portfolio must reach roughly $575,000 by January 2033. Given the timing of the two contributions, this requires an annualised return of about 4.4%. Because facility costs will rise with inflation (assumed 2.5%), our required nominal return is approximately 4.4% + 2.5% = 6.9%.

3. Risk Tolerance
Ability: above average before 2033 (six-year horizon, no withdrawals, outside income covers living costs, a second contribution adds capital); below average after 2033 (fixed obligations and a lump-sum facility contribution).
Willingness: moderate. Laura is an entrepreneur comfortable with calculated risk, but she has asked for a balance between growth and protecting capital, and a broken promise to co-sponsors would damage her credibility.
Overall: moderate; below average for the operating reserve, average for the surplus.

4. Constraints
Time horizon: multi-stage; accumulation 2027-2032 (six years), distribution 2033-2042 (ten years).
Liquidity: none before 2033; $50,000 each January 2033-2042; a lump-sum facility contribution in 2033.
Legal and regulatory: only WInS-listed stocks, ETFs and Treasury bonds; no margin, short selling, derivatives, cryptocurrency, individual TIPS or STRIPS.
Taxes: not considered, per the case.
Unique circumstances: income tied to publishing and speaking, which is cyclical; a project based in Taiwan; credibility with co-sponsors from 2031.

5. Strategic Asset Allocation (target, permitted range)
Stage 1, 2027-2030:
- U.S. equity (VTI): 35% (30-40%)
- International equity (VXUS): 20% (15-25%)
- Intermediate U.S. Treasuries (IEF, individual notes): 35% (30-40%)
- Short-term Treasuries (SGOV): 10% (5-15%)
Expected return about 6.6% (equity 8.5%, Treasuries 4.5%, bills 3.5%). This is slightly below the 6.9% target; we accept the gap rather than add equity risk to money that must fund the payments.
Stage 2, 2031-2032: equity 35% (30-40%), Treasuries 60% (55-65%), bills 5%. Individual Treasury notes maturing 2033-2042 are bought gradually to build the reserve.
Stage 3, 2033-2042: operating reserve 100% U.S. Treasuries, each note maturing just before a payment, no equities; surplus after the facility contribution 50% equity / 50% Treasuries.

6. Facility Contribution and Flexibility
Facility contribution = portfolio value in January 2033, minus the operating reserve, minus a flexibility buffer of at least 10% of the surplus. The 2031 range will be based on scenario analysis of the Stage 2 portfolio, presented in the Final Report.

7. Monitoring and Rebalancing
Annual review each January; rebalance to target whenever an asset class breaches its range. The IPS is reviewed if Laura's circumstances change materially, but the operating reserve rule is never relaxed.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: complete, judge-friendly CFA structure (ability vs willingness, constraints, SAA with ranges) and a sound Treasury-matched reserve in Stage 3.
Slip: adds inflation on top of an already nominal required return (6.9%) although the payments are fixed nominal, then quietly misses its own target; reads like a template, with little of Laura's voice.
