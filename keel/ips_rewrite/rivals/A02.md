# A02
STRATEGY NAME: Ten Lanterns
## PITCH
Ten Lanterns: each of Laura's ten $50,000 residency payments is lit by a Treasury bond maturing as each payment falls due, bought with her two deposits. Her surplus grows in a broad stock fund. The facility gift is sized from that surplus alone, so no promise outruns the money.
## IPS
Laura's goal is simple to state and hard to guarantee: ten $50,000 payments to her Taiwan residency, one each year from 2033 to 2042, funded by the portfolio with a high degree of certainty. Ten Lanterns separates that promise from market risk. Bonds pay the promise. Stocks drive growth, and they never stand behind a payment.

1. Lock the commitment first. We buy US Treasury bonds that mature just before each payment date, one for each year from 2032 to 2041. Held to maturity, each bond returns its full face value in cash on time, whatever prices do in between, so no payment depends on selling anything. The whole ladder is bought with Laura's two deposits before any money goes to stocks. Until then, spare cash waits in short Treasury bonds. If the first deposit cannot buy every rung, the 2028 deposit completes the ladder. Certainty means each payment is bought in full, not forecast.

2. Grow the surplus, and only the surplus. Money not needed for the ladder goes into one broad stock ETF covering large US and developed-market companies. Stocks have returned more than bonds over long periods but can fall sharply, so the surplus carries that risk and the ladder never does. Surplus money pays only for the facility and the flexibility reserve, never for the operating payments.

3. Keep a flexibility reserve. Laura never commits the last $100,000 of her surplus to the facility. That amount stays hers, so overruns, delays or changed costs as the project develops can be met without touching the ladder.

4. Change the mix as the dates approach. Until 2031 the surplus stays in stocks. In 2031, when Laura gives co-sponsors her range, half the surplus moves into Treasury bonds maturing in 2033, so at least half the facility money is locked before anyone hears a number. The rest stays in stocks for upside. Each year we check that the ladder still covers every remaining payment, and close any gap by buying bonds from the surplus before the facility is funded.

5. Set the facility gift from the floor, not the forecast. The low end of Laura's range is what the surplus would still cover if stocks fell 30% and stayed there. The high end is what it covers in a normal market. We never promise above the low end. Anything above it is an upside surprise, not a commitment.

6. Why this fits Laura. Laura takes thoughtful risks, but she wants balance between growth and protecting her goals. Ten Lanterns gives her a promise that markets cannot break, and a growth engine that is never asked to fund a payment it could miss. Her living costs come from outside the portfolio, so nothing is sold before 2033 and nothing needs to be.
