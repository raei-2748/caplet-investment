# A07
STRATEGY NAME: Promise First, Growth Second
## PITCH
Secure the promise, then grow the surplus. Laura's first deposit buys a Treasury ladder maturing exactly when each $50,000 operating payment is due. Her 2028 contribution is invested for diversified global growth, funding the facility contribution while preserving flexibility.
## IPS
**Objective.** Fund Laura's ten $50,000 operating payments (2033 to 2042) with a high degree of certainty, then contribute as much to the Taiwan residency facility as is responsible while keeping her financially flexible. The payments come first; the facility contribution is what is left over.

**Central idea.** Laura's obligations are fixed, known and dated, so the assets behind them should be too. We divide the portfolio into two sleeves with different jobs: a Protection Sleeve that pays the operating commitment, and a Growth Sleeve that creates the facility contribution. Risk sits only where it is affordable.

**Protection Sleeve (2027 deposit).** In January 2027 we buy a ladder of U.S. Treasury notes and bonds, one rung maturing at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042, sized to the $50,000 payment. Because the payments are fixed in nominal dollars, matching them with Treasuries removes market risk and credit risk beyond that of the U.S. government. We define a high degree of certainty as paying every instalment in full in essentially all scenarios, with U.S. government default as the only residual risk. Coupons are reinvested in short Treasuries, and a small cushion absorbs reinvestment-rate shortfalls. If the ladder costs more than the deposit, the 2028 contribution completes it first.

**Growth Sleeve (2028 contribution and any excess).** The remainder is invested in low-cost, broadly diversified equity ETFs, mainly global and U.S. stocks, with short Treasuries as ballast. Diversification limits damage from any single failure. We accept volatility here because a bad outcome would shrink the facility contribution, not endanger the payments. No margin, shorting, derivatives, TIPS or STRIPS are used.

**Liquidity.** Laura makes no withdrawals before 2033, so liquidity needs are low. Every position is a liquid, listed security.

**Glide path.** As 2031 approaches, we reduce equity exposure and move Growth Sleeve gains into short Treasuries, so most of what Laura tells co-sponsors is already secured. The range she communicates will be anchored to those safe assets, with upside described as possible but uncertain. Overpromising would damage her credibility, so the range is deliberately conservative.

**2033 onward.** At the start of 2033 the ladder becomes the operating reserve. It stays in Treasuries and shortens naturally as each rung is paid, so uncertainty falls every year. The Growth Sleeve then funds the facility. We recommend contributing only the amount above a buffer, keeping flexibility for cost overruns and the evolving project rather than committing every remaining dollar.

**Risk management.** The main risks are interest rates moving before the ladder is built, coupon reinvestment, equity drawdowns, and inflation eroding the real value of fixed payments and facility costs. We address them by building the ladder promptly, holding the cushion, de-risking gradually, and testing favorable and unfavorable market scenarios, including a prolonged equity decline, before setting the range. Growth assets are the long-term inflation hedge.

**Review.** We rebalance annually and whenever the Growth Sleeve drifts materially from target. Policy, not headlines, drives decisions.
