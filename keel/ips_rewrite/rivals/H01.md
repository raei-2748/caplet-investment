# H01
ARCHETYPE: US finance-club team; one member's parent is a wealth manager. Classic three-bucket retirement-income strategy (cash / bonds / growth) with annual refills.
COUNTRY / LEVEL: United States (public high school finance club, strong seniors, some professional coaching at home)
STRATEGY NAME: The Three-Bucket Plan
## PITCH
Three buckets, one promise. Laura's money grows until 2033, then splits into Cash (the next two payments), Bonds (the three after) and Growth (the rest). Each January we refill from whichever bucket has earned it, so a falling market never forces a sale and every $50,000 payment arrives on time.
## IPS
Our Goal
Laura has three different jobs for her money, and they are not equally important. The ten $50,000 operating payments are a promise to other people, so they come first. The facility contribution is a gift she chooses, so it comes second. Financial flexibility is her safety net while the residency finds its feet. Our strategy, the Three-Bucket Plan, gives each job its own money and its own level of risk.

Phase 1: Grow (2027-2032)
We invest the 2027 deposit 60% in diversified stock ETFs (VTI and VXUS), 35% in U.S. Treasuries (IEF and individual Treasury notes) and 5% in short-term Treasuries (SGOV). The 2028 deposit goes mostly into Treasuries, because that money will become the backbone of the payment buckets. Starting in 2030, we move about five percentage points a year from stocks into Treasuries. We rebalance once a year, and sooner if any asset class drifts more than five points from target.

Phase 2: Set Aside (January 2033)
Before any money goes to the facility, we carve out the operating reserve and split it into three buckets:
- Bucket 1, Cash: the next two payments ($100,000) in Treasury bill ETFs (SGOV, BIL).
- Bucket 2, Bonds: payments three to five ($150,000) in Treasury notes maturing in 2035, 2036 and 2037.
- Bucket 3, Growth: payments six to ten in a conservative 50/50 mix of stocks and Treasuries, which has at least five years to recover from any crash.
We size the reserve at the full face value of the payments, $500,000, rather than a discounted amount.

Phase 3: Pay and Refill (2033-2042)
Every January the payment comes from Bucket 1. We then refill Bucket 1 from Bucket 2, and refill Bucket 2 from Bucket 3 only if Bucket 3 is above its starting value. If stocks are down, we wait and let the bonds carry the payments. By 2038 Bucket 3 has been fully moved into Treasuries, so the last five payments never depend on the stock market.

What "high certainty" means to us
We test the plan against the worst case we have studied: stocks fall 40% the month the reserve is created and take five years to recover, as in 2008. Because Buckets 1 and 2 hold five years of payments in cash and Treasuries, Laura never has to sell a stock at a loss to pay the residency. If the plan passes that test, we call the payments highly certain.

Facility contribution and flexibility
Whatever remains after the reserve is the facility pool. We recommend Laura contribute no more than 85% of it and keep at least 15% invested as a flexibility fund for cost overruns or her next venture. In 2031 we will turn our conservative and expected scenarios into the range she shares with co-sponsors, so she promises the low end and earns the high end.

Guidelines
Low-cost, broad ETFs and U.S. Treasuries only; no single stocks, leverage or market timing. Planning assumptions: stocks 7%, Treasuries 4%, cash 3%. Inflation (about 2.5%) affects facility costs, not the fixed payments.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: clear, client-first ranking of the three jobs; a concrete, memorable mechanism (refill rule) and a named stress test that judges can follow.
Blind spot: reserving the full $500,000 face value over-reserves and shrinks the facility gift for no added certainty, while payments six to ten still lean on a 50/50 stock bucket; the reserve's interest-rate risk is never matched.
