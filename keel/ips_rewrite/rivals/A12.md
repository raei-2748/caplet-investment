# A12
STRATEGY NAME: Pencil and Ink
## PITCH
Laura sketches in pencil before she commits to ink, and so does our portfolio. Her ten $50,000 residency payments are inked first, with Treasury bonds that mature as each payment comes due. Everything else stays in pencil, a diversified growth portfolio, inked into her facility contribution as 2033 approaches.
## IPS
**What we are trying to do.** Every graphic novelist knows the rule: pencil first, ink later. Pencil lines can move; ink is a promise. Laura's goals split the same way. Ten $50,000 operating payments from 2033 to 2042 are a promise the portfolio must keep with high certainty, so they belong in ink. Her facility contribution is a hope, so it stays in pencil until the picture is clear. Our strategy answers one question: what gets inked, and when?

**The ink: the operating reserve.** The payments are fixed in dollars and dated to the year, so we match them with assets that are fixed in dollars and dated to the year. Starting in 2027, we buy individual U.S. Treasury bonds maturing shortly before each payment date, one "panel" per year from 2033 to 2042. The 2027 deposit inks as many panels as it can while leaving room to grow; the 2028 deposit finishes the ladder before anything else. Held to maturity, these bonds pay a known amount on a known date, so price swings in between do not matter. We define "high degree of certainty" as every payment backed by a Treasury maturing before it is due; the only remaining risk is a U.S. government default. In 2033 the ladder simply becomes the operating reserve. It needs no active management: each year one bond matures, pays that year's $50,000, and the reserve shrinks on schedule. Coupons go to a short-term Treasury ETF as a small cushion.

**The pencil: the growth portfolio.** Everything not needed for the ladder is invested for growth, because Laura has six years and a history of taking thoughtful risks. We hold low-cost, broad ETFs: about 70% global stocks (U.S. and international) and 30% intermediate Treasuries. No single company can sink the plan, and over time stocks are the best tool for outpacing inflation, which matters because facility costs in Taiwan will likely rise while her payments stay fixed.

**Inking the facility contribution.** Starting in 2031, we ink the pencil gradually: each year we move about a third of the growth portfolio into short-term Treasuries. By 2033 the amount Laura can give is locked rather than guessed. This gives her an honest range for co-sponsors in 2031: the bottom is what is already inked, and the top depends on how the remaining pencil performs. Good markets raise the top; bad markets lower it but never touch the floor or the payments.

**Leaving white space.** Great pages need margins. We will recommend a contribution that keeps a meaningful share of the surplus in her hands for cost overruns and new ideas, so one project never uses up her future.

**Our rules.** We never sell ink to buy pencil. We rebalance the growth portfolio once a year, not when headlines get loud. We use only WInS-listed stocks, ETFs and Treasury bonds, with no margin, shorting, derivatives, crypto, TIPS or STRIPS. Each January we review the plan against Laura's goals and redraw only the pencil.
