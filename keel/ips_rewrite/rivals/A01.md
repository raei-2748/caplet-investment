# A01
STRATEGY NAME: Lock the Ladder, Grow the Surplus
## PITCH
Lock the payments, grow the surplus. Individual Treasury bonds maturing each January 2033 to 2042, held to maturity, mean Laura's ten payments do not depend on markets. Everything else grows in a diversified stock ETF, shifting to Treasuries before 2033 to firm up her facility contribution.
## IPS
Central idea. Laura's ten $50,000 payments from 2033 to 2042 are a fixed obligation. We fund them with U.S. Treasury bonds bought now and held to maturity, so market swings cannot change whether the residency is paid for. Growth is reserved for the money she does not need to commit, and that surplus is what funds her facility contribution.

Goals. Laura invests $300,000 in 2027 and $150,000 in 2028, and nothing moves in or out before 2033. Payments come first; the facility contribution is the flexible part, sized after the payments are secured.

Defining certainty. We treat a high degree of certainty as payments funded by bonds that mature on each payment date, with no sale, no outside money and no reliance on market prices. The only risk left is the U.S. Treasury failing to pay, which we regard as remote.

Step 1: Build the ladder (2027-2028). We buy one Treasury bond for each payment year, face value $50,000, maturing each January 2033 through 2042. The 2027 deposit buys as many rungs as it can at today's prices. The 2028 deposit completes any rungs still missing. Holding each bond to maturity means it pays its full $50,000 face value on the payment date.

Step 2: Grow the surplus (2027-2028). Everything not needed for the ladder goes into a broad stock ETF covering the U.S. and world markets, held at about 65 percent, with the rest in a short-term Treasury note as a cash buffer. Stocks are the only way this money can grow enough to fund a facility.

Step 3: Operating reserve (1 January 2033). The reserve is the ladder: 100 percent Treasuries, never sold, paying each year's $50,000 on the payment date. It needs no change as payments approach, because each rung is already matched to its year.

Step 4: Glide path (2029-2032). Stock weight in the surplus falls steadily from 65 percent to about 35 percent by 2032. Laura must describe her 2033 contribution to co-sponsors in 2031, so the 2033 amount should be more predictable than it is today.

Step 5: Facility contribution. After the reserve, the surplus is the only source. We keep at least one-third of it as flexibility for project overruns, and we recommend only what remains, as a range, with the low end set so that a poor market still covers it.

Risks. The main risk is falling interest rates, which raise the price of Treasury bonds bought in 2027-2028 and could make the ladder cost more. The 2028 deposit and the growth sleeve are the buffer. The second risk is a weak stock market in 2031-2033; the glide path and flexibility reserve limit it. We review the plan each year but do not change the ladder.

Why this fits Laura. She is willing to take thoughtful risk, but the capital behind her goals comes first. This plan promises only what the surplus can pay.
