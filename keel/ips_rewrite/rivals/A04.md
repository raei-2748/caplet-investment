# A04
STRATEGY NAME: Ballast and Sail
## PITCH
Laura's portfolio is a sailboat. Its ballast is a ladder of Treasury bonds, each maturing the year a $50,000 payment is due, so the ten operating payments are locked in whatever the weather. Its sail is a broad stock fund, which carries the extra growth that funds her residency.
## IPS
Our idea is ballast and sail. A sailboat needs heavy ballast low in its hull so the sail can catch wind without tipping the boat over. Laura's portfolio works the same way. The ballast, a Treasury bond ladder, guarantees her ten operating payments. The sail, a stock fund, earns the growth that lets her contribute to the residency.

Laura adds $300,000 in 2027 and $150,000 in 2028, then nothing until 2033. Her outside income covers her living costs. So the portfolio has two jobs: pay $50,000 at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042, and fund as much of the Taiwan residency as it responsibly can. She must give co-sponsors a number in 2031, so she needs a range she can keep, not a best case.

**Ballast first.** Before any money goes into stocks, we buy Treasury bonds that mature in the year each payment is due, and we hold them to maturity. A Treasury held to maturity pays face value on its date whatever the market does, so the payments depend on the bonds, not on luck. We use only Treasuries, stocks and ETFs on WInS; no TIPS, STRIPS, margin or derivatives. The 2027 deposit buys the ladder. The real risk is price: if rates fall, the ladder costs more, and the 2028 deposit covers the shortfall.

**Sail second.** Money above the ballast goes into one broad, low-cost stock index fund spread across many companies. Stocks are the engine: they carry the growth that pays for the residency. We never count on them for a payment, and a stock fund can fall sharply in a bad year.

**Changing course.** The sail is largest while payments are far away. From 2031 we shrink it every year on a fixed schedule, not on news headlines, and move the money into bonds that mature in the payment years. By 2033 the operating reserve is all bonds, sized to cover all ten payments. Once a year we check the ladder, like steering: if it runs short, the sail is trimmed first.

**The facility number.** In 2031 Laura gives co-sponsors a range, not a promise. The bottom is the amount we can deliver even in a bad market, with the ladder fully funded. The top assumes a strong market. Our rule: the bottom must always protect the ten payments, so her credibility never depends on the market being kind. In 2033, after the reserve is set aside, the contribution equals the surplus above the reserve, minus a flexibility buffer.

**Flexibility.** Giving every spare dollar away would leave the residency with no cushion. So part of the surplus stays invested in the stock fund and untouched, a buffer Laura can use if the project needs money early.

**Risks and liquidity.** Treasuries can be sold quickly if needed, and the only fixed dates are the payments. We rebalance on a set date each year and write down every change, so decisions follow our rules, not fear.
