# H08
ARCHETYPE: Barbell team inspired by Taleb's Antifragile; ~85-90% ultra-safe Treasuries/T-bills, 10-15% aggressive growth (QQQ, single high-growth stocks).
COUNTRY / LEVEL: South Korea (Seoul international/foreign-language high school; strong readers of investing books; English competent, slightly formal)
STRATEGY NAME: The Antifragile Barbell
## PITCH
Laura's promises sit on the safe end; her dreams sit on the bold end; nothing sits in the fragile middle. About 88% goes into U.S. Treasury bills and notes, so the ten payments are protected, while 12% rides high-growth assets whose upside can fund the residency's facility.
## IPS
Central idea
In Antifragile, Nassim Taleb argues that the safest structure is not a "medium-risk" portfolio but a barbell: most money in something that cannot fail, and a small part in things that can win very big. The middle, where risks look moderate but crash together in a crisis, is the most fragile place to be. Laura's goals fit a barbell naturally. One side is her promises, the ten payments. The other side is her dreams, the facility and her next ideas.

The safe side: 88%
88% of the portfolio is held in U.S. Treasury bills and short-term Treasuries through SGOV, BIL and SHY, plus individual Treasury notes. We avoid long bonds, corporate bonds and balanced funds, because they can fall exactly when Laura needs them. Treasury bills do not lose value in a crisis; investors run to them. With yields near 4%, the safe side alone grows to roughly $490,000 by 2033, more than the ten payments require.

The aggressive side: 12%
12% is invested in assets with large upside: QQQ (6%) and four high-growth companies, NVIDIA, TSMC, Palantir and Tesla (1.5% each). This side can fall 50% or more, but because it is small, even losing all of it costs Laura only 12%. One big winner could fund much of the facility contribution. We accept volatility here because the maximum loss is known in advance.

Rebalancing: a one-way ratchet
Every January, if the aggressive side has grown above 15% of the portfolio, we cut it back to 12% and move the profit to the safe side. If it falls, we do not top it up. Gains are locked; losses are capped. This is how the portfolio becomes antifragile: volatility can only help it.

The operating reserve
In 2033 the safe side becomes the operating reserve. We set aside enough to cover all ten payments: the next two in T-bills and the rest in Treasury notes maturing each year until 2042. Because none of it was ever exposed to stocks, the reserve does not depend on the market in 2033. We define high certainty as payments funded entirely by U.S. government debt, with zero dependence on any risky asset at any time.

Facility contribution and the 2031 range
The facility contribution comes from two places: the safe side's surplus above the reserve, which is predictable, and the aggressive side, which is not. In 2031 Laura can tell co-sponsors something honest: a minimum backed by Treasuries, plus an upside that depends on growth stocks. The floor of her range is the safe surplus; the top adds the aggressive side. Bad markets can shrink the top but never the floor.

Flexibility
After the reserve and the facility contribution, Laura keeps at least half of the aggressive side invested. It is the kind of bet an entrepreneur understands.

Why this fits Laura
Laura wants her money to protect her promises before chasing growth. The barbell gives her both, without the hidden fragility of the middle.
## SIMULATOR NOTES
Does well: a famous, coherent idea told simply; a strict promise-first rule and an honest floor-plus-upside story for co-sponsors.
Blind spot: rolling T-bills to 2033 is itself fragile to falling rates (the "$490,000" assumes 4% for six years, and the reserve cost rises as rates fall), so the "safe" side is not locked; 6% in four single stocks is a lottery ticket that barely moves the facility gift, and the "one-way ratchet" lets that sleeve shrink toward nothing.
