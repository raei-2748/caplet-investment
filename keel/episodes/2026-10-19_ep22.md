---

Nine days. That's the longest we've been in. On Earth it was a bit over three minutes, on a Monday afternoon,
between double maths and the bus.

The question from Caplet was simple to read and hard to answer: why might their IPS draft not reach the semifinal,
the way a former judge would see it? Not "is it good". *Why not.*

**Nora:** Kai. This one's yours.

**Me:** Because I'm the mean one?

**Nora:** Because you're the one who finds the weak point in five minutes. And because you're the one who'll have
to stop yourself from deciding for them.

---

### Arden days one to four

So I did what I do to our plan: "Who says?" to every sentence. Except it wasn't our plan. It was six people's I'd
never met, in words I didn't write, and every time I found something I wanted to fix it, and I wasn't allowed to.

Felix put the five criteria back up on the wall and read them aloud before we started, which he does now before
every review. Jun checked every number against the files they'd sent. Amara read it for Laura. Tess read it as a
judge, out loud, timing herself.

What I learned is that the strongest case against a draft isn't a list of what's wrong. It's the one sentence a
judge would write in the margin. Ours, for theirs, took four Arden days to get to, and it was about whether every
number in the draft had a source a judge could check. It was true, it was fair, and it was theirs to fix or not.

**Me:** Can I just tell them what to write?

**Nora:** No.

**Me:** Can I tell them what I'd write?

**Amara:** If they ask. Labelled. In their voice only if they want it.

**Me:** This is the worst job I've ever had.

**Tess:** It's the most useful thing you've done this term.

She said it flatly, like a fact. I didn't know what to do with it, so I did push-ups.

---

### Arden days five to eight: our own spine

The rest of the stretch was for us. If you spend four days asking why someone else's IPS fails, you start asking
it of your own.

I had an idea for ours. A name.

**Me:** Our plan's a barbell. Very safe bonds at one end, stocks at the other, nothing in the middle. It's a real
concept. Taleb writes about it. Judges would recognise it.

**Jun:** It's accurate.

**Tess:** We already have a word for our plan. Two words. Keel and sail. If we add "barbell" the judge has to learn
two pictures for one idea. We don't need a new word for our own plan.

**Me:** It sounds clever.

**Tess:** That's the problem.

Into the ledger, in my handwriting, because I've learned that's the honest way: **I-16, barbell: rejected as a label.
"We don't need a new word for our own plan." (Tess.)**

Nora wrote the one sentence on the board, current version, and we read it aloud, all six, like a debate team:
"Buy Laura's promise first, then let what's left grow, and never tell a co-sponsor a number she doesn't already
own." Felix said the word "promise" is fine there because it's Laura's. Amara said, "Laura promises; bonds pay." It's
become a thing people say in the room.

### 00:30, the note

Tuesday morning, technically. On the call: Felix, me. Tess for the journal.

I opened order history, found the Nov-2040 trade, clicked Add Note, and read Amara's correction aloud, every
character, with the apostrophes.

**Felix:** Matches. 147 characters.

**Me:** Screenshot. Adding. Screenshot.

It took ninety seconds. It was the smallest thing I've done in WInS and I was more careful with it than with the
first order. Because this one was fixing something we'd said wrong, and I know what it's like to watch someone not
fix things.

### Home

Bà was up when I got in from the shop on Monday night (before the note, before the call). She looked at me across the
kitchen for a long time and then said, in Vietnamese, "What's wrong with your eyes?"

I said nothing was wrong. She said they looked like Dad's did "that year". She didn't say which year. She didn't
have to.

I sat with her and ate the soup she'd kept warm and told her about the competition, the boring parts. Bonds that pay
on dates. A rule that only I press the button. She said, "Good. One person, careful." Then: "Sleep." I did, after
the note.

Jun's number, Monday morning: **$294,770**, Friday's close, **18bp**. On the bus I told Jun what Bà said. He said,
"She's right about the eyes." I said, "She's right about Dad." He said, "No. You're not him. You're the one who
checks." That's the most Jun has ever said about it.
