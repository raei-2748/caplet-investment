Nani was at the kitchen table when I got home on Sunday night, checking the gurdwara kitchen's accounts the way she
does every week, with a pencil she sharpens with a knife. I had eleven notes on my laptop. She asked what they were.

I read her the 2042 one. She listened, and then she said, "And if the person reading it is afraid, what does it
tell them?"

I didn't have an answer. I wrote her question at the top of the document.

---

### The Long Room, room day one

The Guide says a Trading Note should capture "the reasoning behind the decision, including its alignment with your
strategy, the supporting research or analysis, and its expected role". Felix had the line ready, of course. The TNA
instruction asks three things of each trade: why it was made, how it fits the strategy, how it supports Laura's
goals, funding needs or risk.

So we did it properly. Each note, read aloud by me, then four questions: Felix's (is every fact true and sourced?),
Jun's (is every number dated?), Tess's (strict count, under 300 characters?), and Nani's, which I put to them as
mine: *if the reader is afraid, what does this tell them?*

The bond notes were easy. "Pays for Laura's 2040 payment. A U.S. Treasury that matures in November 2039 and repays
its face value, with fixed interest until then." Under 200 characters. Nothing to be afraid of.

The term-fund notes were harder. They had to say "not fixed" without sounding frightening. Tess cut "not fixed,
but" from one and I fought for it, because the "but" is where the comfort is. She let me keep it. She put "comfort"
on a card for the folder anyway.

Kai's VT note was the hardest. He wanted it to say the world fund "is where Laura's dream gets bigger". Felix asked
where the case says that. Kai said, "In my heart." It went in the folder. What stayed was plainer: the fund is the
growth money; it decides how far her gift can rise above what is already bought; it is never used to pay the ten
payments.

**Kai:** You've made it boring.

**Me:** I've made it true. If Laura is afraid, it tells her the payments don't depend on it. That's the kindest
sentence in the note.

He let it go. I wrote that down too, for me, not for the journal.

---

### Room day three: the 2037 rung

The vote Nora had promised. Two options on the board.

**One:** IBTR, the December 2036 term fund. It closes about two weeks before the 1 January 2037 payment. Its payout
is not fixed.

**Two:** the Treasury bond maturing 15 February 2036. Its payout is fixed: coupons and face value. But it pays out
about ten and a half months before the payment, so the money sits as cash until then.

**Jun:** IBTR matches the date. The bond matches the amount. Each one is right about one thing.

**Kai:** The bond. Laura's word is "certainty". A fixed amount, ten months early, is more certain than an unfixed
amount on time. Cash sitting still doesn't lose dollars.

**Felix:** Agree. And it's a Treasury, which is what we tell judges the payments are made of.

**Jun:** But in Laura's real plan that cash sits for ten months doing nothing, and we'd have to say what it sits in.
And IBTR is the same kind of rung as the four before it. One rule for 2033 to 2037 is simpler to explain.

**Tess:** "Simpler to explain" is a reason a judge can see.

**Nora:** Ten minutes, then we vote.

IBTR won, four to two. Kai and Felix lost. Kai said, "Fine. Logged. I still think you're wrong." Felix said nothing,
which for Felix is the same sentence.

Jun said he'd finish sizing the four term-fund rungs plus IBTR on Tuesday afternoon, with a margin for the payout
not being fixed. Nora set orders for Tuesday night, from 00:30 when New York opens.

---

### Surface

We came out at 13:31 Sydney time. Lunch was nearly over. My sandwich was exactly where I'd left it, which always
feels wrong after four days.

Jun's number had come in: **$288,759**, still Friday's close, because no U.S. market had traded since. Kai said,
"Even the bond market takes weekends off." Felix said, "Sunday 4 October. So did we, nearly."

On the way out, Kai said quietly, so only I heard: "If the bond's right, I'll find the evidence. Not the volume."
