# FICTION. Episode 21: The Window opens

**Sunday 18 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Felix · Long Room: 2 room days (Caplet's first question) · 6 days to the TNA**

> three TNA notes ready to paste? Keel agrees its rules for helping a team it will never meet: read their files,

> **Arcs:** 8 (the Window), 5 (two clocks). **Values:** 2, 4. **Ideas:** none.

---

The Window has always been there. It's how Wharton's files reach us, and the Treasury curve, and the fund pages.
On Sunday it carried something else: a question, from six students in another Sydney, entered in the same
competition.

Their team lead had asked whether the three trade notes they planned to use for the TNA were clean enough to paste
into the form. A test question, really: does this work at all?

We read it on the long table. Nobody said anything for a minute. Then Kai said, "They're asking *us*?"

---

### The Long Room, room day one: the rules

Nora wouldn't let us answer until we'd agreed how.

**Nora:** Three things. One sentence each.

**Amara:** They decide. We never vote for them, never write in their voice unless they ask, and never tell them
our story as if it's theirs.

**Me:** We use only what's true. Wharton's files with line numbers, their files with line numbers. If we can't see
it, it's UNVERIFIED. We can't see their WInS or their form. We say so.

**Tess:** We never see their faces, only their files. We never rank them. Not even in here.

**Nora:** And every answer ends with what they need to decide and by when. Their time, Sydney first.

**Kai:** And if they're about to make a mistake?

**Nora:** Then we say so clearly, with the evidence, and then it's theirs.

Kai didn't like that. I could see it. He likes winning arguments, and this is a kind of argument you are not
allowed to win.

---

### Room day two: the four checks

Then the actual question, and the problem with it was me.

I made my checklist the way I always do. Every possible way a paste can fail: length, characters, curly quotes,
spaces at the end, line breaks, the trade's time and date, whether the order was filled or cancelled, whether there
were two versions of the same note, whether the form strips anything, whether the word count includes the note...
Twenty-three lines.

Nora looked at it.

**Nora:** Felix. If you could only do four, which four?

It's the question I hate most. Every check feels the same size from inside. I said that.

**Nora:** I know. Do it anyway. Say "this one matters" first, then prove it.

So I tried. I sorted the twenty-three into three piles: *it fails if this is wrong*; *it looks bad if this is
wrong*; *it's nice to know*. The first pile had four:

1. Each note matches WInS character for character.
2. Each note belongs to an order that was actually filled, not cancelled.
3. No curly quotes or other odd characters.
4. Each note is the final version, if there were two.

The rest went below a line: "can wait". It felt like leaving a door unlocked.

Jun and I ran the four on their export file. Their three notes were under 300 characters, plain text. One trade had
a cancelled earlier version with almost the same note, different at one character in the middle. That's exactly
the kind of thing check 4 is for. And one note used the word "promised", which is on their own list of words to
watch. Not a failure, a flag.

The answer went out through the Window within the hour. It said what we could check, what only they could check
in WInS and the form, and what they needed to do and by when.

**Kai:** Do we get to know if they listened?

**Nora:** No. That's the job.

---

### After: our own notes

On the way out I finished the job Nora gave me yesterday: re-reading our own eleven notes, as someone who didn't
write them, for overclaim words. "Guarantees" was the only clear one. But I found "promise" in three of ours too,
"Laura's promise", the way we say it every day.

**Me:** It's the word on our door. Value one.

**Tess:** It's the right word for what Laura is making. It's the wrong word for what a bond does.

**Amara:** So: Laura promises. Bonds pay.

**Me:** Checked. It holds.

No new curve; it's Sunday. Last: Friday's, **$294,770**, about **18bp**. Band rehearsal is tomorrow at 15:30. Two
alarms. I checked them twice, which Tess says is in the second pile.
