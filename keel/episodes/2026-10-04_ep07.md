# FICTION. Episode 7: The hour that moved

**Sunday 4 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Felix · Long Room: 3 room days (drafting the notes) · 20 days to the TNA**

> **Summary.** Sydney's clocks go forward overnight, so the U.S. open moves from 23:30 to 00:30. Felix redoes every
> time on the board in code and adds the second clock to his habits for good. Amara drafts the first trade notes;
> Tess cuts them, and keeps the cut lines in a folder for the first time. One note can't honestly say what Amara
> wanted it to say, and that turns out to matter.
> **Arcs:** 5 (two clocks), 3 (the folder). **Values:** 2, 4 (not yet on the door). **Ideas:** none.

---

At 2 a.m. the clocks went forward. I know because I was awake to watch the phone jump from 1:59 to 3:00. Por Por
says that is the most Felix thing I have ever done, and she has seen me score mahjong.

I wasn't awake for fun. Kai had said on Thursday that the U.S. open "moves on Sunday", and I'd written it on my
hand, and I don't trust things written on my hand. So at 3:01 I ran it in Python with both time zones: 9:30 a.m. in
New York on Monday 5 October is 00:30 on Tuesday 6 October in Sydney. Last week it was 23:30. The close moves too:
4 p.m. in New York is now 7 a.m. here, not 6.

Then I checked the next change. New York's clocks go back on Sunday 1 November. From then the gap is sixteen hours.
I wrote both on a card and put it in my wallet.

---

### Lunch, room time, the Long Room

We went in at 12:30. The board still said "U.S. open 23:30". I rubbed it out and wrote the new times, Sydney first,
New York in brackets, which is how Nora wants it.

**Nora:** Why does it matter, in one line?

**Me:** Kai's limit orders have to go in while New York is open, and that's now half past midnight to seven in the
morning here, and on the first of November it shifts again. If we get it wrong, an order sits all night and fills
at the open price we didn't see.

**Kai:** Two clocks. Fine. I'm setting alarms.

**Me:** I'll hang a clock in here too. Two. Sydney and the room.

I'd brought them from home: two cheap kitchen clocks from the two-dollar shop. One says Sydney. One I set to room
time when we come in. They're the first things you see when the door opens now.

---

### The notes

Then Amara put eleven note drafts on the table. She'd written them overnight, which I could tell, because the last
three were better than the first three.

Tess read each one aloud, counted the characters with my checker, and cut. She cut a third of everything. She didn't
throw the lines away. She had a blue folder, and she wrote each cut line on a card and put it in.

**Amara:** You're keeping them?

**Tess:** Some of them are the best lines on the team. They're just not true yet, or not this note's job.

The first card in the folder was from the 2042 note: *"This bond is Laura's last promise, kept nine years early."*
Tess said it was beautiful and that a bond bought in 2026 is not a promise kept, it is a promise paid for. Amara
argued for four minutes and then wrote "paid for" herself.

The one that mattered was the 2034 note, for IBTO. Amara's draft said the fund would "pay exactly $50,000".

**Me:** Can I read you something? The fund holds Treasuries maturing in 2033 and closes in December 2033. What it
pays out is what its bonds are worth then, plus what's left of the income. It's not a bond. Its payout isn't fixed.

**Amara:** So I can't say it pays $50,000.

**Me:** You can say we bought enough of it to expect about $50,000, with a little room. You can't say "exactly".

**Jun:** And I'd have to size it with that room. I hadn't yet.

That's when Nora looked at the board and said, quietly, "If we'd placed tonight, that note would have said
'exactly'." Nobody said anything. Kai looked at the ceiling.

Amara rewrote it. Two hundred and forty-six characters:

> *Pays for Laura's 2034 payment ($50,000 on 1 Jan 2034). WInS lists no Treasury maturing 2032-35, so this fund holds
> only Treasuries due in 2033 and closes in Dec 2033, paying out what it holds. Its payout is not fixed, so we bought
> a little extra.*

Tess said, "That one stays." It's the first time I'd heard her say it.

---

### After

Nora sent the Sunday photo at 21:00: the board with the new times, the countdown (20 days to the TNA), and under it
in her handwriting: **Notes before orders. Thank Felix for the clocks.**

My father called at nine, from Hong Kong, as he does on Sundays. We talked about football. At the end he said,
"You sound tired, in a good way." I didn't explain the room. You can't explain the room.
