# FICTION. Episode 15: The example

**Monday 12 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Felix · Long Room: 2 room days (reading Wharton's example) · 12 days to the TNA**

> **Summary.** Felix wants Keel's notes and reflections shaped like the Trading Note example in Wharton's own
> instruction. Kai points out that the example's reasoning (a Treasury index fund "to reduce portfolio volatility")
> is the very reasoning Keel rejected in Ep 3 and Ep 6. The team decides an example shows the form, not the strategy,
> and Felix learns the difference between a rule and an illustration. He misses band rehearsal for the first time
> in three years.
> **Arcs:** 5 (two clocks), 7 (outside). **Values:** 2. **Ideas:** I-02 and I-05 revisited.

---

There is a Trading Note example on page two of the TNA instruction. I'd read it in August. I read it again on the
389 this morning and decided it was the most important paragraph in the competition.

> "We are purchasing shares of an intermediate-term U.S. Treasury bond ETF to reduce portfolio volatility and begin
> preparing for Laura's future operating commitment. The position provides income and greater stability than
> equities, although interest-rate changes may affect its value. This trade supports our plan to balance continued
> growth with reliable future cash flows as the residency funding date approaches."

It's what Wharton wrote when Wharton wanted to show a good note. So, I reasoned, our notes and our reflections should
look like it.

---

### Lunch, the Long Room, room day one

I put it on the table, printed, with our eleven notes beside it.

**Me:** Their example has four parts: what we bought, why, the risk, how it fits the plan. Ours have three: why, how
it fits, what it does for Laura. We're missing "the risk". I think we should add it to the reflections.

**Nora:** Good. That's useful.

**Kai:** Can I read *you* something?

He had never said that to me. He'd clearly been saving it.

**Kai:** "An intermediate-term U.S. Treasury bond ETF to reduce portfolio volatility." That's an index fund. It
never matures. And the reason is volatility, which is the frontier thinking. We rejected both. Ep 3 and Ep 6. If we
copy the example's reasoning, we're arguing against our own plan in Wharton's words.

I opened my mouth to say the example was official. Then I closed it, because I could hear what I was about to say:
that an example is a rule. It isn't. Nowhere does the instruction say "write your note like this one". It says
"Trading Note Example".

**Me:** It's an illustration, not a requirement. Where does it say we have to follow it? It doesn't.

**Kai:** Did you just "where does it say" yourself?

**Me:** Yes.

**Jun:** The example is right for the plan it's in. A team that's balancing a mixed portfolio would buy an index fund
for stability. Our plan buys the dates. Same competition, different strategy. The example shows a *good* note for a
*different* plan.

**Amara:** Then what do we take from it?

**Tess:** The shape. It's short. It names the risk. It says how the trade fits. Every sentence has a job.

**Nora:** So what? One line.

**Me:** Take the form from the example and the reasoning from our plan.

Nora wrote it on the board. Then she wrote under it: **Felix's catch, Kai's catch.** Kai photographed it.

---

### Room day two: the risk line

The useful part of my idea survived. Each reflection should name the risk that came with the decision. Jun's IBTM:
the payout isn't fixed. Amara's Nov-2041 bond: its price moves most when rates move, which doesn't matter if we hold
it to the date, and would matter a lot if we had to sell. Kai's VT: stocks can fall a lot in a year, which is why it
never pays a payment.

And one more line I found while I was in there, three paragraphs further down, which I put in red on the board
because it's the line that will matter on the night: "The Trading Notes must correspond to trades executed in WInS.
Yes, we will verify this."

**Kai:** "Yes, we will verify this." That's the most threatening sentence I've ever read in a PDF.

**Me:** It means exactly what it says.
