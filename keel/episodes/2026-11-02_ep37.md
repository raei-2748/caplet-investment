Felix's mum keeps an audit file. Every client, every year, a spreadsheet the client made and a letter the bank
sent, and she ticks one against the other in green pen. He'd watched her do it on Saturday at the kitchen table.

"Why not just use the spreadsheet?" he'd asked. "They made it."

"That's why," she said, not looking up.

He thought about that at seven on Monday morning, with the round open and his Weet-Bix going soft.

---

The call had four faces and a fifth tile that said *Jun (camera off, on the train)*. Nora had the Kitchen's orders
from last night pasted into the chat, all three scouts, all four Wharton lines.

"New rule," she said. "Before anyone goes out, someone who didn't write the order reads it back against Wharton's
own words. Kai didn't write it."

"I wrote some of it," Kai said.

"You didn't write the Wharton lines."

"Fine." He shared his screen: `WHARTON_WORD.md`, and the orders beside it. He went down them like a checkout.

"'Your WInS portfolio should reflect the strategy your team has developed.' Line one-ten. In the quote block.
Wharton's." Tick. "'Explain why each investment belongs in your strategy.' One-thirteen, wraps onto the next line.
Wharton's." Tick. "'Presents a clear and creative investment thesis.' Criteria, line eighteen. Wharton's."

He stopped on the fourth.

"'Judged together on these five.'" He scrolled. "Line thirty-eight. It's there." Pause. "It's not in the quote
block."

Nobody said anything for a second.

"So who wrote it?" Amara said.

"We did," said Tess. "It's the summary line under the criteria. I wrote the job off it last night." She was already
searching. "It *sounds* like them."

"That's the problem," Kai said.

Felix had the case open before he'd decided to open it. Ctrl-F, *together*. Near the end, under the deliverables.

"'Evaluators will consider the three deliverables together,'" he read. "'Teams should use each deliverable for its
intended purpose while maintaining a clear and consistent investment strategy across all three.'"

"That's harder," Nora said.

"It's a lot harder." Tess was writing it into her job. "Ours just says they're marked on the same five things.
Theirs says the strategy has to be the *same strategy*, in all three. And the TNA's locked."

Felix thought of the green pen. The spreadsheet had been our own words in Wharton's coat. It was close, close
enough that four people had read it last week and nodded. And it said something easier than the real one did.

"I'll put the real line in the Wharton file this morning," he said. "Verbatim. And mark ours as ours."

"File it as a request," said Nora, "so it's on the board, not just in your head."

"It's always in my head," Felix said, and filed it.

---

Then the actual round. Tess had the funds' prospectuses, and the line she was carrying in was already enough to
worry about: the iBond fund "does not seek to return any predetermined amount at maturity". That's the fund's own
word on the thing V50 calls a Treasury.

"Our own reflection already says it," Tess said. "'A net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par.' We wrote that
in October and it's locked. So if the IPS says 'pays exactly what it promises' about the whole ladder, we've
contradicted ourselves in front of the people who are told to read both."

"It doesn't say the whole ladder," said Amara. "It says 'a Treasury held to maturity'."

"Right after 'Treasury bonds and funds'."

"So it's true if you read it carefully."

"That's what Ahaan said we can't do any more," said Kai. "Every sentence plain on one reading."

Nora was taking WInS: why a book bought with $300,000 holds a stock fund when V50 says the first $300,000 buys the
ladder. "Primary files only," she said. "Our notes, our reflection, our own competition file. No new reading."

Felix had the far job: the caplet itself. V50's paragraph two still said *a capsule ... break it open*. Every safety
page he'd read on Sunday said *tablet* and *crushed*, and the harm was the whole dose arriving at once. That was the
harm the ending wanted.

"An Australian page," Nora said. "In its own words. Not a summary of one."

"Not a summary of one," he said.

Jun's tile unmuted, briefly, with train noise. "I'm judging blind. If a true sentence reads worse I'll say it reads
worse."

"Then we'll fight about it at six," said Tess.

"Good," said Jun, and went back to being a grey square.

she'd left it. He didn't take it. But he thought about it.
