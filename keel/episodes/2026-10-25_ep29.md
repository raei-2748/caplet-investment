**Sunday 25 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Jun · Long Room: light (one room day, the sheet) · IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00 (13 days)**

I ran at six, the long loop past the station, because I wanted to be tired before the sheet and not after it.

The sheet was Tess's idea. Last night on the Rooftop she said four of our five fixes this week were our own dates.
Not Wharton's rules, not a wrong formula. Our dates. A number from one Thursday sitting next to a number from
another Thursday, both labelled MODEL, both true, and together a lie. So this morning, before the vote, every number
anyone wanted to say out loud went in one table: value, source, curve date, edition, basis.

It took me forty minutes. The bottom row took me another ten, because I didn't want it to be true.

The buffer numbers, mine, the ones I'd been defending for two days (the coupon short in about 9 paths in 100, about
3 in 100 with $10,000 put aside), ran on J.P. Morgan's 2026 forecast for world stocks: 7.0% a year. Kai's sail case
quoted their new one, released on the 19th: 7.5%. Same page of the ballot. Two editions.

Amara came straight from the gurdwara kitchen, still smelling of dal, and we met in the Long Room at two.

**Kai:** Half a point. It barely moves your tail. Vote the dollars.

**Jun:** You don't know that. I don't know that. That's the point.

**Kai:** It's higher growth. The buffer needs to cover less, not more. Direction's obvious.

**Jun:** Direction's a guess until it runs. And the 7.5% isn't even read at source yet. Felix has a press release.

**Felix:** A press release on their site. The matrix itself, no. I can't open it from here.

**Kai:** So we wait a day for a number that changes nothing.

**Tess:** Which number goes on the ballot, then? Because if it's one from each edition, I'm not printing it.

Nora didn't say anything for a bit, which used to mean she was about to close it.

**Nora:** Two questions. One: do we hold a buffer at all, taken from the 2028 money, ahead of the floor. Two: how big.
The first doesn't care about half a point. The second does. We vote the first now.

**Kai:** A rule without a number is a promise without a price.

**Nora:** Then Monday it gets a price. On one edition.

The rule passed five to one. Kai voted against and wrote his reason in the journal himself, which is how he loses
properly. Tess wrote the size question underneath with Monday's date and "one edition" in brackets.

After, Nora stayed back while the others went for bubble tea. She didn't bring it up. I did.

**Jun:** You asked me on Wednesday if the late nights were something I need or something I'm afraid of.

**Nora:** You don't have to.

**Jun:** It's this. The bottom row. Being the one whose number was wrong in front of everyone. So I check alone at
night, and then I find it alone at night, and nobody sees it until it's fixed.

**Nora:** And today?

**Jun:** Today it was on the table before I'd fixed it. And nothing happened.

**Nora:** Kai voted against you.

**Jun:** Kai voted against the size. That's different.

I texted Kai on the bus home. *Rerun tomorrow at lunch. Both editions, side by side. You drive, I'll check.* He sent
back a thumbs up and then, a minute later, *you're still wrong about the size*. Which is fair. He might be.

At home Hana was on the couch with her anatomy notes. She asked how the vote went.

"We decided what we're deciding," I said.

"That sounds like nothing."

"It's most of it."
