**07:08.** It was Saturday, so I did the numbers at the kitchen table before anyone was up.

Caplet's plan keeps half of Laura's shares back and doesn't promise that half to anyone. I worked the plan through
keeping 30%, half and 70% back, and it gave the same answers as our number list, to the dollar, before I changed
anything. We'd started the morning thinking 70% back would be worse, and the numbers don't show that. If Laura tells
co-sponsors the most she'll give is $115,000, she has about $30,000 spare at today's prices, and every extra $5,000
she offers costs her about $5,300 of that.

Kai had someone test an idea of ours, that the shares would help pay if building costs in Taiwan went up. In the years
shares fell, Taiwan's building costs went up more often than down, so the idea is out. Felix found that Taiwan's
government spent 29.1% more on culture in 2009, the recession year, so the report won't say it cut arts money then.
Outside readers mark each part of our outline out of 5, and we keep an average across the parts. Nobody new read it
this morning, and the average stayed at 3.17.

**09:48.** Caplet asked where to find the sources our files call W8 or T14. Those are short labels we used to keep
tables narrow, and some files never said what they meant. Tess made one page that lists every label, what it stands
for and a link to the source. W8 is one of Wharton's web pages and T14 is a tab of the Research section in Caplet's
report doc. One of our notes listed its labels wrong in its header, and she fixed it.

**12:08.** Caplet's long message at 11:14 said "Literally write like we do," and Amara set up a test. Three writers
wrote the same three short passages from the same plain facts: two who had read only Caplet's writing, and our own
main writer, with all our files. Three readers read Caplet's investment statement twice, then got fourteen passages
with no names on them and said for each whether Caplet could have written it. Nora put the result into the plan for

were picked as Caplet's 9 times in 9, the two fresh writers 13 times in 18, and ours 0 times in 9, and paragraphs from
our old draft of the statement, mixed in with them, 0 times in 6.

**17:48.** Mum had just got in from her shift when Caplet wrote at 17:19: "We've settled on the conceit of 'pencil and
ink'." In that picture, ink is a dollar amount that government bonds Laura already owns will pay on its date, pencil
is an amount none of her bonds will pay yet, and she gives co-sponsors only the figures in ink. Caplet sent a first
version of how the picture fits the plan, "not gospel". Its 2031 range, $140,000 to $160,000, came from a draft
written before 10 November, so Amara and I started our notes on it from our number list, which has about $95,000 to
$115,000. An outside reader then checked that version and our notes, and found nine errors in our notes. We took the
outside reader's fixes into our notes before six. One of them said the ten payments aren't ink until January 2028,
because each bond reaches $50,000 only with interest that hasn't been earned yet.

**19:53.** In the evening, someone Kai asked to read our notes against what Caplet handed in marked that January 2028
date as the worst problem of the night. We hadn't read that fix against Caplet's statement before we put it in. The
statement says the ten bonds, held to the end, "pay what they say", and Caplet's trading notes call it "a
near-ironclad guarantee", and calling the 2027 bonds pencil says the opposite of both. Amara changed it that night:
most of each payment is ink from January 2027, the rest of it and the bottom of the range from January 2028, and the
top of the range before the 2031 meetings.

Readers hear "inked" as signed, so the one place the report defines ink will say "owned". Felix read the studies:
across 38 tests of writing meant to persuade people, using a comparison like this did only slightly better than plain
words, and in about 4 in 10 it did worse. Nora decided the report gets no more than five sentences with pencil or ink
in them.

The average went from 3.17 to 3.25 after tonight's checker and a reader who works with bonds each gave the list of
things Laura should watch a 4. Friday's checker had given it 2.

On Monday new readers try two versions of the part about the range, one of them also comparing it to where a printer
trims a page. Five rows of the outline call the building money the "gift", a word our list of names says not to use.
