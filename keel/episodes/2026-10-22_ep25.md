---

Felix opened the email at 7:40 a.m. on the 389 bus, which is where he reads everything twice.

*Week 4: Wharton Global High School Investment Competition.* The first line was the reminder he didn't need:
Trading Notes Analysis due Friday, 5:00 p.m. ET. Saturday 8 a.m. for them. He'd had it on Nora's whiteboard for a
month.

The second line made him sit up.

> "Update: The Investment Policy Statement will now be submitted as a form on SurveyMonkey Apply. You will not
> submit a PDF. All other requirements remain the same."

He screenshotted it. Then he kept reading, slowly, and by the time the bus reached school he'd found two more
things and highlighted them in his three colours. He messaged Nora before first period.

**Felix:** Three things in the Wharton email. 1) IPS is a form now, not a PDF. 2) TIPS and STRIPS are named as
NOT permitted, "for BOTH contributions, the Investment Policy Statement and Final Report". 3) There's a list of
"tips for competition success". I think our TNA drafts break at least two of them.

**Nora:** Lunch. Library. Everyone. Bring the drafts.

---

### Lunch, library, the table by the window

Nora asked Tess to speak first, as agreed. Tess had printed the three reflections and the email side by side.

**Tess:** Tip four: "Do not focus on portfolio performance. You are not evaluated on whether your WInS portfolio
gains or loses money." Kai, your VT reflection says it's up 2.1% since we bought it. Which job is that sentence
doing?

**Kai:** It's doing the job of proving the trade worked.

**Felix:** The email says that isn't a job.

**Kai:** It's one clause.

**Tess:** It's eleven words out of a hundred. That's eleven percent of the reflection on the one thing they told
us they don't judge.

Kai looked at Jun for help. Jun shrugged.

**Jun:** Two weeks of a stock fund tells you nothing. If you want a number in there, the useful one is what the
fund is *for* in 2033, not what it did since the 7th.

The sentence went. Kai wrote it in his file of best lines that didn't make it.

Then Felix got to the one he'd been dreading. Amara's reflection on their long Treasury bond ended: *"In 2028 we
may add TIPS to protect the residency against Taiwan's rising costs."*

**Felix:** Sixth bullet under "NOT permitted": TIPS. And it says it covers both contributions and the Final
Report. So it isn't just WInS. We can't hold them, can't plan them, can't recommend them.

**Amara:** Fine. TIPS is out. But the worry didn't go anywhere. She's building something in Taiwan in 2033. If we
just delete the word, are we pretending the worry isn't there?

Nobody answered for a second. Then Felix opened the client case on his laptop. He'd marked it in orange weeks
ago.

**Felix:** Page 3: "each payment is a fixed $50,000 and is not adjusted for inflation." So the ten payments don't
need an inflation hedge. They're nominal. That's literally the rule.

**Amara:** That's the payments. What about the building?

Felix scrolled. Green, orange, then a line he'd marked red because nobody on the team had talked about it yet.

**Felix:** Page 4. Teams "should identify and explain their assumptions about ... the effect of inflation on
portfolio projections and facility costs."

**Nora:** So the worry is real, and Wharton wants it. It just isn't a product. It's an assumption.

**Amara:** So I don't get a fund. I get a paragraph.

**Nora:** You get the paragraph every other team will forget. In the Final Report, not the TNA. Can you live with
that?

Amara said she could, and by the end of lunch she'd started a page called "Inflation: what we assume and why".

---

### Jun's number

Jun hadn't said much because he'd been running his morning script on the library wi-fi. He'd pulled
yesterday's Treasury par curve (21 October close) and repriced the ten payments.

**Jun:** Ten payments of $50k, 2033 to 2042, priced for 1 January 2027: **$293,928**. That leaves about **$6,000**
under the $300k, which is about **21 basis points**. MODEL, par curve, 21 October. One decent rally in bonds
before January and the first deposit doesn't cover all ten.

**Kai:** So the safe plan isn't even safe.

**Jun:** The *promise* is safe once it's bought. The *price* isn't. That's different, and it's why we bought the
promise first instead of waiting.

**Nora:** That's tip one. "Let your investment strategy lead." Our trades are the strategy. The reflections have
to say that, every time: why this belongs, and how it supports Laura's goals. Those are the email's words. The
instruction's words are "supported, tested, or refined". Each reflection picks one.

**Tess:** And none of them mention how much money we made.

**Kai:** Or lost.

**Tess:** Or lost.

---

### The form

The last five minutes were about the IPS. Tess had already thought about it.

**Tess:** If it's a form, the font and double spacing stop mattering. What still matters: 50 words, 500 words,
no charts, no links, no citations. "All other requirements remain the same." Forms do odd things to curly quotes
and dashes. From today we draft the IPS in plain text and paste it into a test box before we trust it.

**Felix:** And we don't know yet how the title page goes in. I'll check SMApply when it opens. Until then it's
unverified.

Nora read the board out at 1:28, two minutes before the bell.
