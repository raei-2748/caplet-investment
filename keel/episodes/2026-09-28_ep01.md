# FICTION. Episode 1: Twenty-three thirty

**Monday 28 September 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Nora · Long Room: a light stretch (reading the platform) · Trading opens tonight 23:30 AEST**

> **Summary.** Trading opens at 23:30 Sydney. Kai wants an order in the first minute "so we're first"; Nora says
> nobody trades until the platform has been read. Jun posts the first daily ladder price ($292,264, 27bp of room),
> and the team learns why one rate is not enough. Nora holds the line and doesn't enjoy it.
> **Arcs:** 2 (two captains), 1 (the sail). **Values:** 1, 3. **Ideas:** I-01 (yield curve) adopted.

---

Jun's message came in at 6:58, before I'd even put my school shoes on.

**Jun:** Ten payments of $50k, 2033 to 2042, priced for 1 January 2027 off Friday's Treasury curve (25 Sep):
**$292,264**. Room under $300k: **$7,736, about 27 basis points**. MODEL, payment-date basis. First one. I'll do
this every morning.

**Kai:** Does it come with a horoscope?

**Felix:** Which curve? Par yields?

**Jun:** Par. 25 September close. Bootstrapped.

I didn't know what bootstrapped meant. I said so at lunch, in front of everyone, which was the first test of the
term and I nearly failed it. It's harder to say "I don't know" when you're the one holding the whiteboard.

---

### Lunch, the library, the table by the window

**Jun:** Okay. One sentence. A bond that pays in 2033 and a bond that pays in 2042 aren't worth the same per dollar
today, because money you wait longer for is worth less. The Treasury publishes one rate for each length of time:
one year, two, five, ten, twenty, thirty. That's the curve. I turn it into a price for each of Laura's ten dates.

**Nora:** So why not just use the ten-year rate for everything? It's in the middle.

**Jun:** Because the curve isn't flat. On Friday the 2-year was 4.81 and the 20-year was 5.54. If I priced
everything at one rate I'd be wrong by thousands, in a plan with about seven thousand dollars of room.

**Amara:** So the room is how much safer than "just enough" we are.

**Jun:** It's how much the price can move before the first $300k doesn't buy all ten payments. 27 basis points.
A quarter of a percent.

**Kai:** That's nothing. Rates move that in a bad week.

**Jun:** Yes. That's my point.

**Me:** So what? What does Laura do with that number?

Jun thought about it. That's the thing about Jun: he actually thinks about it.

**Jun:** She buys the promise while it's cheap enough to buy. And we watch the number every day, because if it goes
above $300k the plan has to say where the gap comes from.

Tess wrote it down. That was our first idea in the ledger, which Kai named "the ideas ledger" and started
immediately, because he's Kai: **the yield curve, priced date by date. Adopted.**

---

### 23:10, the group call

Trading opened at 23:30 our time, which is 9:30 a.m. in New York. Five of us were on the call. Amara had fallen
asleep on a draft and texted "go without me, sorry, cut anything".

Kai had the WInS order page open before I'd said hello.

**Kai:** I want something in at 23:30:01. A share of anything. So our first trade is the first second of the
competition.

**Me:** Why?

**Kai:** Because it's a story. Because it's bold. Because nobody else will.

**Felix:** I've read about a third of the FAQ. There's a Session Rules page I haven't opened. Position limits are
on it. I don't know what they are.

**Kai:** It's one share.

**Me:** Kai. What's the deadline?

**Kai:** There isn't one. That's my point. It's free.

**Me:** It's not free. Every trade has a note, and the notes are what they judge. A first trade with no reason is a
note with no reason. We'd have to write "we bought it to be first" and live with it until December.

He went quiet. Not sulky quiet. Kai quiet, which means he's thinking about whether he's wrong.

**Kai:** Fine. Prove me wrong with the rules, then. Felix, read them out.

So at 23:30, instead of trading, we read. Felix read the Session Rules aloud, in order, with the page open. Trading
started 28 September at 9:30 a.m. ET and ends 6 November at 4:00 p.m. ET. Three hundred thousand dollars, no
interest on cash. Two hundred trades. No day trading, no margin, no short selling. No fractional shares. Twenty-five
dollars a stock or fund trade, ten a bond trade. A minimum price for buying a stock. Position limits for funds and
bonds.

It took forty minutes on Earth. In the Long Room it took most of an afternoon, because every rule became a question
(what's a bond "unit"? when do bonds fill?) and Felix wouldn't move on until each one had an answer or the word
UNVERIFIED next to it.

When we came out Kai said, "Okay. That was worth not being first." He said it to the group, not to me, which is how
Kai apologises.

I said, "Decision, owner, date: no orders until the bond list is read and the notes are drafted. Felix owns the
list. Wednesday." Then I went to bed and lay awake thinking about whether I'd just made the team boring.
