### Arden day two: the picks

The instruction says each reflection explains how the decision "supported, tested, or refined" our strategy. Nora
had proposed one of each.

**Jun:** IBTM, the 2033 rung. That's *refined*. The plan said "a bond for each payment". WInS had no bond for 2033.
So we refined it: a term fund of Treasuries that closes in December 2032, with a margin because its payout isn't
fixed.

**Amara:** The Nov-2041 bond, the 2042 payment. That's *tested*. It's the longest rung, the one whose price moves most
when rates move. It tests our idea that the price doesn't matter if we hold it to the date.

**Kai:** VT. *Supported*. The growth money in one world fund, never used for the payments. And I get to write a
reflection about growth without mentioning returns, which will be the hardest thing I do all year.

**Me:** You'll manage.

**Kai:** You'll make sure.

Five to zero for the three, Jun abstaining because he said he'd picked his own trade and that was a conflict. Felix
said that wasn't how conflicts worked. Jun abstained anyway.

---

### Arden day three: the buckets

Amara had been quiet all morning, writing. Then she put her page in the middle of the table.

**Amara:** When we explain this to a co-sponsor in 2031, we can't say "liability matching". We can say this: Laura
has two pots of money. One is the promise pot: it already holds the ten payments, in bonds that pay out on the
dates. The other is the building pot: it grows, and what it grows to is what she can give. If the building pot has a
bad year, the promise pot doesn't notice.

**Kai:** Economists call that mental accounting. It's a bias. People treat money differently depending on which jar
it's in, when money's just money.

**Amara:** Is it a bias if the jars really are different? The promise pot has dates. The building pot doesn't.

**Jun:** She's right. Money is money, unless it's already owed on a date. Then it isn't.

**Felix:** And it matches the case. The payments come first; the facility is what's "responsible" after that.

**Nora:** So we adopt the picture, not the theory. For explaining, not for deciding.

Into the ledger: **I-11, two buckets: adopted for explaining; the buckets are real here because the cash flows are
dated.** Kai wrote "it's a bias" in the against column, and it stayed there, because it's true in general.

I wrote it in the journal, with the time and the words "off the top of my head". I don't know yet why I wrote it
down. I just know I've learned to write down the things people say when they're tired.
