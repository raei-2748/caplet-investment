### The Long Room, room day one: the mock judge

Nora had asked to play the judge. Not as Nora: as a former finalist, someone who had been through the competition
and now read for it. She sat at the far end of the table with our three reflections and the five criteria, and she
didn't look at any of us while she read.

Then she spoke in a voice I'd never heard her use. Slower. Kinder, and somehow more frightening.

**Nora (as judge):** Jun's: clear. It says the plan needed a bond for 2033, WInS had none, so the team refined the plan
with a term fund and a margin. I can see the decision. Kai's: honest. It says what the fund is for and what it
isn't. One sentence is too clever. Amara's.

She looked at me. I felt Nani's kitchen table under my hands.

**Nora (as judge):** Beautifully written. I know how Laura feels. I don't know why *this* trade. If I took the bond's
name out, the reflection would work for any bond. The instruction asks how the decision "aligned with your overall
investment strategy". Where is the alignment?

**Me:** It's in the feeling. That the last payment is the one she'd worry about most.

**Nora (as judge):** Then say why the team chose the *longest* rung as its test. What did it test?

I didn't have an answer that wasn't a feeling. Tess, next to me, didn't say anything, and then, after about three
seconds (I counted), she did.

**Tess:** It tests whether price matters if you hold to the date. The Nov-2041 bond's price moves most when rates
move. On paper it's lost value since the 7th, because yields fell... no, rose... Jun?

**Jun:** Yields have fallen since the 7th. Its price has risen. Either way: its price moves most, and Laura doesn't
care, because she holds it to November 2041 and it pays its face value then.

**Nora (as judge):** That's the reflection.

Tess took my draft, drew a line under my second sentence, and wrote in the margin: *say what it tested*. Then she
took my favourite line out (*the last promise is the heaviest*) and put it in the folder. I let her. It was the right
cut. It's still the truest line in there, for me.

### Room day two: the currency

I had one more thing, and I'd been afraid to bring it.

**Me:** The residency is in Taiwan. Laura's money is in U.S. dollars. When she pays for a building in 2033, she'll pay
in Taiwan dollars. If the U.S. dollar falls against the Taiwan dollar, her gift buys less building.

**Kai:** Hedge it.

**Felix:** With what? Currency hedges are derivatives: forwards, futures, swaps. Derivatives are on the "NOT
permitted" list. And the case says teams aren't expected to estimate the facility's cost.

**Jun:** The exchange rate was 31.82 Taiwan dollars per U.S. dollar on 18 September. It moves a few percent a year,
sometimes more. Five years out, it could be ten or fifteen percent either way. MODEL thinking, not a number to quote.

**Me:** So we can't fix it and we can't price it. Do we ignore it?

**Nora:** What did we do with inflation?

**Me:** We made it an assumption. In the Final Report.

**Nora:** Same.

Into the ledger: **I-17, currency risk (USD against TWD): parked for the Final Report, as an assumption about facility
costs; no hedge, because derivatives are not permitted.** I wrote the "why" myself: *The worry is real; the tool is
banned; the honest answer is to say so.*

### Room day three: Felix's piles

Felix was ranking his checks for the week, the way he started on Sunday. I watched him put a card in the "can wait"
pile: *Re-read the public SMApply FAQ (last read 14 Oct).*

**Me:** Why can that wait?

**Felix:** Because the rules don't change in the week of a deadline. Usually.

had gone out a bit. Jun said tides come back.
