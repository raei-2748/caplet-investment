# FICTION. Episode 39: Hard to follow

**Wednesday 4 November 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Amara · Long Room: light (one room day, the morning pick) · Final IPS for Caplet's review: tonight**

> **Summary.** Ray's note lands overnight: the writing still reads as AI, and the team can't tell what Keel has
> achieved because Keel overcomplicates things. V55 answers the first half (same ideas, plain English); a one-page
> status answers the second. The last round before the hand-over checks only what V55 says against its sources.
> Amara, judging, reads Ray's note twice and decides it's about the team as much as the draft.
> **Arcs:** 7 (outside: the people we work for tell us how we sound). **Values:** 4, 5.
> **Ideas:** none new.

---

Amara read Ray's note on the bus, then read it again, which she didn't usually do with anything.

*The team doesn't know what Keel has achieved. Keel overcomplicates things.*

She scrolled back through her own messages from yesterday. "LS-14 read-back clean." "Exhibit N." "W5 on trial." She
knew what every one of them meant. That was the problem.

---

"Before the round," she said at seven, before Nora could start. "Can we talk about Ray's note?"

Nora had it open too. "Yes. That's why the brief's half the length today."

"It's not just the brief." Amara pulled up the Kitchen's orders from last night and shared them. "Look at this. Rotation
row eight. LS-16. Exhibit N. Deletion test on A33. If I were Ray I wouldn't read past the first line either."

"It's precise," Kai said.

"It's precise for us."

Tess laughed, a short one. "She's right. I wrote half of that."

"So what do we do with it on the last day?" Felix asked. "We're not rebuilding the office by tonight."

"No," said Amara. "But every line we write today that a student will read, we write for someone who's never opened
the repo. No codes. If it needs a code to make sense, it's not ready."

Nora typed it into the round as she said it. "Agreed. That goes on today's whole office, not just us."

"Vote?" said Jun.

"Does it need one?"

"No," said Jun. "I just like saying it."

---

Then the round, which was small on purpose. V55 had landed overnight, plainer than anything before it, and Nora
re-aimed the jobs at it rather than at yesterday's text.

"Felix, the option line's changed. It used to say 'unless the market rises'. Now it's 'can expire worthless'."

"Softer," Felix said. "Easier to make true. But nobody's checked those words at a regulator's page, so I will."

"Tess, the checkers' sources."

"Checking the checkers," Tess said. "Somebody has to, once."

Amara had V55 open on her side of the screen. She stopped on paragraph six.

"*We assume about 7.5% a year from them, but no payment relies on it.*" She looked up. "Ahaan took model numbers out.
Is this one?"

"It's J.P. Morgan's world-share figure," said Kai. "Not ours."

"Then the sentence should be able to show that if someone asks. I'm not judging it, I'm sending it to the Window."

"Fair," said Kai. "I'd rather the Window says it's fine than you do."

"So would I."

---

On the bus home she wrote the same thing three times in her notes app and deleted it twice. The one she kept said:

*If it needs a code to make sense, it's not ready.*

Then she sent it to the group chat, and Tess replied with a thumbs up, which from Tess counted as a speech.
