# FICTION. Episode 2: The ticker that wasn't

**Tuesday 29 September 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Felix · Long Room: 2 room days (the bond list) · 25 days to the TNA**

> **Summary.** Felix reads the whole WInS bond list and finds the hole in it: no Treasury matures between 15 Feb
> 2031 and 15 Feb 2036, so Laura's 2033-2036 payments have no bond of their own. He also catches a "2033 Treasury"
> fund from a forum post that holds company bonds. He puts the five judging criteria on the wall. Kai buys him a pie.
> **Arcs:** 5 (two clocks). **Values:** 2. **Ideas:** sets up I-03.

---

I read the bond list on the 389 on the way in. It doesn't fit on a phone screen, so I read it in pieces and wrote
the maturities on the back of my bus ticket. By Pyrmont Bridge I had a column of dates with a gap in the middle that
I didn't believe, so I read it again.

At lunch I asked Nora for the Long Room. She asked how long. I said I didn't know, which she says is the first time
I've ever not known how long something would take.

---

### The Long Room, room day one

The WInS bond page lists forty-three U.S. Treasuries. I typed every maturity into a table, sorted it, and drew the
ten payment dates next to it: 1 January 2033 to 1 January 2042.

**Me:** There's nothing maturing between 15 February 2031 and 15 February 2036. Nothing. The first four payments,
2033 to 2036, have no bond that pays out just before them.

**Jun:** You're sure? Not just a bad page?

**Me:** I read it on the bus, again on my laptop, and again on the school desktop. Three times, three devices.

**Kai:** So the "buy one bond for each payment" plan is dead on arrival. Love that for us.

**Jun:** Not dead. Four rungs out of ten. What's on the list after 2036?

**Me:** Enough. A Treasury roughly every year from 2036 to 2042. So the last six payments could each have a bond.

**Amara:** And the first four? Those are the ones she needs first.

**Nora:** Options. Two, not five.

Jun had one by the end of the afternoon: funds that hold Treasuries maturing in a single year and then close, paying
out what they hold. iShares calls them iBonds. WInS showed one for December 2032. He thought there would be others.

**Me:** I'll check each one is a Treasury fund. Not "probably". Each one.

---

### Room day two, and the ticker

That's where the pie comes from.

Kai had found a forum thread, a student finance board, where someone recommended "the Dec 2033 iBonds fund" for
exactly our problem. It had the right year in its name. The ticker looked right. He'd already put it in his
shortlist.

I opened the fund's own page. The name had "iBonds" and "Dec 2033" in it, and also one more word, in smaller
type: **Corporate**. It held company bonds, not Treasuries.

**Kai:** It says iBonds.

**Me:** It says iBonds Dec 2033 Term Corporate. Can I read you something? The trading page lists what's allowed:
"stocks", "ETFs", "Government/Treasury bonds". A corporate bond fund is an ETF, so it's probably *allowed* on
WInS. But it isn't a Treasury. If we tell a judge the payments are funded by Treasuries, and one rung is company
debt, that's not "probably not allowed". That's untrue.

**Kai:** You're saying I nearly put a company bond fund under Laura's promise because a stranger on a forum said
so.

**Me:** I'm saying the forum is a lead, not a fact.

He was quiet. Then he left the room, which you're not supposed to do alone, and came back twenty minutes of room
time later with two pies from the canteen, which is somehow always open when the Long Room is, which nobody can
explain.

**Kai:** Beef for the footnote. Don't make it weird.

I didn't make it weird. I wrote the rule in my table: **every fund in the plan is checked on its own issuer page,
with the full name, before it goes on any list.**

---

### The criteria on the wall

The last thing I did was the one I'd promised Nora on Sunday night. Where does Wharton say how the semifinal is
chosen? It's on the Deliverables page, under Evaluation Criteria, five paragraphs. I printed them and stuck them
on the wall above the long table:

Investment Strategy. Client Knowledge and Objectives. Portfolio Analysis. Articulation of Competition Experience.
Creativity and Presentation.

Amara read the Articulation one aloud: "Demonstrates teamwork, communication, and learning; clearly explains the
team's research and decision-making process; and reflects meaningfully on the team's growth and response to
challenges."

**Amara:** So they want the story of how we decided, not just what.

**Tess:** Then the journal isn't a hobby. It's a deliverable.

She said it like a fact. It was the most I'd heard her say in a meeting.

---

When we came out it was 13:22 outside. Jun's number for Monday's close had arrived: **$290,291**, about **34
basis points** of room. Yields had gone up, so the ladder was cheaper. Kai said, "See, the market likes us." Jun
said the market doesn't know we exist.
