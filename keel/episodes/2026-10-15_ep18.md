*Journal, lunch, the Long Room, room day one.*

The screenshots needed filing. Eighteen from the first night, four from the second, named by ticket, and the drive
folder had become a mess, because Kai names files the way he talks. My job today was boring and I was glad of it.

Each "before" screenshot shows the ticket with the note typed in the box, just before Kai pressed. I was renaming
them into order when I read the Nov-2040 one properly for the first time. Not heard. Read.

> *"...This Treasury matures in November 2040 and repays its face value, which guarantees Laura's 2041 payment."*

Guarantees.

I read it again. On 7 October, at about 01:50, Kai read it aloud on the call and Felix said "Checked" and I counted it
(241 characters) and nobody blinked, because a Treasury that repays its face value really does pay for the 2041
payment. That's the plan. It's true in the way everyone means it.

It isn't true in the way Jun means things. A U.S. Treasury pays in full unless the U.S. government doesn't pay. That's
extremely unlikely and it isn't zero. Jun's list of words he never uses about a market: "guaranteed", "risk-free",
"definitely". It's in his profile. It's on the wall.

And the note can't be edited. Felix said so on the 2nd. Notes can be added to, not changed. Whatever Wharton reads
for that trade will say "guarantees".

It isn't one of our TNA picks. Nobody at Wharton might ever read it. But the plan's whole honesty rule, never
promise a number she doesn't own, sits on the word "certainty" being defined carefully, and here was our own saved
note saying "guarantees".

---

*Room day one, later.*

Amara wrote that note. She drafted all eleven. Most of them, I'd cut by a third. This one I'd cut too: the earlier
version said "keeps Laura's promise forever". I'd been proud of getting "forever" out. I'd missed "guarantees" because
I was busy being proud.

Amara came into the room at 13:05 Sydney time, which was afternoon in the room, with tea and a face that meant she hadn't
slept. The library kids. She'd written them notes. She'd been the one asked, at home, "why weren't you there?"

She sat down across from me and said, "What are you filing?"

I said, "Screenshots."

That's all I said.

---

*Room night.*

I've thought about it all evening. Here are the reasons I gave myself:

1. It isn't a TNA pick.
2. It can't be changed anyway.
3. It's true in the ordinary meaning.
4. Amara's having a bad week.
5. I'll say it tomorrow, properly, with the fix ready, so it isn't just a problem.

Four of those are reasons. Number five is the one I always use. It's the poster in Year 9. It's the group report in
Year 10. "I'll say it when I'm sure. I'll say it when I've got the fix. I'll say it when it's kinder."

Jun's log, Tuesday. Amara's note, today. That's two things I've seen this week and not said. They're both about
people I like. I think that's the pattern, and I think I've just found it.

Jun's number came in while I was writing: **$291,750** for Wednesday's close, about **29bp**. The room is shrinking.
Kai replied "😬" and Felix replied "14 Oct curve" and nobody replied to me because I hadn't said anything.
