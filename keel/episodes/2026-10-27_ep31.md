# FICTION. Episode 31: Same number, different year

**Tuesday 27 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Felix · Long Room: light (one room day, a lunch check) · IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00 (11 days)**

> **Summary.** Felix has a chemistry yearly at 9 and twenty minutes of train. Yesterday Kai's reading pile "fixed"

> On the train Felix reads J.P. Morgan's release and finds the fix is the mistake. EAFE was 7.5% in *last year's*

> **Arcs:** 2 (Kai loses properly, again), 3 (Felix: rules as care, not policing). **Values:** 3, 4. **Ideas:** I-25.

---

The yearly was at nine. I'd done my equilibrium questions twice and Le Chatelier once, and on the train I told
myself I'd do him again, and instead I opened the thing that had been bothering me since dinner.

Kai's note from last night was three lines in the group chat. *Our VT reflection says 7.5% as per JPMorgan. That's
their EAFE number, not world. World is 7.0. We shouldn't repeat it in the IPS.* He'd attached the 2026 table, row
highlighted. It looked airtight. It also looked like the kind of airtight that's only true on the day you check it.

Because I'd read the 2027 report on Sunday, for something else. And the press release is short.

> "Global equities are projected to return 7.5% (USD), increasing from 7.0%."

Nineteenth of October. Four days before we submitted.

I read it twice at Strathfield and once more at Burwood, in case it changed. Last year: world 7.0, EAFE 7.5. This
year: world 7.5. Kai had matched the right number to the wrong year. Jun did the same thing on Sunday, the other way
round. We have now made the same mistake twice in three days, which Tess will say is a pattern and which I'm
going to have to agree is a pattern.

I texted the screenshot to the group at 8:41 and turned my phone off for the exam. Le Chatelier went fine, I think.
A system under stress shifts to undo the stress. Which is also what happened in the group chat while my phone was off.

At lunch, the back table.

**Kai:** So I was wrong.

**Felix:** You were right about last year.

**Kai:** That's what Jun said to me yesterday. It's becoming a catchphrase.

**Jun:** It's becoming a method.

**Amara:** The scary bit isn't the number. It's that we nearly put a line in the IPS that said our own TNA was wrong.
To a judge. Who'd have read both.

**Tess:** And the TNA's locked. We'd have been arguing with ourselves in public.

**Nora:** So what changes?

Jun had already written it on a napkin, which is very unlike Jun.

**Jun:** Every run, first line: curve date, which edition, how many paths. If it's not stamped it's not a number.

**Kai:** Fine. But I'm re-running the range thing at 7.5 tonight, because if the half point moves the top, I want to
be the one who finds it.

**Felix:** It won't, much. The re-run on Monday moved one figure by a point.

**Kai:** Then I'll find that it doesn't. Out loud.

Nora asked whether this meant we should hurry the draft, now that one argument was settled. Nobody wanted to. Tess
said the draft we'd written on Monday read like it was written on Monday, which was a fair hit on all of us, and
Nora put it to a show of hands: no new draft before Friday. Six hands. Kai's went up last, but it went up.

I got the chemistry back in my head on the walk to the next period. A system under stress shifts to undo the stress.
It doesn't say it gets there quickly.
