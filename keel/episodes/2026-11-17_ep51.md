On Sunday we took half a percent off what the bond funds will pay, for costs beyond their listed fee, and the range
Laura gives co-sponsors in 2031 came down a little. We left it off the ten yearly payments, which rest on the same
funds. A reader asked about that on Monday night, and Jun worked out what it meant: at that rate the payments come up
$1,181 short, all of it on the 2036 and 2037 payments. At 07:00 we decided to fix it on Wednesday morning.

**07:44.** Another reader checks our notes against what Caplet handed in. In the part where the team looks back, our
notes gave the old model's 3.2% chance of a missed payment, about 1 in 31, as the reason Caplet dropped its first
plan, the one heavy in shares. Caplet's statement gives its own reason, "the actual nature of a writer's income".
Felix changed the notes to that reason and kept the 3.2% only as a figure from the old model's test.

**08:18.** I got something wrong in my instructions for the morning's two writers: I described the four readers who
played funders on Monday as "program officers at foundations". Both writers left that line out of what they wrote, and
I've fixed the instructions since.

**Evening.** Leo didn't have his book at tutoring, so we spent the hour on Priya's maths test for Thursday, and I read

**19:13.** Jun redid the sums tonight instead of waiting for Wednesday, with the half percent on the ten payments too.
He also fixed the main script, which had been counting a year slightly wrong, a gap of $1.14. The 2028 deposit also
buys some extra bonds, so the ten get paid even if the cash waiting between payments earns nothing. Those now cost
$36,774, up from $35,894. Add the $2,388 from the same deposit that buys the last bit of the ten payments, and the
first part of that deposit is about $39,000. The range for co-sponsors stays about $95,000 to $115,000. We'd also
tested the range with fifteen different forecasts for shares, and thirteen of them now still give $115,000 as its top,
where before it was fourteen. We hadn't put that count on our list of things to recheck after a change. Tess put the
new figures through fourteen files.

Felix's research note found a forecaster we had missed. Goldman Sachs puts world shares at 7.7% a year over the next
ten years. Our numbers use J.P. Morgan's 7.5%, and on Sunday we'd changed "the top" to "the upper third". With Goldman
in, J.P. Morgan ties sixth of sixteen, so "upper third" was only true of our own list of fifteen. The notes now say
"above the middle of published forecasts".

In the morning the line we're saving to surprise a judge had been reworded to compare the first plan's chance of a
missed payment, about 1 in 31, with none for Caplet's plan from January 2028, and three readers had rated it 4.0 for
how much it surprised them, up from 2.7 in an earlier test. The evening's checking reader went back to it. The case
asks that Laura's ten payments be met with "a high degree of certainty", and Caplet's statement already says what it
did with that: "we decided to take that one step further". Our line had judged "high" by a test of its own: was the
plan as safe as anything the deposits could buy? Kai sent me the reader's page and I rewrote the line. Now it says
that the first plan, which paid every payment in about 30 of every 31 runs of the old model, would have counted as
"high" for most people, that Caplet dropped it because of how a writer is paid, and that going one step further took
the chance of a missed payment from about 1 in 31 to none from January 2028. Nobody has scored the new version yet.

Caplet's picture for the report is pencil and ink. Ink is any figure that US government bonds Laura owns will pay on
its date, and pencil is any figure no bond she owns will pay yet. My two evening writers wrote the team's look-back
and the opening page of the holdings section. Readers who had read Caplet's own writing said whether each passage
could be the team's. They said yes to the plain versions 12 times in 12, and 6 times in 6 to the opening page that

the seal on a painting is the painter's signature, so the outline's line calling Laura's statement "a seal" would make
