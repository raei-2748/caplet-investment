# FICTION. Episode 28: Rules before the first tile

**Saturday 24 October 2026, Sydney (afternoon) · Point of view: Felix · Long Room: none (a list, not a stretch) · IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00 (14 days)**

> **Summary.** Felix rereads one line of the IPS file at Por Por's mahjong table and turns Friday's checklist around: the IPS is the

> Report against Keel's IPS v1. Nearly everything is there; the review rule is not. Kai asks for the line that requires

Saturdays belong to Por Por's table. Four women from church, the good tiles, the scoring sheet in her handwriting,
and me at the end of the dining table with the IPS file because the noise helps. She never lets a game start until
everyone has agreed the scoring. Mrs Leung once tried to argue a limit hand after the first discard. Por Por didn't
even look up.

I'd read line 73 maybe eleven times before. *"Once submitted, the IPS becomes the official record of your team's
investment strategy. Your team may not revise its investment strategy after the submission deadline."* This time I
read it next to line 68, the bit that says the reserve numbers and the 2031 range come later, *"in the Final Report
using the strategy established in the IPS."*

So the numbers can wait. The rules can't. Friday's checklist asked what the IPS had to cover. That was the wrong way
round. Anything we do in December has to point back to a sentence we froze in November, or it reads as a change.

So I turned it around. Every job the case gives the Final Report (Laura's profile, lines 96 to 99, 101 to 104, 117 to 120,
146 to 148), and beside each one the sentence in our IPS v1 that makes it a rule rather than a December idea.
Twelve rows. Ten found their sentence straight away. One, the buffer, is tomorrow's vote and has nowhere to sit yet.
The last one had nothing.

I rang the others at four. Jun was at the pool. Tess picked up on the first ring, which she always does.

**Felix:** Our IPS never says what we do if something changes. If WInS stops listing a fund, if Laura's plans move.

**Amara:** We hold to 2033. That's in.

**Felix:** Holding is a rule for when nothing happens. In December we write a section on how well we implemented it.
If a fund disappears between now and then and we swap it, that's either the plan working or the plan changing.
Depends on whether we wrote it down.

**Kai:** Who says? Show me the line that wants a review rule.

I looked. I'd known the answer before he asked, which is the worst way to look.

**Felix:** There isn't one. Line 76 says they'll judge "how effectively it implemented that strategy". The rest is my
reading. My reading is that you can't implement a rule you never wrote.

**Kai:** Then say that. "My reading." Not a quote.

**Felix:** My reading.

He let it go, which is how Kai agrees.

**Nora:** You've got twelve rows. If we only fix three before Tuesday, which three?

Two weeks ago I would have asked for the night. I'd have sorted the twelve into my three piles and brought back
twelve again, with colours.

**Felix:** The shortfall order, because the ladder's nearly at $300k and it isn't the fine print anymore. Tomorrow's
buffer vote, because whatever we pick has to land in a sentence. And the review rule, because it's the only one
that's missing.

**Nora:** That took a minute.

**Felix:** About.

**Tess:** Which job is that sentence doing, and how many words is it? We're at 484 on the strict count. Sixteen left.

**Amara:** I can give you some. "A project that will change once it is real" can lose "once it is real". It's
already the point.

**Tess:** Four. Twelve to go.

**Amara:** You'll get them.

We agreed one sentence, twenty words or fewer, written by Amara on Monday, cut by Tess on Tuesday. Nora wrote it on
the board under the sail vote: *review rule: Amara, Mon 26 Oct.* Jun texted from the pool an hour later, just
"agree, and say what we substitute", which is the part we'd forgotten. A Treasury maturing earlier, never later. If
we swap something, the promise gets safer, not riskier.

After the call Por Por came over to take back her good pen.

"Your rules are finished?"

"The list is. The sentence isn't."

She looked at the twelve rows the way she looks at a hand someone has declared too early.

"Rules before the first tile," she said. "After, only arguing."

Then she took the pen.
