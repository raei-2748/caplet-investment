# FICTION. Episode 6: The frontier

**Saturday 3 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Nora · Long Room: 5 Arden days (what kind of plan this is) · 21 days to the TNA**

> **Summary.** Kai arrives with Markowitz's 1952 paper and wants to run an optimiser over all of Laura's money.
> Felix answers with a pension fund's liability-matching report Kai himself had lent him. Jun shows why "risk" means
> something different for Laura than for the efficient frontier. The team rejects modern portfolio theory as the
> method and keeps its lesson inside the growth money. Kai wants to trade on Sunday night; Nora says not until all
> eleven notes are drafted, and doesn't know yet whether she's right.
> **Arcs:** 2 (two captains), 1 (the sail). **Values:** 1, 3. **Ideas:** I-05 rejected, I-06 adapted.

The bakery at six. Manoushe, za'atar, the oven door that sticks. I took five orders at once without writing any of
them down, and thought about Kai the whole time, because he'd messaged at 5:12 a.m.:

**Kai:** Read Markowitz last night. The actual 1952 paper. We've been doing this wrong. Long Room at 2?

I said yes. Then I said "bring the paper". Then I said "and bring an argument against yourself". He sent back a
thumbs-up and a picture of a bell curve wearing sunglasses.

---

### The Long Room, Arden day one

Kai had printed the first three pages of "Portfolio Selection" and a textbook chapter. He talked for twenty minutes
without stopping, which is about his limit, and it was good.

**Kai:** Markowitz says you don't judge an investment on its own. You judge the whole portfolio: its expected return
and how much it wobbles. Mix things that don't move together and the wobble falls faster than the return. There's
a curve, the efficient frontier: the best return you can get for each level of wobble. You pick your point on it.
So let's do it properly. Put all of Laura's money, the $300k and the $150k, through an optimiser: stocks, bonds,
the lot. Pick the point that matches her risk. Done. That's the most respected idea in finance. Who says it doesn't
apply?

**Me:** Is that fair, everyone? Kai's point: judge the whole mix by return and wobble, and pick the best mix on the
frontier.

Nods. Then Felix, who never goes first, went first.

**Felix:** Can I read you something? It's from the pension report you lent me on Wednesday.

Kai put his head on the table.

**Felix:** Not a quote, a summary, because it's twelve pages. A pension plan's first job is to meet its promised
payments. So it hedges those payments first, with assets whose cash flows match them, and invests only the surplus
for growth. They call it liability-driven investing. That's Laura. Ten promised payments, then a surplus.

**Kai:** That's just MPT with extra steps.

**Jun:** No. Can I show something?

He drew two lines on the board.

**Jun:** Line one: the price of our bond ladder over the next six years. It wobbles. If rates jump, it falls. On the
frontier, that's risk. Line two: the cash it pays Laura on 1 January 2033, 2034, all the way to 2042. That doesn't
wobble at all, unless the U.S. government doesn't pay. The optimiser sees line one. Laura lives on line two.

**Amara:** So the frontier thinks the safe thing is risky.

**Jun:** For this job, yes. It measures the wrong risk. Laura's risk isn't "the value moves". It's "a payment is
short".

**Kai:** Okay. But for the growth money, MPT's right. Diversify. Don't put the $150k in one stock.

**Jun:** Completely. There, it's right.

---

### Arden days two to four

It took three more days to agree on the wording, which Tess says is normal and I say is the cost of having Amara and
Tess in the same room.

**Amara:** We can't write "liability-driven investing" in anything Laura or a co-sponsor reads. It sounds like a
lawsuit.

**Tess:** Then we don't use the name. We use the picture. The keel and the sail.

**Kai:** So the most respected idea in finance loses to a boat.

**Tess:** For this client. In this case. In the ledger it says "rejected as the method; diversification kept".

The ledger got two rows. **Modern portfolio theory: rejected as the method for the plan; kept inside the sail.**
**Liability matching: adapted; never named.** Kai wrote the reasons himself, which surprised me, and they were fair.
He said: "A rejected idea with a reason is still research." Tess wrote that in the journal under "who said what".

---

### Arden day five: the button

Then Kai said it.

**Kai:** We've got the plan. Bonds for each payment, term funds for the gap, a world fund for the growth. Let's
place the orders tomorrow night. Monday morning our time at the latest. Every day we wait, the ladder price moves.

**Me:** No.

**Kai:** Why?

**Me:** Because we don't have the notes. Felix said a note may never be editable. We've drafted none of the eleven.
We haven't decided the 2037 rung. If we place on Sunday night, the notes get written at midnight, in the order
screen, by whoever's awake.

**Kai:** Jun's number says the ladder is cheap right now. Thirty-nine basis points of room. It could be twenty by
Wednesday.

**Jun:** It could be fifty. I don't know which.

**Me:** Then the price is a coin toss and the notes aren't. Decision: no orders until all eleven notes are drafted,
counted and read aloud, and the 2037 rung is voted. Owner: Amara for the notes, Kai and Jun for the rung. Target:
orders the night of Tuesday 6 October.

**Kai:** And if the room's gone by Tuesday?

**Me:** Then I was wrong and I'll say so in front of everyone.

He looked at the door. Argue hard, decide once. He didn't say anything else. That's how I knew it cost him.

I surfaced us at 17:30 Earth time. Netball was at four; I'd missed it. Lina asked why I looked "like after an exam".
