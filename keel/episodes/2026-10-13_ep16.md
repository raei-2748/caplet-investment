*Journal, lunch, the Long Room, room day one.*

Kai had a new one. He announced it like a magician.

**Kai:** The Kelly criterion. 1956. It tells you exactly how much of your money to bet when you have an edge, to
grow your wealth fastest over time. Professional gamblers use it. Some investors swear by it. Let's size the growth
money with Kelly.

**Jun:** What's our edge?

**Kai:** The stock market goes up over time.

**Jun:** Kelly needs the odds and the payoff, known. We have a guess at the average and a guess at the volatility.
If we overestimate the edge, full Kelly bets too much, and over-betting is how you go broke in Kelly's own maths.

**Felix:** And it maximises long-run growth. Laura's test is a promise and a building in 2033. Not "fastest growth
over an infinite future".

**Amara:** What does she feel in a bad year?

**Kai:** Like she's been Kelly'd.

**Nora:** Verdict.

**Kai:** Rejected. Needs known odds, which we don't have, and it's built for infinite games, which Laura isn't
playing.

I looked at the room clock. Nine minutes. A record. I wrote it down. Kai asked me to write down that the real
reason he brought it was that he wanted to say "Kelly criterion" in a meeting. I wrote that down too.

*Room night, after the house-rule bedtime. The log.*

I keep the Long Room log: Sydney date, minutes, room days, purpose, who was in. Tonight I was adding the
stretches since the 4th, and I did something I don't usually do: I checked the log against the files. Every file we
save in the room carries the room clock's time.

The team's files fit. Drafts in the day, nothing between the house-rule bedtime and the house-rule wake-up.

Jun's didn't. The sizing sheet: saved at room-time 5:52, room day four of the 6 October stretch. The Sharpe table:
4:10, day two of the 9 October one. Two scripts at 3 a.m. Something tonight, while I was writing this, at 1:40.

I looked across the room. His bunk was empty. He was at the far end of the long table, headphones on, the screen
light on his face.

Nobody works in the room alone. That's on the door, in Nora's marker. It's there because of the fourteen-hour night
in Ep 3. It's there because people are worse at everything when they don't sleep, and because the room makes it
very easy not to.

I could have said something. I was right there. I could have said "Jun, it's 1:40." I know exactly what Nora would
want me to do, and what the journal is for, and what I've written at the top of my own page since Year 10: *Why
didn't you say so?*

I didn't. I told myself it's his. It's how he works. It's not a rule about the plan, it's a rule about a person, and
it's not mine to enforce. And he's Jun: he'd be embarrassed for a week.

All of that is true. None of it is the reason. The reason is that I don't know how to say it without it sounding
like an accusation, and I'd rather be silent than clumsy.

I'm writing it here because the journal is supposed to be true, even when I'm the one it's about.
