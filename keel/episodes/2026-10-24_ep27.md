# FICTION. Episode 27: Four basis points

**Saturday 24 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Nora · Long Room: light (two room days, Lab and Library) · TNA in; IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00 (14 days)**

> **Summary.** The first morning after the TNA. Thursday's U.S. close cuts the ladder's room to $1,139 (4bp, MODEL), so
> the January-gap rule becomes the expected case. In the Lab, Jun's one-dial test beats Kai's roast. In the Library,
> Felix's defence-in-depth reading breaks exactly where it matters: the back-ups all get wet in the same rain. The
> only layer that stays dry is a small Treasury buffer. Nora deletes a decision she typed too early and holds it for the sail vote.
> **Arcs:** 1 (Nora holds a question open), 2 (Kai: the leap is the honesty rule), 7 (the tiredness said out loud). **Values:** 2, 3, 5. **Ideas:** I-20, I-21.

---

The bakery opens at six on Saturdays and the first hour smells like burnt sugar and bleach. I was halfway through
the second tray of knots when my phone buzzed on the bench. Jun, 6:58, like a clock.

**Jun:** Thursday's close is up. Ten-year down 17 points. The ladder costs $298,861 on the first of January. Room
under $300,000: $1,139. Four basis points. MODEL, on the 22 October curve, payment-date basis.

**Kai:** Four. We had twenty-one on Wednesday.

**Jun:** On the bond-maturity basis it's already over. Minus a couple of thousand.

**Kai:** So the January-gap rule isn't the fine print anymore. It's the plan.

He was right, and I knew what I wanted to do with it. I typed *Decision: buffer from the 2028 money, owner Jun,
by tonight* and looked at it while Mrs Nguyen asked me twice for the tongs. Then I deleted it. Tess told me once
that I close questions the way other people close doors on a draught. I wrote instead: *Lab at eleven. Nobody
decides anything before the sail vote.*

---

The Lab is the corner of the Office with the good whiteboard and the bad chair. Kai's roast from Wednesday was still
on the board in his capitals: ONE NUMBER FOR A CURVE THAT TWISTS? Jun had written the test under it: take the cost
on 24 September, move it $289 for every 0.01% the ten-year moves, and see if it misses the full repricing by more
than $1,500 on any close.

**Jun:** Twenty closes. Worst miss, $652, on the 7th. Day to day, never more than $269. Thursday it said $298,841.
The real number is $298,861.

**Kai:** Twenty dollars.

**Jun:** Twenty dollars.

Kai looked at the table for a long time, which for Kai is about four seconds. Then he picked up the marker and wrote
the verdict himself: *ADOPT. I was wrong. The curve twists, just not enough to matter.* Jun didn't say anything.
He went downstairs and came back with two cans and put one on Kai's side of the bench, which in their language is a
paragraph.

**Amara:** So the whole promise has one dial.

**Jun:** For explaining. Not for the IPS. The IPS doesn't carry numbers.

---

The Library is Felix's. We were all a bit flat by then, so when he said "Can I read you something?" nobody groaned.
It was a nuclear safety report from 1996 that Kai had left on the pile, about defence in depth: you stack levels of
protection so one failure never reaches the people outside.

**Amara:** That's us. The bond is level one. Then the leftover, then Laura's half, then the gift above the floor.
The artists are the people outside.

**Felix:** Keep going. It says the levels have to be independent. Different, so one cause can't take out two.

Amara stopped. Tess said it first, which is new and which I'm still getting used to.

**Tess:** Ours aren't. Yields fall, the coupons come up short. And yields fall in bad years, and in bad years the
stocks fall too. The leftover's gone since Thursday. They all get wet in the same rain.

**Kai:** So the analogy breaks.

**Felix:** It breaks *there*. That's the useful part.

I could feel the room wanting me to say the answer. I didn't have it, so I asked for it. "Which layer stays dry?"

Amara got there slowly, out loud, the way she does when she's checking her own sentence as she goes.

**Amara:** Not stocks. Not the gift, it's half stocks. Something that doesn't shrink when yields fall. A short
Treasury. A small one, from the 2028 money, with one job.

**Kai:** A safety net for the safety net.

**Jun:** A *different* net. Give me ten minutes.

It took him twenty-five. With nothing extra, the payments need more than every back-up in about 9 paths in 100. With
$10,000 held back, 2.9. With $15,000, 1.3. Alone, the $10,000 is short in a quarter of the paths; stacked under the
other two, it does most of the work. MODEL, 22 October curve, coupon reinvestment only.

**Jun:** And every dollar of it comes off the floor. $10,000 of buffer is $10,000 less Laura can promise.

---

Kai did the Courtroom in his Spinnaker cap. His best charge wasn't the rival's. It was ours: the half Laura keeps
had three jobs in our draft. It backed the payments, it was the cushion for costs, and it was her defence against
a stronger Taiwan dollar.

**Tess:** Which job is that sentence doing?

**Kai:** All three. Same twenty-six thousand.

We ruled it amended: one sentence, ranked, payments first. Kai looked almost disappointed to win.

---

At six, in the Kitchen, I said the thing I'd been holding all day. "The buffer and the sail are the same money. Both
come out of the 2028 deposit. So we don't decide the buffer today. We decide it tomorrow, with the sail, in one
vote."

**Kai:** You're giving me a reason to lose the sail argument.

**Nora:** I'm giving you a number to lose it with. Or win it.

Tess was washing mugs. Without turning round she said, "Can I say something not about Laura? I'm really tired.
I think all of us are." Nobody made a joke. Jun said "yeah" first, which surprised everyone, Jun included.

We went home at seven. That's the earliest since the twenty-eighth.
