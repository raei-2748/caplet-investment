# FICTION. Episode 32: The one that isn't bought

**Wednesday 28 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Nora · Long Room: light (one room day, the morning pick) · IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00 (10 days)**

> **Summary.** First morning of the new research rounds. Nora has to pick one question and three places nobody has
> looked. Last night's draft gave the team a sentence it likes: make every promise safe on the day it becomes one.
> Nora finds the one promise the sentence doesn't cover yet, and makes that the question. Kai wants "five new ideas"
> instead; he loses 4-2 and takes the near field anyway. Tess is on a shift until four and scouts from the stockroom.
> **Arcs:** 5 (Nora chooses instead of collecting), 2 (Kai loses and still turns up). **Values:** 1, 3. **Ideas:** none new.

---

The bus from Epping is the only place I can think before eight, so that's where I pick the question.

Last night we finally had a sentence. Jun said it first, and Tess made him say it again slower: *every promise Laura
makes is made safe on or before the day it becomes a promise.* The ten cheques are promises from the first page, so
they're bought first. The bottom of the 2031 range is bought three years before she names it. The top becomes a promise
on the day she says it out loud, and that's the day it moves into bonds. Everything before that is a hope, and hopes
can carry risk.

I liked it so much that I didn't trust it. So on the bus I tried to break it.

The cheques are $50,000 each. Five of them come from bond funds that close in December. The other five come from
bonds that pay interest twice a year until they mature, and some of that interest has to be reinvested to make the
later cheques whole. If rates fall, the reinvested interest earns less. When it bites, the typical gap in the model
is about $3,200, though it's more like $16,000 if the 2028 deposit never comes. Small, but real, and right now our plan only covers it once, in January 2033, partly from Laura's half of the
stocks. Which means one promise, the smallest one, is still a hope until 2033.

That was the question. Not a new idea. The hole in the idea we already had.

**Kai:** (group chat, 7:12) can we do Q4 instead. five new strategies. that's what's missing
**Nora:** We have forty-four ideas in the bank. We have one sentence. I want to know if the sentence is true.
**Kai:** it's mostly true
**Tess:** "Mostly true" is what a judge writes in the margin.

We voted on the bus, which is a stupid way to vote and also the only way at 7:15. Four for the hole, two for new
ideas. Kai and Amara. Amara said she'd rather hunt flaws in a question she didn't pick, which is fair and slightly
terrifying.

Then the three places. The rule is one near, one adjacent, one far, and nobody goes where we've already been.

Kai gets structured settlements, the way a court orders money paid on fixed dates and someone has to buy those dates on
the day of the judgment. He wrote back *ok that's actually our problem* within a minute, which is the fastest Kai has
ever agreed with me.

Felix gets construction. His mum audits builders, and builders hold back a slice of every payment in case something
goes wrong later. Who owns that slice when nothing goes wrong is exactly our question about Laura's half.

Tess gets the theatre. She's in the Coles stockroom until four, so she'll read on her break. A stage manager calls
every cue live and none of them can be taken back. I want to know what happens at 7:59 when one is missing.

I wrote the lens at the top: *it is January 2042 and the tenth cheque was $3,000 short. Explain why.* Everyone has to
answer that in their field's words first.

At school Jun found me at the lockers.

**Jun:** You picked the bit that breaks my sentence.

**Nora:** It's our sentence.

**Jun:** That's worse. Now I have to fix it.

He didn't seem upset. He seemed like someone who'd been handed the interesting job.
