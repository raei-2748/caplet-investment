  it could fail.

  of our reviewers catch them.

*19:59.* Tess's rules went in, with her pages for Friday to Sunday. Every event in here has to be in the office log
at its real time, which is why I'm copying times off it.

*20:01.* Three files came back in the same minute, and I read them after piano, at about nine.

Felix's was on who reads our report. In 2024-25, 24 Wharton students reviewed the reports and 1,818 teams finished.
If each report was read once, that's about 76 each. I messaged him that 76 was a lot and he said only if each one is
read once. Aberdeen's judges at the 2024, 2025 and 2026 finales were someone who runs bond portfolios and a US credit
specialist. He wrote that readers like that will notice a loose sentence about bonds straight away, and gave an
example: an iBond fund pays back whatever its bonds are worth when it closes, which isn't a fixed amount the way a
single Treasury's is.

Kai's came out of the same job: thirty ways of reading the whole report. He wrote that on Fri 27 Nov each review
round should read the draft as someone else. Round one would read it
as the fortieth report of someone's day, round two as someone who manages bonds for a living, and round three as a
co-sponsor.

Amara's card, fifty rows on whether Laura's career moves like shares, I read twice. For American college graduates as
a group, pay moves with the share market of the year before, a correlation of about 0.52, and hardly at all with the
same year. In 2009 shares rose almost 26 per cent while US school book sales fell 13.8 per cent, and schools buy her
books. Caplet's IPS says that in a recession her advances, fees and licences "come later and smaller", and the card
says the data fits those words.

She also wrote that the usual textbook line treats a career as steady, like a bond, and tells a young worker to own
more shares because of it. Her note says the report has to name that and say why Laura is different: she has no pay
cheque, and apart from the 2028 deposit no money goes in or out of her portfolio before 2033.

Two lines in her card were marked as risks for the report to state. Caplet's IPS says the bottom of the range for the
co-sponsors "will remain constant", and that only holds once the Treasury fund maturing in 2032 is bought in January
2028; before then a recession can lower it. And the IPS's reasons to change the plan name "a missed deposit", and say
nothing about a smaller one.

Hana came in for my charger while I was on the 2009 numbers.

In the same minute Tess rebuilt the idea list: 1,432, up from 1,317 at lunch. Sixty-five came from Felix's file and
fifty from Amara's card. Nora's note for today on how the argument moved mentions Amara's two risks and Felix's point
about bond wording, and Monday's plan changed with them. It now starts with the numbers on Friday's close for the plan
Caplet sent, hardest parts first: what happens when the bonds' interest has to be reinvested at lower rates, and the
gap between what an iBond fund pays back and a round number.
