# FICTION. Episode 40: Words nobody owns

**Thursday 5 Nov 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Kai · Long Room: quiet (a freeze) · IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00**

> **Summary.** Freeze day. The draft is Caplet's now, and Keel has promised to answer only what it's asked. Then Nora
> finds a rule of Ray's from the night before, sitting on another thread's branch: "do not invent terms, like floor".
> The final uses "floor" three times. Kai, who likes the word, has to decide whether it's a word or a fact.
> **Arcs:** 7 (outside: the people we work for tell us how we sound). **Values:** 2, 4.
> **Ideas:** none new.

---

Kai got to the Long Room at ten past seven and nobody was talking, which on a freeze day was the point.

Nora had two windows open. One was the hash of the final, the same sixteen characters as last night. The other was a
table row he hadn't seen.

"Ray, yesterday," she said. "To the pitch people. 'Do not invent terms, like floor.'"

"Floor's not invented," Kai said, before he'd thought about it. "It's what desks call it. Capital-protected notes have a
floor. Portfolio insurance has a floor. It's in every textbook we read."

"It's not in the case," said Felix, from the end of the table. He had the case open, because Felix always had the case
open. "The case says operating reserve. Facility contribution. Credible range. It never says floor."

"So it's a word, not a fact."

"It's Ray's word about our words," said Nora. "And it's a rule he gave us. That makes it the one thing we're allowed to
say today."

Kai turned it over. They'd spent a week arguing that a freeze meant silence, that Caplet's rewrite was Caplet's, that
the only thing worth interrupting a student for was a broken fact. Tess had put it on the Rooftop board last night:
*how do we tell a broken fact from a choice that's theirs?*

"It's neither," he said slowly. "It's a broken instruction."

"Is that a third thing?"

"It's the first thing, wearing a different jumper." He pulled the final up beside the case. "If Ray says no 'floor' and
we hand over a draft with three of them, that's us not listening. Same as a wrong number."

Jun, who'd been quiet, said, "Cheque's on the list too. And 'promises'. The pitch has both in its first six words."

That hurt a bit. The pitch had been through six judges.

"We don't fix it," Nora said. "We list it. Where each one sits, and the case's words beside it. The people finishing
the draft swap them, or Caplet does."

"And 'ladder'?" Kai asked. "That's a market word too."

"Ray didn't name it."

"Then I'm not naming it either."

Felix nodded at that, which from Felix was a ruling.

---

The note went up at seven-twenty. Seven lines, no codes. Kai read it twice the way Amara had taught them, as someone
who'd never opened the repo, and decided it would do.

Then the room went quiet again, which was still the point.
