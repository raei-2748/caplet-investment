Monday is the phone shop's slow morning, so the owner lets me open up before school, and I watched a man try to return
a screen protector he'd already put on. He said it wasn't protecting anything. The owner told him that was the point
of a good one. I thought about that for the rest of first period and couldn't work out if she was right.

At lunch Jun and I took the back table in the library, laptop between us, his rule: I type, he reads. Half a point on
the stock return, old edition against new, same random seeds so nothing else could move.

It moved nothing. Nine in a hundred stayed nine. Three stayed three. One figure went from 29 to 28.

**Jun:** So you were right yesterday.

**Kai:** About the direction. You were right that we didn't know it.

That would have been the end of it, except I'd done one more thing on Sunday night that I hadn't told anyone, because
I wasn't sure it wasn't a trick. The buffer comes out of the same 2028 money that buys the floor bond. So with a $10,000
buffer, the floor Laura can quote drops by $10,000 too. I'd wondered what you'd get if you skipped the buffer and just
quoted $10,000 lower.

I ran it while Jun watched.

Point one one percent, with the buffer. Point one one percent, without it, quoting lower. Same number, to the second
decimal.

Jun didn't say anything for a while. Then he took the laptop, which is the most Jun way of disagreeing, and did it
again by hand on a page of his maths book.

**Jun:** It's the same. Algebraically. The buffer is the quote with a sticker on it.

**Kai:** Then why does it feel different?

**Jun:** Because in the bad years, with the buffer, Laura still has eight thousand left. Without it, she has nothing.
That bit's real. It just isn't safety for the payments. The payments are covered either way, once the floor is last.

We had Sunday's decision in the journal in Tess's writing, 5-1, my name on the losing side with my reason underneath.
The truce from 8 October says you can reopen anything, with evidence, at a meeting. Not in a group chat. Not angry.

I asked Nora for ten minutes at three.

In the Long Room I put the two numbers on the board and didn't say anything else. That was Felix's advice; he says a
number with a speech attached sounds like a lawyer.

**Amara:** So the buffer doesn't protect Laura's promise.

**Kai:** The lower quote does. The buffer is one way of making the quote lower.

**Felix:** Then the IPS can't say the buffer is a safeguard. That sentence would be false.

**Nora:** What's left of Sunday?

**Jun:** The floor goes last in the order. That one's free and it's true. And there's a real question about the
eight thousand. Is it the residency's, or Laura's?

**Tess:** Which job is that sentence doing? Because it's a different job from safety.

Nora wrote three lines on the board. *Floor last. Quote a margin. Who owns the slack: a question for the half Laura
keeps.* We voted the first two six-nil. Jun voted yes on both and then asked for the third to be written down as his,
so it wouldn't get lost, which Tess did, with his name.

I want to say I felt like I'd won. Mostly I felt like I'd found a screen protector that wasn't protecting anything,
and the honest thing was to say so out loud, about an idea my friend had defended for three days.

On the way out Jun handed me a can of Coke. Second one this week.

**Jun:** You still lost on the size.

**Kai:** There isn't a size anymore.

**Jun:** That's what I mean.
