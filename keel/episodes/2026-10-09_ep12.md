# FICTION. Episode 12: Too fast

**Friday 9 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Amara · Long Room: 3 Arden days (roster; Kai's Sharpe idea) · 15 days to the TNA · Roster due Sat 10 Oct 08:00**

> ratio as the plan's target; the team rejects it because it says nothing about whether the payments are met. Jun

---

The roster was the easiest thing we've done and Nora treated it like the hardest.

Wharton wants it by Friday 5 p.m. in New York, which is 8 a.m. Saturday for us. Nora's rule is two days early, but
the roster opened late, so "early" was Friday lunch. Felix read the format line aloud from the IPS instruction:
"Format: First Name, Last Initial". Then he read our six names aloud in that format. Then Tess read them back to him.
Then Nora typed them. Then Felix read what she'd typed.

**Kai:** We're the only team in the world that's proofread our own names four times.

**Felix:** The title page has to match the roster. If one letter is different, it's our names that don't match.

**Nora:** Submitted. 12:58. Screenshot in the drive. Done.

Six letters and a full stop each. It looked very small for something we'd been working towards since August.

### The Long Room, Arden day one: Sharpe

Kai had read about the Sharpe ratio on Thursday night, between customers at the shop. He came in with it written on
his hand, which is apparently a team habit now.

**Kai:** Return above cash, divided by volatility. How much reward you get for each unit of risk. Every fund manager
quotes it. If we want to show "effective use of investment concepts", we put our plan's Sharpe ratio in the Final
Report. Higher than the other teams'. Done.

**Jun:** Of which part?

**Kai:** The whole thing.

**Jun:** The ladder's price wobbles, so on Sharpe it looks like a bad investment: a bond return with bond volatility.
But it does its job perfectly. A plan that sold the ladder and bought more stocks might have a higher Sharpe ratio
and miss a payment.

**Felix:** And the case's test is "a high degree of certainty". Not a ratio.

**Me:** Laura isn't going to ask how efficient the plan is. She's going to ask whether the money is there on the first
of January.

**Kai:** So nobody likes my ratio.

**Nora:** Say why in one line, and it goes in the ledger.

**Kai:** Our test is "paid in full", not "efficient". Rejected. Fine. But I want credit for bringing it.

**Tess:** You get a row.

He did. **I-10, the Sharpe ratio as the target: rejected.** Kai drew a small sad face next to it. Tess let it stay.

---

### Arden day two, and the table that came too fast

We went to bed at the house-rule time. Kai and Felix on the bunks by the door, Nora by the clocks, me by the
skylight, Tess and Jun in the middle. I woke at Arden 4 a.m. to write (I always write best at 4 a.m. on day two),
and Jun's bunk was empty.

He was at the long table with his laptop and his headphones, and the screen had a table I'd never seen: the Sharpe
ratio of five different mixes of Laura's money, side by side with the chance each mix misses a payment. He'd done it
to prove Kai's point wrong properly, with numbers, not just words.

**Me:** Jun. It's four.

**Jun:** I know. I'm nearly done.

**Me:** You're not supposed to work alone.

**Jun:** You're here.

I was. I sat down and wrote, and he worked, and neither of us said anything else. At breakfast he showed the table,
and it was beautiful, and it said what we'd already decided: the mixes with the best Sharpe ratio were the ones
most likely to miss a payment.

**Kai:** When did you do this?

**Jun:** Last night.

Kai looked at me. I looked at my tea. Nora said "Good. That's the table," and moved on, because she was thinking
about the roster screenshot. Tess wrote something in the margin of the journal and didn't say what.

I didn't tell anyone. He wasn't alone; I was there. That's what I told myself. I'm not sure that's what the rule
means.

---

### Surface

We came out at 16:40. The roster was in, the book was built, the TNA was fifteen days away, and for the first time
since September nobody had anything urgent.

Jun's number had come in: **$290,349** for Thursday's close, about **34bp** of room. Rates had fallen a bit, so the
promise was dearer. Kai said, "Good thing we bought it already." Jun said, "Yes," and nothing else, and I realised
that's what Jun says when he's very tired.
