# FICTION. Episode 34: Eight words

**Friday 30 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Jun · Long Room: light (one room day, the morning pick) · IPS due Sat 7 Nov 09:00 (8 days)**

> **Summary.** Round 2 won the IPS a framework line and paid for it with the reason the 2031 range exists. Jun, whose
> repair won last night, is flaw-hunter today on the question his own line opened: why a range, and what does each end
> cost? He reads the Archive's note on the train, finds his rule already sitting in a trade note from 7 October, and
> decides that isn't a reason to go easy on anything.
> **Arcs:** 1 (the sail: Jun breaks his own line first). **Values:** 2, 4. **Ideas:** none new.

---

The Archive's note was waiting when Jun got on the 7:12, and the bit that mattered was a single line from Tess:
*I opened the notes to check B2 against them and found B2 sitting in one.*

He scrolled up to the IBTR note. Seventh of October, three weeks before anyone had said "framework" out loud: a date,
a reading off the market, and a sentence about what the 2028 deposit would do if rates fell. The gift takes the hit,
the cheque doesn't.

So he hadn't invented the rule last night. He'd found it in the model, and the model had got it from Ray, who'd
typed it into a trade ticket in a free period. Jun sat with that through Strathfield. It was a nicer feeling than
winning. It was also, he realised, exactly the kind of feeling that makes you go soft on the thing you're meant to
hunt.

---

Nora had the round open on the Long Room screen by the time he got in. *Q10. Why a range, and what each end costs.*

"You're hunting," she said. "On a question your line made."

"I know." He put his bag down. "That's why I'm going first."

Felix looked up from his laptop. He'd already pasted three search results about share floats and starred none of
them, which was very Felix.

"Go on, then."

"Every field we're sending people to," Jun said, "quotes a range to strangers. A float sells to fund managers. An
appeal asks the whole school. The weather bureau talks to everyone in Sydney. None of them is one person naming a
gift when the rest of her money is already promised to ten cheques. So whatever comes back, I want where it breaks
before where it fits."

Kai grinned. "LS-04. You've been reading the lessons."

"I wrote half of them."

"You wrote one."

"I wrote one *well*."

Tess, who was eating toast over the bin, said the only useful thing anyone said before nine. "The why we lost was
eight words. 'Too low costs gifts; too high, her word.' If the scouts can't find a practitioner who'd say that, we
shouldn't say it either."

---

Jun's flaw sheet for the day had one line by recess:

> **The range's two ends don't cost the same person.** Too low costs the *co-sponsors* (less matched, a weaker
> signal that the residency can work). Too high costs *Laura* (her word). B3 puts them in one clause as if one person
> pays both. Is that true, and does a judge care?

He showed it to Amara, because she was judging tonight and wasn't allowed to read the scouting, but she was allowed to
read a question.

"A judge cares," she said, "if the sentence pretends it's simpler than it is. The case gives both costs in two
different sentences. Maybe that's the answer: two people, two ends."

"That's ten words, not eight."

"Then it's ten words." She went back to her chemistry.

---

Felix messaged the group at 12:40 from the library: *SEC page loaded. Not saying the number until I've read the whole
rule.* Then, four minutes later: *Kai, your rain thing is going to win, isn't it.*

Kai sent a cloud emoji and nothing else.

On the way home Jun stood at Strathfield again while the train held at the platform until its time. He'd mentioned
it in the Kitchen last night, the slack sitting before a fixed time instead of spread along the line. Nobody had sent
it anywhere. He typed it into TERRITORY as a candidate and left it there. It wasn't today's question. Today's question
was eight words, and whether two different people paid for them.
