# FICTION. Episode 3: Funds don't mature

**Wednesday 30 September 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Kai · Long Room: 4 room days (how to hold the promise) · 24 days to the TNA**

> **Summary.** Nora opens the stretch by calling the September plan settled: two Treasury index funds, IEF and
> TLT, mixed to match the payments' interest-rate sensitivity. Kai reopens it and wins: index funds never mature, so
> they can't hand Laura cash on a date. The team switches to one bond or term fund per payment. Kai then overreaches
> on the growth share, and Nora writes the Long Room's house rules on the door after a fourteen-hour argument.
> **Arcs:** 2 (two captains), 1 (the sail). **Values:** 3. **Ideas:** I-02 rejected, I-03 adapted.

---

I'll be honest about how this started, because Tess will be anyway.

We went into the Long Room at 16:10 and Nora said, before her bag was even down: "The bond side's decided. Jun's
September mix: IEF and TLT, about two-thirds and one-third, so the bonds move like the payments. Today is the
growth side."

And I said: "Who says?"

Not to be annoying. Okay, a bit to be annoying. But mostly because I'd been reading a pension fund's explainer on
the bus (Felix's bus habit is contagious), and something in it didn't fit.

---

### Room day one: the mix

Jun drew it on the board. He's good at this when he's not trying to be good at it.

**Jun:** Laura's ten payments together behave like one bond with a duration of about ten years: if rates rise one
percent, they get about ten percent cheaper. IEF's duration is about seven years, TLT's about fifteen. Two-thirds
IEF, one-third TLT, and the mix moves like the payments. If rates move, both sides move together. That's
immunisation.

**Amara:** In words Laura would use?

**Jun:** If rates change, the money and the bills change by the same amount.

**Me:** Today.

**Jun:** What?

**Me:** They change by the same amount *today*. What about next year? Those funds never mature. IEF holds seven-to-
ten-year bonds forever. When its bonds get too short, it sells them and buys new ones. In 2033 IEF will still be a
seven-to-ten-year fund. Laura's first payment will be due in a week. Her bills shrink every year. The fund doesn't.

The room went quiet in the way it goes quiet when Jun is checking something in his head.

**Jun:** You'd have to rebalance. Every year. Sell IEF, buy something shorter.

**Me:** Every year for ten years, at whatever prices that year has. So "high degree of certainty" means "high
degree of certainty that we'll trade correctly ten times".

**Felix:** And the payments are dated. Lines 88 to 89: ten payments "at the beginning of each year". The case is about
cash on dates.

**Nora:** We decided this in September.

**Me:** We decided it before we'd read the bond list. Felix read it yesterday. That's new evidence. Isn't that what
"decide once" is for? You can reopen with new evidence?

She looked at the door, where the values are. I could see her deciding whether I was right or just loud.

**Nora:** Is that fair? Kai's point: index funds match the payments' sensitivity today but not their dates, so
they need trading every year. Felix's evidence: individual Treasuries for the later payments exist; the early ones
need term funds. Have I got it?

**Me:** That's it.

**Nora:** Then you're right, and I closed it too early. Two options. One: IEF and TLT, rebalanced yearly. Two: one
bond or term fund per payment, held to the date. Vote.

Five to zero for option two. Jun voted last and said: "Cash-flow matching. It's simpler than what I built. I should
have seen it." Tess wrote that down too.

---

### Room days two and three: I overreach

So, emboldened, I said the growth money should be 80% stocks. Laura's $150k in 2028 has five years to 2033 and none
of it is needed for the payments. "Who says she can't take risk with money she doesn't need?"

Jun said he'd model it. Amara said, "What does she feel in a bad year?" I said, "Rich, eventually." That was a
mistake. Amara didn't talk to me for most of a day, which in the Long Room is a long time.

We argued for fourteen hours straight. I know because Tess logged it. Nobody slept. By the end Jun was writing code
with his eyes closed and Felix had read the same paragraph of the case nine times.

---

### Room day four: the door

Nora woke us up (she'd slept two hours, on the bunk by the clocks) and wrote on the inside of the door, under the
values, in marker:

> **House rules.** Sleep every sixteen hours. No stretch longer than fourteen room days without a day outside.
> Nobody works in here alone. Anyone can call "surface".

**Me:** Is this because of me?

**Nora:** It's because of all of us. You're just the loudest example.

Then she called surface. We came out at 16:14 Sydney time, four days later, and I felt like I'd been at a
basketball camp, except my legs were fine and my brain wasn't.

I said sorry to Amara on the bus. She said "rich, eventually" was a good line and also the meanest thing anyone had
said about Laura all week. Both true.

Jun's number for Tuesday's close, by the way: **$289,499**, about **37bp** of room. Cheaper again. I didn't say the
market likes us. Growth.
