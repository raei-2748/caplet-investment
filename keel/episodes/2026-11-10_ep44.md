**07:04.** Jun messaged at seven, a long one. Monday's top-up for the ten payments, the one he put in before school
yesterday, doesn't hold. Say the bonds paid out their interest and we couldn't earn anything more on it: the ten
payments would come up $22,192 short. He'd tried it against 585 past stretches of US interest rates and it fell short
in 35 of them, the worst starting in July 2007 and missing by $12,117. The only money left to cover a gap like that is
the half of the shares nobody's been promised, and the IPS Caplet sent says none of the ten payments will depend on a
share price.

His new version keeps each payment's own bond the way the trading notes describe it. Then part of the 2028 deposit
buys a bit more of six of them, and what those pay sits in a short-term Treasury fund until the next payment is due.
All ten get paid even if that money earns nothing while it waits, in all 585 stretches. Counted from January 2027 it
costs $335,771, which is $12,335 more than Monday's, and the gift comes down with it. Last night the middle of the
range was about $110,000 to $130,000, and this morning it's about $95,000 to $115,000.

Jun also wrote the range down as a rule. Each end is what its fund would pay if no more interest came in, cut a
little and rounded down to the nearest $5,000, and nothing gets said to anyone before half the shares are moved in
2031. I was doing Lina's sandwich with the phone on the bench.
