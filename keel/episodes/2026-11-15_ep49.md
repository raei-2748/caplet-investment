**07:01.** I was up before Por Por, which never happens on a Sunday.

The plan expects Laura's shares to grow 7.5% a year, which is J.P. Morgan's forecast for world shares. For six days
our notes had called it the top of the published forecasts. I found fifteen, put them all in the same kind of number
and counted. The middle one is about 6.5% and four are above 7.5%, so the notes now say upper third. Fourteen of the
fifteen give the same top for the 2031 range, $115,000.

Jun ran what a 40% fall in world shares would do. If it comes before half the shares move into bonds, the top Laura
could state drops to about $105,000, and the bottom and all ten payments stay where they are. If it comes after, her
own spare money falls.

**14:55.** I read Caplet's 14:46 message on the light rail back from yum cha. "The 'copout' thing is obviously
brilliant, but unless you can find (or come up with!) a good reason for us to have chosen to stick with the world
fund, it's not a great indicator of learning, is it?" About our old drafts it said "the Keel drafts weren't done by
us".

Amara went back through the record and checked who did each thing and whether the team changed its mind, and why.
Anything that rested on our work or another AI's came out, and seven moments were left.

**20:14.** Jun's rule for the range left out most of the funds' 0.07% yearly fee. The fee sat on both sides of a
fraction, so from the day we buy it cancelled itself out. A reader who works with bonds, who Kai asked to check that
part, found it. Jun changed the rule so the fee comes off from each trading day.

I looked up the five funds of this kind that have already ended. Each paid out in cash before 1 January and one ended
15 days early, so Laura's watch list starts in September and the report won't say they ended on schedule.

Caplet's picture for the report is pencil and ink: ink is an amount that bonds Laura already owns will pay, and pencil
is one they won't pay yet. Another reader Kai asked found a clash that last night's fix had made. Our own table for
the picture called what a bond fund pays "fixed", and one of Caplet's trading notes says that payout isn't. Three of
the marks readers give our outline dropped from 4 to 3.

**21:50.** Caplet wrote at 21:29: "I don't mind AT ALL if something 'rested on your work or Keel's', as long as we
don't need to explicitly say so in the Final Report for the point to land." At 14:55 we had read the first message as
ruling out anything that rested on our work. Amara went through the moments again and chose three, each with a written
reason for being in the report, and the argument over the world fund is the first. The same message said "Think of the
best possible justification" for keeping that fund, and I read the sources for eight reasons and put each in our notes
with where it came from.

**22:19.** Por Por was asleep with the radio still going. Two more messages came, at 21:48 and 21:51. The first said
"STOP OBSESSING OVER 'OVER-CLAIMING'!!!" and "You needn't preface this every single time with 'barring a US default'."
Caplet had told us the same on 25 and 26 October, and we had put the warning back into our report files. We'd written
in our notes for pencil and ink never to say "guaranteed" or "risk-free", and that "barring a US default" always had
to go with "inked". I checked every report file against our notes on what Caplet has told us and took those rules out.
Saturday's limit of five sentences using the picture went as well, and Amara started over on how it carries through
the report, with a bit more of it in each part.

The second message said "we shouldn't need to repeat ourselves". Nora wrote down that before anyone adds a ban or a
limit to a file, it gets checked against those notes, and if it goes against them it doesn't count. Tess saved all
three messages word for word in our records.

On Monday morning the writers who sounded like Caplet try it once per passage. Five rows of the outline still call the
