### The Long Room, room day one: the question

The Window brought a second question from Caplet: what do the published long-term forecasts say about returns from
world stocks? A question I like, because it has an answer you can look up.

The best-known one is J.P. Morgan's Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions. The 2026 edition, U.S. dollar matrix, data
as of 30 September 2025. I'd used it for the bad-year table in Ep 9. Kai had read it too, cover to cover, which he
announced as he sat down.

**Me:** "AC World Equity": 7.00% a year compound. 8.28% arithmetic. Volatility 16.78%.

**Amara:** Why are there two?

**Me:** Arithmetic is the average of the yearly returns. Compound is what you actually end up with over many years.
They differ because a fall hurts more than the same-sized rise helps: lose 20% and you need 25% to get back. The more
volatile something is, the bigger the gap. For projections you use compound. If you use 8.28 by mistake, five years
of growth comes out about six percent too high.

**Kai:** So if someone used the bigger number—

**Me:** —their projections would be too rosy. Not a lot each year. A lot by 2033.

**Felix:** Where does it say which one to use for growth?

**Me:** It doesn't say. It labels both. The maths says compound for "what you end up with". I'll write it that way
and say why.

We sent Caplet the numbers, with the source and the date, and the one sentence about compound versus arithmetic.
Nothing about what they should assume. That's theirs.
