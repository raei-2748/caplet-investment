Okay. The world fund.

I'd wanted it since August. Vanguard Total World Stock, VT: one fund, roughly ten thousand companies, dozens of
countries, a fee of six hundredths of a percent a year. If you want growth and you don't want to pretend you can
pick winners, that's the cleanest thing there is. Even my nan would sign off on it, and I mean that as praise.

So of course on Wednesday afternoon, in the Long Room, Amara said, "What about Taiwan?"

---

### Arden day one

**Amara:** Hear me out. She's building a residency in Taiwan. She's moving her life there. A Taiwan fund for the
growth money is a story a co-sponsor would understand in one second. "Laura's money grows with the place she's
building in."

**Me:** It's a good story.

**Amara:** Thank you.

**Me:** I hate that I'm about to argue against a good story.

I'd read this one in a textbook chapter on human capital, and it had stuck because it was about people, not
prices.

**Me:** Your job is an investment too. Economists call it human capital: what you'll earn. Laura's income comes from
her books and her projects. Her residency is in Taiwan. Her move is to Taiwan. If Taiwan has a bad decade, her
project struggles, her co-sponsors there struggle, and then her growth money falls too, all at the same time.
Everything in her life is already a bet on Taiwan. The portfolio shouldn't double it.

**Jun:** It's the bad-year test. The worst year for a Taiwan fund is likely to be a bad year for the project too.

**Felix:** And the case says teams are "not expected to estimate" the facility's cost, and "do not need to account
for" the legal side of a residency in Taiwan. We haven't studied Taiwan's market. We can't claim to know it well
enough to bet on it.

**Amara:** So the most emotional idea I've had is the riskiest one for her.

**Nora:** Is that fair, Amara? Say it back.

**Amara:** A Taiwan fund adds risk that moves with her life. A world fund doesn't. Fine. Rejected. But I want it in
the ledger with the reason, because it's the reason that's clever, not the idea.

Into the ledger: **one world fund over home bias: adopted. A Taiwan fund: rejected; it would add risk correlated
with Laura's own life.** Amara wrote the reason herself. She wrote it better than I'd said it.

### Arden day two: the truce

Nora asked me to stay after. Not alone; Tess stayed too, as the journal, which is the rule.

**Nora:** Last night. 00:20. You were right. And you did it ten minutes before the button.

**Me:** I did it before, not after.

**Nora:** I know. That's why I said yes. But if it had been 00:29 and the evidence was weaker?

**Me:** Then you'd have said no and I'd have been angry.

**Nora:** And you'd have pressed the button angry. That's the bit I don't want.

I didn't have an answer to that. My dad presses buttons angry. I didn't say that.

**Tess:** What would we lose if there was a cut-off?

**Me:** The chance to catch something at the last minute.

**Tess:** Then make an exception for that. Safety, not strategy.

So we wrote it. Nora wrote the first half; I wrote the second.

> **The truce.** A logged decision can be reopened with new evidence, at a meeting, with the evidence on the table.
> Not within an hour of an order, unless it's a safety problem: a wrong ticker, a broken rule, a price that fails the
> check. Then anyone can stop the button.

**Nora:** Argue hard.

**Me:** Decide once.

She held out her hand like we were at a debate. I shook it like we were at a debate. Tess wrote down that we did.
