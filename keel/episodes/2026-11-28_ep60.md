*Nora's notes. Harris Park. Times are when things went into the office log.*

**Friday 22:43.** We work for Caplet, a real school team in the Wharton investment competition. Their case is about
Laura, a writer who has promised ten payments of $50,000, one a year from 2033, to an artists' residency she is
founding in Taiwan, and who also wants to help pay for its building. Most of her money goes into US government bonds,
called Treasuries. In 2031 she tells the building's other funders a range for her part, about $95,000 to $115,000.

Friday was meant to be the writing day, starting at 06:00. When Tess sat down to write Friday's diary at 22:43 there
was nothing to write from. No new file, no new row in any of our logs, nothing on any branch since Thursday night.
She wrote one line in the timeline saying so and went to bed.

Friday was also the last day of term for Lina's swimming. I sat on the bleachers with Mum's thermos and a maths
worksheet I didn't finish.

**Saturday 21:15.** Caplet's newest draft was in a new tab of their Final Report document. We don't change their
document, so everything we suggested went into a file of ours, each edit quoting their exact words so they can find
it with Ctrl+F. The numbers are being checked somewhere else, so Jun didn't re-run anything.

Amara and Tess started with the order of Section 3, the long section where the recommendation lives. They didn't
touch a sentence until the paragraphs were in place. Section 3 now answers six questions in turn: what Laura has to
provide, why the ten Treasuries bought in January 2027 weren't enough on their own, how the payments are made safe,
what she can offer the other funders, what she keeps, and what could go wrong. That took ten paragraph moves, one new
heading, "The operating reserve", and one renamed, "What Laura keeps". Most of it is Caplet's own paragraphs in a new
place. Then 24 paragraphs got sentence edits, each with a line saying why.

Tess exported the tab as a PDF to count pages. It came to 14 before the reference list, and Wharton's limit is 12.
Section 2 runs three pages and one line, with one line, "But what if it earns nothing?", falling onto a fourth page.
Section 3 is eight pages and four lines. After the edits Section 2 fits, but Section 3 comes out about two lines
longer, so about six lines have to go. Tess listed seven cuts to choose from, each taking out a whole idea rather than
squeezing a sentence, and marked the set that saves about six.

**Saturday, three readers.** Amara gave the draft to a reader who had never seen the plan. It read Laura's case, then
the report, once, top to bottom. Then it wrote the recommendation from memory and listed every sentence it had to read
twice. A second reader got the first round of edits, and a third the second round.

All three said "mostly". The first had the plan right but stopped at the table of holdings, because the competition's
$300,000 practice account holds the whole set of Treasuries and the shares together, where Laura's real plan spends
two deposits. It also met three different sizes of the reserve and couldn't tell which one was the reserve. The third
reader, on the second round, said "The plan's mechanics were clear on one reading. The confidence statement and the
purpose of the range were not." All three stopped at one sentence that had the team, not Laura, asking the other
funders to rely on a figure. It now has Laura asking.

Jun found one place where two tables seemed to argue. A table showed what Laura could hold in January 2031 coming in
1.5% below $95,000, and a page later the report says she gives $95,000 even if her shares are worth nothing. Both
are true, because she pays the building from what her Treasuries pay out at the end of 2032, not from what she holds
in 2031. He wrote one sentence that ties the two together.

He also left two notes for whoever is checking the numbers. One: the report says Laura's first $300,000 falls $2,388
short of the ten Treasuries. But $2,388 is what the gap costs in January 2028, when the second deposit pays it.
Counted in January 2027 it's $2,287, so either the date or the figure should change.

Dad had tiles drying in the garage, so dinner was at the kitchen bench.

**Saturday 21:50.** The file went into the office. A list of things we didn't touch went with it, because each was
the team's own choice. Laura's own words to the other funders are kept exactly as written, even though one reader took
"we can discuss where in that range" to mean the top depends on them.

**Still open.** Which of the seven cuts the team takes, and whether anyone reads the last round of fixes before Sat 5
Dec. The numbers check isn't in our files yet.
