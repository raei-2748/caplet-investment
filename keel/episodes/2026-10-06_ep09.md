### The Long Room, room day one

Kai had been reading again. Retirement planning this time, the chapter on sequence risk.

**Kai:** It's not just how much the market falls. It's *when*. If you need money in year five and the crash is in
year four, you're forced to sell at the bottom. Same average return, completely different outcome. So the order of
the years matters as much as the average.

**Me:** Yes.

**Kai:** So the growth money should be... hang on.

He stopped. I've known him since Year 7, and I've seen him lose arguments to other people maybe ten times. This was
the first time I've seen him lose one to himself.

**Kai:** So if a bad year lands just before 2033, the gift falls right when Laura announces it.

**Amara:** And if the payments depended on the stocks, she'd have to sell at the bottom to pay them.

**Kai:** Which they don't, because the keel pays them.

**Nora:** So what? One line.

**Kai:** The growth money never pays a payment. Ever. That's what makes it allowed to be risky.

Tess wrote it on the board. Kai's idea, Kai's line. Into the ledger: **sequence-of-returns risk. Adopted. It's the
reason the sail never funds a payment.**

---

### Room day two: the table

They wanted a number for "bad year". I don't like giving one number, so I gave a table.

I used J.P. Morgan's 2026 long-term assumptions for world stocks: 7.00% a year compound, 16.78% volatility. A simple
model, each year independent, lognormal. MODEL, and I said so twice.

| How bad | One year | Five years together |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year in 20 | about **-17%** | about **-20%** over the five years |
| 1 year in 100 | about **-25%** | |

**Me:** And this model is too kind. Real markets have worse years than a smooth model expects. History has crashes
in it. I'll do a history check next, with real index years, before anyone quotes these.

**Kai:** So on $100k of stocks, a 1-in-20 year is seventeen grand.

**Me:** On this model. And it could be two years in a row.

**Amara:** And how does Laura feel in that year?

**Kai:** Like she shouldn't have listened to me.

**Nora:** Kai. A number.

**Kai:** Sixty. Maybe. Of the growth money, not of everything. And I want Jun's history check before it's final.

That was further than I'd expected him to move. I didn't say so. You don't celebrate when your best friend changes
his mind; you just make the next table better.

### Room days three to five: the margins

The rest of the stretch was mine. Five term-fund rungs, 2033 to 2037, each a fund whose payout isn't fixed. I sized
each one so that, if its bonds paid out exactly what the curve says, it would leave a little over $50,000, and if
the fund's income was lower than I'd assumed, it would still roughly cover the payment. A margin, not a guarantee.
I wrote both in the note column. Felix checked the fund names again on the issuer pages.

The others slept at the house-rule times. I didn't, quite. I told myself it was fine because Amara was awake too,
writing, so I wasn't alone. Amara was asleep on her notebook by room-time 3 a.m. I kept going until the room clock said
six. I'm writing this down because value five says to. I haven't told anyone.
