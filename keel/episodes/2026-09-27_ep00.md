# FICTION. Episode 0: The door

**Sunday 27 September 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Tess · Long Room: 3 Arden days (writing the values) · Trading opens tomorrow 23:30**

> **Summary.** The night before trading opens, the six spend three Arden days in the Long Room arguing about what
> Keel stands for, and come out with a mission, a vision and four values pinned to the door. Kai nearly walks out
> over "decide once"; Amara wins "the promise comes first"; Felix gets his line on the wall.
> **Arcs:** founding; 2 (two captains). **Values:** all four written. **Ideas:** none.

---

*Tess's journal. Started properly tonight, because tonight is the first thing worth remembering properly.*

It was Nora's idea. She said a team that hasn't agreed what it stands for will find out under pressure, which is the
worst time to find out. So at 7 p.m. on Sunday we climbed the back stairs of the library, Felix unlocked the Long
Room with the key the librarian pretends she doesn't know we have, and we went in.

The Earth clock on Felix's laptop said 19:02. When we came out it said 19:05. Inside, it was three days.

---

**Day one** was mostly Kai.

**Kai:** Values are posters. Every team has "integrity" and "teamwork" on a slide. Judges have seen it a thousand
times. Who says we need them?

**Nora:** Nobody says. I'm asking what we do when two of us want different things at 11 p.m. the night before a
deadline.

**Kai:** We argue.

**Nora:** And then?

That was the first real silence. Amara broke it.

**Amara:** Then we ask what Laura needs. That's the only thing above all of us.

She'd read the case four times by then. She read us the line she had underlined in August, about the ten payments
being funded "with a high degree of certainty", and then her own note under it, which she labelled as hers:
*She is promising money to people who will build their lives around it.*

**Amara:** If we can't keep our own promises to each other, deadlines, drafts, the things we say we'll do, why would
anyone believe we'd keep hers?

Nora wrote **The promise comes first** on the board. Nobody argued. It was the only one that night that nobody
argued about.

---

**Day two** was Felix and Kai, and it went badly before it went well.

Felix wanted something about sources. He said it the way he says everything, which is quietly and with the page
open.

**Felix:** Last year a team at the regional final quoted a rule from a 2015 guidebook. It cost them. I don't want a
value that says "be accurate". I want one that's a question. "Where does it say that?"

**Kai:** So every time I have an idea, you ask me for a citation.

**Felix:** Every time anyone states a fact. Ideas are free.

**Kai:** That's the most Felix sentence ever spoken.

**Jun:** It's also right. A number without a date is a rumour.

Felix got his line. Then Nora proposed the third one, and Kai stood up.

**Nora:** "Decide once." When the vote is logged, it's logged. We carry it.

**Kai:** No. That's how teams get timid. The best idea in the room loses a vote at 9 p.m. because everyone's tired,
and then we're not allowed to say so? I'm out if that's the rule.

He actually walked to the door. He didn't open it. He stood there, with his back to us, for long enough that I
wrote down the time on the room clock (day two, 16:40).

**Tess (me):** What would we lose if we cut it?

**Nora:** Speed. Trust. The person who lost has to know we'll still carry it.

**Kai:** And what do we lose if we keep it?

**Felix:** The argument. Only if the argument stops before the vote.

That was it. Kai turned round.

**Kai:** "Argue hard, decide once." Both halves. If the arguing half isn't on the door, I'm not signing.

Nora wrote both halves. She looked tired. She also looked like she'd wanted exactly that.

---

**Day three** was quieter. Jun asked for a value about mistakes. He didn't say why. (I know why. I was at the
science fair in Year 10.)

**Jun:** We're going to get things wrong. I'd rather we were the team that writes down what it got wrong and what
it would do differently. And nobody gets blamed for a mistake they report.

**Kai:** Even mine?

**Jun:** Especially yours. You'll make the interesting ones.

**Leave it better, and say what you learned** went on the board. Number four. (The fourth on the door now is a
different one. I'll get to that in October, when it happened.)

Then the mission. Amara wrote eleven drafts. I cut ten.

> **Mission:** Help Laura keep her promise and build what she dreams of, with a plan a stranger could check.

And the vision, which was Nora's, and which is also, if you read it carefully, the semifinal:

> **Vision:** By December, a judge who reads our three documents can repeat our idea in one sentence, find every
> number's source, and tell that six people built it together.

Felix asked where Wharton says how the semifinal is chosen. Nora said she'd show him on Tuesday. He wrote it on his
list.

---

We came out at 19:05. My legs felt like I'd been on a long flight. Kai's mum was picking him up and she said he
looked "different, like after a camp". He said it was the library lighting.

I pinned the sheet to the inside of the door. Four values, one mission, one vision, six signatures. Kai signed last
and biggest.
