The first was in our watch list. We'd said that if a bond fund moves its pay-out date past a payment, Laura swaps it
for a government bond, and any extra cost comes out of her spare money. Until 2033 that spare money is held in shares,
and Caplet wrote "none of your 10 operating payments will depend on a share price". Amara changed it so a swap is
paid from the bonds kept for the payments. The second was a sentence saying that with only what Caplet set aside, a gap
could have been filled only from the shares, which the reader said a judge would take as Caplet's plan breaking its
own promise. Amara took it out.

"capital-protected". Felix found it while reading about plans that promised people a minimum and then couldn't pay it:
the 1987 crash, Orange County's fund in 1994, Britain's pension funds in 2022, Silicon Valley Bank.

of that deposit now goes to the payments, and if interest rates fall the ten bonds cost more, so that part grows. Jun
worked out how far rates would have to fall before "most" stops being true: more than about 1.3 percentage points
before January, which came up in about 1 in 3,600 of the made-up futures in his program. If it ever did, everything
left would go to that fund and none to shares.
