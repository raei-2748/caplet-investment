On Sunday the committee argued for forty minutes about a biscuit tin.

There are two. The everyday one pays for flour, gas and the lentils that run out every second week. The other, the
blue one on the top shelf, is for weddings and funerals, the days when two hundred people turn up and you can't say
no. Mrs Kaur wanted to borrow from the blue tin for a new stove. Uncle Harjit said that the moment you borrow from
the blue tin once, it's just a tin.

Nobody wrote down a rule. They voted, the stove waited, and everybody went home slightly cross.

I told the group this on the train this morning because Nora's question for today is basically that argument.

**Nora:** Wharton wants a framework. Rules for when things change. We've got rules, but they're everywhere. Para 4,
para 9, a clause in para 10. A judge would have to collect them.

**Kai:** Or they won't, and they'll write "no clear framework" in the margin.

**Amara:** So who writes the rule down before the stove breaks?

**Felix:** Countries. Chile has two funds, one for bad years and one for pensions, with rules about what goes in and
what comes out. Norway spends a fixed share of its fund's expected return, not what it feels like that year. I've
read that, but not at the source, so it's UNVERIFIED until today's scouting.

**Amara:** Then that's mine.

Jun has the other half: rules people make for themselves ahead of time, and when they're allowed to break them. His
coach makes the runners write their lap splits before a race, plus one line on when to give up on the plan. Jun says
the one line is the hard part. Nobody ever writes it.

Nora took the far one, which is the first time she's scouted since we started the rounds. Her mum's pharmacy has a
chart behind the counter from the hospital: you don't guess which patient is getting worse, you score a few things,
and each score has an answer already agreed. Nora said it's the least romantic thing she's ever wanted to put in an
investment policy, and she wants it anyway.

**Kai:** Here's my problem. A chart says what to do. It doesn't say what the rule costs. If the rule says "sell
nothing early", what does Laura give up when selling early would've been right?

**Nora:** That's your job today. Not mine.

**Kai:** I know. I'm just warming up.

At lunch I wrote the question at the top of my notebook the way Mrs Kaur should have written it on the blue tin: *who
may take from this, for what, and who decides when the rule bends?* If Chile has an answer, I want it in their words,
not mine.
