**06:54.** I'd asked someone who funds artist residencies for a living to read our plan for the part where Laura asks
other people to help pay for the building. Their notes were in when I came down. They said they'd meet her, and their
reason was the running costs: Laura already has ten years of them in Treasuries before the place opens. Then they had
two questions. In 2031 half the shares get moved into Treasuries, and after that the money for the building comes out
at the top of its range in every test we ran, so which number should they count? And how much does she want from them?

So the plan says they count the bottom of the range, promised in writing, which only the US government missing a
payment could lower. What Laura asks them for is two things: money for the building, which they only pay if the whole
building is paid for by a set date, and money every year for the programmes from 2033.

Felix read Taiwan's rules for government grants and company foundations in Chinese, on the government and foundation
sites. They give a year at a time, in Taiwan dollars, so the plan stopped asking Taiwanese funders to match Laura with
one big promise up front and asks for programme money a year at a time.

I also took "almost certain" out of how Laura says how sure she is. In the studies we read, people hear it as about
86 in 100, and they agree with each other much more on a number than on a word.

**07:14.** Kai had lined up the other test for this morning: someone who'd never seen any of it, reading the opening

clarity. We'd given it 4, and that took our average from 3.25 to 3.17.

They also couldn't make $268,652 in one table agree with $302,287 in the next, or $444,080 with $444,500. One set is

and they're waiting on Caplet's export. She put the morning's work into the main copy at 7:14 and I went for the bus.

**19:50.** Tonight's reader doesn't work in finance. They read the part on the ten payments, the part on what comes
back to Laura and the first chart, and got all four questions about them right. They gave it 2 out of 5 for clarity,
because "gift", "floor", "backstop", "overflow", "excess", "cap", "moved half" and "forward rates" all turned up
without anyone saying what they were.

So I made a list with one plain name for each thing and the words we stop using for it: her facility contribution
for Laura's money for the building, the top-up for the extra bonds bought with the start of the 2028 deposit, and her
flexibility for the half of the shares nobody's been promised. I did it at the kitchen table after dinner.

A second funder, from a Taiwanese company's foundation, read the same part on their own. They'd also count the
bottom, and they said they'd promise money in principle once they knew the building's total and a closing date. The
plan now says the residency sets its total before the 2031 meetings, and everyone's promises have to be in by the
time Laura pays hers in December 2032.

The bond reader Kai brought in wouldn't pass the table of things to watch. The two boxes that decide whether the plan
ever changes both just said someone would decide later. Felix found how other people define them. The US government
has missed a payment if it hasn't paid on one of its bonds within five business days, which is the test S&P uses, and
Laura's deposit is missed if nothing has arrived by 31 March 2028. Kai cut the table from 33 rows to seven.

Tess had been asked to set up the portfolio section like a medical pilot study. We dropped that name, but while she
was looking for what a pilot gets checked against, she went back to Caplet's spreadsheet from before the first trade.
It has a target number of units for every holding. All ten Treasury holdings in the account match their targets
exactly, and the world share fund is 167 shares against 164, bought last with the cash left over. The trading notes
already say "scaled to the model portfolio", so that section now treats the account as Laura's plan at two-thirds size
and checks it against those targets.

Jun checked our Taiwan dollar file against the Federal Reserve's, month by month for 516 months, and it matches. Then
he was asked to break one of our lines: the money for the building always lands inside the range with room to spare,
and the range never shrinks to one number. The first part held in every run. The second failed in about 1 in 790 of
them, the ones where rates drop about two points before 2028, where the range comes down to a single number.
