---

Kai lost the argument on Saturday night and slept fine, which surprised him.

He'd backed A09 since Thursday. Bonds for the cheques, stocks for the rest, lock the bottom in 2031. It was what he'd
do with his own money, and he'd said so in every room that would listen. Then the Lab ran it on their own paths and
the answer came back plain: the rival's cheques were just as safe, its gift was bigger on average, and about one year
in fifteen it would have to stand in front of co-sponsors and promise under a hundred thousand. Usually right after a
crash, when everyone else was giving less too.

He'd voted for LR3 anyway. Five-nil. Then the Archive had gone back through it at quarter past eight and found that
the $19,000 in the paragraph came from a different engine.

"On our design it's about fifteen," Tess had written. "Two of the three numbers are exactly right. The third isn't."

So they'd been five-nil on a number that was wrong by four thousand dollars, and nobody in the room had asked which
plan it came from. That was the bit that kept him up, slightly.

---

The Sunday call started late because Jun's starter needed feeding.

"It's on a schedule," Jun said, holding a jar up to the camera. "Six a.m. Miss it and the week's gone."

"That's the most Jun sentence ever said," Amara said.

Nora had the round open. "Voice round. Nobody's writing about numbers today, but every number stays in, with where
it came from. Felix is hunting exactly that."

"I'm hunting exactly that," said Felix.

Kai had the auction field. He'd never read a catalogue essay in his life. Twenty minutes in he had three tabs open
and a new respect for whoever wrote them: four hundred words to make a stranger believe a painting was worth a number,
with a separate page, the condition report, that just listed everything wrong with it. Craquelure, upper left. Minor
retouching along the lower edge. Frame not original.

"They don't apologise," he said, mostly to himself.

"Who?" said Tess.

"The condition report. It's just there. Here's the crack. Here's the price. Bid or don't." He scrolled. "Our price
paragraph reads like we're sorry the floor costs fifteen grand."

"Are we?" Tess asked.

He thought about it. "I was. On Thursday."

---

Amara's guides were worse, or better, depending on how you looked at it.

"They tell you at the bottom," she said. "Before you've paid for the helicopter. 'If we're not at the summit by two,
we turn around.' And people sign it. Because a guide who doesn't have a turnaround time is a guide you don't want."

"That's our cap," Nora said.

"That's our cap," Amara agreed. "And at the moment it reads like small print."

Jun had his annuity explainers up, a regulator's plain-English page on what you give up when you swap a lump sum for
an income. "They're boring," he said. "On purpose. Nobody's being clever. That's why you believe them."

Kai wrote it down: *not clever, on purpose.* It was going to be a problem for a team that had spent a week polishing
lines.

---

By lunch he had his paragraph, the price one, rewritten the way an auction house would say it. The cost first, in
one sentence, with "in our model" right there in front of it. Then what it bought, in one more. No apology. No
"admittedly".

He sent it to Felix before Nora, because Felix was the one who'd catch him.

Felix sent back one line: *Which plan is the fifteen from?*

Kai checked. Ours, the Archive's re-run, the same engine as the floor. He wrote it into the paragraph's note and sent
it again.

*Fine,* Felix wrote. *Now read it out loud.*

He did, in his bedroom, with the door shut. It sounded like someone who'd lost an argument and decided the other
side was right. Which, he supposed, it was.

## What Caplet can take from today
1. **A price said plainly reads as confidence.** Practitioners who sell a promise to doubters state the cost first and
   don't apologise for it. Our price paragraph can do the same.
2. **Every number should say which plan it came from.** A five-nil vote stood on a figure from the wrong model for an
   evening. "In our model" is half the source; "on this plan" is the other half.
