### Lunch, the Long Room, room day one

**Me:** In one line: if rates keep falling before January, Laura's first $300,000 won't buy all ten payments.

**Kai:** But we've already bought ours.

**Me:** In WInS. Our plan is for Laura in January 2027. The WInS book shows what we'd buy. Laura buys on 1 January,
at January's prices. And that's what I want to show you. I owe you a history check. I said in Ep 9 I'd test the
bad-year table against real history. I've done something better first: I tested the *ladder* against this year.

I'd run the same pricing on every Treasury curve published in 2026, every trading day since 2 January.

**Me:** On 173 of the 198 trading days this year, the ten payments cost **more than $300,000**. The last day they
cost more was 9 September. We've only had room for the last five weeks. We've been planning as if room is normal.
It's the exception.

Nobody said anything. Then Amara said what everyone was thinking.

**Amara:** So we were lucky.

**Me:** We were lucky with the timing. The plan isn't luck. But the plan has to say what happens if January looks
like most of 2026, not like the last five weeks.

**Nora:** Options. Two.

**Kai:** One: the gap comes out of the 2028 money first, before any stocks are bought. Two: we buy fewer payments in
January and finish the ladder in 2028.

**Me:** Same thing, in the end. Either way the 2028 money finishes the promise before the sail gets any. The only
question is which rung waits. I'd buy the latest payments first in January and leave the gap in the 2033 rung,
because the 2028 money arrives five years before 2033 and can fill it with a bond that matures then.

**Felix:** Then the IPS has to say that rule, because it's a rule for "as the client's needs change". The Final Report
can't invent it after the freeze.

**Nora:** Logged as a proposal. It goes in IPS v1. Jun, write it in one sentence.

**Me:** "If the ten payments cost more than $300,000 in January, the gap is the first use of the 2028 deposit."

**Tess:** That one stays.

Into the ledger: **I-14, history windows as well as models: adopted. Every MODEL number gets a check against real
history before it's quoted.** And I wrote, in the "what we'd do differently" column, for me: *I should have run the
2026 history before we celebrated 42bp.*

### Room day three, late

That should have been the episode. It wasn't.

We'd stopped for the night. House-rule bedtime. I was about to get up and go back to the table (I'd only meant to
check one more curve) when Tess sat down on the end of my bunk.

**Tess:** I need to say two things. One's about you and one's about Amara, and I've known both for days. I'm saying
them now because you just wrote "what I'd do differently" in front of everyone, and I haven't.

**Me:** Okay.

**Tess:** Yours first. I've seen your file times. 5:52, 4:10, 3:00, 1:40. You work in here alone after we sleep. I saw
it on Tuesday and said nothing. I'm sorry.

I didn't say anything. My face did the science-fair thing. Hot.

**Tess:** I'm not telling Nora tonight. I'm telling you first. Because it's yours to say.

**Me:** It's just checking.

**Tess:** I know. It's always just checking.

Then she went across the room and woke Amara, gently, and they talked by the skylight for a long time. I heard
"Nov-2040" and "guarantees" and then Amara saying, "Why didn't you say on Thursday?" and Tess saying, "Because you'd
had the worst week and I'd rather be silent than clumsy, and that was wrong." And then Amara laughing, a bit
wet, and saying, "You're never clumsy. That's the problem."

I didn't go back to the table. I lay there and listened to the room clock and thought about what I'd say to Nora.
