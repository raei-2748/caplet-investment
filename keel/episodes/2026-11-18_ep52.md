*Kai's journal. Wednesday, at home in Canley Vale. The times are when things went into the office log.*

**06:30.** Mum had an open home at nine, so she was ironing at six with the radio on. Bà wanted the Vietnamese news
instead and got it. I read the morning's work at the bench.

The part of our notes on assumptions and limits had been marked 2 out of 5 for clarity three times in a row. Jun rebuilt
it around one question for each of Laura's promises: what would make it fail, what would cause that, and one figure. At
today's rates the ten yearly payments still get made if the 2028 deposit comes in about three-quarters smaller. In 2031
Laura gives the building's co-sponsors a range, about $95,000 to $115,000. The top figure rests on bond funds. It only falls
through if their extra costs, beyond the fee they list, add up to more than about 2% of everything they pay out.

The morning's reader was new to the subject, and marked it 3. They got stuck twice. Our word "margin" meant
two different things in two places, and next to "the two funds" they wrote "which two?" Before the morning's work was
saved at 07:37, Jun's fix named them: the fund bought in January 2028, and the one the shares are moved into before
2031. Tess put the outline's average mark, out of 5, at 3.08, up from 3.00 last night, with no part left at 2.

**07:37.** Amara's two writers had a go at what Laura might say to co-sponsors in 2031. The readers answered questions
on what they said and got all 12 right, and ranked them lowest of the practice pieces. They picked out words no student would
use, like "close date", "custodian" and "the building's first draw". Amara added those to the list of words to leave
out on the 27th.

**Afternoon.** Shift at the phone shop after school. A man wanted the photos from his old phone moved to his new one
and couldn't remember his password, so that was most of the shift. I read the evening log on the walk home.

**Evening.** Nora had more researchers and readers going than the twenty we can run at once, so the readers who check the fixes
started about an hour later than she'd planned.

Felix's research note found Fidelity International's forecast from this month, 7.6% a year for world shares. Our
numbers use J.P. Morgan's 7.5%. Since Tuesday the notes had called that above the middle of published forecasts, with
the middle at about 6.5%, which was the middle of the fifteen we'd read before Goldman Sachs's 7.7% turned up. With
Goldman and Fidelity there are seventeen, and the middle is about 6.9%, still under J.P. Morgan. Felix took the number for
the middle out of the notes and out of the text under one of the tables.

**19:44.** Tonight's reader played someone who builds bond portfolios for a living. They started on our numbers at
19:44, and the first thing they wrote up was "There is one fund." This morning's two funds are the same one, the fund
that pays out in late 2032. Laura buys it in January 2028, and buys more of it before 2031 with the money from the
shares she sells.

They gave the part on assumptions and limits 2.5 out of 5 for how exact it is, where it had been 5. If that fund closed
early, just after the shares went into it, and the cash then sat earning nothing, the top would come up $4,589 short,
and our notes hadn't allowed for that. Our range also said "on today's figures" with no date. The reader worked the
range out again on US government bond rates from 13 November, a week after the competition froze our trading, and got
$105,000 to $125,000, which is $10,000 higher at each end. Jun put that on our main page of figures as a note and left
the range on the bond rates of 6 November.

In Amara's test tonight, the best of the practice pieces on what could go wrong put one risk in each sentence and
finished on a short one: "No fall in share prices can." All three readers, who'd been given Caplet's own writing to
compare, said it could be Caplet's. The pieces said two
funds, and every reader repeated two funds when asked what could make the top fail. For Laura's words to co-sponsors,
the spoken versions ranked above the written ones.

On Tuesday night Jun redid the sums for the ten payments. Tonight he found he'd left Monday's figures typed into the
script for our test of Taiwan building costs and the Taiwan dollar rising together, so one set of results on the main
page had been two days out of date. He changed the figures and ran it again.

**20:48.** The evening's work was saved. Today the office sent out 86 researchers and readers between the two
sessions and brought in 28 research notes. Tess has the outline's average at 3.04, from 3.08 this morning, because of
the mistakes the bond reader found in the part on assumptions, among them the second fund and the fund ending early, which
we hadn't allowed for. The range now says which day's bond rates it was worked out on, 6 November, the day trading froze, and the
line about the funds' costs names the one fund. The part on assumptions stays at 2.5 until the next bond reader checks
it. A second reader new to the subject asked tonight why Laura gives a range at all if the bonds are certain. That goes
to Thursday morning's session.
