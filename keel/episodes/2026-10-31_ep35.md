# FICTION. Episode 35: The rival's best plan

**Saturday 31 October 2026, Sydney · Point of view: Tess · Long Room: light (one room day, the morning pick) · Final IPS for Caplet's review: end of Wed 4 Nov (4 days)**

> **Summary.** Caplet's Friday message landed five minutes before the Long Room voted, and nobody read it first. Now
> it's Book 10: the deadline moves to Wednesday, the panels are called out for agreeing about single lines, and the
> team is told to think like a human judge. Tess, flaw-hunter for round 4, has to find the sentence in V39 that loses
> to the rival plan two judges preferred, or admit there isn't one.
> **Arcs:** 6 (say it the day you see it: Tess names the team's miss before anyone else). **Values:** 3, 4. **Ideas:**
> none new.

---

Tess had the Saturday shift at Coles from eleven, which gave her three hours and one job.

She'd read Book 10 twice on the bus home last night, and the bit that stuck wasn't the overdrive or the new deadline.
It was *your judges almost seem symbolic rather than purposeful*. She'd sat on four of those panels. She'd written the
notes that said "the deletion line lands" and "the framework is visible", and every one of them had agreed with the
one before.

At 7:05 she messaged the group before Nora could open the round.

*We voted at 18:20 on a draft for a review that got cancelled at 18:15. I was in the room. I'm saying it so nobody
else has to.*

Kai replied with a thumbs up. Then: *we all were.*

---

Nora's gather had one question on it, and it wasn't about a sentence.

"A09," Nora said, when they were all on the call. "Same ladder for the cheques. About eighty per cent world stocks on
top. They only move the bottom into bonds in 2031, when Laura names it. Two of six judges put it first."

"Two of six put it first because two of six were built to like stocks," Jun said.

"Then they're the two that disagree," Amara said. "Which is what Caplet asked for."

Tess had V39 open beside A09's page. Her job, written into the round by the Rooftop, was specific: quote the line
in V39 that misses A09, or the charge falls.

She read it the way a judge would: the whole page, no notes, kettle on.

V39 beat a three-quarters-stocks plan, and it beat a 60/40. It had a line conceding that the 60/40 did better on
average, then a "But". It never argued with A09 at all. A09 wasn't wrong in any sentence V39 could point to. It just
wasn't there.

"There isn't a line that loses to it," she said. "There's a paragraph missing."

"That's worse," said Felix.

"That's better," said Kai. "A missing paragraph is one we can write."

---

Kai was judging tonight, and he'd been the loudest A09 fan since Thursday, which everyone knew and nobody minded.

"I'm going to say one thing and then shut up until the panel," he said. "If our answer is 'ours is safer', I'm
marking it down. Safer than what, by how much, and what does Laura pay for it? Our floor is bought in 2028. Theirs is
whatever eighty per cent stocks are worth in 2031. If the stocks fall forty per cent in 2030, their bottom falls with
them before she's named anything. Fine. Say that, with the number. If they don't fall, theirs is bigger. Say that too."

"That's the lens," Nora said. "What does ours give up?"

Jun had his farmer tab open already. "Farmers who sell some of the crop before harvest never sell all of it. If they
did, they'd be a bank. The bit they keep unsold is the bit they're allowed to be wrong about."

"So which half of Laura's money is she allowed to be wrong about?" Amara asked.

Nobody answered, which Tess decided was the most useful silence of the week.

---

At 10:52, in the Coles break room, she wrote her charge for the Courtroom in one line and didn't tidy it:

> **V39 never meets the ladder-plus-stocks rival.** Not a weak sentence: a missing argument. A growth-minded judge
> finishes the page thinking "why not just more stocks?" and nothing we wrote answers them.

Then she wrote the second line, the one Book 10 would want, and found it harder:

> **What Caplet would say next:** "You've told us what your plan does. You haven't told us why a sensible person
> wouldn't do the obvious thing instead."

She pressed send, put her name badge on, and went to stack the cereal aisle.
