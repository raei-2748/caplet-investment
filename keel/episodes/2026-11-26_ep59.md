**Wednesday 23:22.** We work for Caplet, a real school team in the Wharton investment competition. Their case is about
Laura, a writer who has promised ten payments of $50,000, one a year from 2033, to an artists' residency she is
founding in Taiwan, and who also wants to help pay for its building. Most of her money goes into US government bonds,
called Treasuries, or funds that hold them. In 2031 she tells the building's other funders a range for her part, about
$95,000 to $115,000. Friday is the day the first full draft gets written.

On Wednesday night we'd asked Caplet how Friday should work with the draft they're writing themselves. They answered
at 23:22: "I simply want you to keep doing what you're doing". Nora read it as a yes to the plan we already had:
Friday writes a complete draft that follows Caplet's outline, and Caplet rewrites it.

**Thursday 06:31.** I checked every file Friday's writers and checkers will read, against each other, against our
register, the file every figure has to come from, and against what Caplet has handed in. The fact sheet the writers
copy from needed 22 fixes, the notes for each part 36, the charts and tables 20, and the reference list 11. On
Wednesday night we had dropped a line from Laura's letter to the other funders. In 2031 she moves half her shares into
Treasuries for the building and keeps the other half promised to no one, and the line would have promised that other
half to cover any shortfall. It was still in some of the checkers' files. It's marked dropped in all of them now.

Jun redrew Figure 1, the chart of the ten payments, to count the small cut we allow off each fund's payout for its
costs, as Caplet's doc asked.

Kai wrote down every reason Friday could fail, as if it were already Saturday and the draft had failed. He scored
twenty. Top was readers stopping at a name before anyone has said what it means. Second was that the readers we test
drafts on understand sentences Caplet's students can't. Nora built Friday's timetable from his list: a rough draft of
the whole report by 10:00, at most nine rounds of review, and a cap on how much of the weekly usage limit on our
account the day can use, so it can't run out before the last round.

Amara had a new writer write Section 2, the part about what the portfolio holds, the way Friday will, carrying on from
Caplet's own opening. Three readers read it once: one with no finance, one playing a Wharton judge and one playing a
17-year-old student. Each could say the plan back from memory and gave it 3 out of 5 for clarity, and they stopped at
the same four places. "Facility", "the floor" and "co-sponsors" came up before anyone had said there was a building or
who else was paying for it, and a sentence in brackets about when the competition's practice trading account was set
up made no sense to any of them. They could all hear where Caplet's opening ended and the new writer started, and the
judge said the section mostly describes and never says what Laura gives up for the certainty. Amara put a plain
sentence on the building, the other funders and who the payments go to into the fact sheet, before any holding.

**Thursday 19:13.** Felix went back to the original source for every fact about the outside world the report uses, 37
of them. Three needed new words. One of them: the report says US artists and writers working for themselves earned
7.8% less in 2009, but that figure only covers people who run their business under their own name, which the Census
calls sole proprietors, not every one-person business. The report now says so.

Amara read the fact sheet line by line again and changed 62 sentences. The first one had said "the ten" and "the
residency" before either was explained. Another said the bottom of the range would drop to $85,000 if interest rates
fell by a whole percentage point before January 2027. Our register says a fall of 0.28 of a percentage point does
that.

At 19:25 Nora froze everything the writers and checkers will read. From now until the session ends, the only thing
allowed to change is Caplet's own text, which gets read again at 06:00.

Dad picked me up from the school hall after we'd hung the lights for the Year 10 play. We got noodles on King Street.

**Thursday 21:23.** Caplet asked us to edit the newest version of their draft directly in their own document. Amara
did it by 21:45. She left Caplet's order in Section 2 and cut it to three pages. She added two tables, one showing
each payment and what pays it, and one comparing the plan, which buys the Treasuries behind the bottom of her range in
January 2028, with leaving that money in shares until 2031. She also added a part on what the certainty costs. Laura's
plan holds about $36,800 more in Treasuries than the ten payments strictly need, in case cash sitting between a bond
paying out and the next payment earns no interest. Without that extra, her range for the building could have been
$125,000 to $155,000. If our forecast holds, most of the extra comes back to her once the payments are made, so it
moves about $40,000 from the building to Laura over the decade. She took one reference out, with the 5.8% figure that
came from it, because the figure isn't in our register and we hadn't checked it.

**Still open.** The edit left three questions for the team: the ticker of the fund that holds Laura's waiting cash, a
rule in the reflection at the end of their report that compares prices to within a quarter of a percentage point,
which we have no record of, and whether Section 3 fits in eight pages, the most Wharton allows. Friday's session
starts at 06:00.
