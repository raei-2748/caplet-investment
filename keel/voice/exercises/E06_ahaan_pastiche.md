# E06 · In Ahaan's manner: "Buy and hold your breath"

> Exercise, AI DRAFT (Claude, 27 Oct 2026). Practice, not IPS text, not for submission. Brief: a pun heading; the
> opponent in its own short sentences; a real concession, then the turn; an objection beaten by a distinction; one
> homely image; numerals; spaced en dashes; the ending calls back to the title.

The case for an all-stock plan is seductive in its simplicity. Stocks have beaten bonds over almost every long stretch of modern history. Laura has ten years. Markets recover. Why pay for certainty she doesn't need?

It is a good argument, and it is not without ammunition – over most ten-year windows of the last century, a patient stockholder would indeed have finished ahead. But the argument quietly assumes that Laura has one ten-year window, when in fact she has ten one-year windows, each with a $50,000 bill nailed to the door. A market that falls 40% in 2033 and has recovered by 2036 has, on average, done perfectly well. It has also sold Laura's first 3 cheques at the bottom.

That is the trouble with averages. A river can be five feet deep on average and still drown a six-foot man, and a portfolio can earn 7% a year on average and still miss a January.

Time does not make a stock loss smaller. It makes it rarer – which is a great comfort to anyone who can wait, and no comfort at all to a payment that can't.

So we refuse the all-stock plan. Not because stocks are bad (we own about 10,000 of them), but because they are being asked a question they cannot answer: will the money be there on the first of January? A bond can answer that. A stock can only shrug.

Buy and hold is a fine philosophy. Buy and hold your breath every December is not.

## Critique
- **Worked:** this is the closest to Ahaan's fingerprint of all the exercises (long building sentence, then the blunt
  ones; numerals; a concession with "not without ammunition", his own phrase; a callback ending). "Ten one-year
  windows, each with a $50,000 bill nailed to the door" is the sequence-of-returns concept explained without its name,
  which is north star 4 done right.
- **Worked:** the image (the drowned six-foot man) is borrowed from Marks and *argues*; it isn't decoration.
- **Still plastic:** "seductive in its simplicity" is lifted from Ahaan's own essay ("The pitch is seductive in its
  simplicity"). Pastiche is the point of the exercise, but in a deliverable it would be copying the student's tic
  back at him. "A stock can only shrug" is one cute line too many next to the ending. Choose.
- **Accuracy:** "40% in 2033, recovered by 2036" is a scenario, not a forecast; fine as an example. "7% a year" is
  near J.P. Morgan's 7.5% global equity assumption; say "about 7%".
