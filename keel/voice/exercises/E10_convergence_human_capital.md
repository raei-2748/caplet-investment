> Facts: case p.2 (the 2028 deposit's sources); D4's ordering; the idea of human capital (Final Report cites it).

The most dangerous assumption in Laura's case hides inside a sentence that reads like good news: at the start of 2028 she will add another $150,000, earned from publishing advances, speaking engagements, licensing and other ventures.

It is good news, of course. But it is also a promise made by the same economy that sets the price of everything else she owns, and that is the part most plans forget. Advances shrink when publishers are nervous, speaking fees vanish when conferences are cancelled, licensing deals wait for better times – and the times that make publishers nervous are, more often than not, the same times that send investors running into Treasuries, which pushes bond prices up at precisely the moment Laura would need to buy them. Bad news travels in pairs. Her second deposit, in other words, is not cash in waiting. It is an investment in her own career, carrying a correlation to the market that no prospectus will ever disclose.

Economists call this human capital: the present value of everything a person will earn, which for most people is their largest asset and their least diversified. A tenured professor's career behaves like a bond, steady and largely indifferent to the stock market; a writer-entrepreneur's behaves rather more like a share. Laura is the second kind. A portfolio that ignored it would be doubling a bet she has already made.

So the order of the plan follows from the nature of the money. The $300,000 she already has – money that exists, that no recession can cancel – buys the ten payments first, because the payments are the only part of the plan that cannot tolerate disappointment. The $150,000 she is still earning goes where disappointment is survivable: it tops up whatever the first deposit could not cover, and then buys the floor and the stocks that decide how generous she can be.

If the second deposit arrives in full, nothing about the payments changes. If it arrives short, the gift shrinks long before any payment notices.

We would rather the bad news land on the part of the plan that was built to absorb it.
