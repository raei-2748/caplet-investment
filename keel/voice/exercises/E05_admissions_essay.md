# E05 · Admissions-essay register (Johns Hopkins "Essays That Worked"): "Safe for"

> Exercise, AI DRAFT (Claude, 27 Oct 2026). Practice, not IPS text, not for submission. Brief: open on a claim that
> sounds wrong, one concrete detail, one pivot sentence, a short ending that turns back to the start. Real events
> only: the team's first growth-first plan (~75% stocks) failed the council's test (CLAUDE.md, 27 Sep); TLT's 2022
> loss is public market history (about a third, total return).

The first thing this competition taught us about bonds is that "safe" doesn't exist. Only "safe for".

Our first plan was three-quarters stocks, with the bonds to be bought later, when the payments got close. It looked sensible and it failed the first hard question we asked it: if Laura's 2028 deposit never arrived, about 4 in every 10 of our modelled futures missed a payment. We had built a plan that was safe for a world where everything went to schedule.

Then there was 2022. A fund holding the longest US Treasuries – lending to the safest borrower on earth – lost about a third of its value in a single year. Nobody defaulted. Nothing was wrong with the bonds. The people who owned them had simply needed their money on a different day from the one the bonds were built for.

That was the sentence we kept coming back to. A Treasury that matures in 2035 is about as safe as money gets, for someone who needs the money in 2035. For someone who needs it in 2031, it is a bet on interest rates with a very respectable name.

So we stopped asking which assets were safe and started asking which dates were. Laura has ten: the first of January, every year from 2033 to 2042, each worth $50,000. Each now has a bond of its own, timed to mature just before its January.

It changes how the whole plan reads. The stocks aren't the risky part any more; they're the part without a date. And the bonds aren't the safe part. They're ten small promises, each one safe for exactly one January.

## Critique
- **Worked:** the opening is a claim a judge wants to argue with, and the essay pays it off with a real mistake
  (the 75%-stocks plan) and a real market event. The pivot is one sentence. The ending answers the first line ("safe
  for ... exactly one January").
- **Worked:** "a bet on interest rates with a very respectable name" is the best dry line in the study.
- **Still plastic:** "That was the sentence we kept coming back to" is a little written-for-effect; and the last
  paragraph is two antitheses in a row ("aren't X; they're Y" twice). One is voice, two is a tic. Cut one.
- **Use:** this is the shape for the Final Report's opening and its Articulation section. The "4 in 10" is the
  27 Sep model (`strategy_mc.py`, growth-first with no 2028 deposit: 40.8%): re-run before any judge sees it.
