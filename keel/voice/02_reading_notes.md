# Reading notes: what the good ones do (27 Oct 2026)

AI-generated (Claude Code) voice study, not for submission. Quotes are short and verbatim from the source fetched on
27 Oct 2026 unless marked *(from memory, check before quoting)*. Each entry ends with what Keel steals. The patterns
they add up to are in `04_patterns.md`.

One finding first, because it changes how to read the rest: the people worth copying are not "good at sentences".
Every one of them has **a position, an opponent and a person behind the prose**. The sentences come from that.

---

## Investor letters and memos

### Warren Buffett, 2008 and 2011 letters
Source: https://www.midgardfinance.com/buffett/letters/2008/ , https://www.midgardfinance.com/buffett/letters/2011/
- **Confesses early and specifically.** "During 2008 I did some dumb things in investments." "So far I have been
  dead wrong." He names the mistake, its size and whose fault it is ("If we lose money on our derivatives, it will
  be my fault."). Trust comes from the confession, so the boasts later are believed.
- **Homely images that carry an argument.** Prices vs value: "getting a quote every day on a farm from a
  manic-depressive neighbor". Models: "Beware of geeks bearing formulas." Each one is a whole position compressed
  into a picture a twelve-year-old could hold.
- **Numbers turned into things you can feel.** A dollar "at only 2% inflation it will be worth roughly 14¢". All the
  world's gold as a cube about 68 feet a side, set against all US cropland plus 16 Exxon Mobils: "Can you imagine an
  investor with $9.6 trillion selecting pile A over pile B?" The number becomes a choice the reader makes.
- **Defines the word before arguing with it.** Investing is "forgoing consumption now in order to have the ability
  to consume more at a later date". Then every asset is judged by that one definition.
- **A label on bonds.** "Right now bonds should come with a warning label." And, quoting Shelby Cullom Davis: "Bonds
  promoted as offering risk-free returns are now priced to deliver return-free risk." His warning is about
  purchasing power. (Keel's use of this is idea I-1 in `07_ideas_from_reading.md`: Laura's debts are in dollars, so
  the warning is the one label that doesn't apply to her.)
- **Steal:** one real confession per piece; the farm-neighbour move (an everyday scene that *is* the argument); every
  big number gets a "that means" in human terms.

### Howard Marks, Oaktree memos ("Sea Change", 2022; "Further Thoughts on Sea Change", 2023; "Risk Revisited Again", 2015)
Sources: https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/sea-change.pdf ,
https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/further-thoughts-on-sea-change3dda4540-569f-4415-9a5b-004fbabf06d4.pdf ,
https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2015-06-08-risk-revisited-again.pdf
- **Opens on his own age and memory, then the claim.** "In my 53 years in the investment world, I've seen ... but I
  remember only two real sea changes. I think we may be in the midst of a third one today." Authority, then a
  sentence that risks being wrong.
- **One image that is the whole thesis.** The airport moving walkway: for 40 years investors "were on a moving
  walkway, carried along by declining interest rates". The image explains why everyone looked clever. It recurs.
- **A number, then a one-word reaction.** "This represented a decline of 2,000 basis points. Miraculous!" The
  number does the arguing; the exclamation tells you how to feel it.
- **Quotes, then uses the quote in the very next sentence.** Galbraith's "extreme brevity of the financial memory"
  is followed at once by why that applies to today's investors. A quote is never left as decoration.
- **Interrogates himself.** "What's the downside? How could this be a mistake?" then answers each risk in turn.
- **Definitions that become slogans.** Dimson's "Risk means more things can happen than will happen", then Marks's
  own flip: "Even though many things can happen, only one will." And the warning about averages: "the six-foot-tall
  man who drowned crossing the stream that was five feet deep on average." "We can ride out volatility, but we never
  get a chance to undo a permanent loss."
- **Wry, dry, never cute.** "Oaktree's investment philosophy doesn't prohibit having opinions, just acting as if
  they're right."
- **Bond knowledge said plainly.** In "Further Thoughts" he lists why bond price swings matter less when you hold to
  maturity, because of the pull to par. That is Caplet's iBond argument, in the voice of the most-read memo writer in
  the business.
- **Steal:** the walkway move (one image that explains a cause); the self-interrogation paragraph; "number, then a
  one-word reaction"; every quote earns its keep in the next sentence.

### Michael Mauboussin, "Probabilities and Payoffs" (Morgan Stanley / Counterpoint Global)
Source: https://eatonvance.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_probabilitiesandpayoffs.pdf
- **Borrows a story from another field to carry a statistic.** Babe Ruth "was the career leader in strikeouts, a
  measure of offensive failure" and still the greatest hitter: how often you're right matters less than how much you
  win when you are.
- **Says the idea in kindergarten words after the technical version.** "In other words, how often you are right is
  not all that matters."
- **Reads a chart's shape out loud.** "Note that 25 percent lose money, the modal outcome is a gain of between 1 and
  1.5 times, and there are few extreme values." He describes the distribution, not just its average.
- **Steal:** frequency vs magnitude as a lens (Caplet's range can be wrong two ways); describe the shape of the model
  results ("most land here, a few land there, none land below") instead of quoting a single percentage.

### David Einhorn, "Private Profits and Socialized Risk" (Grant's conference, 2008)
Source: https://hedgefundalpha.com/conferences/david-einhorn-2008-speech-private-profits-and-socialized-risk/
- **Opens on a scene, then a deadpan fact.** A fund near failure, then: "You see, it had leveraged itself more than
  thirty to one."
- **The thesis as an accusation in plain words.** Managements "did exactly what they were incentivized to do:
  maximize employee compensation."
- **Numbers used to embarrass.** Everyone knows hedge funds take 20%; "Nobody talks about the investment banks' 50%
  structures, which have no highwater mark".
- **Sarcasm as understatement.** "The banks have also done a wonderful job at public relations."
- **Steal:** follow the incentive (who is paid, and for what) whenever we explain why our build beats a product a
  bank would sell.

### Bill Ackman, Pershing Square Holdings 2024 letter
Source: https://pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2024-Annual-Report-1.pdf
- **Grades himself in the first line.** "solid but not spectacular NAV performance of 10.2%". Candour as an opener.
- **A mistake stated as a mechanism**, not an apology: "we had underestimated the degree of near-term revenue
  declines and operating deleverage".
- **Steal:** say how good the result is in the first sentence, in an adjective a sceptic would choose.

### Stanley Druckenmiller (Lost Tree Club, 2015)
The transcript was not reachable from here (https://theideafarm.com/miscellaneous/speech-at-lost-tree-club/ shows only a
summary). Nothing is quoted. What the summary shows: conviction and concentration as a stated method, and one
number to anchor a career. Re-read when a transcript is reachable.

## Bank and sell-side research

### Michael Cembalest, J.P. Morgan "Eye on the Market"
Sources: https://am.jpmorgan.com/au/en/asset-management/institutional/insights/market-insights/eye-on-the-market/ ,
"Smothering Heights" and "The Alchemists" PDFs on that page
- **Titles are puns on famous works**: "Smothering Heights", "Pillow Talk", "The End of the Affair", "The
  Alchemists". The pun is never explained. It is a wink to the reader who gets it.
- **Talks to the reader in brackets.** "(warning: tech jargon overload ahead; skip to page 20 if you want to get to
  the power discussion)". "I'm not a futurist and generally avoid this kind of thing". He admits his limits and
  keeps going.
- **Firm views in five words.** "I'm not sold on this argument." Then the evidence.
- **Gives the other side, then names its bias.** He sets out the academics' case for faster data-centre build-outs,
  then notes the studies were funded by people who want that answer.
- **Steal:** the pun title; one bracketed aside to the reader per page; "I'm not sold" bluntness.

### Morgan Stanley, "Thoughts on the Market" (Tirupattur, Sheets)
Source: https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/podcasts/thoughts-on-the-market/2026-global-economic-outlook-feedback-vishy-tirupattur
- **The call comes first, as a sentence with a shape.** "we think that 2026 brings a credit cycle that burns hotter
  before it burns out."
- **Settles a debate with a chiasmus.** "The market is not the economy and the economy is not the market."
- **Disagreement named.** "Our economists disagreed with President Lagarde".
- **Risks as scenarios.** "The most significant risk for global credit remains recession." Then what it would do.
- **Steal:** the view in the first sentence; one risk named as a story ("if X, then Y happens to Z").

### Goldman Sachs "Top of Mind"
Only landing pages were reachable here (https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/top-of-mind/2021-4-themes-in-charts). What
is known of the format: one question per issue, interviews with people who disagree, the house view stated after
them. **Steal the structure, not the prose:** pose one question, let two good opponents answer, then rule.

## Essayists

### Paul Graham, "How to Do Great Work"
Source: https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html
- **Aphorisms with no throat-clearing.** "The way to figure out what to work on is by working." "Great work often
  has a tincture of strangeness." "Style is doing things in a distinctive way without trying to. Trying to is
  affectation."
- **Corrects himself in public.** "'Never give up' is also not quite right." "Or maybe being good is simply too vague
  a standard." Thinking is visible on the page; that is what makes it sound like a person.
- **Ends on a question to the reader.** "The discoveries are out there, waiting to be made. Why not by you?"
- **Steal:** the self-correction ("not quite right"), which no hedging AI ever does because it never commits first.
  And "trying to is affectation": the motif must not try.

### Matt Levine, "Money Stuff" (Bloomberg)
Source (open excerpt): https://news.bgov.com/daily-labor-report/matt-levines-money-stuff-nothing-is-securities-fraud
- **Running themes stated like laws.** "Everything is securities fraud". He builds a world with its own physics.
- **Explains a mechanism the way you'd explain it to a smart child**, then finds it funny: "if a bad thing happens at
  a public company, some enterprising shareholder class action lawyers will sue it." The joke is that the plain
  version is true.
- **Steal:** the toy explanation (what happens, step by step, to whose money) before any jargon word. This is the
  single best cure for Genius Lab prose.

### Morgan Housel (Collaborative Fund)
Sources: https://alearningaday.blog/2020/05/20/the-three-sides-of-risk/ , https://humbledollar.com/2021/01/those-messy-humans/
- **Opens on a personal story with a death in it.** "We knew we were taking risks when we skied. Never, not once, did
  we think we'd pay the ultimate price."
- **Turns it into a three-part tool.** Risk has three sides: "The odds you will get hit. The average consequences of
  getting hit. The tail-end consequences of getting hit." Then the verdict: "Tail-end events are all that matter."
- **Reframes a dull word.** "Room for error ... is often viewed as a conservative hedge ... But when used
  appropriately, it's quite the opposite."
- **Steal:** a framework of three that is actually a tool (Housel's three sides of risk sort Caplet's two goals
  cleanly, idea I-6); reframe the boring part of our plan as the bold part.

### George Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" (1946)
Source: https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/Drew.Whitworth/webdesign/ext_orwell.html
- The four questions: "What am I trying to say? What words will express it? What image or idiom will make it
  clearer? Is this image fresh enough to have an effect?"
- The disease: "less and less of words chosen for the sake of their meaning", "ready-made phrases".
- **Steal, with a warning.** Orwell is a cure for vagueness, not a voice. Keel took his rules so far that its prose
  lost every long sentence and every idea-word (`03_why_plastic.md`). Ahaan's essays break Orwell's "short word"
  rule all the time and are better for it.

## Admissions essays (Johns Hopkins "Essays That Worked")
Sources: https://apply.jhu.edu/hopkins-insider/left-and-right-dont-exist/ , https://apply.jhu.edu/hopkins-insider/red-over-black/ ,
https://apply.jhu.edu/hopkins-insider/queens-gambit/ ; counter-examples from
https://www.collegeadvisor.com/essay-guides/johns-hopkins-essays-that-worked/
- **The good ones open on a claim that sounds wrong or a voice mid-scene.** "The first lesson I learned as a student
  pilot is that left and right don't exist." "No, Dante. Stop, think, and look at the entire board." A grandmother
  dealing solitaire.
- **One physical detail does the work of a paragraph.** "another Cessna with maroon stripes, the sun's reflection
  glinting off its windows". "Her polished fingernails scratched against the cards".
- **The pivot is one sentence.** "However, those words did not merely pertain to chess."
- **The ending is short and turns back to the opening.** "Your move." "Now, with each day, I do more."
- **The weak ones are the plastic we are trying to leave:** "Since childhood, mathematics and science had some special
  magic for me." "Unity and teamwork are values that I adore dearly". Abstract, unearned, about the writer's
  feelings rather than a thing that happened.
- **Steal:** a first line a reader would argue with; one sensory detail; a two-word ending that answers the first line.

## Ahaan (the three essays of 24 Oct, and the five books)
Private text: `/mnt/project-files/voice/ahaan_essays_2026-10-24.txt` (never in the repo). The 20 moves are in
`../ips_rewrite/research/R03_voice_study.md`. What the reading above adds:
- **He already does what the professionals do.** Marks's "number, then reaction" is his "Miserably." Buffett's
  homely image is his "revenge with a wig on". Levine's mechanism-in-plain-words is his "it is simply what caused
  deliberation feels like from the inside." Cembalest's pun titles are his headings. So copying the greats and
  copying Ahaan point the same way, which is the strongest reason to trust the direction.
- **What none of the professionals does and he does:** moral stakes. He writes as if something matters. The
  investing writers get away with less because their readers already care about money. A competition judge reading
  the 400th IPS doesn't, yet. Caplet's stake is real (a residency for artists in Taiwan, and nobody else may pay the
  cheques), and Keel's drafts treat it as a line item.
- **His books are a second sample of his voice, unguarded.** Measured in `03_why_plastic.md`: more "we", more
  questions, and asides like "(Multiple books of the Bible, I suppose?)" and "You are a very intelligent little
  robot". The essays show his formal register; the books show the joke he can't resist. Keel's voice needs both.
