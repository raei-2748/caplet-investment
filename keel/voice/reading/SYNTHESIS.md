# What the reading adds up to (synthesis 1, after 100 pieces, 27 Oct 2026)

AI-generated (Claude Code) voice study, not for submission. Built from `LOG.md` entries 001-100. Each point gives the
entries it rests on, so it can be checked. Ray's brief: read for **voice, tone, rhythm and structure**, insights
second; and (27 Oct) write for **Shark Tank investors, not examiners**. The emotion moves are now section 7 of
`../06_voice_guide.md`.

## 1. Where the voice lives (it is not in the sentences)
1. **Opinions, repeated with patience.** Marks has written the same four or five beliefs for 35 years (028-039,
   055-062) and Buffett opens every letter with the dullest sentence in finance (072-076). Their voices are their
   positions. Caplet's seven beliefs (guide section 1) are the right start; they must show up in every paragraph.
2. **Self-mockery buys bluntness.** Marks's wife and his repetitiveness (061), Buffett's "monumentally stupid
   decision" (072), Asness's rejected paper (046), Keynes's dentists (077), Graham's "lump of rock" (022). Every writer
   who is rude about other people's ideas is rude about himself first.
3. **Brackets that talk back.** Asness ("uh ok, boomers"), Damodaran ("an academic code word for really small"),
   Cembalest ("warning: tech jargon overload ahead"), Armstrong ("ha!"). The bracket is where a person interrupts
   their own formal sentence. In the IPS: one bracket to translate a technical word into five plain ones.
4. **Two registers on one page.** Cembalest's body is sober and his titles and footnotes are jokes (091-094); Asness's
   footnotes argue with his main text (047). The Final Report can do this; the IPS can't (no footnotes), so its one
   bracket does the job.

## 3. Endings
- **The best ends land on a thing, an echo or a test:** ankle weights coming off (027), "This is water." (078), "What
  can't you say? And why?" (025), a T-bone with hash browns (074), the order "good until canceled!" (097).
- **The worst ends summarise what the writer learned:** two of three Hopkins essays that *won* end on "challenges as
  opportunities for growth" (089, 090). Plastic survives even in winning essays, and always at the end.
- Rule for Caplet: the last sentence is a thing or a decision, never a lesson.

## 4. How the best writers handle the other side
State it at its strongest, in its own words, then answer: Asness (045, 048), Damodaran (096), John Locke winners
(080-084), Marks (038). Two refinements worth stealing:
- **"What I am not claiming"** before the reader can object (083).
- **Quote the opponent's case as a numbered list, then answer each step** (048).

## 5. Feeling (Ray's Shark Tank point): see guide section 7
Six moves: put the reader somewhere and never name the feeling; a promise with a name on it; accept a cost in public;
a number that measures a loss; dread, then relief; the reluctant narrator.

## 6. What not to copy
- **Pitch-deck checklists** (065): advice about telling stories that tells none.
- **John Locke formality** (080-087): fair, signposted, citation-heavy. Fairness yes; "Firstly ... Secondly" no.
- **Housel's exact lines** (050-054): too well known; a judge who reads him will hear the borrowing. Shapes are free.

## 7. Concrete things for Caplet's IPS and Final Report (to try in the Writing Room, not yet adopted)
1. **The ten cheques.** The payments are promises with names on them (F2, from King 070). "None of them bounces."
2. **"We buy to keep."** Buffett's line (073) is Laura's ladder exactly: no exit strategy, because the exit is the date.
3. **The bank would sell her the same promise with a fee.** Asness on buffer funds (048): protection paid for with
   capped upside, option costs and fees. Caplet's version builds it at home and keeps the upside.
4. **Liquid to whom?** Marks (061): an asset is not liquid or illiquid in itself. Laura's rungs are illiquid only to
   someone who wants the money before its date, and she doesn't.
5. **Two feelings, two clocks.** Housel's shape (054): scared for the near years, brave for the far ones. Use the
   shape, never his line.
6. **The downgrade that didn't move yields** (095): a cleaner way to say why "US default" is the residual risk and why
   it barely matters to a ladder held to the date.

## Synthesis 2 (after 187 pieces, 27-28 Oct night)
New since synthesis 1 (`LOG.md` 166-187): two inaugurals (Lincoln, Kennedy), Bagehot ch. III-IV, four 2020 John Locke
first prizes, five Damodaran posts from 2025, three 2028 Hopkins essays, Zuckerberg's S-1 letter, Stripe, Cembalest,
O'Leary and a Shark Tank teardown. Thirteen further pieces were re-reads of earlier entries (logged separately, not
counted); where a re-read sharpened a point, its original number is cited.

1. **Where the bad news sits.** Lincoln puts it in the middle, never first or last (166); Kennedy turns it into a
   privilege ("a long twilight struggle", 169); Zuckerberg warns investors in one plain sentence and then explains
   (176). Synthesis 1 said "bad news first and smallest"; the better rule is **bad news early, small and in the
   middle, with the hope last**. The IPS's limit sentence belongs in its middle third.
2. **What AI can't copy is the working.** Damodaran was impersonated by an AI scam that copied his face and phrasing
   but not his reasoning or his long-held views (185). Voice is a consistent philosophy with its working shown, not a
   style. This is Book 9's "logic" route arrived at from the other side: show the causal chain, not the conclusion.
3. **Endings.** Two of the Hopkins winners end on "challenges as opportunities for growth" or close to it (089,
   090): plastic survives in winning work, and always at the end. The best endings in this batch are short and
   concrete: "This is the task of ethics." (173), "I am sorry!" (174), and the self-harm essay's "If it does not, it
   can be restrained." (084). Caplet's IPS should end on a test someone else could run, or on one thing.
4. **Openings.** Two new shapes worth a draft each: the **withheld reveal** ("Picture a country...", named a
   paragraph later, 170) and the **verbless list** ("Unicorns, rainbows, pink, and glitter.", 182). Both make a
   reader lean in before they know the subject.
5. **Emotion moves, ranked.** F3 (cost in public) and F6 (self-mockery) are the commonest; F2 and F4 the rarest and
   the most remembered. Ranking and use are in guide section 8.
6. **Pitches.** O'Leary: attention in the first seconds, the core in about 90 seconds (178). A pitch can win the room
   and still be wrong (179). Caplet's 50 words must be true in 2042, not just in the room.
7. **Counter-examples kept.** The John Locke winners that open on a dictionary definition (085) or end on a moral
   (172) won on argument. Fairness and structure: yes. Their openings: no.

**Investing finds (in the idea bank as FRB-17 to FRB-21):** risk-free is a horizon, not an asset (130, re-read);
dated cheques can't wait on stocks, since a 1929 buyer was back to even on the price index only in 1954 (097,
re-read); cash for a known need is a shock absorber, not a pogo stick (187); the market's price of a US default was
about 0.27% after the 2025 downgrade (184); private assets look calm only because appraisals lag (096, re-read).
