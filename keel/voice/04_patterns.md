# The pattern library: moves worth stealing (27 Oct 2026)

AI-generated (Claude Code) voice study, not for submission. Each move: who does it, why it works, one Caplet example
written for this page (an exercise line, never IPS text), and how an AI ruins it. Sources and quotes:
`02_reading_notes.md`. The guide that picks from this library: `06_voice_guide.md`.

## A. Openings (the first sentence decides whether the judge reads or skims)
| # | Move | Who | Why it works | Caplet example | How AI ruins it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **The claim that sounds wrong** | Hopkins pilot ("left and right don't exist"); Graham | The reader's first reaction is "no", so they read on to win the argument | "A Treasury bond is not safe. It is safe *for a date*." | Makes it a riddle with no payoff, or explains it in the same sentence |
| A2 | **Memory, then a risky claim** | Marks ("In my 53 years ... I think we may be in the midst of a third") | Earns the right to be bold, then is bold | "Every team in this competition will promise Laura her payments. We bought them." | Adds three hedges to the risky claim |
| A3 | **Grade yourself first** | Ackman ("solid but not spectacular"), Buffett | Candour buys belief for everything after | "Our first plan was perfect and illegal." (the STRIPS plan, 6 Oct) | Grades with an adjective nobody would argue with ("robust") |
| A4 | **The opponent, in their own short sentences** | Ahaan ("You knew what you were doing. You could have done otherwise.") | Voices the case you will beat, at full strength | "Stocks beat bonds over ten years. Laura has ten years. Why pay for certainty?" | Strawmen the opponent so the win is cheap |
| A5 | **A scene with one physical detail** | Hopkins essays; Einhorn | The reader is somewhere before they are told anything | "The US market opens at 12:30 a.m. in Sydney." | Scenery words ("in today's ever-changing landscape") |
| A6 | **The view in sentence one** (sell-side) | Morgan Stanley ("burns hotter before it burns out") | A busy reader gets the call even if they stop | "Laura's payments should be owed to her, not hoped for." | Hides the view in paragraph three behind "context" |

## B. Building the argument
| # | Move | Who | Why it works | Caplet example | How AI ruins it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Define the word, then judge everything by it** | Buffett ("investing is forgoing consumption now ...") | One definition does the work of a page of rules | "We take 'a high degree of certainty' to mean that every cheque is owed to Laura by the government that issues the dollar." | Defines and never uses the definition again |
| B2 | **Concede for real, then turn on one word** | Ahaan ("not without ammunition ... But"), Marks | Shows you've thought; the turn lands harder after a real concession | "Stocks probably will beat these Treasuries. But 'probably' is a word you can't cash in January." | The concession is a token ("while some may argue") |
| B3 | **Beat an objection with a distinction** | Ahaan (the experience of choosing vs whether it was determined) | Splits the opponent's argument into two things it was blurring | "Time makes a stock loss rarer. It doesn't make it smaller." | Piles on evidence instead of splitting the claim |
| B4 | **Self-interrogation** | Marks ("What's the downside? How could this be a mistake?") | You attack your own plan before the judge does | "Where could this break? In one place: the coupons." | Lists "risks" with no answers, or answers with "we will monitor" |
| B5 | **Follow the incentive** | Einhorn | Explains why something exists by who gets paid | "A bank sells a capital-protected note because it keeps the spread between what it pays you and what the parts cost." | Moralises instead of explaining the mechanism |
| B6 | **The toy explanation** | Levine | Step-by-step what happens to whose money, before any jargon | "Here is the whole trick. You give the bank $100. It spends about $80 on a bond that grows back to $100 ..." | Starts with the term ("a principal-protected structured note is ...") |
| B7 | **Read the shape, not the average** | Mauboussin | Judges trust a writer who describes the spread, not just the middle | "Most runs land inside the range; about one in four falls short of the top; a few in a hundred fall below the bottom, and those are the runs we plan for." | Quotes one percentage with three decimals |
| B8 | **Borrowed framework that sorts the problem** | Housel (three sides of risk) | A tool the judge can reuse is remembered | "The payments are judged on the tail, the gift on the average. So they get different assets." | Uses a framework as a name-drop with nothing sorted |

## C. Numbers
| # | Move | Who | Why it works | Caplet example | How AI ruins it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Number, then a one-word reaction** | Marks ("2,000 basis points. Miraculous!"), Ahaan ("Miserably.") | Tells the reader how big it is | "Headroom on 22 Oct: $1,139. On $300,000. Snug." | Adds "which is significant" |
| C2 | **The number as a choice** | Buffett (pile A or pile B) | The reader decides, so the reader agrees | "Would you rather be owed $50,000 in January 2035, or own $50,000 of stocks that will probably be worth more by then?" | Both piles described in neutral jargon |
| C3 | **Shrink it to a human scale** | Buffett (14¢) | A big number nobody can feel becomes a coin | "A 2-point fall in rates for good costs the plan about $21,000, or a little under half of one cheque." | Converts to another abstract unit ("basis points of NAV") |
| C4 | **Numerals, rounded, few** | Ahaan ("4 airlines now control 80%") | One number per point, the one that wins it | "1 in 15" instead of "6.8% of 200,000 paths (MODEL, seed 20261016)" | Every figure, unrounded, with a provenance stamp |

## D. Wit and images
| # | Move | Who | Why it works | Caplet example | How AI ruins it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **One image that *is* the thesis, used twice** | Marks (moving walkway) | Delete the image and the argument goes with it | "A coupon bond is a postdated cheque cut into pieces and posted to you twice a year; someone has to keep the pieces until January." | A new metaphor every paragraph, each decorative |
| D2 | **Homely simile inside a formal sentence** | Ahaan ("revenge with a wig on"), Buffett ("two hungry mosquitoes in a nudist camp") | The register drop is the joke | "A bond fund that never matures is a bond with amnesia." | Over-elaborate or cute ("a symphony of yields") |
| D3 | **Steal the opponent's saying and turn it** | Ahaan (rising tide, boats "chained to the bottom"), Marks's adages | The reader's own proverb now argues for you | "Time in the market beats timing the market, unless the market is the one choosing the time." | Invents a fresh metaphor instead of hijacking a famous one |
| D4 | **Pun title, never explained** | Cembalest ("Smothering Heights"), Ahaan ("Laissez-faire, or laissez-unfair?") | A wink to the reader who gets it | "Maturity, underrated" | Explains the pun; or puns in the body text too |
| D5 | **The bracket that winks** | Ahaan ("(A non-exhaustive list)"), Cembalest | The writer leans over and talks to you | "(Laura's books are about messy roots. Her bonds are not.)" | Brackets as provenance stamps |
| D6 | **Dry understatement** | Einhorn ("a wonderful job at public relations"), Levine | Lets the reader supply the outrage | "Lehman's protected notes were protected by Lehman." | Sarcasm that explains itself |

## E. Turns of thought (what makes a writer sound like a person)
| # | Move | Who | Why it works | Caplet example | How AI ruins it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Correct yourself in public** | Graham ("'Never give up' is also not quite right") | Visible thinking; only possible after a firm claim | "We said the payments were the safe part. That's not quite right: they are the part we can make safe." | Hedges before committing, so nothing to correct |
| E2 | **Confess one real mistake** | Buffett, Ackman | Trust; a person on the page | "We built our first plan from STRIPS. On 6 October Wharton told us WInS doesn't sell them." | Invents a mistake, or confesses something flattering |
| E3 | **Admit your limit and carry on** | Cembalest ("I'm not a futurist ...") | Confidence without arrogance | "We can't tell you what rates will do. We can tell you what they can't do: no rate on earth changes what a Treasury pays at maturity." | Disclaimers in every paragraph |

## F. Endings
| # | Move | Who | Why it works | Caplet example | How AI ruins it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Call back to the opening and flip it** | Ahaan (dinosaurs ... "We do."), Hopkins ("Your move.") | The piece closes like a door | Opens on "safe for a date"; ends "Ten dates. Ten times safe." | Restates the thesis in new adjectives |
| F2 | **End on the alternative you refused** | Ahaan's TNA reflections | The last thing the judge reads is a decision | "... which is why there is no bond fund in this plan that never matures." | "In conclusion, our strategy is robust and client-centred." |
| F3 | **The plain instruction** | Marks ("We have to behave accordingly.") | Ends on action, not on a feeling | "So we buy them in January, and then we leave them alone." | Ends on a feeling ("we are confident that ...") |
| F4 | **A question handed to the reader** | Graham ("Why not by you?") | Best in the Final Report's last line; risky in a policy | "Which of these ten Januaries would you have left to chance?" | Asks a question and answers it in the next sentence |

## G. Spending rules (the part AIs never learn)
- **Kickers are money.** One per section, three per IPS at most (the Writing Room's 27 Oct finding). The rest end on a
  number or a decision.
- **One image per piece**, introduced once and called back once. A second image must be from the same world.
- **One confession per piece.** Two is self-pity.
- **One "X, not Y" per 400 words.** Ahaan's rate. Make it a chiasmus or cut it.
- **One long building sentence per paragraph**, one short sentence that lands after it.
