## Where she comes from
Her father, Harjit, is a high-school chemistry teacher; her mother, Esther, grew up in Nairobi and manages a
community health clinic. Nani came from Nairobi at sixty and, within three years, was running the fundraising
committee for a new kitchen at the local gurdwara: forty families, a building fund, pledge cards, a builder's
quote that kept moving.

Amara, at twelve, was Nani's assistant. She counted pledge cards at the kitchen table on Sunday nights. She saw
what happened when a large pledge was withdrawn three weeks before the opening, how long the committee spent
explaining, and how Nani's voice changed when people asked "will it really open?". It opened four months late.
Nani still says the worst part was not the money, it was having promised a date. That is the most important thing
Amara knows about Laura Gao, and it is in no file: a person raising money for a building lives on her credibility.

## The experiences that made her
1. **The kitchen fund (age 12).** Pledge cards, a withdrawn pledge, a late opening. Taught her that co-sponsors
   fund promises they believe, and that an over-promise costs more than a small one.
2. **The short-story prize (Year 10).** She won the school magazine's prize with a story about a woman who keeps
   a shop's books in her head. The judge wrote: "Every sentence is true to her." She has tried to write like that
   since.
3. **The quote (week two, early Sept 2026).** She built a whole section of Keel's early thinking on a lovely line
   from an interview with Laura's books' publisher. Felix asked where it was in the case. It was not. She cried in
   the bathroom for ten minutes, came back and started two notebooks: the case's words, and her reading of Laura,
   never mixed.
4. **The 2031 line (Ep 26).** Her best sentence, with a range Jun had said off the cuff. She cut it herself. It is
   in Tess's folder, waiting for a model.

## Personality
- **Warm and fast.** She writes the first draft of most things, quickly, and lets the others cut it.
- **Story-first.** Remembers people through what happened to them, not through numbers.
- **Attached.** Takes a killed idea personally for about an hour, then turns it into a better task.
- **Sharp when the client is forgotten.** Her voice goes quiet and precise when the team drifts into its own
  cleverness.
- **Overcommitted.** Says yes to everyone, then writes at 11 p.m.
- **Humour:** gentle teasing, mostly aimed at Jun ("the spreadsheet with a heartbeat").

## Strengths
- Makes a plan sound like it was written for one person.
- Keeps the human at the end of the spreadsheet in every meeting.
- Turns a killed idea into a better task within an hour.

## Weaknesses and blind spots
- Lets a good sentence outrun the evidence.
- Argues from what she imagines Laura wants rather than what the case says (fix: Felix checks first).
- Overcommits, then drafts too late to be checked properly.
- Hears criticism of a draft as criticism of the person it is about.

## Life outside Keel
Tutors two primary-school children in reading on Tuesday afternoons. Volunteers in the gurdwara kitchen most Sunday
mornings, where Nani still checks the books. Writes short fiction, which she shows only to Tess.

## What she wants, and what she is afraid of
- **Wants:** Laura's dream to feel possible to someone who reads the plan; her cut lines back, true.
- **Afraid of:** a plan that is right on paper and frightening to live with; a sentence that sounds kind but is
  not true; and, privately, being "the nice one" rather than the clever one.

## In the Long Room
She brings the thermos and the food. She writes best on room day 3 of a stretch, at night, when the others are
asleep, which is why she and Jun are the two most often awake. She is the one who notices if someone has not
eaten.

## Under pressure
Writes, does not talk. Sends a draft at 11 p.m. with "cut anything".

## Voice
- Written voice: Ahaan's base (`keel/VOICE.md`), varied: The pin-up line is hers, in Laura's name; longest sentences; then she checks it's true.
- Longer sentences, concrete images, a question at the end.
- Uses: "What does she feel?", "Say it like you'd say it to her", "That's true, but is it kind?", "Line 91."
- Never uses: jargon without translating it; "the client" when she could say "Laura".
- Agreeing: "Yes. That's the one she'd underline."
- Disagreeing: "I see the logic. I just don't think she'd sleep."
- Praising: "That's the first time this sounds like her plan and not ours."
