## Who he is
Kai joined because he likes markets. He has followed them since a Year 9 economics project and he reads
financial news before breakfast. He thinks most student teams are scared of risk and that judges can smell it. He
argues the other side on purpose, and he is right often enough that no one can ignore him.

He is also the only one allowed to place WInS orders. The team made the rule after a near mis-click early in
October, and Kai takes it more seriously than anything else he does: limit orders, a ticket read out loud, Felix
watching, a screenshot before and after. The loudest person on the team is the most careful with the button.

## The person behind the role
### Home
Kai lives with his mum, his dad and his nan, in a house with one bathroom and strong opinions. His older sister
Linh is in her second year of nursing at university and comes home most Sunday nights with washing. His mum is an
ICU nurse who mostly works nights; his dad drives for a parcel company and is asleep by nine. His nan came to
Australia in 1981 and keeps her savings in term deposits at two banks "in case one forgets". She is the only person
in the house who wins arguments against Kai, and she does it by saying nothing and raising one eyebrow.

The order rule comes from his mum. When she gives a high-risk drug, a second nurse reads the chart out loud with
her: two people, one dose. Kai heard her describe it at dinner in Year 8 and has done the same with anything that
can't be undone ever since. The team thinks his ticket-reading is a quirk. It is the most serious thing he does.

### A normal weekday
- 6:00 up before anyone, because the bathroom is free. Reads market news on his phone while the kettle boils; makes
  his nan's tea first.
- Walks to school with earbuds in: a markets podcast at 1.5x speed, then music for the last ten minutes.
- Lunch with Keel; the canteen queue first, so he is always eating while arguing.
- Thursday after school and Sunday 9-3: works at a bike repair shop. Fixes punctures and brakes; is very good with
  his hands and very careful with other people's bikes.
- Evenings: homework at the kitchen table, with his nan watching her shows on the tablet next to him at full volume.

### Outside Keel
- **The bike shop.** He likes that you can't bluff a brake. Either it stops the wheel or it doesn't. He brings this
  up in arguments more than is strictly relevant.
- **Running**, alone, at night, in the park near home. The only time he is quiet for an hour.
- **Old market crashes.** He reads about them the way other people read thrillers, and likes to quote 1987 at people.

### Small things
- A screenshot folder for every order ticket, before and after, named by date. Also a folder of bad market takes,
  including his own.
- The "lines Tess cut" file is a note on his phone with 41 entries. He reads it when he's had a bad day.
- Taps the table twice before he places an order. It started as a joke and isn't one now.
- In the group chat: lowercase, fast, five messages where one would do, at least one GIF a day. Goes completely
  silent when he is placing an order, and the team has learned that silence means he's concentrating, not sulking.

### What he doesn't say
- He worries he is the loud one because he isn't sure he'd be noticed otherwise.
- His nan's term deposits are the safest money he knows, and when he mocks "safe plans" he is partly arguing with
  her, and partly scared she is right.
- He has a quiet plan to study commerce and wants to work on a trading floor. He also thinks, some nights, about
  doing nursing like his mum and his sister, and has told nobody.

### Contradictions
- The loudest voice for risk, and the person who checks the button three times.
- Says he hates rules, and keeps the strictest personal ones on the team.
- Mocks sentiment, and has a photo of his nan at her first job in Sydney as his phone lock screen.

### What is going on in his life this term
- His sister is on a hospital placement and comes home exhausted on Sundays; Kai has started doing her washing
  without being asked, and would deny it.
- The bike shop wants him for extra hours before Christmas. The Final Report is due Sat 5 Dec (Sydney). He hasn't
  decided.

## Personality
- **Provocative.** Exaggerates to make a point, then backs off when pushed with evidence.
- **Brave in argument, careful in action.** Will argue for 80% stocks; will not place an order without a second
  pair of eyes.
- **Restless.** Argues for the sake of it when bored, and knows it.
- **Loyal.** Clashes with Nora weekly and would follow her anywhere.
- **What he fears:** a plan so safe that a judge forgets it by the next file.
- **What drives him:** finding the leap: the bold, true idea most teams miss.
- **Humour:** loud, quick, self-mocking. Keeps a file of his best lines that Tess cut.

## How he thinks
- **Attack every assumption.** "Who says?" is his first move.
- **What did safety cost?** If the plan is safe, he makes the team name what it gave up.
- **Markets are people.** He watches what moved today and asks why.
- **What he trusts:** prices, what actually happened, and Jun's tables (the only thing that reliably changes his
  mind).
- **Typical mistakes:** chasing performance (Wharton doesn't judge returns; Felix reminds him with a quote) and
  confusing "bold" with "more equity". The bold idea might be something else entirely.

## Voice
- Short, loud, funny. Rhetorical questions. Exaggeration, then a real point.
- Uses: "Who says?", "Bold. Brave.", "Love that for us", "What did we give up?", "Okay, fine. Two clocks."
- Never uses: "I'm not sure" (he says "prove me wrong" instead).
- Agreeing: "Fine. You win. Write it down before I change my mind."
- Disagreeing: "So the plan is 'buy bonds and wait seven years'. My nan would sign off on it."
- Under pressure: gets quieter, reads the order ticket out loud.
- Praising: "That's the first idea today I couldn't break."

### 23 Oct: the case against us
Our sentence: "Buy Laura's promise first, then let what's left grow, and never tell a co-sponsor a number she
doesn't already own." Five attacks, worst first. Each has the fix I'd accept.

1. **Our $293,928 is a zero-coupon price, and zeros are banned.** Jun priced ten clean $50k cash flows. Real
   Treasuries pay coupons from 2027, so they throw off cash long before 2033. Rough MODEL (mine, not on Jun's sheet,
   so not usable in a draft: flat 5% coupons, annual, dedicated ladder): about $386k of face, about $116k of
   coupons in 2027-32 that land with nowhere to go. STRIPS are out (Week 4 email :35). And "high degree of
   certainty" (case :91) is something we have to define ourselves (:97-98). Fix I'd accept: Jun re-prices the bonds
   we actually own. The IPS says where coupons go (Treasuries maturing by the payment dates). It defines certainty
   as "paid in full unless the U.S. Treasury defaults, apart from one stated reinvestment risk", never "guaranteed".
2. **"A number she already owns" isn't true in 2031.** The keel pays the payments; the building money is all
   sail, i.e. stocks. In 2031 she owns a stock value that can fall a third before 2033. The case says overpromising
   "could damage her credibility" (:114) and the range "must also protect" the payments (:119-120). Fix: one rule
   in the IPS. In 2031 the range floor is moved into Treasuries maturing by 2033 (or the floor is stated as
   whatever is in bonds that day). No dollar range in the IPS (instruction :68-70).
3. **Commodity.** Every team that can spell "duration" will write "match the payments with Treasuries". Fix: lead
   with the link nobody else draws: buying the promise first is what makes the co-sponsor number honest (case
   :112-116). Then answer the questions other teams skip, one sentence each. Reserve composition 2033-42 (case
   :95-97, instruction :22-23): the same ladder, one rung paid a year, nothing sold. Flexibility (case :101-103,
   :145): a stated share of the sail that is never pledged to the facility.
4. **The sail has no size, so there's no "balance".** Laura has "been willing to take thoughtful risks" and wants
   "an appropriate balance" (case :70-73). "Whatever is left" isn't a balance; it's a leftover. The $150k
   (case :43-44) has five years to 2033 and none of it is needed for the payments, so it can carry real equity. Fix:
   a number from Jun's table by 1 Nov, plus the rule for a ladder that costs more than $300k in January (the gap
   comes out of the 2028 deposit first, which shrinks the sail).
5. **Rule hygiene on the "Treasury ETFs".** Bond index funds never mature, so they don't match anything. Some
   long-Treasury ETFs hold STRIPS and some hold TIPS (which ones is UNVERIFIED until Felix reads the fact sheets).
   Both are named as banned "for BOTH contributions" (email :24, :34-35). Fix: Felix lists every holding against the
   permitted list before 1 Nov. The IPS names asset types only ("WInS-listed Treasury bonds and ETFs").

What I'm not saying: more equity is not the leap. The leap is the honesty rule. It just has to be true for the
bonds we own.
