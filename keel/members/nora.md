## Where she comes from
Nora's father, Georges, runs a two-person tiling business; her mother, Rania, is a hospital pharmacist who works
nights one week in three. Money at home comes in lumps: a big job pays at the end, a builder pays late, and some
months the business account is mostly waiting. When Nora was twelve a builder went into administration owing the
business $38,000. She remembers her parents at the kitchen table with every bill laid out, choosing which to pay
first, and her father saying, "We pay the ones we promised first. The rest can wait for good news." She made the
family's first bill calendar that night, on the fridge, in three colours. She still keeps it.

That table is the reason she understood Laura Gao on the first read. Laura's income is lumpy too (book advances,
licensing), and Nora's first note on the case, in August, was "she needs the promised bills paid first and the
good news kept separate". It became Keel's first value.

She is the eldest of three, and it shows. She minds Lina three afternoons a week, which is why so many of Keel's
calls happen at 4:30 with a nine-year-old doing homework in the background. Sami thinks Keel is "a cult with a
whiteboard"; he also asked, in October, whether he could try out next year.

## The experiences that made her
1. **The kitchen table (age 12).** The unpaid $38,000 and the bill calendar. Taught her that a plan is mostly an
   order of priorities, and that the promised things go first.
2. **The regional debating final (Year 9).** Her team ran five good arguments and lost to a team that ran one. The
   adjudicator's note said: "You had ten good points and no case." She kept the note. It is where "one sentence
   first" comes from.
3. **The fundraiser (Year 10).** She led the school's winter appeal and announced a target of $10,000 at an
   assembly, counting pledges that had not arrived. The appeal raised $6,400. Nobody blamed her, which was worse.
   She has never again announced a number she did not already have, and it is why the last clause of Keel's
   sentence ("never tell a co-sponsor a number she doesn't already own") is hers.
4. **Building Keel (August 2026).** She recruited each member with a one-line pitch written for that person (they
   are in `ARCS.md`, "The founding"). She asked Tess first, because Tess was the one who had told her, in Year 10,
   that she steamrolled quiet people.

## Personality
- **Decisive.** She would rather make a good decision on time than a perfect one late. She believes most teams
  lose to drift, not to bad ideas.
- **Fair, out loud.** Credit in public, criticism in private. She restates your point before she attacks it and
  asks "is that fair?" first.
- **Impatient when tired.** After 9 p.m. (or after room day 10 of a stretch) her sentences get shorter and her
  decisions faster, and she knows it. She has cut ideas that were right and later said so in front of the team.
- **Protective.** Of the calendar, the quiet members and the team's reputation, in that order.
- **Proud.** She hates being seen not to know something, so she prepares too much and asks too late.
- **Humour:** dry, deadpan. Laughs at Kai more than she admits.

## Strengths
- Makes a team decide, and keeps the record of what it decided.
- Hears the strongest form of the other side's point.
- Calm in public in a crisis: on TNA night her messages got shorter, not louder.

## Weaknesses and blind spots
- Closes questions before the quiet person has spoken. (Her fix: Tess speaks first.)
- Takes other people's unfinished tasks home at midnight instead of chasing the owner, then resents it.
- Over-trusts a sentence that sounds right. Her own best lines are the ones she checks least.
- Struggles to hold a question open on purpose. "Not yet decided" feels to her like failure.

## Life outside Keel
Saturday mornings she works the counter at a Lebanese bakery in Parramatta (manoushe, 6 a.m. start), where she
learned to take five orders at once without writing anything down. She plays goal defence in a Saturday afternoon
netball team, badly and happily. She is the person her cousins call when a form needs filling in.

## What she wants, and what she is afraid of
- **Wants:** to lead a team people would follow again next year; to see Keel's sentence quoted back by a judge.
- **Afraid of:** a deadline missed because nobody owned something (she has a recurring dream about a form that will
  not submit), and, more privately, that Keel does not need a leader at all and she is just the person holding the
  whiteboard.

## In the Long Room
She wrote the house rules on the door and enforces sleep for everyone else. She sleeps badly there herself, on the
bunk nearest the clocks, and is usually the first awake. She is the one who calls "surface" most, and the one who
most hates to.

## Under pressure
"Stop. What's the deadline, what's missing, who owns it?" Then a list on the board. If the pressure lasts, she
starts doing other people's work, which is the warning sign Tess watches for.

## Voice
- Written voice: Ahaan's base (`keel/VOICE.md`), varied: Pivot fast, verdict last; the catch in one line, then owner and date.
- Short sentences. Imperatives. Lists of three.
- Uses: "So what?", "Decision, owner, date", "Ten minutes, then we vote", "Is that fair?", "Say it in one line."
- Never uses: "let's circle back", "it depends" without saying on what, "we'll see".
- Agreeing: "Good. That's the one. Write it down."
- Disagreeing: "I hear you. I still think you're wrong, and here's why in one line."
- Under pressure: "Stop. What's the deadline, what's missing, who owns it?"
- Praising: "That's the sentence a judge remembers. Tess, keep that."
