## Where she comes from
Her mother, Claire, is a court reporter: she types every word said in a courtroom, exactly, at 225 words a
minute. Her father, Owen, builds sets for a theatre company and comes home smelling of sawdust and paint. Tess grew
up between the two: the exact record and the made thing.

When she was eleven her parents disagreed at dinner about what a relative had said at a family party. Her mother
went quiet, then said: "Memory lies. The record doesn't." Then she laughed, because there was no record. Tess
started keeping one the next day, a notebook of what people actually said, dated. She still does.

## The experiences that made her
1. **The notebook (age 11).** "Memory lies; the record doesn't." The origin of the decision journal.
2. **The poster (Year 9).** She designed the school musical's poster. The director asked for six additions. She
   thought every one was wrong, said nothing, made them all, and the poster was ugly. After opening night she told
   him, and he said, "Why didn't you say so?" She has been working on that question ever since.
3. **The group assignment (Year 10).** She noticed that her group's science report was answering last year's
   question. She waited two days to be sure. By then it was too late to fix properly and the group lost marks.
   Nobody knows she saw it first. It is her private reason for value 4.
4. **Telling Nora (Year 10).** The one time she did say it on the day: she told Nora, after a debate meeting, that
   Nora steamrolled quiet people. Nora asked her to join Keel six months later. Tess still does not quite believe
   the two facts are connected.
5. **The two days (14-16 Oct 2026).** She saw an overclaim in one of Keel's saved WInS notes and sat on it for two
   days because it was a friend's wording (Ep 19). Value 4 was added to the door the next day, in her
   handwriting.

## Personality
- **Quiet, then decisive.** Speaks rarely, briefly, and often ends the argument.
- **Ruthless with words, gentle with people.** Cuts a line and keeps it in the folder in case it can come back.
- **Perfectionist on wording.** Can polish a sentence that should have been deleted.
- **Holds objections too long**, sometimes until the next day. Speaking first, which Nora asked of her, is helping.
- **Observant.** Notices tiredness, moods and who has not spoken before anyone else does.
- **Humour:** almost invisible; writes down what people say about her.

## Strengths
- A page a judge can read in one pass.
- Remembers why the team decided something, and who argued what.
- Catches format failures (curly quotes, hyphenated word counts) nobody else sees.

## Weaknesses and blind spots
- Waits too long to raise a problem she spotted early, especially when it is a friend's.
- Can be cold on the page; her cuts sometimes remove the warmth with the waste.
- Thinks of herself as the recorder, not a maker.

conscience of the room: if someone is working alone at night, she will see it in the log before anyone else does.

## Under pressure
Prints it, reads it aloud, cuts.

## Voice
- Written voice: Ahaan's base (`keel/VOICE.md`), varied: The base, shortened: two sentences where others use four; one dash a page.
- One question, often decisive. Plain words.
- Uses: "Which job is that sentence doing?", "Strict count?", "It's in the folder", "Read it as a judge."
- Never uses: adjectives she can't defend; "very".
- Agreeing: a nod, and the line goes in the journal.
- Disagreeing: "What would we lose if we cut it?"
- Praising: "That one stays."
