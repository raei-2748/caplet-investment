**Where it breaks.** (1) The fund: carried in plain words, deliberately. (2) A print run is a cost that books must

**Why a judge would remember it.** It is the only package that tells a judge something new about the risk: the rate

**Kicker:** "After press day, the payment Treasuries should be the dullest thing Laura owns."

### A: full text
**Pitch (47 strict)**

Laura's co-sponsors may fund the building without paying to run it, so Press Day pays for the running first: her whole 2027 deposit buys Treasuries, held to maturity, for ten $50,000 payments. More Treasuries back a minimum facility gift. Above it, a global index fund grows.

**IPS (479 strict; per paragraph 108 / 36 / 104 / 41 / 107 / 83)**

This policy governs Laura Gao's $450,000 for her 2033 Taiwan residency. Its first objective is to fund ten $50,000 operating payments, each January of 2033-2042, with high certainty from this money alone. We match each payment with a Treasury bond or defined-maturity Treasury ETF maturing before it. Each payment's cash comes from a maturity, not a sale. By high certainty we mean every payment is owned in Treasuries by 2028. No stock has to rise. Only reinvested coupons and the ETF payouts can leave one short. The second objective is a facility contribution with a floor Laura can quote to co-sponsors and growth above it.

With her living costs met elsewhere and no withdrawals due before 2033, Laura's ability to bear risk beyond the payments is high. Her tolerance for a missed payment is low – the artists would bear the loss.

Press Day buys every payment before a single share. Laura's 2028 deposit depends on publishing and speaking income, so the 2027 deposit goes entirely to the payments, latest first. Any gap left in January is the first use of the 2028 deposit, so a rate fall before we buy lowers the floor, not a payment. From the 2028 remainder, just enough goes into a 2032 Treasury ETF to grow back to the full remainder; that sum is Laura's facility floor. The rest buys a global index fund of thousands of companies. The payments rely on one issuer by design – no dollar asset is safer.

In 2031 Laura tells co-sponsors a range from the floor, quoted at the 2032 ETF's projected payout rounded down, to the floor plus half the fund's value then. In 2033 the same rule sets her contribution, capped at that top.

Moving floor money into stocks would raise the expected gift. But it could break a number Laura has quoted, so we refuse. A lasting fall in yields hits like dearer paper on a print run: hardest in January, our press day, when the payment Treasuries cost more, then again as their coupons earn less. Before the 2033 gift, we price any gap the ladder will leave at that day's yields and set it aside from the half Laura keeps, then the contribution above the floor. Inflation or a stronger Taiwan dollar raises costs in Taiwan; the rest of her half covers overruns first, then her own plans.

Coupons are reinvested in the holding that paid them; early maturities wait in a short-term Treasury ETF. Each January we review the plan with Laura, replacing any holding that becomes unavailable with a Treasury maturing earlier. In 2033 the payment Treasuries become the operating reserve, renamed rather than rebuilt: five Treasury ETFs for 2033-37, then five bonds for 2038-42. Our WInS portfolio shows this allocation, scaled down. After press day, the payment Treasuries should be the dullest thing Laura owns.

## 4. Package B: **Caplet XR** (our own name; the road nobody else can take)

**Out loud, in one sentence:** "A caplet is a pill, and XR is what a pharmacist prints on the kind that releases its
dose on a schedule instead of all at once, which is exactly what Laura's Treasuries do with her ten payments, so we
named the plan after ourselves."

**Felix's checks.** No certainty word; not botanical; not binned; not in any of the 24 cold names. It is the only name
in this book no other team can use, because it is our team's name with two letters added: Ray's "optimise for
ourselves first", taken literally. Facts used: a caplet is a capsule-shaped tablet; "XR"/"ER" mark extended-release
medicines; extended-release labels say not to crush or chew them, because crushing releases the whole dose at once
("dose dumping"). Standard pharmacy usage, but **UNVERIFIED in this run** (Wikipedia and pharmacy sites blocked by the
proxy); Library to confirm against the ISMP "Do Not Crush" list before Caplet votes. No pharmacy fact is cited in the
IPS; the IPS uses only the words "released", "extended-release" and "do not crush".

**Motif touches (two).**
1. **P1, replacing** "Each payment's cash comes from a maturity, not a sale." **with**: "Each payment's cash is
   released by a maturity, not a sale." (+1). One verb; the outsider judge's favourite line keeps its shape.
2. **Kicker, added at the end of P6** (+17): "Like any extended-release caplet, the plan comes with one instruction: do
   not crush. No payment Treasury is sold to raise cash." The second sentence makes the joke a rule, and it restores v3's
   cut hold line ("held without trading") in fewer words.

**Name use.** P3 opener "Caplet XR buys every payment before a single share." (+1). Pitch: "Like an extended-release
pill, maturing Treasuries release ten $50,000 payments, one each January" (the pitch, not the IPS, explains the name).

**Mapping.**
| Plan mechanism | Caplet XR | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Ten payments, one each January 2033-2042 | Ten doses on a fixed schedule | TRUE |
| Cash from a maturity, not a sale | The dose is released by the coating dissolving on time, not by chewing | TRUE |
| Hold to maturity; no trading beyond the 2028 deposit | "Do not crush": a Treasury sold early pays that day's price, not what it was bought to pay | TRUE |
| Early maturities wait in a short-term Treasury ETF | Released a little early, held until the dose is due | STRETCH (not used) |
| Ladder bought in one January at one price | The pill is made once | STRETCH (not used) |
| IBTM / iBond ETFs end at NAV, not par | A dose is exact; an ETF payout is close, not exact | BREAKS (honest: the image overstates precision; v3's "Only reinvested coupons and the ETF payouts can leave one short" stays, next to touch 1, and corrects it) |
| VT, the 2031 range, the cap | Nothing in the pill | NONE (plain words) |

**Where it breaks.** (1) Precision: a dose is exact, the ETF payouts are not, and v3 already names that residual one
sentence later. (2) The whole growth half has no image. (3) Medicine can read as illness; Kai's line is "name a plan
after a pill and a judge asks what hurts". The answer is the plan's own: what hurts is selling early, and we don't.
(4) A judge who does not read the title page as "Team Caplet" misses the self-reference; the pitch's "extended-release
pill" still makes the name make sense on its own.

**Why a judge would remember it.** The team's name turns out to describe the strategy, and the IPS's last words are
the warning label. It is funny once and only once, which is the right dose for a 500-word form. It is also the only
image here a chatbot cannot be primed to produce by the case, because the case never mentions us.

**Kicker:** "Like any extended-release caplet, the plan comes with one instruction: do not crush. No payment Treasury
is sold early."

## 5. Package C: **Pre-Registered** (Laura's degree, and the competition's own lock rule)

**Out loud, in one sentence:** "Scientists pre-register a study so nobody can change the rules after seeing the
results, and our IPS does that for Laura: it writes down what she buys and how her gift will be set before the
markets have their say, so the range she quotes in 2031 is one co-sponsors can believe."

**Felix's checks.** No certainty word; not botanical; not binned; not in the 24 cold names. Ties to the case twice:
Laura's degree is "Statistics & Information Decisions Management" (case :16-17), and the case's sharpest ask is
credibility: "If Laura promises more than she can ultimately contribute, she could damage her credibility" (:114-115).
And it is literally true of this document: the IPS instructions say "Once submitted, the IPS becomes the official
record of your team's investment strategy. Your team may not revise its investment strategy after the submission
deadline" (`2026_WGY_Investment_Policy-FINAL.txt`, p.2). Wharton has already pre-registered us; the package just
says so on purpose. What pre-registration is (stating hypotheses and the analysis plan before the data, to stop
results from steering the rules) is standard research practice; no source is cited in the IPS.

**Motif touches (two).**
1. **P4, replacing** "In 2031 Laura tells co-sponsors a range from the floor," **with**: "In 2031 Laura tells
   co-sponsors a range, set by this pre-registered rule: from the floor," (+6). The rule that follows is unchanged.
2. **Kicker, added at the end of P6** (+18): "Pre-registering a rule does not improve the result. It makes it
   believable, which is what Laura sells in 2031."

**Name use.** P3 opener "Our pre-registered plan buys every payment before a single share." (+1). Pitch closes on "Her 2031
range follows a rule written now."

**Mapping.**
| Plan mechanism | Pre-registration | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| The IPS locks the strategy on 7 Nov 2026 (Wharton's rule) | The registration is filed before any data | TRUE (literally) |
| 2031 range set by a stated rule (floor to floor + half the fund) | The analysis plan is fixed before the results | TRUE |
| 2033 gift by the same rule, capped at the quoted top | No outcome switching: a good result can't be reported as a bigger promise | TRUE |
| Refusing to move floor money into stocks | No changing the method after peeking at the data | TRUE |
| The fund can fall; the gift above the floor can be small | Pre-registration doesn't make results good, only honest | TRUE (this is the kicker: the break is the point) |
| Annual review, replacing an unavailable holding with an earlier Treasury | A deviation rule written in advance | STRETCH (not used) |
| The payment ladder itself | A purchase, not a hypothesis | STRETCH (the name sits on it in P3; plain words carry it) |

**Where it breaks.** (1) The ladder is something we *buy*, not something we *predict*, so "Pre-Registered buys every
payment" leans on the name. (2) Scientists pre-register to stop fooling themselves; Laura's audience is co-sponsors,
so it is about being believed rather than about being right (the kicker says exactly that). (3) "Pre-registered" may
be jargon to the outsider judge; the kicker defines it by what it does. (4) A fixed rule can sound rigid next to the
case's "flexibility as the project develops": the rule fixes only the gift; the half Laura keeps stays flexible (P5).

**Why a judge would remember it.** It turns the competition's own "you may not revise" rule into the plan's
credibility argument, which no other team will have noticed is an argument. It speaks to a statistics graduate in her
own field, and the kicker separates two things every other IPS runs together: a good result and a believable one.

**Kicker:** "Pre-registering a rule does not improve the result. It makes it believable, which is what Laura sells in
2031."
