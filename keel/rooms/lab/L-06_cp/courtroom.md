**B, Caplet XR. Charge: the strongest line is a rule the text itself breaks, and the image is medicine for an arts client.** P6 says "replacing any holding that becomes unavailable with a Treasury maturing earlier". The self-reference only works if the judge connects "Caplet" on the title page to the IPS, and it reads as the team admiring its own name. The pharmacy usage is still UNVERIFIED. The pitch spends 11 of its 48 words on the simile.
**Verdict: AMEND.** Replace "No payment Treasury is sold early." with "No payment Treasury is sold to raise cash." This keeps the replacement rule honest and costs +2. If Caplet won't make that change, B FALLS.

**C, Pre-Registered. Charge: jargon in the name, and the kicker uses a commercial verb about a charity.** "Pre-Registered buys every payment" uses an adjective as the subject, which reads as a misprint. The outsider judge won't know the term until the last line. The kicker, "...which is what Laura sells in 2031", makes her co-sponsors customers and her credibility the product. That is the wrong tone for a residency that relies on goodwill. "It makes it believable" also has an unclear "it". The idea itself holds up. Wharton's rule says the strategy "may not" be revised after submission. Fixing the gift rule in advance is a real credibility mechanism, and it answers the case's own warning about promising "more than she can ultimately contribute".
**Verdict: AMEND.** Change the kicker to: "Pre-registering a rule does not improve the result. It makes the range believable, which co-sponsors need in 2031." This is about the same word count. Second fix: in P3, write "Our pre-registered plan buys every payment" (+2). In the pitch, any added word needs a cut first, because there is only one word of margin.
