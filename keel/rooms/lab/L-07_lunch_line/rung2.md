### A1: the stock fund is the interest the floor ETF will earn, taken up front (SURVIVES, exactly)
- **Test (arithmetic, exact by construction).** `ibtm_cost = R/(1+y/2)^(2t)`; `B0 = R - ibtm_cost`. So the fund is, dollar for dollar, the ETF's total gain to maturity. 22 Oct curve: forward yield for a 2 Jan 2028 buy to 15 Dec 2032 = 4.915% (ips_numbers method; the bootstrapped method gives discount factor 0.7857, same to $100). Of a $150,000 remainder, **$117,936 (78.6%) goes to the ETF, $32,064 (21.4%) to the fund.** The ETF turns $117,936 into $150,000: the $32,064 gain is the fund. With IBTM's real 31 Dec date: $117,677 / $32,323.
- **"If every stock went to zero she would lose only the interest": true, with three conditions.** (i) It is the remainder that comes back, not the $150,000 deposit: the 2027 gap is the first call on the 2028 money, and P(gap) is about 1 in 2 (A16 below), so "her deposit back" would be false half the time; "remainder" is true. (ii) The ETF pays net asset value on a date, not par: the model's floor payout over the remainder is 100.0% at the median and 98.7% at the 1st percentile (results.json `floor_paid_vs_remainder`, line 191 of the script), so say "projected". (iii) "Only the interest" is nominal. Against putting everything in the ETF she forgoes $40,782 (150,000 grown at 4.92% to 190,782); against inflation at 2.5% (JPM 2026 LTCMA) her remainder loses about $17,300 of purchasing power over the 4.95 years (arithmetic). Honest enough for an IPS line; not for "no loss".
- **Where it breaks.** It does not say what the stocks will do, only that principal is never in stocks. It is the same as v3.1's "just enough ... to grow back to the full remainder", said from the other side. The ETF's price is only approximately yield-driven (fees, spread, NAV drift; IBTM's expense ratio not checked here, UNVERIFIED).
- **Textbook?** Yes: a zero-coupon floor plus equity bought with the discount is the "principal-protected" / bond-floor structure, and a finance teacher will say so. It matters little. Two things are not textbook in the plan: it is done with plain Treasury ETFs and a plain index fund and no product, and v3.1 never says it in the one sentence that makes it click: **the stocks are paid for with interest, not principal.** It also finally explains v3.1's "obvious trade": moving floor money into stocks is spending principal; the plan spends only the interest.
- **Serves:** Investment Strategy (why stocks are there at all), Client Knowledge (low tolerance for loss), Creativity (one image, no ornament).

### A6: does "any rise stays with Laura" overclaim? (YES, small; fix it)
- **Rule:** gift = floor + half the 2033 fund, capped at floor + half the 2031 fund (ips_numbers lines 208, 227).
- **Worked path (arithmetic):** fund $40,000 in 2031 (top = R + $20,000), falls to $20,000 in 2032, recovers to $30,000 in 2033. Gift = R + $15,000, **$5,000 below the top; the $10,000 recovery lifted the gift by $5,000.** So a rise after a fall does not "stay with Laura"; half of it goes to the gift. Equally "a fall after 2031 moves the gift toward the floor" is only true below the 2031 value: a rise to $50,000 and back to $40,000 changes nothing.
- **How often (MODEL, 200,000 monthly paths, 7% compound, 16.78% vol, median fund $32.3k in 2028 -> $39.5k in 2031):** the gift ends at the top in 71% of paths; below it in 29%; in 23% of paths (about 4 in 5 of the below-top cases) the fund rose from its 2031-33 low into 2033, so the late rise raised the gift. Median size small: gift $2,180 below the top, $1,318 gained from the trough. Not large, but a sharp judge can find it in a minute.
- **Minimal true replacement (same strength):** "any gain past that top stays with Laura." ("that top" is the top named in the previous sentence.) Strict words: 8 vs 5, **+3**. Full fix of both clauses: "Stocks ending below their 2031 value move the gift toward the floor she already owns; anything above it stays with Laura." 21 vs 19, **+2** over the whole sentence (the replacement sentence, not the 3 above). Recommend the second: it is true both ways, and it states the cap's reference point (the 2031 value) in plain words.
- **Textbook?** Capped participation in a fund: yes. Not an aha; a correction. Serves IS, CK (an honest rule).

### A5: half sensitivity (TRUE, useful number, not a line)
