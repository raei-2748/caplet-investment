## Part B: 20 cross-domain mappings

**B1. Piano tuning and the wolf interval.**
- Mechanism: Tuning by pure fifths around the keyboard leaves one interval badly out, the "wolf". Tuners choose where the error goes and spread it across the scale (temperament).
- Mapping: The ten payments are the pure intervals that must sound right. A gap is the wolf. Latest-first puts the wolf on the 2033 payment, the one the 2028 money can reach and temper.
- Breaks: The wolf is a fixed ratio set by the scale. The gap size is set by rates and is random. No tuner moves the wolf into a stock fund.

**B2. Basso continuo (figured bass).**
- Mechanism: Baroque players hold a written bass line and improvise harmony above it. The bass is written; the harmony is free.
- Mapping: The payments are the bass notes, fixed in count and timing. The stock fund and gift are the upper voices, free to move as long as they do not disturb the bass.
- Breaks: In music the upper voices must fit the bass. In the plan the gift does not need to fit the payments. The bass is held, not played.

**B3. Mechanical watch power reserve.**
- Mechanism: A mainspring stores energy and releases it through the escapement in steady ticks. Winding refills it. A reserve indicator shows how much remains.
- Mapping: Payments = the energy released on a calendar. The 2028 deposit = the winding that keeps the watch going. The stock fund = the spring's surplus tension above what the going train needs.
- Breaks: A spring loses force as it unwinds. The plan's payments are nominal and fixed. A watch has no market price.

**B4. Aviation reserve fuel.**
- Mechanism: Pilots plan trip fuel, then carry a fixed reserve to reach an alternate and hold. Extra fuel is a margin for weather.
- Mapping: Trip fuel = the ten payments, planned before take-off. Reserve = the floor, the amount that must remain. Extra fuel = the stock fund, used only if the flight runs long.
- Breaks: Fuel burns at a rate that cannot be bought in advance. The stock fund does not burn and it earns, which fuel does not.

**B5. Bridge contracts and overtricks.**

- Breaks: The declarer sees the cards after bidding; Laura commits before the market moves. Overtricks have no market value.
