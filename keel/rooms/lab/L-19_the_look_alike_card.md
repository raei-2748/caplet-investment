**For a judge, first.** Caplet rewrites the final IPS in its own words tomorrow, and a reworded sentence can quietly
turn false. Hospitals stop nurses grabbing the wrong one of two look-alike drug names by printing the letters that
differ in capitals. We made the same thing for the IPS: one page, each fact beside the false look-alike a rewrite
lands on, capitals on the part that splits them. Its first version missed most breaks. Rebuilt on what real rewrites
broke, it caught every break in two fresh rewrites it had never seen, with no false alarms.

## Hypothesis (the Kitchen's request, 3 Nov; Amara's door)
One page of fact-versus-look-alike rows, built from the false versions our drafts once carried, catches most of the
fact breaks a Year 11 rewrite of the final IPS makes, without handing Caplet any wording.

**The finding that matters most:** all five rewrites, by five different writers, dropped one word: "one redeemed
**for** each January" became "one redeemed each January" or "maturing each January". Nothing in the book matures in
a January (the funds end in December; the bonds in February, May and November; the submitted Feb-2037 note itself
says "10.5-month gap"). It is the most fragile word in the IPS, and a judge with the WInS book open can see it.

## Verdict
**ADOPT, for Caplet's rewrite (not the IPS).** The card goes beside the final folder tonight (request to the Archive).
Offer to the rewrite thread: P2's (the Caplet XR paragraph) "one redeemed for each January" -> "one redeemed **before** each January" (+0, 493
strict), so the IPS itself uses the sturdier word it already uses in the pitch and P3 ("one before each January",
"each maturing before a January payment"). Honest weakness: the held-out test is small (two breaks, both the same
fact), the strangers are models, and v2 was fixed on Jun's rewrites; what it shows is that a student can use it, and
that the one break everybody makes is on it. Two borderline drops in the held-out pair ("roughly", "main") are not on
v2; see v3.
