- **K2. The house defines its range by what a bid inside it can do, calls that its opinion, and puts the limit in the very next sentence.** Online-Only Auction - Conditions of Sale and Authorship Warranty (Phillips Auctioneers LLC, New York), effective as of 2 June 2021, https://www.dist.phillips.com/content/web/docs/forms/NY_Online_Conditions_of_Sale.pdf; the same estimate sentence also appears in Phillips' "Guide for Prospective Buyers", https://phillips.com/buysell/newyork/guide (no date shown), accessed 1 Nov 2026.
  Quotes (exact, from the fetched text): "Any bid within the high and low estimate range should, in Phillips' opinion, offer a chance of success."; "However, many lots achieve prices below or above the published estimates." (The same section adds that estimates may not be relied on as a prediction of price; paraphrased.)
  **The move (one line):** Promise a use, not an outcome (a bid in this range has a chance), say whose opinion it is, then state the limit in the next sentence as plainly as the promise.
  **Mechanism:** The range is described by what you can do with it, so the reader can test it against their own plan without trusting a forecast. The "However" sentence is short and flat, so the limit reads as part of the promise, not as small print.
  **Breaks:** This is a legal clause, and the lines that follow say Phillips is not liable for any difference between estimate and price; Laura's range cannot disclaim, since the case says overpromising could damage her credibility and the range must protect the payments. The clause hedges every sentence ("should", "in Phillips' opinion", "may"), which synthesis 4 names as the AI habit of spreading doubt over every line; only the two-sentence shape is borrowable. "A chance of success" has no number, and Caplet has one (about 97 times in 100).
  **Could touch:** The range paragraph ("In 2031 she gives co-sponsors a range ...") and the sentence that carries the 97-in-100 figure.
  **In the reading already?** Dry in part: synthesis 4, "A range with a hit rate, and the direction we could be wrong" (FRB-56, FRB-60), already holds the hit rate and the direction of error. Not in it: defining the range by the act it allows a co-sponsor, and starting the limit sentence with its own word ("However") straight after the promise.

## The rewrite: the price paragraph, with the move "say the cost out loud first, the way a condition report names the flaw, then where it sits, then why it's worth it"

AI DRAFT (Claude, 1 Nov 2026). 127 words.

> In our model, our plan costs Laura about $15,000 on average by 2042, counting the gift and what she keeps. The strongest rival, matched to name our $145,000 median bottom, usually wins. It keeps our ladder but puts the 2028 deposit mostly in stocks, and in January 2031 buys its bottom with whatever they fetch that morning. Laura has been willing to take thoughtful risks, the case says, and our first plan was three-quarters stocks until our own model sank it. But the rival has a flaw – and we can say where. In the twentieth of modelled paths where 2028-30 is worst for stocks, it names about $90,000 to co-sponsors against our $140,000 after the 2031 set-aside, with stocks and yields moving independently. We'd pay the $15,000.

What the move changed: the price now comes first, with its source and its date, before the rival is even introduced, and the rival's weakness is given an address (the worst twentieth of modelled paths, 2028-30) the way the restorer gives one ("the clouds above"), with "But" doing the work of the report's "However"; the closing decision is unchanged.

Fact notes carried in: the case's wording is "has been willing to take" thoughtful risks, so "has taken ... all her career" is gone; the $90,000 v $140,000 pair is tagged as the world where stocks and yields move independently (where recessions push yields down with stocks it is about $85,000 v $135,000; the $50,000 gap and the $15,000 hold in both, and that second pair is left out for length). Every number from V47 is kept: $15,000, $145,000, 2042, three-quarters, the twentieth of paths, 2028-30, $90,000, $140,000, 2031 set-aside. Not yet checked with GPTZero.
