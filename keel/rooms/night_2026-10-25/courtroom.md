## 1. Kai prosecutes (hard issues only)
**v3.1.** At 489 it is over our own 470 cushion. "Any rise stays with Laura" overclaims: a recovery after a fall past
2031 lifts the gift in 23% of model paths (L-07 rung 2, MODEL). The floor is defined twice ("that sum", then "the
2032 ETF's projected payout"). "Each payment's cash comes from a maturity, not a sale" sits awkwardly next to its own
admission that early maturities "wait in a short-term Treasury ETF". **Superseded.**

**P0.** The new line passes. "The rest, the interest that ETF will earn, buys a global index fund" is clear on the
second read, and it is true at the projected yield. That is an identity in the model: the remainder minus the ETF's
cost equals the ETF's gain to maturity ($32,064 of a $150,000 remainder on the 22 Oct curve, MODEL). "Will earn" is a
projection stated flat, and it stays flat, because "projected floor" and "the ETF payouts can leave one short" already
carry the caveat. Its truth also depends on the ETF's monthly payouts being reinvested, and P0's coupon sentence says
they are. **Two hard issues.** (a) P0 says "the 2027 deposit goes entirely to the payments". WInS cash *is* the 2027
deposit ($300k). Yet the frozen book holds VT (167 sh x $158.83 = about $26.5k, about 9%) and IBTM doing floor duty
(4,503 sh x $21.65 = about $97.5k, about 32%) (fills × quantities from `competition/COMPETITION.md` section 8, on
about $303.4k). Once "Our WInS portfolio shows this allocation, scaled down" is cut, a judge reading the IPS beside the
portfolio sees a contradiction. The Writing Room was right that the sentence is not *required*, but it is what keeps
two locked things consistent. (b) "set it aside from the half Laura keeps, then the contribution above the floor" is
missing a "from", and it reads as garbled.

**The optional tail: "If every share fell to zero, Laura would lose only that interest."** On its own it is true: the
fund's cost is the projected interest, nominal, and the remainder comes back through the floor. Together with the
next paragraph, though, it is false. The gap left by a lasting fall in yields is set aside "from the half Laura
keeps", and that half is the stock half. If shares are at zero and yields have fallen for good, the coupon gap (about
$21k at -2 points, MODEL, number sheet 16 Oct) has no stock money to come from, and the IPS does not say what pays it
next. A faculty judge would ask exactly that. The tail also says the interest line again from the other side, and
Ray's cutting rule removes whole repeated ideas. To fit P0 under 470 (469 + 12 = 481) it would have to replace 12 or
more words, which means the annual review (8) plus "renamed rather than rebuilt" (4), and both of those do other jobs.
**Verdict: it lands, and it stays out.** It belongs in the Final Report or in Q&A, said together with the answer to the
joint case.

**A, Press Day.** It pays for its motif with the two cuts, and both of them hurt. First, "the floor" now first appears
in "lowers the floor, not a payment", before it is defined. Second, "reinvested coupons" in P1 now has no home. The
print-run line is a colon reveal wrapped in an extended metaphor, which is the exact pattern Ray says reads as AI. It
ranks "hardest in January" with no number shown (3:1 in L-03, MODEL), and "press day" is really two days (Jan 2027, and
Jan 2028 for any gap). "The dullest thing Laura owns" is the best closer of the night, and it is true.

**B, Caplet XR.** "Each payment's cash is released by a maturity, not a sale" is narrowly false for 2038 and 2039. The
locked notes on the Feb-2037 and May-2038 bonds say their proceeds wait 10.5 and 7.5 months in a short-term Treasury
ETF, and that ETF is then sold to pay. Because B cut the parking sentence, it hides the one place it does sell. The
kicker "No payment Treasury is sold to raise cash" survives only if the short-term ETF does not count as a payment
Treasury. Two more problems: a medicine image for an artists' residency, and a pitch that spends 11 of its 49 words on
the simile.

**C, Pre-Registered.** It has the same two cuts as A. "Pre-registered" appears four times, at 2 strict words each.
The Library's charge still stands: Wharton *forces* the lock ("may not revise its investment strategy"), so a judge can
answer "you were made to". "Does not improve the result. It makes the range believable" is the "not X, it's Y" shape
that teachers and detectors flag. Even so, "Her 2031 range follows a rule written now" is the strongest pitch line on
trial.

**D, Maturity Match.** It is P0 plus a name, so it inherits P0's two hard issues. The name describes half the plan
(the payments), says nothing about the floor or the fund, and is the name a rival would reach for too, because cash-flow
matching is textbook. As a noun it can act as the subject ("Maturity Match buys"), so Kai's L-06 subject charge does not
apply here.

**Did the A/B/C cuts hurt? Yes. The coupon cut hurts more.** The objective-2 preview is P0's definition of
"responsible", the twin of "By high certainty we mean" (the VOICE.md "we take X to mean Y" test). The coupon sentence is

and "managing the operating reserve's asset composition over time", and the interest line now relies on that
sentence. The packages paid 33 words of substance for their motif, and on v3.1, unlike v3, there was no slack to pay
