**Nora, to her dad.** She went first, because she always goes first.

**Nora:** Baba, there's a woman, Laura. She's promised ten payments of fifty thousand dollars, one every January from
2033. She's got three hundred thousand now and another hundred and fifty the year after – no wait, the second lot
depends on her book and her speaking work, so it might come late. So the first money, all of it, goes on the ten
payments. Not shares. Each payment gets its own government bond that matures before the date, so the cash just turns
up. Nobody sells anything. It's like quoting a job – you order the tiles before you promise the date, not after. And
if the first lot doesn't quite cover it, the gap is the first thing the second lot pays for.

**Kai:** "Just turns up." Say why not shares.

**Nora:** Because shares can be down in the month you need them. A bond that matures on a date isn't down on that date.
Next.

A little over fifty seconds. Her dad, apparently, said "You'd order the grout last," which Nora took as a pass.

**Felix, to his dad.** He stood up for this. He does that when he's afraid of being imprecise.

**Felix:** Dad, "high certainty". It doesn't mean nothing can go wrong. It means I can point at each payment and say

**Jun:** And the government?

**Felix:** One issuer, on purpose. No dollar asset is safer. He asked that too.

**Amara:** I love "which bond owns it". Take it as a pin-up line.

**Tess:** It's literally true, and it's seven words.

**Felix:** Thank you. Can I stop standing now?

**Kai:** Right, Minh, picture the leftover money in a fund holding thousands of companies. Now a crash. A crash can't

**Tess:** Dad, you know when the director signs off the set and then wants a new wall? Same. Once the plan's in, we

**Kai:** He'll say, "More shares, more money."

**Tess:** He did. I said yes, it'd probably raise the average. But it could break a number she's already told people.

**Jun, to his mum.** He had been waiting, and it showed.

**Jun:** Mum, say interest rates drop and stay down. Two things go wrong. One: before we've bought the bonds, they
cost more, so Laura's gap gets bigger, and that comes out of the second deposit, not a payment. Two: after we own
them, the coupons they pay can only be reinvested at the lower rate, so they grow less. That's the only bit that can

**Tess:** That number isn't in the IPS.

**Jun:** It's in my table.

**Amara:** Your mum doesn't want a basis point.

**Jun:** She asked about her loan. She said, when rates fall that's good news. And I said, no wait, it's good if you
owe money. It's bad if you have to buy a future.

Silence on the call for about two seconds, the longest of the night.

**Amara:** Say that again.

**Jun:** It's good for someone who owes and bad for someone who has to buy a future. Laura's the second one. Why?

**Amara:** That's the sentence. I've been trying to write that sentence for a week.

**Jun:** It's just what I said to my mum.

**Amara:** You're allowed to have said it.

**Amara:** The number's in the Final Report.

**Jun:** The number's in the Final Report.

Kai ate the last dumpling. Felix ate two sesame sweets and said Por Por would know. Nora said it was 00:21 and that

It was only after we hung up that I looked at the page. Six people, six minutes, one rule. The IPS says "A lasting
