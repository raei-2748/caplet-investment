## 1. The question
Case :110-116: a "meaningful personal contribution may signal that the residency is financially viable, demonstrate
Laura's commitment ... and make potential co-sponsors more willing to contribute", but promising "more than she can
ultimately contribute" could "damage her credibility". The case is describing a signalling problem, so test the plan
the way economists test a signal: is it **costly**, **verifiable** and **committed**?

## 2. Real evidence (checked 26 Oct 2026)
- **Seed money works, and works big.** List & Lucking-Reiley (2002, *Journal of Political Economy* 110(1): 215-233),
  a university capital campaign field experiment: "Increasing seed money from 10 percent to 67 percent of the campaign
  goal produced a nearly sixfold increase in contributions"; a refund promise raised giving "a more modest 20 percent".
  (Abstract, poverty-action.org publication page, read 26 Oct.)
- **That a lead commitment exists matters more than its size.** Karlan & List (2007, *American Economic Review*):
  announcing a match "increases the revenue per solicitation by nineteen percent" and the chance to give "by 22
  percent", but "larger match ratios (2:1 and 3:1) relative to smaller match ratios (1:1) have no additional impact".
  (J-PAL evaluation page, read 26 Oct.)
- **Insiders' own money is the classic signal.** Leland & Pyle (1977, *Journal of Finance* 32(2): 371-387): an
  entrepreneur's willingness to put their own wealth into the project signals its quality. Citation checked
  (EconPapers); the one-line gist is the standard textbook reading, not re-read at source (UNVERIFIED at source).
- Limits: both field experiments are mail and phone donors, not institutional co-sponsors (Courtroom asked to roast).

## 4. The finding (for the synthesis memo)
**The bottom of the range does the signalling; the top does almost none.** Field evidence says donors respond to the
existence of a committed lead amount, not its size, so stretching the top buys little and is the only part that can
break Laura's credibility. The plan should treat the floor as spoken for from 2031 (never a reserve of last resort),
pay coupon gaps from dedication and the 2028 deposit instead, and present the top as a rule, not a promise.
Investing knowledge underneath (north star 4, uncited in the IPS): costly signalling, seed-money and matching
evidence, insider ownership, commitment devices. Supports D1b (quote the bottom with a margin) and argues against D1a.
