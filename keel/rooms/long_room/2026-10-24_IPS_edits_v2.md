Laura's co-sponsors may fund the building without paying to run it, so Root & Branch funds the running costs first. Its roots, matched Treasuries, cover ten $50,000 payments, and more Treasuries back her minimum contribution. Its branch, a global index fund, grows her gift above that minimum.

**IPS (494 strict)**

This policy governs Laura Gao's $450,000 for the Taiwan creative residency she opens in 2033. Its first objective is to fund ten $50,000 operating payments, each January from 2033 through 2042, with high certainty from this money alone. We match each payment with a Treasury bond or defined-maturity Treasury ETF maturing before it, so each payment depends mainly on the US government paying its debts. The second objective is a facility contribution with a floor Laura can promise co-sponsors and growth above it.

With her living costs met elsewhere and no withdrawals due before 2033, Laura's ability to bear risk beyond the payments is high. Her tolerance for a missed payment is low, since the artists would bear the loss. Because her 2028 deposit depends on publishing and speaking income, the 2027 deposit goes entirely to the payments.

Root & Branch secures the roots (the payments and a facility floor) before investing in the branch. The 2027 deposit funds the latest payments first, since their cost moves most with interest rates. Any gap left in January is the first use of the 2028 deposit. From the 2028 remainder, just enough goes into a 2032 Treasury ETF to grow back to the full remainder; that sum is Laura's facility floor. The rest forms the branch, a global index fund of thousands of companies. The roots rely on one issuer by design, since no dollar asset is safer.

In 2031 Laura tells co-sponsors a range from the floor, quoted at the 2032 ETF's projected payout rounded down, to the floor plus half the fund's value then. In 2033 she contributes the floor plus half the fund's value, capped at that top, so she never gives more than she quoted. A range built to hold protects her credibility better than a narrower one she might miss.

Moving floor money into stocks would raise the expected gift; we refuse, because it could break a number Laura has quoted. One yield sets both the ladder's January price and what its coupons earn later, so a single fall works twice. Inflation or a stronger Taiwan dollar raises costs in Taiwan. The half Laura keeps backs the payments first, then cost overruns, then her own plans.

Until 2033 the portfolio is held without trading beyond investing the 2028 deposit. Coupons are reinvested in the bond that paid them; early maturities wait in a short-term Treasury ETF. Each payment's cash comes from a maturity, not a sale. Each January we review the plan with Laura, replacing any holding that becomes unavailable with a Treasury maturing earlier. In 2033 the payment Treasuries become the operating reserve: five Treasury ETFs that close one a year to 2037, then five bonds to 2042. Before the 2033 gift, we price any gap the ladder will leave at that day's yields and set it aside from the half Laura keeps, then the contribution above the floor. Our WInS portfolio shows this allocation, scaled down.
