**The move.** Write the sceptic's question in their words, then answer it in the first word, and let the next clause
turn against your own sales pitch ("Yes, even if the sales materials suggest otherwise"). **Where it sits in the
reading:** mostly dry. `../../voice/reading/SYNTHESIS.md` section 4 and Synthesis 5 item 2 already say "the objection
first, in the reader's words". What is new is the one-word answer and the clause aimed at the seller, which is us.

**So (checked beside the source).** Two things. (1) V47's line "Banks sell this as a capital-protected note. Ours swaps
the bank's option for shares, which can't expire worthless" is **true**: the SEC describes the same build (a
zero-coupon bond plus an option). (2) The SEC names two costs of the bank's version that XR doesn't carry: the
protection rests on the bank's credit, and the buyer gives up dividends. XR's floor is Treasuries, and its fund pays
dividends. **What it does not show:** that XR has no costs. Ours has fund fees and the growth the floor gives up (about
$15,000 on average by 2042, A26, MODEL). A flat yes is only honest if we say ours too.

**Breaks first.** The IPS form has no headings, so the question can't be a heading; it has to be a sentence. And a
question-and-answer in an IPS can read as a gimmick if it's used more than once.
