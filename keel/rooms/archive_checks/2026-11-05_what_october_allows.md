## The twelve ideas (as the checker saw them)
C1 We decide in advance where the plan is allowed to be wrong: only in money no payment depends on.
C2 If a Treasury holding pays out less than a payment needs, the shortfall is paid from Laura's world-share fund.
C3 The safe asset is whichever one matches the debt: for fixed-dollar payments, a Treasury maturing just before each
payment; cash and inflation-linked bonds are both risky for Laura.
C4 We rebalance the bonds as rates move, so their duration keeps matching the payments'.
C5 Laura's income from books, speaking and licensing rises and falls with the stock market.
C6 The Treasury funds give up a fixed payout (they pay NAV) for being sellable any day, which an odd-lot bond is not.
C7 One holding does two jobs: the 2032 fund backs the first payment and sets the 2031 minimum figure.
C8 Inflation-linked Treasuries protect the ten payments from rising prices, so we hold some.
C9 If yields fall before the bonds are bought, the 2028 deposit tops up the ladder first; the facility shrinks, never a payment.
C10 Each of the ten payments is guaranteed to the dollar.
C11 Later bonds' coupons pay earlier payments, so fewer bonds are needed.
C12 Laura's 2031 minimum figure is a fixed $150,000.
