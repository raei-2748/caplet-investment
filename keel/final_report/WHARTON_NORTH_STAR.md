## 0. The Final Report instructions (verbatim, filed Mon 9 Nov)

> FINAL REPORT REQUIREMENTS
>
> The Final Report is the culmination of your team's work in the competition. It should evaluate how effectively your
> team implemented its investment strategy, applied it to Laura's financial goals, and developed its final
> recommendations. The report should draw on your prior deliverables, final WInS portfolio, and analysis without
> repeating the content of your Investment Policy Statement (IPS) or Trading Notes Analysis.
>
> There is no single correct strategy or recommendation. Teams may reach different conclusions about portfolio
> construction, risk, funding certainty, the operating reserve, the facility contribution, and financial flexibility.
> Your report will be evaluated based on the quality of your analysis, evidence, assumptions, judgment, and
> communication.
>
> How the Deliverables Fit Together. Each deliverable serves a distinct purpose, but evaluators will consider all
> three together.
> - Trading Notes Analysis: Demonstrate how selected investment decisions reflected your team's strategy during the
>   trading period.
> - Investment Policy Statement: Establish your team's investment strategy and decision-making framework.
> - Final Report: Evaluate how effectively your team implemented its strategy and present recommendations for Laura.
>
> The strategy established in your IPS should remain consistent in the Final Report. The Final Report is not an
> opportunity to create a new strategy or revise the IPS. Evaluators will assess whether your Trading Notes Analysis,
> final WInS portfolio, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations align with your strategy and Laura's financial
> goals.
>
> Minimum Requirements. Your Final Report must:
> - Include all required sections.
> - Meet the minimum and maximum page requirements.
> - Actual holdings.
> - Use only permitted investments.
> - Address Laura's financial goals and circumstances.
> - Demonstrate consistency with the strategy established in the IPS.
> - Follow all formatting, citation, and submission requirements.
>
> Reports that do not meet these minimum requirements may be removed from semifinal consideration. Meeting the
> minimum requirements makes a report eligible for evaluation. It does not guarantee a strong evaluation.
>
> Permitted Investments. For all long-term portfolio modeling, teams may use only the following investment types:
> stocks available on WInS; exchange-traded funds available on WInS; government or Treasury bonds available on WInS.
> The following are not permitted: margin trading; short selling; stock-secured debt; cryptocurrency; derivatives,
> including options, futures, and swaps; individual TIPS; individual STRIPS; any investment other than the permitted
> investments listed above.
>
> Required Format and Submission
> - Submit the report through SurveyMonkey Apply by the stated deadline.
> - Submit the report as a PDF file no larger than 5 MB.
> - Use 12-point Times New Roman font.
> - Double-space the report.
> - Use 1-inch margins.
> - Include page numbers in the bottom-left corner.
> - Cite all sources, images, media, and generative AI use in APA format.
> - Include a separate References page.
> - Do not include external links that evaluators are expected to open.
>
> Tables and graphics may be single-spaced when needed for readability. Graphics should support the narrative and
> clarify the team's analysis rather than distract from it.
>
> Required Report Length: Title Page 1 page (minimum and maximum); Portfolio Implementation 2 pages minimum, 3 maximum;
> Integrated Recommendation and Reflection 6 pages minimum, 8 maximum; Total, excluding References, 9 pages minimum,
> 12 maximum.
>
> Section 1: Title Page (one page). Include: official team name; advisor's first and last name; finalized team member
> names; team WInS username. The information must match your official team roster.
>
> Section 2: Portfolio Implementation (two to three pages). Present your final WInS portfolio and evaluate how
> effectively it implemented the investment strategy established in your IPS. Explain how the portfolio's
> construction, allocation, diversification, and most important holdings reflect your strategy and support Laura's
> financial goals.
>
> Include a table listing every holding in your final WInS portfolio. The table must include each investment's ticker
> or identifier, name, asset type, amount allocated, and percentage of the portfolio. [Example table, "for
> illustrative purposes only": Ticker | Name of Investment | Asset Type | Amount | % of Portfolio, e.g. "532281 T-Bond
> 4.500% 15-May-2038 Treasury Bond $90,000 30%", "N/A Cash Cash $15,000 5%", "Total $300,000 100%".]
>
> Your table should reflect the composition and allocation of your final WInS portfolio, expressed using the original
> $300,000 investment. The total must equal $300,000 and 100%. Do not use the ending market value of your WInS
> portfolio or incorporate WInS gains and losses into Laura's long-term financial projections.
>
> Use your accompanying narrative to evaluate:
> - How the portfolio's construction and allocation reflect your strategy
> - How diversification and risk considerations influenced your decisions
> - The intended roles of the most important holdings or asset classes
> - How effectively the portfolio aligns with your IPS and Laura's financial goals
>
> Focus on the holdings and portfolio decisions most important to understanding your strategy. You do not need to
> explain every individual holding.
>
> Section 3: Integrated Recommendation and Reflection (six to eight pages). Apply the investment strategy established
> in your IPS, as demonstrated through your final WInS portfolio, to develop an integrated recommendation for Laura.
> Support your recommendation with reasonable assumptions and analysis.
>
> Your recommendation must:
> - Explain how your strategy supports the ten-year operating commitment
> - Recommend an appropriate operating reserve
> - Recommend a responsible facility contribution while preserving financial flexibility
> - Provide a credible dollar range that Laura could communicate to potential co-sponsors in 2031
> - Explain the level of confidence associated with that range and the factors that could affect it
> - Provide language Laura could use when communicating with prospective co-sponsors
>
> For your long-term analysis, begin with Laura's $300,000 investment at the beginning of 2027 and add the additional
> $150,000 contribution at the beginning of 2028. Do not add WInS gains or losses to Laura's projected portfolio
> value.
>
> Clearly identify important assumptions, uncertainties, and limitations. Explain what Laura or her asset management
> team should monitor as 2031 and 2033 approach and how changing conditions could affect your recommendations.
>
> You are not expected to: estimate the facility's total cost or calculate the complete funding gap; prepare a
> construction budget or detailed business plan; account for Laura's personal taxes; address the legal or regulatory
> requirements associated with establishing the residency.
>
> Team Reflection. Conclude your report by explaining how your team's research, collaboration, disagreements,
> decision-making, and learning influenced its final recommendation. Focus on the overall competition experience and
> do not repeat the analysis presented in your Trading Notes Analysis.
>
> Evaluation Criteria. Evaluators will consider all three deliverables together using five high-level criteria.
> - Investment Strategy: The team establishes and applies a clear and coherent strategy that reflects mid-term and
>   long-term thinking, appropriate diversification, and consistency across the IPS, Trading Notes Analysis, and Final

> - Client Knowledge and Objectives: The team demonstrates a meaningful understanding of Laura's circumstances and

> - Portfolio Analysis: The team uses quantitative and qualitative analysis to evaluate its final WInS portfolio and

> - Creativity and Presentation: The team presents a compelling, well-organized, and authentic recommendation.
>   Supporting data, projections, graphics, and co-sponsor communication are used purposefully and effectively.
>
> Final Standard. Addressing every required element is necessary, but it does not by itself demonstrate strong
> analysis. The strongest reports will connect the team's established strategy, portfolio decisions, analysis,
> assumptions, and understanding of Laura's goals into a cohesive and well-supported recommendation.
>
> There is no single correct answer. Your goal is to demonstrate that your team can apply its investment strategy
> thoughtfully and responsibly to a realistic client scenario.

## 1. The five criteria (SMApply deliverables page, unchanged since 27 Sep; the only marking scheme)

> **Investment Strategy:** Presents a clear and creative investment thesis; demonstrates disciplined planning across
> Laura's changing time horizons and cash-flow needs; uses appropriate diversification; and maintains consistency with
> the team's IPS.
>
> **Client Knowledge and Objectives:** Tailors the strategy to Laura's circumstances, priorities, risk considerations,
> and residency goals; demonstrates a thoughtful understanding of the client; and presents recommendations that can
> earn her confidence.
>
> **Portfolio Analysis:** Demonstrates understanding and effective use of investment concepts and tools; integrates
> quantitative and qualitative analysis; and uses reasonable assumptions and projections to evaluate funding
> reliability, the facility contribution, and financial flexibility under varying market outcomes.
>
> **Articulation of Competition Experience:** Demonstrates teamwork, communication, and learning; clearly explains the
> team's research and decision-making process; and reflects meaningfully on the team's growth and response to
> challenges.
>
> **Creativity and Presentation:** Presents a compelling, well-organized narrative in an authentic team voice; uses
> data effectively to support conclusions; demonstrates original thinking and meaningful reflection; and communicates
> Laura's potential facility contribution and investment uncertainty clearly and credibly to prospective co-sponsors.

## 3. What judges look for (Competition Guide, verbatim)

> The competition recognizes thoughtful strategy, research and analysis, client understanding, disciplined decisions,
> management of risk and uncertainty, communication, and creativity.
>
> Judges want to understand not only what your team decided to do, but also the reasoning, assumptions, and
> tradeoffs behind those decisions.
>
> The goal is not simply to identify investments you think will perform well. The goal is to develop a cohesive
> strategy in which your investment decisions work together to support the client's objectives across a range of
> possible market outcomes.

## 6. What this means, in Keel's words (our reading, not Wharton's)

- **Since Mon 9 Nov the instructions (section 0) settle the shape:** two sections, 2-3 and 6-8 pages, about 2,700-2,900
  words of prose in all, the Team Reflection last; the page budget and the new requirements (the holdings table at cost,
  monitoring as 2031 and 2033 approach, liquidity, no WInS return as evidence, no repeating the IPS or Trading Notes) are
  in `CASE_MAP.md` section E. The bullets below were written before they arrived and still hold.

- The Final Report's job is to **evaluate and recommend**, not to restate the IPS and not to redesign it. Its four
  subjects are named three times: the operating commitment, the facility contribution, financial flexibility, and
  communication with co-sponsors, each "across varying outcomes".
- "You may identify decisions you would make differently": an honest list of what Caplet would do differently is
  invited, as long as the strategy itself stands.
- The portfolio is "evidence of the decisions your team made": the WInS book is shown as implementation, never as
  performance.
- "Explain why each investment belongs": every holding (the ten Treasuries, the fund maturing late 2032, VT) needs
  its reason and its job for Laura.
- "Authentic team voice" and "teamwork, communication, and learning" are scored: real Caplet events only.
- "Clearly and credibly to prospective co-sponsors": the co-sponsor communication is judged as communication, so it
  needs a real reader test, not only correct numbers.

## 7. The daily test (every note, card, number and plan decision)

1. **Criterion.** Which of the five criteria does it serve, by name? If none, it ranks low in the store.
2. **Purpose.** Which part of the report does it serve: Section 2 (the portfolio's implementation) or one of Section
   3's parts (the commitment and reserve, the facility contribution and flexibility, the 2031 range and its confidence,
   words for co-sponsors, assumptions and monitoring, the Team Reflection)? If none, it ranks low.
3. **Varying outcomes.** Does it hold, or say what happens, when markets go badly as well as well?
4. **Consistency.** Is it consistent with the submitted IPS, the Trading Notes Analysis and the frozen WInS
   portfolio? Does it avoid redesigning the strategy after the fact?
5. **Reasoning.** Does it show the reasoning, assumptions and trade-offs, not only the decision?
6. **The tips.** Does it lead with the strategy, know the client, keep WInS results out of Laura's projections, and
   explain why each investment belongs?
7. **Limits.** Only WInS-listed stocks, ETFs and Treasuries; no TIPS or STRIPS; no facility budget, taxes or Taiwan
   legal work.
