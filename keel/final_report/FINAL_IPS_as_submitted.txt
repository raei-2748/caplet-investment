Elevator Pitch

Laura’s career is already a giant stock she can’t sell. So we hedge it! We’ll fund the 10 operating commitments with a Treasury ladder, immune to markets. Yet another Treasury fund will decide the floor of her facility contribution, and a world stock ETF will determine the ceiling.

IPS

Dear Laura,

You’d think that someone with your taste for thoughtful risk and 6 years to wait would usually own shares. So, naturally, that was our plan – until we stopped and considered the actual nature of a writer’s income. In good years the advances come, and the speaking fees, and the licences. In a recession they come later and smaller, and during that same period, shares fall and co-sponsors cut their budgets. In effect your career is a large holding of shares, and one you cannot sell. And so, we believe, your portfolio should be a counterbalance to that – definitely not a contributor.

You asked that the payments be met with a ‘high degree of certainty’, so we decided to take that one step further – barring a US default, none of your 10 operating payments will depend on a share price, or on anybody else’s money, when they fall due. The money for the operating commitment will go straight into Treasuries, because it’s fixed and non-negotiable, and only money you haven’t promised yet goes into shares, where it can grow.

In January 2027 we’ll use your first deposit to buy 10 Treasuries (T-Bonds or iShares iBonds Term Treasury ETFs, whichever works best for each year), each maturing just before it’s needed. Held to the end, these pay what they say – no price fluctuations for us to fear. At current yields, this would cost roughly $300,000 – in other words, your entire first deposit. But worry not – our plan isn’t dependent on yields staying put! Even if yields fall and the total cost of the ladder ends up being above $300,000, we’ll simply complete it with the 2028 deposit.

The building is a different matter. Most of the 2028 deposit will fund a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, whose payout is the bare minimum you’ll be able to promise your co-sponsors without doubt. The rest will go into a world share fund of thousands of companies – VT, Vanguard Total World Stock ETF – and its returns will help determine your maximum facility contribution. That means markets will be able to increase or decrease the top of your planned band as we get closer to 2031, but the bottom will remain constant, built on the ever-reliable Treasuries. Importantly for you, half of the value of the shares will be promised to no one. That is your flexibility – for building costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar or a co-sponsor falling short. Before you meet co-sponsors in 2031, the other half will move into Treasuries, so whatever figure you choose is money you already own.

From 2033 the Treasuries are the operating reserve. There are only 2 possible reasons we would change our plan – a missed deposit or a Treasury default. A fall in the market is not one of them.

You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that’s your job. With these promises, ours is to take none.

Yours,
Caplet
