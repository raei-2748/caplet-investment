**One word to watch.** The draft says "if the second deposit is *insufficient*". The IPS's reason is a *missed* deposit, and the
draft itself shows that a $75,000 deposit still funds all ten payments. A reader could take "insufficient" as a third
reason to change the plan. "Reconsider the plan if the second deposit has not arrived by 31 March, or is too small to
complete the ladder" keeps to the IPS and says what "insufficient" means.

**The $36,774 of extra Treasuries** is new since the IPS. The draft frames it the right way: "The principle established in
our IPS remains unchanged. What has changed is our understanding of how much it costs to honour it." It also fits IPS
l.11 ("one step further"). No conflict.

**The Trading Notes.** "In our Trading Notes, we described VT as the investment in which we allowed ourselves to be wrong" is
a fair paraphrase of the VT note ("VT is where we allow ourselves to be wrong").

## 5. Are the numbers consistent with each other?

Yes. Every identity holds:
- Table 1: $268,652.18 + $26,524.61 + $3,513.79 + $1,309.42 = $300,000.00. The weights are 89.55 + 8.84 + 1.17 + 0.44 = 100.00%.
- Table 2: every row sums to $50,000. The rounded columns sum to $442,929 and $57,071 against totals of $442,930 and $57,070, which is
  rounding, and the note says totals use unrounded figures.
- The second deposit: $2,388 + $36,774 = $39,162; $150,000 - $39,162 = $110,838 = $81,244 + $29,594.
- $300,000 + $39,162 = $339,162. At a $75,000 deposit, $75,000 - $39,162 = $35,838.
- Table 3: $130,471 - $123,036 = $7,435 and $100,477 - $76,556 = $23,921, as the text says.
- The range ($95,000 to $115,000) matches Laura's words, the "$95,000 facility contribution" if shares were worth nothing,
  the 40% case ($95,000 to $105,000) and the "ceiling of at least $115,000".

- Dates agree throughout: Jan 2027, Jan 2028, the 31 March check, Jan 2031, each December from 2032 to 2041, and "sixteen years".

**One sentence that reads as if it clashes with Table 3, though it does not.** "Even if her shares were worth nothing, both
deposits and our stated assumptions would still support ... a $95,000 facility contribution". Table 3 meanwhile shows 1.5% of
simulations below $95,000. Both are true. The $95,000 is on the 6 November curve; in the simulations, yields move before
January 2028 and change what $81,244 buys. Adding "on the 6 November 2026 curve" to that sentence removes the apparent clash.
(The earlier "This is an illustration, not a promise" covers it in principle.)
