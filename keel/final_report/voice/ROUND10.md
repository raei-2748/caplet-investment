## Understanding (the check questions)

- **3e: all twelve answers right.** Every reader named a US default and a 2028 deposit that never comes or comes far
  smaller, said a fall in share prices could not break a payment ("even to zero", four readings of the turned
  passages), and gave "about 2% of what the late-2032 fund pays" as what would make a stated figure fall short. The one
  fund was heard as one fund in every reading.
- **3b: all twelve answers right on the figure and both reasons.** About $115,000 on 6 November 2026 rates; meaningful
  as $115,000 of her own $450,000, owned before she asks and named first; responsible because nothing is promised before
  it is owned and the payments never lean on it; about $21,200 kept for overruns, a stronger Taiwan dollar or a
  co-sponsor falling short. Two readers on T's passages also carried "rounded down, the excess to her flexibility".

## What the readers heard

- **The turn as the closing line's gloss worked for meaning in all six readings** ("made the share-price point stick";
  "let me picture shares going to zero leaving every payment intact"), and for voice when it sat in dashes as an aside
  in a sentence that was already talking (S2: "Put the shares at zero – rub out every pencil line, since from January
  2028 they're the only pencil left – and the page still reads"). Where the same image came at the end of a passage laid
  out as definitions ("For the ten payments, failing means ... For a stated figure, failing means ..."), readers heard a
  risk register and the image could not save it (T2). This is round 5-6's pattern again: a turn that is the only place
  its point is made, glossed in its own sentence, adds voice; a turn on top of a checklist does not.
- **"Ink only goes one way" helped all six readings understand why the figure is rounded down**, and S4's gloss in dashes
  ("she can always state more later, never take a stated figure back") was heard as the most spoken of the 3b passages.
  T4 lost for its shape, not its image: a slogan pair ("a size, not a promise"), "It is meaningful because ... It is
  responsible because" as a template, and two citations stacked in one sentence.
- **What the readers marked down, every time:** the plan's own shorthand used as if the reader knew it ("the room under
  the figure", "fund costs beyond the fees"), definitions opening each paragraph, slogan pairs, and stacked citations.
  Reader 3 heard round 9's closing line, "No fall in share prices can.", as clipped and leaning on the sentence before
  when it followed a definition-led paragraph; in S2, where it followed a spoken sentence ("It's the risk we'd point to
  first"), no reader faulted it.
- **What separates the top from the bottom (all three readers):** a person in the passage ("It's the risk we'd point to
  first", "What makes it meaningful is whose money it is and when it shows up", "We'd recommend"), contractions, a
  concession said out loud, the dash that turns a thought; at the bottom, an editor's or auditor's report about Laura,
  correct and clear, with no one talking to her.

## What changes (for SKELETON 3e and 3b, CONCEIT V2.2 and the Fri 27 Nov writers)

1. **3e keeps its one turn, in the place tested today:** as the gloss of the short closing line of the first note, in
   dashes, inside a sentence that is already talking (S2's shape). Round 9's rule ("if it loses again, 3e is plain")
   did not trigger: the turn held 4 of 6 against plain's 3 of 6, and won by three places within the stronger writer. It
   stays the only place in 3e that the shares-at-zero point is made, and it is dated "from January 2028".
2. **3b keeps "ink only goes one way" as its one turn**, glossed in dashes with what it shows and why (rounded down, the
   excess to her flexibility, half the shares pencil on purpose). Its claim must stay true to the plan: she can state
   more only by choosing to give from her flexibility later, never by raising a stated range (card Q154, Thu 19 Nov:
   the plan never raises its range).
3. **The plan's shorthand stays out of the prose or is glossed once in plain words:** "the room under the figure" is
   said as "what is left under the figure once it is rounded down", "fund costs beyond the fees" as "the funds' running
   costs beyond their stated fee" (WORDS keeps the short names for the notes and tables).
4. **No paragraph opens on a defined term** ("For the ten payments, failing means ..."); open on the thing that could
   happen, said by someone. No slogan pairs; at most one citation per sentence; "meaningful" and "responsible" argued in
   the order a person would argue them, never as a "because ... because" template.
5. **Writer variance is the larger effect.** S and T read the same diet and the same brief and landed nine places apart
   on average. On Fri 27 Nov each part gets at least two fresh writers and the main session keeps the passage the blind
   readers rank higher, as the plan already says (SKELETON, "Book 17's amendments").
