# Voice round 13: one long explanation that holds its reader (Sun 22 Nov 2026)

You are writing one continuous piece of 2,000 to 2,400 words for Team Caplet, six school students in Sydney entered in
the Wharton Global High School Investment Competition. Their client is Laura Gao (a case, not a real client of
theirs). The piece is practice for their Final Report, not the report itself.

## 1. Read first
1. `/home/user/caplet-investment/keel/final_report/voice/CORPUS.md`, whole: everything the team has written itself.
   Read its IPS (a letter to Laura) twice, the second time slowly, as if aloud. This is the voice to write forward
   from. Do not learn from section 4 ("Not their voice").
2. `/home/user/caplet-investment/keel/final_report/voice/FACTS_PLAIN.md` (version 3): the facts of the case and the
   plan. Every figure comes from there.
Do not open any other file in the repository.

## 2. Who reads the piece
Each passage will be read **on its own**, by an intelligent reader who has not read the IPS or any other part of the
report, and who knows only this about the case:

> Laura Gao, a writer and illustrator with a business degree, is planning a creative residency for artists in Taiwan,
> opening in 2033. She puts $300,000 into an investment portfolio at the start of 2027 and $150,000 at the start of
> 2028; that second sum comes from her book advances, speaking fees and licensing. Nothing else goes in or out before
> 2033. From the portfolio she must pay $50,000 at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042 (ten payments) to run the
> residency, and they must be met "with a high degree of certainty". At the start of 2033 she sets money aside for
> those payments (an "operating reserve"), and beyond that she wants to give as much as is responsible towards the
> residency's building (her "facility contribution") while keeping flexibility as the project develops. In 2031 she
> tells co-sponsors (other funders of the building) a range in dollars for that contribution, and how confident she
> is. Overpromising would hurt her credibility. A team of school students advises her; it may use US Treasury bonds,
> Treasury funds, shares and share funds.

Nothing else. If a passage uses something the reader has not been told, the reader has nothing to go on.

The reader reads the piece once, straight through, at a normal pace, and is then asked what they remember.

## 3. The piece
Explain Laura's plan from the money she puts in to the figure she tells co-sponsors in 2031: what each deposit buys and
why then, how the ten payments are made certain and what that costs, what goes to the building and how it is decided,
what she tells co-sponsors in 2031 and why it is a range, and what is promised to no one and what could test it. Write
as the team ("we"), as its IPS does, for Wharton's judges as readers of a report (never address "the judges" or "you,
the reader"; never copy the IPS's sentences). Use only the figures the explanation needs. Headings are allowed but not
required. 2,000 to 2,400 words.

## 4. Output
Write to the file named in your instructions, exactly in this shape:

=== PIECE ===
(the piece)
=== NOTES ===
(five to eight sentences, written after the piece: what you did to hold a reader across the whole length, and where
you think a reader is most likely to lose the thread)

Do not commit, and do not edit any other file.
