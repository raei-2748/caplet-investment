# Voice round 6: reading 3

Reader 3. I read like a busy investment professional: I skim first, then read closely only what holds my attention.
I read the IPS twice, the second time slowly. Its writers sound young and sure of themselves. They talk straight to
Laura, enjoy a cheeky aside ("But worry not", "naturally", "ever-reliable", "Fair enough"), use dashes for a turn,
write numbers as numerals ("10 Treasuries", "6 years"), and let a sentence run a little uneven rather than polish it.
Their flourishes are short and colloquial, and the one crafted turn comes at the end. They never use an extended
metaphor.

### Passage 1

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It moves the way the IPS does. It makes a practical admission, gives a quick list of virtues, says "simply"
  ignore the noise, then "The catch is", so it reads like a student explaining a trade and owning its cost. Only "terminal
  payout is a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par" sounds a notch more textbook than the IPS.
- **Meaning:** Where WInS had no Treasury for a year, the team used iBond funds, which act like bonds held to the end
  and are more liquid, but which pay out net asset value rather than a promised par.
- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 2

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** This is the IPS's voice at full strength: a slightly awkward comma ("uncertainty, through duration-matching"),
  an eager "In other words", a bold claim ("near-ironclad guarantee"), a bit of colour ("restless markets") and a
  "Notably –" tacked on at the end. It sounds like keen students who mean it, and it held my attention on the skim.
- **Meaning:** The 2037 Treasury exists to remove uncertainty rather than grow wealth, and since the payments are fixed
  in nominal dollars, a nominal Treasury matches them and TIPS are not needed.
- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 3

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** A plain, chronological account in a conversational register ("It wasn't all smooth, mind you", "Then 7
  October."). It owns the mistake without dressing it up and explains each rule by its reason, which is how the IPS
  argues. It is a touch more even and finished than the IPS, and it spells out "ten" where the IPS writes "10".
- **Meaning:** The team set targets before trading, met them except for VT's three extra units (which was planned for),
  added a 0.25-point stale-price rule after 29-30 September, and after the 7 October mis-click let only Ray trade.
- **Check question (Buying it):** Before trading the team set target units for each of the ten Treasury holdings, said
  VT would be bought last with the leftover cash, and set a cash floor of $1,000. All ten Treasuries hit their targets
  exactly, VT came in at 167 units against 164, cash stayed above $1,000, and fees were $200.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 4

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** It is clipped and aphoristic, with short declaratives, colons and a closing reversal ("Her spare money
  shrinks before her promise does"). That reads like a compressed analyst's note, not the warm, slightly sprawling
  letter-writers of the IPS. Its "one weakness", reinvesting the bonds' interest, also sits oddly beside the IPS's
  ladder, where each Treasury matures just before its payment.
- **Meaning:** In 2031 Laura states a range from her owned floor to a top that adds half her shares, moved into
  Treasuries first; any reinvestment shortfall is priced and covered from her kept shares before she names it, or the
  range comes down.
- **Check question (The 2031 range):** The passage doesn't really answer it. It gives no figures and doesn't say which
  end a co-sponsor counts, though it implies the top is named only once it is in Treasuries, because a stated number
  can't be halved later.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 5

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It sounds like people with a bit of attitude ("so it was hard to fudge later", "trouble either way round",
  "it's the right one to miss"), uses numerals like the IPS, and makes its point and its reason in the same breath. It
  reads like the IPS's writers telling what happened.
- **Meaning:** Measured against targets set before trading, the book came out on plan apart from VT, with a stale-price
  rule and a one-trader rule added after two near-misses; the 2028 deposit can't appear in the $300,000 account.
- **Check question (Buying it):** Target units for each of the 10 Treasury holdings, VT bought last from the leftover
  cash, and cash never below $1,000. All 10 Treasuries landed exactly on target, every bond inside the 0.25-point band,
  cash above its floor, fees $200; VT was 167 units against 164.
- **Image:** "In a market a click is ink" is vivid for a second, but it got slightly in the way: it bends ink from
  "owned in Treasuries" to "can't be undone", so it blurs the term the report defined.

### Passage 6

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It explains the oddity in a friendly, human way ("which makes it an odd sort of range. Most ranges are a best
  guess ... but hers isn't"). The second paragraph is a dense run of costs that I had to read twice, which is
  IPS-like in its unevenness. "Never the range, never a payment." reads like a line someone tidied.
- **Meaning:** Laura leads with $115,000; both ends are owned in Treasuries by the time she speaks, so the range is
  certain and needs no percentage, and only her flexibility can still change after the move.
- **Check question (The 2031 range):** The top, $115,000 on today's figures, because it is her facility contribution,
  what she actually gives. She states a range because the two ends were bought at different times, the bottom
  ($95,000) in January 2028 and the rest in the 2031 move. The gap records that history and is not a margin for error.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 7

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Relaxed and spoken ("we'd rather not answer with a percentage", "never earns another cent of interest",
  "Before the move it's a different story"), and the fund-cost paragraph is laid out more cleanly than in 6. It keeps
  the IPS's habit of putting a point and its reason in one sentence.
- **Meaning:** Laura leads with $115,000, her range is two purchases at two dates rather than a measure of doubt, its
  ends are priced cautiously, and she names nothing until the 2031 move is done.
- **Check question (The 2031 range):** The top, $115,000, because it is what she gives and what co-sponsors count. The
  range exists because the bottom was inked in January 2028 and the rest before the meetings, so its width records when
  each part became hers, not how unsure anyone is.
- **Image:** It helped. "Two inkings with two dates" made it quick to see that the gap is about timing, not doubt,
  though "inkings" is a slightly awkward noun.

### Passage 8

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It is lively and conversational: a rhetorical question answered "Yes, though not without two scares", plus
  "That matters more than it sounds", "$200 in fees all up" and "The catch is". It reads like the IPS's writers
  enjoying the story. "stops a stale one cold" and "a market has no undo button" are a little more performed than
  anything in the IPS.
- **Meaning:** The team bought what it said it would, with two scares (stale prices and the 7 October mis-click), each

- **Check question (Buying it):** Exact target units for each of the 10 Treasury holdings, VT last with the leftover
  cash, cash never under $1,000. All 10 Treasuries hit their targets, every bond inside the band, cash never under
  $1,000, $200 in fees; VT ended at 167 units against 164.
- **Image:** Not the pencil-and-ink image ("no undo button" is a plain everyday image, and it works).

### Passage 9

- **Why:** Competent and correct, but flat and impersonal, like a textbook paragraph. It has no aside, no "we

- **Meaning:** The team uses the cash-flow-matching approach, one Treasury per payment held to maturity, so no payment

- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 10

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It is the clearest and most natural of the range passages. It leads with the number that matters and then
  explains in plain, slightly cheeky speech ("isn't us hedging our bets", "cautiously – almost stubbornly", "nothing

  all the work." is the one line that feels like a slogan.
- **Meaning:** The top, $115,000, is what counts; both ends are owned by the time Laura speaks, priced cautiously, so the
  range is certain, and stating it before the 2031 move would leave the top unowned in about 11 of 100 model futures.
- **Check question (The 2031 range):** The top, $115,000, because it is her facility contribution, what a co-sponsor
  plans around. She gives a range because the bottom ($95,000) has been held since January 2028 and the rest arrives

- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 11

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Most of it is plain and team-like ("Why 0.25?" is good). But the passage stops mid-paragraph to set up and
  explain a printing image ("our key plate – on a press, the plate with the most detail is printed first ..."). That
  reads like a writer adding a crafted device, which the IPS never does. The opening "that's the plan working, not a
  lack of nerve" is also a neat reversal of a kind the IPS doesn't use.
- **Meaning:** The 89.55% Treasury book is deliberate; the ten Treasuries were bought first to exact targets and VT last,
  and two rules (the yield band and one trader) were added along the way.
- **Check question (Buying it):** Target units for each of the ten Treasury holdings and for VT, plus a $1,000 cash
  floor. All ten Treasuries landed exactly on target, VT was 167 units against 164, cash stayed above its floor, and
  fees came to $200.
- **Image:** Not the pencil-and-ink image. Its own key-plate image was a detour: it does say why the Treasuries went
  first, but I had to learn a printing fact to get there.

### Passage 12

- **Why:** The plain parts sound like the team ("pitched low on purpose"), but the order wanders: confidence is

- **Meaning:** The top, $115,000, counts; both ends are owned and cautiously priced after the move, so the range is

- **Check question (The 2031 range):** The top, $115,000, because it is the money she gives and the number a

- **Image:** It got in the way: the tacky-ink smear doesn't line up with the definition, and I had to stop and puzzle

### What separates the top from the bottom

The passages I ranked highest sound like the IPS's writers talking. They are a little uneven and a little bold, they
hold opinions openly ("near-ironclad", "vain illusion", "allow ourselves to be wrong"), and their asides are short,
colloquial and slightly cheeky, the way "worry not" and "ever-reliable" are in the IPS. The middle group (the
buying-it and range passages) keeps that conversational tone but has a few tidied lines, such as fragments like "Never
the range, never a payment." The lowest passages lose the person in one of three ways: flattened into a textbook (9),
compressed into aphorisms and neat reversals (4), or carrying a laboured image that stops the reader (the key plate
in 11, the tacky ink in 12). The IPS's writers never extend a metaphor, so an image only sounded like them when it was
used in passing as a working word (7's "two inkings").
