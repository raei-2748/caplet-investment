## What the readers heard (sentences about how passages felt, never rules)

- **The range's own turn works.** "Two inkings, two dates" helped all three readers: "made it quick to see that the gap
  is about timing, not doubt"; "helped me see the range as two purchases rather than a guess"; "'Inked in January 2028'
  fixed the bottom's date in my mind". Two of them stumbled only on "inkings" as a noun. This is the one image so far
  that readers say made 3c's hardest point easier, so it is the candidate for 3c on 27 Nov, used as a verb ("inked in
  January 2028") rather than a noun.
- **"A click is ink" lands as one quick clause.** All three heard K2 as the team's; one said it "makes the point that an
  order can't be undone" in one clause, one that it "bends ink" from its defined meaning (a click is not a figure owned
  in Treasuries). It works because the plain reason sits in the same sentence ("there's no rubbing it out").
- **"Let the ink dry" fails.** All three said the tacky-ink smear did not map onto stating the top a year early ("I had to
  stop and puzzle"); K4 fell from 3 of 3 plain to 1 of 3. The image needed decoding, and the decision it stood beside
  (wait for the move) is already plain without it. Retire it as a practice turn.
- **The key plate is a detour.** Two readers noted it is a printing image outside pencil and ink, set up mid-paragraph;
  it explained the buying order but cost the voice (L2 2 of 3, rank 9.7).
- **Across the readers:** an image read as the team's only "when it was used in passing as a working word"; the IPS
  "states its one metaphor in plain words and moves on" and "never extends a metaphor". The middle-ranked passages lost
  ground for the same reason the panel judges marked Section 3 down: they "load too many figures into single
  sentences, so the explaining stops while the arithmetic happens".
- Both writers left out a fact the brief had wrong: it described four simulated funder readers as program officers.
  They were right to (standing order 22: nothing that was not a person is described as one); the brief is corrected.
