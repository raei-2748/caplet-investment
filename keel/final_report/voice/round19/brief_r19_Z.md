## 2. Step one: carry the letter on (about 120 words)
The IPS ends: "You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that's your job. With these promises, ours is to take none."
Start straight after that line, as the same people in the same letter to Laura, and keep going: what the ten payments
will rest on in 2033, and what could still go wrong. This is a warm-up to get their voice in your ear.

## 3. Step two: two parts of the report
Now the same people write their report. Its readers are the competition's judges, so Laura is "Laura" or "she", and
the team is "we". Figures are at Treasury prices of 6 November 2026 ("at today's rates" once is enough).

**A (about 220 words): the ten-year commitment and the operating reserve.** What a judge must carry away: all ten
payments are paid from January 2028 even if cash waiting between a bond's end and its payment earns nothing; why the
team holds them to that standard (interest arrives years before the payment it helps pay, and the rate it will earn
meanwhile is not known); the price, the first $39,162 of the 2028 deposit, about a quarter of it ($2,388 completes the
ten, $36,774 buys the extra Treasuries); what comes back if later rates turn out as today's prices imply (about $46,600
in 1 January 2033 money, all of it Laura's by 2042); the operating reserve from 2033 as a schedule of what pays each
payment, and why the payments' money is kept apart from the building's money. One outside comparison, in one or two
sentences: the California State Lottery's investment rule and the one way it differs from Laura's plan (the fact sheet
has both).

**E (about 220 words): assumptions, limits and what to watch.** What a judge must carry away: only two things change
the plan, and neither is a market price; how each promise could fail, one figure each (the ten payments; the top she
states; her flexibility), each with who carries the loss; the hard case from history in one or two sentences; what
the monitoring table watches, in one sentence (the table itself is not yours to write); and, last, what no fall in
share prices can change. One outside comparison, in one or two sentences: the 2022 gilt crisis and why it cannot
happen to Laura (the fact sheet has it). If the fact sheet's "Pencil and ink" helps you say the last sentence, you may
use it there, explained in the same sentence.
