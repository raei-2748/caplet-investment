## What was found

1. **Section 2 carries its argument.** Every reader answered all five memory questions right, every figure included:
   the deletion test and why, why the WInS portfolio holds IBTM and VT, the ten's cost and the 2028 finish, the three
   dated steps, one issuer and IBTM's quarter, the 2009 receipts, the check, the cost, the one-trader rule and the
   rare year.
2. **It is followed less well than its content is remembered: 63% of sentence readings first time** (the lead and
   construction 61%, spread and buying 67%), against C3 followed 15 of 15 and version 61 followed 13-14 of 21 (Caplet
   followed 4). The writer's text ran about 50 words over its 575.
3. **Nine sentences stopped all three readers, and the reasons are names, not length** (each becomes a note in
   `../SECTION_NOTES.md` 2.0-2.5):
   - the comics sentence arrives before its purpose (the ink definition follows it): say what the report needs, then
     the craft fact, in one sentence or the definition first;
   - "WInS" is never set up (P2.6, and again in P3.4): say "the competition's trading platform, WInS" at first use;
   - "the floor" first appears inside a broken clause ("and, bought again in 2028, the floor"): set it up in its own
     clause, "more of the same fund, bought in January 2028, is the floor: the bottom of her range";
   - "the cheapest to finish a year later" has no reason, and $2,388 beside $2,287 looks like a contradiction: say
     why (the shortest-dated piece loses least if rates fall while it waits) and that $2,388 is the same gap a year
     on, or give one figure only;
   - "2037 aside" gives no reason: say the team's recorded choice (a fund ending two weeks before the payment over a
     bond paying out ten and a half months early, which cost a little more), or leave 2037 with the forced years and
     say nothing;
   - the extra $36,774 arrives without its job: say in the same sentence that they keep the ten paid even if cash
     waiting earns nothing;
   - IBTM's quarter is given a "because" that explains fund against bond, not why so much sits in one place: say what
     the concentration risks (a fund's payout is not fixed, and it can end early) and who covers it (her letter, Table
     3 row 4);
   - a deposit three-quarters smaller leaves the ten paid, but readers asked what it does to the building: say the
     bottom and the top fall with it, the payments do not;
   - "The year was rare" is vague until after the colon: lead with what was rare (the first $300,000 bought nearly all
     ten), then the measure.
4. **Two sense faults were the fact sheet's own wording (version 12), fixed in version 13 tonight.** "Every dollar
   promised, or to be promised, is bought in Treasuries before any money goes into shares" is false for the top,
   which waits in VT until 2031 (the professor caught it); and "nothing is rebalanced because each holding is matched
   to the date of what it pays" is wrong for VT, which matches no date (the shares have no target weight, so there is
   nothing to rebalance to). A writer copies the fact sheet's reason word for word, so a loose reason there becomes a
   loose sentence in every writer's text.
5. **Two more sense faults to keep out:** a 0.25-point check proves no gross error, not a good price (give the fills'
   gap as well, NUMBERS 8m); and the one-trader rule is said as what it is (one person places every order) beside
   the event that led to it, never as what stops a mis-click (the click's layer was the lack of an undo, as the tree
   says for 3f).
6. **"We" ran at 2.29 per 100 words**, the highest of any report part since round 14 (Caplet's IPS 2.4): buying and
   checking are the team's own acts, so Section 2 is where "we" comes naturally.
