### What separates them

The passages I ranked highest sound like people talking to someone: a "we" with opinions, one idea per sentence, a turn that comes out of their own reasoning, and small human roughness or enthusiasm ("as per JPMorgan", "we'll simply ignore price noise", "Less than it looks!"), with numbers in support rather than packed into every clause. The lowest ones are compressed into noun stacks and lists of figures, lean on crafted fragments and aphorisms ("Most, not all.", "Her spare money shrinks before her promise does"), and bring in images from outside the plan (printing plates, undefined "ink") that the reader has to decode. They read like an expert's brief or an editor's polish rather than students explaining a plan they believe in, and the lowest one also quietly changes the plan the IPS describes.
