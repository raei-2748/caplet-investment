# Voice round 15: the depth questions and their answer key (written Mon 23 Nov 2026, before any text was read)

Readers see only the questions. The key comes from `voice/FACTS_PLAIN.md` version 6 and `NUMBERS.md` (8p, 8r, 8u).
A scorer marks each answer R (right: the key's essentials), P (partly right: one essential, or right with no reason),
W (wrong: contradicts the key, or claims what the plan does not do) or N (the reader says the text did not say, or
gives no answer). A confident answer the text could not have supported counts W, not R.

| # | Question (as readers see it) | Key: the essentials for R |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Suppose world share prices halve during 2029. What happens to the range Laura can state in 2031, and to the ten $50,000 payments? | The ten payments do not change (they are in Treasuries). The bottom does not change ($95,000 on today's figures; held since January 2028). Only the top falls, because the shares moved in January 2031 buy less (a 40% fall gives about $95,000-$105,000; if shares fell to nothing, the range is one figure, the bottom). |
| 2 | Suppose instead share prices halve during 2032, after the shares have been moved. What changes? | Nothing about the figure she stated or the payments: both ends are held in Treasuries from January 2031. Only her flexibility (the half never moved) falls. |
| 3 | Why not move all of her shares into Treasuries before 2031 and promise more? | The other half is promised to no one so that it can cover what the IPS names: building costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar, a co-sponsor falling short. With nothing kept back she would have nothing to meet those without breaking a promise. (P if "to keep flexibility" with no named use.) |
| 4 | Why can Laura be sure of the bottom of the range from January 2028, three years before she states it? | Because in January 2028 the money for it buys a Treasury fund (IBTM) that pays out in December 2032; the bottom is what that fund pays counted cautiously (as if interest waiting earns nothing, less a margin for costs, rounded down), so no later market move can lower it. (P if "it is in Treasuries" with no link to what it is counted from.) |
| 5 | Why does she give co-sponsors a range rather than one figure, and why can't the top be known today? | The bottom is held from January 2028; the top depends on share prices until the shares are moved in January 2031, so seen from today it is uncertain (a top of at least $100,000 in about 91 of 100 simulated futures, at least $115,000 in about 57). Once moved, both ends are held. (P for either half.) |
| 6 | What, if anything, could stop the top she states being paid in December 2032? | At least two of: the fund's costs beyond its fee taking more than about 2% of what it pays; the fund ending early, merging or moving its end (up to about $4,600 short, made up from her flexibility); the broker failing (a delay); a US default. W if the reader says "nothing at all can", since the text cannot have supported that. P for one. |
| 7 | If the building ends up costing 25% more than planned, who pays the extra? | Her flexibility (the half of the shares never moved, plus anything the fund pays above the top) covers a rise of about 25% in the cost of her share, if shares earn the 7.5% a year assumed; beyond that the residency's other funders, a smaller design or a later start. (P for "her flexibility" alone.) |
| 8 | Why are the shares moved in January 2031, and not on the day she meets co-sponsors or a year earlier? | So that the Treasuries behind any figure she names are already held for her before she names it; moving after naming would leave a stated figure resting on share prices, and naming a top a year early (January 2030) would let the move buy less than that top in about 11 of 100 simulated futures. (P for "so the figure is already owned" with no reason against the alternatives.) |
| 9 | If interest rates fell to 1% from 2028 and stayed there, what would happen to the ten payments and to the range? | The payments are still met: the extra Treasuries bought in January 2028 cover them even if waiting money earns nothing. The stated figures do not change, because both ends are counted as if waiting interest earns nothing. (Before January 2027 a fall in rates would make the payments dearer, completed from the 2028 deposit, and lower the bottom.) P for one half. |
