# Text M

In a recession, Laura's advances and fees can come later and smaller, share prices can fall and co-sponsors can cut their budgets, all in the same downturn. So we asked how much of her facility contribution she could promise in 2031 that no such year could take back.

It is paid from her second deposit. The first, $300,000 in January 2027, buys ten Treasuries, each maturing just before one of the $50,000 payments. Nothing comes in after the $150,000 of January 2028, so every promise she makes is paid for from it, and we spend it in order of what can least afford to be wrong.

The payments come first, because they're fixed. About $39,000 (on today's figures: Treasury prices at the close on 6 November 2026) finishes the ten and buys extra Treasuries, so that all ten are met even if the interest they pay along the way earns nothing while it waits. From January 2028, no fall in interest rates can send the building's money to the payments.

The floor comes second. We put $81,244 into IBTM, a fund holding Treasuries that mature during 2032; it closes in mid-December that year and pays out what they're worth. On today's figures this purchase pays about $102,000. We count it the way a co-sponsor about to plan a building would want it counted: as if none of the interest it pays out earns anything more, less 0.5% for running costs beyond the fund's fee, rounded down to a whole $5,000. That leaves $95,000, the bottom of her range, worked out once, in January 2028, three years before she says it. The amount follows our IPS, which sent "most" of the deposit here.

The shares come last. The remaining $29,594 buys VT, a fund of thousands of companies worldwide. No promise rests on it, so it's the one place we let markets decide: shares should earn more than Treasuries, and how they do sets how far above the bottom her top reaches. In January 2031, before the first meeting, we'll sell half and buy more IBTM, so that whatever top Laura names is paid by Treasuries already held for her. The other half stays in VT, promised to no one: her flexibility, for a building that costs more than planned, a Taiwan dollar that strengthens, or a co-sponsor who falls short. Half was our choice, not a model's answer. Keep only 30% back and she could state about $5,000 more, but her flexibility would cover a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar in only about half of simulated futures, against 93 in 100.

We count the top the same cautious way, on both purchases of IBTM together: $115,000 on today's figures. So in 2031 she states $95,000 to $115,000, and gives co-sponsors the top to plan around. Seen from today it is uncertain: our model runs 200,000 futures, with world shares growing 7.5% a year in the middle case (J.P. Morgan's assumption) and a yearly spread of about 18%. The top comes to at least $100,000 in about 91 of 100 futures, and to at least $115,000 in about 57. Once the shares are moved, nothing about it is uncertain: the Treasuries that will pay it are already held, and she can say so in one breath: "$115,000, all of it held in Treasury funds before I say it, $95,000 of it since January 2028."

Each end of the range moves only until we buy it. If shares halve before January 2031, the half we move is worth half as much, and the part of her top above the bottom roughly halves with it; if they fell to nothing, her range would be the bottom alone. After the move, a fall in shares shrinks only her flexibility. Lower yields before January 2027 would make the payments dearer and the floor smaller (1 point lower gives a bottom of $85,000), but once we've bought the floor, rates at 1%, or at zero, leave the bottom standing while the fund runs to its end, because it was counted as if interest earned nothing. A dearer building is met first from her flexibility, about $21,200 at the start of 2033 if shares earn the 7.5% assumed; a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar would take about $11,500 of it. Four times since 1991, building costs and the Taiwan dollar together made the same building 28% or more dearer in US dollars within two years, about $32,200 on her $115,000 and more than all her flexibility. A gap that size falls to the other funders, a smaller design or a later start, and those work only before building begins, which is why the building waits for the money.
