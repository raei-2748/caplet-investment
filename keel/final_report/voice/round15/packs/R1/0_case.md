# What you know before you start


Laura Gao, a writer and illustrator with a business degree, is planning a creative residency for artists in Taiwan,
opening in 2033. She puts $300,000 into an investment portfolio at the start of 2027 and $150,000 at the start of
2028; that second sum comes from her book advances, speaking fees and licensing. Nothing else goes in or out before
2033. From the portfolio she must pay $50,000 at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042 (ten payments) to run the
residency, and they must be met "with a high degree of certainty". At the start of 2033 she sets money aside for
those payments (an "operating reserve"), and beyond that she wants to give as much as is responsible towards the
residency's building (her "facility contribution") while keeping flexibility as the project develops. In 2031 she
tells co-sponsors (other funders of the building) a range in dollars for that contribution, and how confident she
is. Overpromising would hurt her credibility. A team of school students advises her; it may use US Treasury bonds,
Treasury funds, shares and share funds.
