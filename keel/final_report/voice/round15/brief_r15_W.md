# Voice round 15: the facility contribution and the 2031 range, whole (Mon 23 Nov 2026, morning)

You are writing practice text for Team Caplet's Final Report to the Wharton Global High School Investment Competition.
Caplet is six school students in Sydney. Their client is Laura Gao (a case, not a real client of theirs). What you
write is practice, not the report itself.

## 1. Read first, in this order
1. `/home/user/caplet-investment/keel/final_report/voice/CORPUS.md`, whole: everything the team has written itself.
   Learn from sections 1-3; section 4 is there as a contrast, not as a model.
2. `/home/user/caplet-investment/keel/final_report/FINAL_IPS_as_submitted.txt`, the team's Investment Policy Statement,
   a letter to Laura. Read it twice, the second time slowly and as if aloud. Notice who does each thing in it: "we"
   buy, "you" decide, and the reason comes in the same breath as the step.
3. `/home/user/caplet-investment/keel/final_report/voice/FACTS_PLAIN.md` (version 6): the facts of the case and the
   plan, with the reason for each step. Every figure you use comes from there, in its words where it gives them.
Open no other file in the repository.

## 2. Step one: carry the letter on (about 200 words)
The IPS ends: "You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that's your job. With these promises, ours is to take none."
Start straight after that line, as the same people in the same letter to Laura, and keep going into what the letter
never reached: what happens before the 2031 meetings, and what she will be able to tell her co-sponsors then. Write it
the way they would have if the form had allowed another page. This is a warm-up to get their voice in your ear.

## 3. Step two: one piece for the Final Report (about 650-750 words)
Now the same people write their report. Its readers are the competition's judges, so Laura is "Laura" or "she", and the
team is "we", doing things in the sentence the way it does in the letter: we bought, we chose, we will move. Write new
sentences in their voice rather than reusing the IPS's own. Figures are "on today's figures" (Treasury prices at the
close on 6 November 2026); use only the figures the piece needs.

The piece is read **on its own**, by an intelligent reader who has not read the IPS or any other part of the report
and knows only this:

> Laura Gao, a writer and illustrator with a business degree, is planning a creative residency for artists in Taiwan,
> opening in 2033. She puts $300,000 into an investment portfolio at the start of 2027 and $150,000 at the start of
> 2028; that second sum comes from her book advances, speaking fees and licensing. Nothing else goes in or out before
> 2033. From the portfolio she must pay $50,000 at the start of each year from 2033 to 2042 (ten payments) to run the
> residency, and they must be met "with a high degree of certainty". At the start of 2033 she sets money aside for
> those payments (an "operating reserve"), and beyond that she wants to give as much as is responsible towards the
> residency's building (her "facility contribution") while keeping flexibility as the project develops. In 2031 she
> tells co-sponsors (other funders of the building) a range in dollars for that contribution, and how confident she
> is. Overpromising would hurt her credibility. A team of school students advises her; it may use US Treasury bonds,
> Treasury funds, shares and share funds.

So a step whose reason is not on the page has no reason for this reader.

**The piece: Laura's facility contribution and the range she gives co-sponsors in 2031.** What pays for her
contribution and when each part of it is bought; why half her shares are moved into Treasuries before 2031 and the other
half is promised to no one, and what that half is for; the range she states in 2031, what is uncertain about it seen
from today and what is already held once the shares are moved, and how confident she can be. It is one piece, the
contribution first and then the range, as the team would write it for the judges.
