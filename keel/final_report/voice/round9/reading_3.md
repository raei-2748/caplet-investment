# Reading 3, voice round 9 (blind)

What I heard in the IPS: a "we" talking straight to Laura, keen and a little cheeky ("So we hedge it!", "But worry not",
"Fair enough; that's your job"). It leans on spaced dashes to land a point, says "in other words", reaches now and then for
a bigger word ("ever-reliable", "counterbalance"), and lets a long sentence run slightly past its grammar. Under the
enthusiasm it is plain: it says what the money does, year by year. It reads like clever teenagers who enjoy this.

### Passage 1

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** This reads like condensed notes. Clauses are stacked with commas and colons ("the range down to one figure, the
  bottom, because no promise rests on a share price"), so each sentence carries too much and none of the IPS's warmth or
  forward push comes through. The pencil sentence is the one lively moment, and it feels like a different hand.
- **Meaning:** Her payments can fail only through a US default, a missing or much smaller 2028 deposit, or fund costs eating
  past the margin; a stated figure fails only if its Treasuries pay less or late; shares at zero break no promise;
  flexibility is a limit, not a promise.
- **Check question (assumptions and limits):** A payment could fall short or come late through a US default, a 2028 deposit
  that doesn't come or comes much smaller, or fund costs beyond the fee eating through the small margin. A fall in share
  prices could not, because no promise rests on a share price. For a stated figure to fall short, its Treasuries would
  have to pay less or late: a default, or extra fund costs above about 2% of what the two Treasury funds behind the top pay.
- **Image:** It helped. The aside "put the shares at zero before the meetings" translates it at once and "the page still
  reads" is vivid, but it sits in the middle of a dry list and slows the paragraphs around it.

- **Meaning:** Where WInS lists no Treasury for a year they bought iBond funds, which act like bonds held to the end and

### Passage 5

- **Meaning:** Laura tells a co-sponsor her ten payments are already funded, she will give $115,000 to the building in

### What separates the top from the bottom

The passages I ranked highest move the way the IPS does. A "we" thinks aloud, spaced dashes deliver the point,
"in other words" and "simply" appear, a word sometimes reaches a little too far ("vain illusion", "restless markets",
"terminal payout"), and sentences sometimes run past their grammar. They sound like keen students who like the subject. The
middle passages are plainer but keep the IPS's habits: the fact first, then a short line that shuts the door ("No fall in
share prices can"). The lowest either squeeze the ideas into crowded notes (1) or have a seasoned writer's smooth control,
whether a textbook opening (4) or polished one-liners (9), with none of the IPS's rough edges or enthusiasm.
