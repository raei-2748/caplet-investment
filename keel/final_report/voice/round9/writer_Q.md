The ten payments fail only if one is short, or late, on its 1 January. No fall in shares, however steep, can do that. A US default could, and so could a 2028 deposit that never comes or comes far smaller (Section 2), since a downturn delays a writer's income first, or fund costs beyond the fee outgrowing our margin while waiting cash earns too little.

A figure Laura has stated fails if its Treasuries pay less, or late – in other words, it's her word to co-sponsors that breaks. Short of a default, the funds' other costs would have to pass about 2% of what the two Treasury funds behind the top pay, even if waiting cash earns nothing. A missed deposit just means nothing is stated, and falling shares shrink what she can state, never what she has.

Her flexibility is a limit, not a promise. With anything the funds pay above the top, it covers about a 25% rise in the US-dollar cost of her share of the building at today's rates. The worst two-year spans since 1991 rose by more, and if it runs out, nothing promised breaks.

Of the case's five areas, the return we assume for shares barely moves the top, timing is the 2028 deposit, outside money never touches the payments, and inflation changes what they buy, not whether they're paid.

The IPS's two reasons to change the plan, a missed deposit and a Treasury default, are a separate list: exits for when it can't be carried out.
