**First pass, from memory**

1. The plan: Laura's money is mostly put into US Treasuries, one holding per payment, so each of her ten yearly payments is covered. A smaller slice goes into world shares for growth.

2. Why nearly 90% is in Treasuries: the ten payments are fixed in date and amount, while Laura's career income is uncertain. Her uncertain career should not decide whether she can meet the payments. So the payments come first, and the shares are only the extra.

3. Why IBTM is so large: the account was built as Laura's plan would look after both deposits, then scaled down. IBTM is bought twice, once for the 2033 payment and once as the "floor" that sets the lowest figure she can promise co-sponsors. That makes it about a third of the account.

4. The next section must answer: whether the payments really reach $50,000 each, given that they depend on reinvesting the interest, and nobody knows what that interest will earn.
