**From memory, three sentences.** The team was given $300,000 in a trading game and put nearly 90% into US Treasuries, one for each of ten payments Laura has to make, because her income from writing is uncertain and her payments are not. The rest went into a world shares fund (VT) so her money can grow without the payments depending on it. They checked the bond prices against the real market before buying, and the last paragraph warns that the payments only reach $50,000 if the interest is reinvested, which nobody can promise.

**Read twice or not understood**
1. "How should we invest when the consequences of our risks extend beyond ourselves?" Fine, but "our risks" is vague. Whose risks, Laura's or the team's?
2. "Taking risks has served Laura rather well... from a career in technology to the pages of bestselling books." I did not know she was an author or had worked in tech. Nobody told me who Laura is.
3. "a decade of payments whose dates and amounts are not." The word "uncertain" is missing, so I had to decode it. Her payments are certain, her income is not.
4. "ten payments we could not afford to miss." Who is owed them, and why? Not said.
5. "2033 payment and facility floor", "the floor, whose payout sets the lowest figure she can promise her co-sponsors". "Facility" is never explained, and "co-sponsors" is only half explained. "Buys it twice, from the first deposit... and from the second" assumes I know about the two deposits, which come a sentence later.
6. "Our equity investment serves a different purpose: expanding what Laura might achieve." Achieve what? I was lost until "Facility growth and flexibility", which is still vague.
7. "(We built the account in October, before sizing one more set of Treasuries the plan now buys first, so in the plan the floor and VT are smaller.)" This made no sense even after rereading. What is "one more set"?
8. "Each... closes in mid-December and pays out what its holdings are then worth", but the table says Dec 2032 funds pay the 2033 payment. The reason is not given.
9. "a bond maturing in February 2036 would have paid out ten and a half months early." Early against what? It is never said that the 2037 payment is due in January.
10. "A yield too low would have had us overpay; one too high could have meant a bond nobody was trading." The second half was unexplained.
11. "Passing ruled out only a gross error, but gaps that small told us..." The "but" contradicts itself.
12. "Eric fell asleep before it." Who is Eric? It also seems unrelated to the point.
13. "reinvested and earns interest of its own"; "about $445,400 of the $500,000". It is not said why the total is $500,000 (ten times $50,000), and "Section 3 must answer" refers to something I have not seen.

**Liked most:** "Why should the former decide whether she can meet the latter?"

**Would cut:** the pencil and ink paragraph ("In pencil, a line remains a possibility..."). It is pretty, but the pencil and ink idea is never used again here.

**Same writers?** Mostly no. The opening paragraphs ("Rather unenterprising for an entrepreneur, perhaps", "Perhaps this distinction is best understood through Laura's own craft") sound like polished magazine writing. From "Two things the account could not hold" onward it sounds like students explaining things ("The buying itself tested us once", "Eric fell asleep"). The shift in voice is noticeable.

**Ease: 3/5.** The first half is easy but vague. The table and the middle paragraphs are dense, and the jargon (basis points, facility, floor, co-sponsors) arrives before I was told what it meant.
