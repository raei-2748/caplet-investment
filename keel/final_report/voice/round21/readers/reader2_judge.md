Why IBTM is about a third: the account mimics Laura's plan after both deposits, scaled to $300,000, and IBTM is bought twice there, once for the 2033 payment and once as the floor for the co-sponsors.

STOPS AND DOUBTS
1. "a decade of payments whose dates and amounts are not" - unclear: not what? (fixed? uncertain?) Reads as the opposite of the facts.
2. "Each has its own Treasury holding, which pays out shortly before that payment is due" - doubt: Table 1 gives IBTM two jobs, and the Feb 2037 to May 2038 bonds mature up to about eleven months early. "Shortly" is wrong for those.
3. "the floor" / "the lowest figure she can promise her co-sponsors" - term used before it is defined; no size given.
4. "Laura buys it twice: from the first deposit..., and from the second" - claim without numbers; I cannot check that a third follows.
5. "(We built the account in October, before sizing one more set of Treasuries the plan now buys first...)" - unclear; reads as a confession that the table is not the plan. How much smaller are VT and the floor?
6. "VT ... gets what is left" - no reason why 8.84% rather than any other; no reason for shares at all beyond "growth".
7. "a Treasury that pays nothing until it matures. These, called STRIPS" - fine, but the "Two things the account could not hold" opener only delivers one reason clearly.
8. "one too high could have meant a bond nobody was trading" - claim without its reason.
9. "Every bond passed ... from 10 basis points under to 6 over" - are those the bonds' own yields? Which sign is good?
10. "Eric fell asleep before it" - who is Eric? Trivia against a table of $300,000; breaks register.
11. "Counted without that reinvestment, the ten holdings as bought pay about $445,400 of the $500,000" - the key fact appears in the last paragraph with no prior setup; is the shortfall about 11%? Reinvested rate needed?
12. IBTM 32.50% is $97,489.95, but the 2033 payment is only $50k on $500k scale: a judge will want the split of IBTM between payment and floor.
Arithmetic checks: subtotal, total and 89.55% all add up.

THREE QUESTIONS STILL OPEN
a. How is the $97,490 in IBTM split, and what is the floor in dollars?
b. Why this much in VT and cash, not more or less? What does the 8.84% do for the facility?
c. How much does the table differ from the real plan (the October caveat), and does that weaken the claim that it implements it?

DESCRIBING, NOT EVALUATING
The Table 1 walk-through (who holds what), the fund mechanics ("closes in mid-December and pays out what its holdings are then worth"), and the 29-30 September story are mostly description. The best evaluation is "Passing ruled out only a gross error" and the final paragraph. Nowhere does it weigh the plan against an alternative (e.g. longer bonds, a bigger equity share) or name the cost of 90% Treasuries.

VOICE
Mostly consistent, but three registers: lyrical opening ("In pencil, a line remains a possibility; in ink, it becomes a commitment", "Rather unenterprising for an entrepreneur, perhaps"), plain explanatory middle, and a diary tone in "Eric fell asleep before it" and "tested us once". "Entrusted each investment to do" and the title question sound most mannered. The last paragraph is the strongest voice and is the one to match.

SCORES
Portfolio Analysis: 3/5 - table is clean and reconciles, the reasoning on IBTR and the yield check is good, but the floor, VT size and the October caveat are left unexplained.
Creativity and Presentation: 3.5/5 - pencil and ink opening is memorable and the closing hook works, but the Eric aside and register shifts dent the polish.
