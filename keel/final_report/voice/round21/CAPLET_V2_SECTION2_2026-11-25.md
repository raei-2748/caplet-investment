### **Portfolio Implementation****
*“How should we invest when the consequences of our risks extend beyond ourselves?”*
Taking risks has served Laura rather well. They brought her from a career in technology to the pages of bestselling books. Yet while the rewards of her artistic risks are hers to share, the consequences of her investment risks may be others’ to bear.
Perhaps this distinction is best understood through Laura’s own craft. In pencil, a line remains a possibility; in ink, it becomes a commitment. Her portfolio needs both: the freedom to pursue ambitions not yet realised and the means to honour commitments already made.
In practice, this meant devoting nearly 90% of our WInS portfolio to US Treasuries. Rather unenterprising for an entrepreneur, perhaps. But consider what Laura already has: a career whose rewards are uncertain, and a decade of payments whose dates and amounts are not. Why should the former determine her ability to meet the latter?
Our starting point, then, was not a return we hoped to achieve, but ten payments we could not afford to miss. Each has a corresponding Treasury holding. Our equity investment serves a different purpose: expanding what Laura might achieve without making her operating commitments dependent on that growth.
The resulting portfolio reflects this division of responsibilities. Table 1 shows what we bought and what we entrusted each investment to do.
