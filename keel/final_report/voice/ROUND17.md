# Voice round 17: Laura's words to co-sponsors, range first against figure first (Tue 24 Nov 2026, morning)

The day's job (Day 17, the co-sponsor part) asks how "how sure" is said so people hear it right. Two orders were on
file for Laura's words: the tree's Branch 5 (panel 5, Mon 23 Nov) states the range first, both ends with their dates,
then why she signs the top; the fact sheet (`FACTS_PLAIN.md` version 7, l.81) gives the one figure to plan around
first. The risk tested was the one the RBA's review found: listeners keep part of what is said and drop the rest. Keel's
prediction and the deciding rule were written before anyone read (`round17/PREDICTION.md`, 06:37 Sydney).

## Set-up

- **Writer.** One fresh writer who read only Caplet's writing (`CORPUS.md`, the IPS), the facts (`FACTS_PLAIN.md`
  version 7) and the case (brief `round17/brief_r17_X.md`). It carried the IPS on from its last line (warm-up, "we" 1.4
  per 100 words), then wrote the team's lead-in (owned against signed, Lehman and Spelman; 110 words) and Laura's words
  twice: R, range first (204 words), and F, figure first (215 words). Only the three building-figure sentences differ;
  the builder checked that the ten shared sentences keep their order. Texts in `round17/writer_X.md`.
- **Readers.** Six blind readers in two arms, one of each role per arm: the head of giving at a Taiwanese family company
  that has sponsored arts venues; a programme officer at a US foundation that funds artists' residencies; a grants
  officer at Taiwan's Ministry of Culture. Each read version 61 first (calibration: Caplet followed 4 of its 21
  sentences), then Caplet's own IPS paragraph C3 (control), then the lead-in with R (readers 1-3) or F (readers 4-6),
  marking every sentence Follow 1-3 and Sense with a paraphrase; then five questions from memory. Scores in
  `round17/SCORES_R17.md`. Readers are simulated, never people.

## Results

**1. The order made no difference to what anyone booked.** All six booked $115,000, paid January 2033; all six named
the $95,000 as the part held since January 2028; all six named Lehman and Spelman and what the contrast shows. Keel
predicted that at least one range-first reader would book the bottom or hear "anywhere from $95,000 to $115,000"; none
did, because both versions also say she signs for the top. On the pre-registered rule this is a tie, and a tie keeps
panel 5's order, which follows the case's own order (a range, then how confident). The three funders who read the notes
the same morning each said a range said first would make them book $95,000 (`research/panel/cosponsor_notes_test_2026-11-24/`);
when they read the words themselves, the signing sentence carried the figure either way.

**2. Both orders share one fault, and every reader found it.** With the top held and signed, all six asked why a range
is stated at all, and what could make the gift $95,000. Range-first readers stopped at the why-the-top sentence (3 of
3: "which figure is the promise?"); figure-first readers stopped at "..., so my range is $95,000 to $115,000" (3 of 3:
"the 'so' does not follow") and at the why-the-top sentence (3 of 3). In memory, all six said "by her words, no, it
cannot end lower", then that they were unsure, and two made the question the one thing they would ask her: "under what
circumstances she would pay less, down to $95,000" (reader 4). Round 16's readers asked why the top; its repair worked,
and the question moved to why a bottom. Nothing in her words says what the bottom now is.

**3. Laura's words are now followed first time.** 41 of 48 sentence readings in range first (85%), 39 of 48 in figure
first (81%), against about half in round 16 (28 of 57). Caplet's own paragraph was followed whole by five of six.

**4. Two smaller repairs, both arms.**
- "In all, I am committing about $615,000": five of six stopped ("ten times $50,000 plus $115,000 is exactly $615,000;
  'about' makes my gift office ask what part is uncertain"). The three funders on the notes said the same and asked for
  the two parts separately.
- The lead-in's "offer co-sponsors a signed pledge letter as well as the money held for her" was read first as handing
  over the money itself (four of six).

**5. How sure was heard right.** No reader asked for a probability. The strong words on the Treasuries ("guaranteed by
the US government, and no market can change what they will pay") were paraphrased right by all six and questioned by
none. Four of six asked for a statement of the holding; reader 5 asked for one showing "the amounts they are due to pay
by December 2032, adding up to the $115,000", the point the funders on the notes raised about a fund's market value.

## Keel's prediction, scored

Two of five held (both ends still named; funders ask for a statement, never odds), two held in half, one failed (the
order changed nothing). The miss is the lesson: Keel tested the order of two sentences when the readers' question was
what one of them means.

## What changes (same morning; `research/COSPONSOR_NOTES.md` section 8)

- **Order.** Range first stays (the tie rule). The fact sheet's l.81 changes to match the tree.
- **The bottom says what it is now.** One sentence after the two ends: the $95,000 never depended on her shares (it
  would be there if every share had become worthless), and since January 2031 neither end depends on them, so no market
  can change either. Then why she signs the top. This answers both readers' questions with facts already in the
  register (NUMBERS 4: after the move nothing moves either end; the deletion test).
- **What could still make the fund pay less** is answered in the letter, not by a list: the sum is $115,000, and if the
  fund pays less she makes up the difference herself (funders on the notes, readers 1 and 3; this round's readers 1 and
  4). The register already has her flexibility cover an early fund end (NUMBERS 3).
- **Her whole commitment** in two parts with no "about": $500,000 for the running costs, 2033-2042, and $115,000 for
  the building, $615,000 in all (WORDS: never "about" before a figure co-sponsors are to count).
- **The lead-in** says she shows co-sponsors the Treasuries held for her and gives them a signed pledge letter.
