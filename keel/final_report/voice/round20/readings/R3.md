## From memory

1. **If every share became worthless:** all ten $50,000 payments would still be paid, and the building would still get the bottom of her range, about $95,000 at today's rates. That is because of the order of spending: every dollar she has promised, or will promise, is put into Treasuries before any money goes into shares. Finance calls this dedication when it is done for known payments, and her plan extends it to the figures she hasn't promised yet.

2. **Why the late-2032 fund and the world share fund:** the team's WInS portfolio is Laura's plan as it will stand after the January 2028 deposit, scaled down to $300,000. So it holds the floor (more of IBTM, the fund that closes in December 2032) and VT, the world share fund, not just the ten payments' Treasuries. At today's rates the ten cost about $302,300, roughly $2,300 more than her first deposit. The 2028 deposit finishes the shortfall, which is part of the 2033 payment, for about $2,400.

3. **The rule and why no rebalancing:** the order of spending sets the allocation, not a target mix. The team acts on only three dates (January 2027, January 2028 and January 2031), each time buying what backs a promise before she makes it. Nothing is rebalanced in between because each holding is matched to the date it has to pay.

4. **Concentration, why accepted, and her career:** all the promised money has one issuer, the US government, spread across dates rather than issuers. The team accepts that gladly because any second issuer could only add a weaker promise. The single biggest holding is IBTM, about a quarter of everything in late 2032: it carries the 2033 payment, the bottom and the top, because no Treasury bond maturing close to 2033 was listed. Her career touches the plan in one place only, the 2028 deposit. Writers' and artists' receipts fell (about 8%) in 2009 even as shares rose, so no shares are bought until that deposit has finished paying for the ten, and the deposit could come in about three-quarters smaller with every payment still met.

5. **How well it was bought:** every bond's yield was checked against the official Treasury yields (within 0.25 points) before ordering and again at the fill, and all passed. The eleven trades cost about $200 in commissions, around 0.07% of $300,000. After a mis-click on 7 October cancelled an order with no confirm step, only Ray places or cancels orders. The team also bought near the cheapest day for the ten since 2002, though the plan didn't depend on that. **What I'd ask the team to make clearer:** the sentence saying the piece left over is part of the 2033 payment, "the cheapest to finish a year later". It is stated as a fact with no reason given, and a judge with some finance will want to know why the 2033 piece, rather than any other, is the cheap one to leave for 2028.
