# Voice round 20: Section 2, Portfolio Implementation, whole (Wed 25 Nov 2026, evening)

You are writing practice text for Team Caplet's Final Report to the Wharton Global High School Investment Competition.
Caplet is six school students in Sydney. Their client is Laura Gao (a case, not a real client of theirs). What you
write is practice, not the report itself.

   Learn from sections 1-3; section 4 is there as a contrast, not as a model.

   a letter to Laura. Read it twice, the second time slowly and as if aloud. Notice who does each thing in it: "we"

3. `/home/user/caplet-investment/keel/final_report/voice/FACTS_PLAIN.md` (version 12): the facts of the case and the

   built", "The history and the evidence the team gathered" and "Pencil and ink".

## 2. Step one: carry the letter on (about 120 words)
The IPS ends: "You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that's your job. With these promises, ours is to take none."
Start straight after that line, as the same people in the same letter to Laura, and keep going: what we bought for her
in October, and why each piece is there. This is a warm-up to get their voice in your ear.

## 3. Step two: Section 2 of the report, whole (about 575 words)
Now the same people write their report. Its readers are the competition's judges, so Laura is "Laura" or "she", and
the team is "we". Figures are at Treasury prices of 6 November 2026 ("at today's rates" once is enough). The section
is called "Portfolio Implementation". A table of every holding at cost (Table 1) sits inside it; the table is not yours
to write, but you may point to it. Write it in five paragraphs, in this order, each with a short heading of your own.

1. **The lead (about 100 words).** What would be left of Laura's promises if every share she owned became worthless,
   and why. The name finance gives the method, and what Laura's plan adds to it. Then what "ink" and "pencil" mean in
   this report, in the fact sheet's terms, explained in the same sentence or the next, since the rest of the report
   uses them.
2. **What the portfolio is (about 130 words).** Why a $300,000 portfolio holds a late-2032 Treasury fund and a world
   share fund when Laura's first $300,000 buys only the ten payments' Treasuries; what each holding does; what the ten
   cost, and how the part $300,000 cannot buy is completed.
3. **How it was built (about 110 words).** The rule that decides the allocation; which payments rest on funds and
   which on bonds, and why; the three dated steps; why nothing needs rebalancing.
4. **How it is spread, and what is not spread (about 130 words).** Where the money is spread and where it is
   concentrated, and why the team accepts each concentration; where Laura's career reaches the plan, and what the plan
   does about it.
5. **How well it was bought (about 100 words).** The checks before and at each trade, what the trades cost, the rule
   about who places orders and the event that led to it, and how rare a year 2026 was for the ten payments.
