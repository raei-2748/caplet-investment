## What Keel thinks is going on (a hypothesis, to be tested)

Five ways a sentence can be decoded and still make no sense:
1. **The image does the explaining.** A name or metaphor (XR, the dose, crushing) is offered as if it were a reason.
   Decoded, the sentence says nothing the reader can use, or says something circular ("the plan works as long as
   nobody breaks the plan").
2. **Money that cannot exist yet is spent now.** A future amount (interest still to be earned, the difference between
   a cost and a payout) is put into a fund today. Decoded, it describes a transaction that cannot happen. It is really
   an equation from Keel's spreadsheet told as an event.
3. **A "because" or "so" that does not connect.** The stated reason does not produce the stated result unless the
   reader supplies a premise that only Keel holds.
4. **Things given properties they cannot have.** Payments "cost", the ends of a range are "owned", shares "risk only
   that interest". Each is shorthand for a longer true thing, but as written it is a category mistake.
5. **An answer to a question nobody has asked yet.** A sentence defends against an objection from Keel's own checks
   (a margin, money waiting at zero interest) before the reader knows there is a worry.

And four ways a sentence is hard to follow: a pointer with a far or double owner ("it", "the ones", "that half");
words left out for the reader to supply ("any which the 2027 one couldn't"); Keel's private names before they are
defined (the range, the bottom, the top, a rung, the 2028 holding); and three or more ideas in one sentence.
