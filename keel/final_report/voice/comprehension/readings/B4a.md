## What the clearest text does that the others don't

**P408** is the clearest. It introduces its outside idea, the pension regulator's rule, in one sentence and defines the only term it needs ("its 'sponsor'") the moment it appears. Then it gives the regulator's own example (the house builder) before mapping anything onto Laura. When it does map, it says the mapping out loud and names Laura's role precisely: "Laura's plan has a sponsor too, her own writing income", "the regulator's house builder, in Laura's world". Each sentence takes one step (rule, example, Laura's sponsor, why that sponsor is risky, only one call, what the plan does), and no sentence leans on a figure or term from outside the passage. It even marks where the analogy stops: "which is the team's own step, not the code's". P410 tries the same move and fails because it never says who plays which part. P405 is close behind for the same reason, with one risk per sentence, until its last sentence borrows "the small margin" from somewhere else.

## Any pattern

- **Analogies mapped by assertion.** The failures cluster where an outside example is laid onto Laura in a single word ("That half is the bought deal", "the exception both rules name") without saying who plays which role. P408 names the role and works; P410 doesn't and collapses.
- **Things carried in from outside the passage.** "Her condition", "the small margin held for them", "the move", "a top", "the meetings", "this one" all point at something the reader of that page hasn't been given.
- **Absolute words that the passage's own evidence doesn't reach.** "The only way left", "the oldest test", "exact", "whatever the ten cost", "nonexistent inflation risk". Each time, the sentence before supports something smaller.
- **Evidence that covers part of the claim.** P401.3 (seed size, not going first), P406.6 (currency and escalation, not overruns) and P402.2 (the pencil habit matches only the gains half).
- **Stacked conditions and figures in one sentence.** P411.1, P411.5 and P402.6 each carry two or three qualifications plus numbers, and the reader has to take them apart to find the claim.
- **Jargon in the team's own trade reflections** (P403, P407): "net asset value", "par", "odd-lot", "duration-matching", "TIPS", all unexplained. The clearest of those, P412, explains its one term in the next sentence.
