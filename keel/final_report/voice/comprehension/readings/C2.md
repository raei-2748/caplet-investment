## The sentences that made least sense

1. **F86 (dot gain).** The image works against you. A press prints darker, so printers lighten the file first. Your worry runs the other way: money coming out lighter (smaller) than the figure named. Inside that, three layers of brackets hold a figure ("about 2%"), a stray order ("never a year"), an old figure ("about 3%") and reviewer codes. Give the plain fact in one sentence, the cushion and what it is a percentage of, and drop the image unless its direction matches.
2. **F30.** "One step further" is quoted as if I knew it, and I don't. "Never the reason" doesn't say for what. A deleted phrase is spliced into the middle of the sentence. Say plainly that the first plan already counted as highly certain at 96.8 in 100, and that the team went further anyway. Then say what that bought.
3. **F20.** Within two sentences the extra Treasuries are there "so that the ten are paid" (F19) and are also "beyond the ten", never to be said to complete them. The reserve is the money for the ten, so a part of it "beyond the ten" needs explaining: is it a safety layer, a buffer, or what? Later, F92 has them locking in "the rest" of each payment, which is the thing F20 forbids saying.
4. **F40-F41.** If the top ($115,000) is what she gives and the range is "certain", the bottom's job disappears and the range looks pointless. "The part held since January 2028" doesn't say part of what. "Once both ends are owned" makes the ends of a range into things you can own. Then comes a ban on writing "certain" beside the figures, with no reason given. Say what the bottom is for once the top is locked.
5. **F85.** This one numbered line holds four items. "The 7.5%" turns up from nowhere. "No comics words ... because the printed page only shows ink" confuses which figures she states with which words she uses. "The range would be one figure" is only true if the shares fall before half are switched, and the sentence doesn't say so.
6. **F53.** "The 2003 meetings" and "before the contribution" refer to events that didn't happen. You are replaying 2000-2005 as if it were her 2027-2033, but you never say so. "But" sets up a contrast with F52 that isn't one.
7. **F29 with F31.** "That day" has no date. Two sentences later, F31 says the first model's "never missed one" was built into its code, not found by it, which makes me doubt the "none" I was just given. Say whether that "none" still stands and where it comes from.
8. **F16, F32, F89 (and the brackets in F2, F27).** These are edit history ("struck", "re-sized", "coherence reader 8, M6", "card Q109"). They tell a writer nothing, and they are numbered as if they were facts. Move them off the page.

## What the clearest text does that the others don't

## Any pattern

- **Edit history inside the facts.** Sentences marked "struck", "re-sized", "corrected" or "reordered", with reviewer codes (coherence reader 8, M8; card Q120; NUMBERS 8s), fail every time: F16, F27, F29-F32, F86, F88-F89. They also break up the sentences they sit in.
- **Writing rules welded to facts.** "Never say ...", "printed once, in Figure 1's note item 4", and "never put 'certain' in the same sentence" give no reason, so I can't tell what is true from what is house style (F20, F26, F41, F98).
- **Things used before they are introduced, or never introduced:** "that day", "one step further", "natural hedge", "the 7.5%", "the 2003 meetings", "the fee", "the margin the range carries" (F27, F29, F30, F52, F53, F85).
- **The page breaks its own rules.** It strikes "priced the change" and then says "priced" twice (F55, F90). It bans saying the extra Treasuries complete the ten and then has them ink "the rest" of each payment (F92). It separates "owned" from "inked" and then calls the ink rule the IPS's "money you already own" rule (F71). It says Treasuries "pay what they say" when some are funds that don't (F91).
- **Conceit items merged and images pointed the wrong way.** From F77 on, bullet headings are run into the previous sentence, so one numbered line carries two to four ideas. Some images contradict the fact they illustrate: an eraser beside "never rub out", and dot gain (darker) beside money coming out lighter.
- **The range logic.** The top is what she gives, the top is locked before she states it, and the range is "certain". Put together, these leave the bottom with no clear job, and every sentence about the range inherits that confusion (F40, F41, F85).
