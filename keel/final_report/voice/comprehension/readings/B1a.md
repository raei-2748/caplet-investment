## The sentences that made least sense

1. **P102.12 (and P106.13, the same sentence).** You have just told me, two sentences in, that a US default and a missing or shrunken 2028 deposit are how the ten payments fail. Then you say that the IPS's two reasons (a missed deposit, a Treasury default) are "not ways a promise fails". To a reader they are the same two events, so the passage contradicts itself. If you mean the IPS's list is a different kind of list (what makes us change the plan, not what breaks a payment), you have to say that the same events sit on both lists and why that doesn't matter. Otherwise cut the sentence.
2. **P102.7.** One sentence carries three things I've never been told about: "fund costs beyond the fees" (I thought a fee *was* the cost), "the room under it" and "cash waiting earning nothing". I couldn't tell whether the 2% is the size of the cushion or the size of the costs. Each of these needs to be set up before it is used, or left out.
3. **P104.13 (and P108.12).** "The IPS's two reasons to change the plan" are never named in the passage, so I'm told that a list I can't see belongs somewhere else. It answers a question I hadn't asked and points at nothing.
4. **P106.8.** The same trouble as P102.7. "The room under the figure" also made me unsure which way the gap runs: is the room below the figure, or what the fund pays above it?
5. **P109.4.** "net asset value", "par", "liquidity" and "odd-lot" all arrive unexplained. Once I guessed my way through, it undercut the sentence before it: if the fund's payout isn't a promise, "not merely a forecast" sounds too strong.
6. **P104.12 (and P108.11).** "The case's five areas" is a frame I don't have. You also never say why the share-return assumption would "barely move" a top that is partly bought with shares, when an earlier sentence (P101.5 and its versions) says a share fall *does* lower what she can state. I couldn't judge the claim.
7. **P105.3.** Sentence 1 says the bonds remove uncertainty, but this sentence counts down years of her money "being exposed" to markets. Is the money exposed or not? The image of a good year is nice, but the logic underneath it is muddled.
8. **P107.8.** "Co-sponsors work the same way": the same way as what? Capping grants and leaving overruns to the owner is a reason for Laura to keep money back, not the same behaviour as her round-down rule. "Points the same way" (P111.9) fixes it in two words.

## What the clearest text does that the others don't

**P111** (with P110 a close second). It names the thing in the first sentence ("a facility contribution of about $115,000"), so I never wonder what the number is for (compare P101.1's bare "Our recommendation is about $115,000"). It gives each sentence one job: "One Treasury fund maturing in late 2032 pays it." comes first, then how she buys it, then why that matters. It states the rule in plain words and lets the consequences follow from "so": "She can always state more later but never take a stated figure back, so her figure is rounded down...". That does everything "Ink only goes one way ... half her shares stay pencil" does, without making me decode an image nobody set up. And it doesn't use the private labels ("the top", "the room under it", "cash waiting", "fund costs beyond the fees", "the IPS's two reasons") that cost me time elsewhere. Its only snags are "the move" and "the day trading froze", which it shares with other texts.

## Any pattern

The failures are almost all words that live in the writer's head and not on the page: "the move", "the top", "the room under it", "cash waiting", "fund costs beyond the fees", "the case's five areas", "the IPS's two reasons", and the pencil/page image. Each is used as if I had already met it. The second pattern is the tidy closing sentence that sorts something into a category ("exits, not ways a promise fails", "a different list", "the same way"). These sentences answer a question I hadn't asked, and twice they seemed to contradict what the passage had just said. Third, the elliptical "No fall in share prices can." works only when the verb is in the sentence right before it. Every version puts a sentence about contract dates in between, and P108 never gives the reason at all. The trade-note passages (P105, P109) fail differently: they pile up finance jargon and slogans ("In other words", "exactly that", "perfectly") that claim more than they explain. The plain versions (P110, P111) carry the same ideas at no extra length.
