## The sentences that made least sense

1. **P302.8.** It says every pledge is released together and then that yours goes in first. Both can't be true unless "every" means "every conditional pledge", which you didn't write (P305.9 does). "Close date" also needs a plain-English gloss for a co-sponsor.
2. **P303.6.** "Its danger was never a crash but one date." A date can't be a danger, and a crash just before that date is exactly the danger. Say what P304.7 says: share prices in the wrong place on that one day. "All ten would have been bought" also makes the payments the thing bought. You mean the bonds or the reserve for them.
3. **P303.7.** "Priced the change and nothing more." The reader is never told what price was found, and the next sentence gives a miss rate, not a price. "Nothing more" answers an objection nobody raised (that the model made the decision). Either say what the model measured or cut the sentence.
4. **P304.6.** This is the same problem as P303.7, and it comes before the reader knows what the model measured, so "the change" has nothing to be priced against yet.
5. **P309.3.** If the bonds already remove the uncertainty (sentence 1), then the passing years can't reduce her exposure, because there is none to reduce. Either the bonds are exposed until maturity, which contradicts sentence 1, or the sentence is true of any holding and says nothing.
6. **P301.4.** "Their terminal payout is a net asset value" follows "our T-Bonds and iBonds", but the T-Bonds do repay par. Name the iBonds. The liquidity gain also needs a reason to matter, since the passage has just said everything is held to the end.
7. **P309.1.** "This one" points at a bond the reader can't see. "Won't create wealth" is false, because Treasuries pay interest. "Duration-matching" is unexplained and is not quite what a bond-per-payment ladder does.
8. **P302.6.** The "so" makes the currency the reason the gift won't rise, but the reason is that the sum is fixed. P305.7 and P306.8 say "fixed in US dollars" and avoid the problem.

## What the clearest text does that the others don't

**P306** is the clearest. Every sentence has a subject and a full date ("I bought them in January 2027 and completed the set in January 2028"; "since my shares moved into Treasuries in January 2031"), so nothing has to be supplied. P302 and P307 drop the subject ("Bought in 2027, finished in 2028") and the year ("in January"). P306 also uses everyday words where the others use trade terms: "closing date" rather than "close date", "before construction needs its first payment" rather than "the building's first draw", and "the firm holding my Treasuries" rather than "the custodian". It gives each step its own sentence: the condition (10), what follows from it (11) and her own gift (12). That makes the logic easy to check. It also makes its one slip easy to see, "every pledge" set against "my figure comes first", which P305's "every conditional pledge" avoids. P305 is the most airtight logically, but it is a little denser.

## Any pattern

- **Scope words reach further than the writer meant.** "Their" (P301.4), "every pledge" (P302.8, P306.11) and "already paid" (P307.2) each make a claim about more things, or a different time, than the writer intended. The fix is usually one qualifier: "the iBonds'", "every conditional pledge", "paid for".
- **An image or shorthand verb stands where a mechanism should be.** Examples are "in pencil" (glossed only by a dash or used as its own "because"), "priced the change", "never a crash but one date", "standing between Laura and a payment deadline", and "the same side as her work". Where the next clause spells out the mechanism, the sentence works (P304.7, P303.4). Where it doesn't, the reader has to rebuild it (P303.6, P303.7, P304.6).
- **Connectors promise more than they deliver.** "So" in P302.6, "In other words" in P309.2 and the dash in P309.3 each present a link that isn't there.
- **Overstatement creates contradictions within a passage.** P309's "remove uncertainty", "won't create wealth", "exactly" and "nonexistent" leave its own third sentence with nothing to say.
- **One argument-level gap in P303 and P304.** The plan is dropped because her income shrinks in the same recessions as shares. But the replacement plan is safe only "once the 2028 deposit's first dollars arrive", and that deposit also comes from her income. Neither passage says why that dependence is acceptable when the first one was not. (Presumably only a small part of the deposit is needed, but the text doesn't say so.)
