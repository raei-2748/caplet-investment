## The sentences that made least sense

1. **P303.7 (and its twin P304.6), "priced the change and nothing more" / "just priced the change".** I cannot tell what change, what "priced" means, or why I am being told "nothing more". It reads like a reply to an accusation I never heard. Say what the model measured and what it showed, or cut it.
2. **P302.8, "every pledge releases together – mine goes in first, unconditionally".** The sentence contradicts itself. If every pledge releases together, hers cannot go in first. Say "every conditional pledge", as P305 does. It is also a long sentence with no main verb, carrying "close date" and "releases" unexplained.
3. **P309.3, "Every year the bonds sit untouched is a good year".** The passage has just said the bonds remove uncertainty and give a near-ironclad guarantee. Now it says her money is still "exposed" to markets and each year shrinks the exposure. Both cannot be true. "Every year is a good year" is also true of anything.
4. **P301.4, "their terminal payout is a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par".** It uses three unexplained terms (net asset value, par, odd-lot) in one sentence, and "their" could be both kinds of bond. It also praises liquidity right after saying the funds will be held to the end and price swings ignored, so the trade-off does not land.
5. **P303.6, "its danger was never a crash but one date".** A date is not a danger, and a crash just before that date is precisely the danger, so the contrast is false as worded. P304.7 fixes it with "share prices in the wrong place on that one date". "All ten would have been bought" also makes the payments sound like things you buy.
6. **P306.12, "My figure comes first, with no condition."** It comes straight after "they all release together". First in what sense? Either hers is not one of "every pledge", or it does not come first.
7. **P309.1, "including this one – maturing 2037" and "through duration-matching".** "This one" points at a bond that is not on the page, and the mechanism is a term the reader is not given.
8. **P306.5, "They're guaranteed, and I'm not asking anyone for them."** The pronoun changes owner. "Them" in the sentence before meant the Treasuries, and here it has to mean the payments.

## What the clearest text does that the others don't

**P305** is the clearest. Every sentence is a full sentence with a verb and a subject, so nothing hangs: compare "Bought in 2027, finished in 2028" (P302, P307) with P305's dates attached to a named Treasury. Every date carries its year: "January 2031", where P302 and P307 say only "in January" and leave me to work out which. It names each thing in the same sentence where it appears: "the custodian holding the Treasuries", not a bare "the custodian" (P302). Above all, it makes the pledge condition consistent: "every conditional pledge releases together", then "Mine comes first and carries no condition". The quantifier excludes her own gift, so the two sentences do not fight, as they do in P302 and P306. Its only snags are "the building's first draw" and what "completed" attaches to. P306 is nearly as clear and even replaces "draw" with "before construction needs its first payment", but it slips on "they/them" and on "every pledge ... my figure comes first".

Among the team's reflections, **P304** beats P303. It gives the reason before the image, says "would have fallen with her career instead of balancing it" rather than "on the same side as her work", and makes the danger "share prices in the wrong place on that one date", not "one date".

## Any pattern

- **Compression:** the failures are where a writer squeezes: verbless fragments and dangling participles ("Bought in 2027, finished in 2028", "Capital for the facility, conditional on...") that leave out who did what.
- **Unexplained terms:** trade words dropped in without explanation (net asset value, par, odd-lot, duration-matching, close date, draw, custodian, nominal, TIPS). Each one costs a re-read, and three in one sentence (P301.4) lose me.
- **Answers to questions nobody asked:** sentences rebut a claim that was never made ("priced the change and nothing more", "never a crash"), so I have to imagine the argument they are answering.
- **Scope that shifts:** a pronoun or quantifier changes what it covers between sentences ("they/them" in P306; "every pledge" and then "mine first" in P302 and P306).
- **Images that pull against the literal claim:** an investment "standing between Laura and a deadline" sounds like an obstacle, and a "good year" of exposure follows a guarantee of no exposure. "In pencil" works only when it is explained in the same sentence, as in P303.6 and P304.7.
- **A gap in Laura's letters:** all four versions say the running costs are paid and then ask for yearly programme money, without saying what that money is for.
