## The sentences that made least sense

1. **P204.6.** Three loose ends in one sentence. "That" at the start refers back to the previous sentence. "That room" has no owner, because the 2% was presented as a threshold, not as room. "Rounding down made the rest" never says what was rounded. Say what fails, what the 2% is a threshold for, and what was rounded down before you use those words.
2. **P211.3** (and its twin **P206.3**). The reason for the deposit ("since/because a downturn delays a writer's income first") sits in the middle of a three-item list. In P206.3 it reads first as the reason for the whole list. In P211.3 the "or fund costs ..." that follows reads as a second reason for the deposit, when it is a third cause. "First" (before what?) and "waiting cash" are never explained. Give each cause its own clause and put the reason right after the item it belongs to.
3. **P204.10** (and **P206.9, P209.8, P211.10**). It announces "the case's five areas" and lists four. The case as I was given it names no five areas, so the reader can't find the missing one. "Timing is the 2028 deposit" sets a category equal to an event; the meaning has to be supplied ("the only timing risk is whether the 2028 deposit arrives").
4. **P209.4.** The "because" explains only why the headroom is larger than the margin. It doesn't show where "about 2%" comes from or what the 2% is a threshold for. As a reason it is partial, and "rounding down" is again unexplained.
5. **P211.6.** "Never what she has" is false as written, because falling shares do shrink what she owns. It only works if the reader adds "stated". In a sentence about promises, that one dropped word turns a true claim into a false one.
6. **P205.3.** Every year the bonds go untouched is "a good year" whatever happens, so the claim is empty. It also clashes with sentence 1: if the bonds already remove uncertainty, her money isn't "exposed" during those years to begin with. "Restless markets might decide" is an image doing the work of a reason.
7. **P204.7.** "The move" is never introduced in this passage. The claim (falling shares only lower what she can state) may well be right, but the reader can't tell before what event it holds.
8. **P204.11** (and its twins). It is called "a separate list" (in P206, "something else"), but its two items are the same events the passage has just named as causes of failure. The only distinction offered is the label "exits", so the sentence answers a confusion the reader didn't have until this point.

Close behind: P205.2 ("In other words" brings in a new claim, not a restatement), P201.7 ("comes first" never says she still pays), P202.6 (the currency, not the fixed figure, is given as the reason it won't rise).

## What the clearest text does that the others don't

**P203** (with P208, its near twin as a letter, almost level). Each sentence carries one claim, and its reason sits right next to it. "Fixed in US dollars, so it will not rise" uses the right reason, being fixed, where P202.6 leans on the currency. "Since January 2031, when I moved shares into Treasuries" says why that date matters. "One year at a time, which is how Taiwan's funders give" gives the reason for the request. Where other versions use a vague phrase, P203 spells out the unconditional part: "comes first and does not depend on anyone else", not "comes first" on its own (P201.7) or "comes first either way" (P202.9). It names its subjects ("My contribution", "My own $115,000") instead of leaning on "it". It explains "its own organisation" by saying what that organisation does ("which owns the site and runs the building"). And it names what is not on offer ("neither my ten payments nor the rest of my money") instead of "nothing else of mine". Every date is a calendar date, and no word depends on another passage to be understood.

## Any pattern

Nearly all the failures are in the four analytical passages on how each promise fails (P204, P206, P209, P211) and in the trade reflection P205. The spoken passages (P201-P203, P208) almost all pass. The failing sentences share four habits:

- **Working terms used as if already defined:** "the fee", "the margin", "the move", "the top", "waiting cash", "rounding down", "the case's five areas", "pencil line". Each is probably defined somewhere else in the report, but read on its own the sentence points at nothing.
- **Reasons placed where they attach to the wrong thing.** A "because" or "since" sits in the middle of a list (P206.3, P211.3), or explains only part of the claim (P209.4). Connectors also promise a relation the sentence doesn't deliver: "in other words" before a consequence or a new claim (P204.5, P205.2, P211.4), "and" where the logic is "but" (P206.8, P209.7, P211.9).
- **Labels offered as reasons:** "timing is the 2028 deposit", "exits", "through duration-matching", "a good year", "restless markets". The name stands where the mechanism should be.
- **Compression that changes the meaning.** Ellipsis ("never what she has", "her word to co-sponsors with it") and counts that don't match their lists (five areas, four items). Each saves a word or two and costs the reader a re-read, or turns a true claim false.

The passages that work say who does what, when, and why, in that order, inside the sentence itself.
