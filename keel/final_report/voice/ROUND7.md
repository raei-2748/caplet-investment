## What the readers heard (sentences about how passages felt, never rules)

- **The whole reflection is heard as the team's, and loses ground where the record piles up.** Both plain reflections
  were heard as the team's by all three readers (mean rank 5.0 and 5.3, the best practice passages in the pack). What
  carried them was "the team's candour and its people": "which turned out to be generous", "timing and luck, not skill",
  Eric asleep before the 00:30 open, Darren's "copout", and one line a reader "could hear the IPS writers saying"
  ("certain payments can't lean on luck twice"). What held them below Caplet's own was density: "far denser with dates
  and exact figures than anything in the IPS", "reads like a careful log turned into prose", and a closing sentence
  that names "the knife edge" as if the reader had already met it. Both writers said the same in their notes: the third
  moment, carrying a bar, four alternatives, a near miss and three reasons in about 220 words, is where the explaining
  stalled. Both had to drop figures (13.7%, 0.8991) to stay under 600 words.
- **The definition at first use costs nothing, and lands best when its examples come from Laura's life.** Both opening
  pages with the definition were heard as the team's by all three readers. M3, which gives pencil three examples from
  her own life ("the 2028 deposit before it arrives, the value of her shares, her next advance") and sets them beside her
  own words about not knowing how a book will do, was the reading two readers singled out: it "made the idea feel like
  hers, not a device", and "VT is the one row in pencil" made the point "at a glance". N3's "owned, not merely signed
  for" did the opposite of its job: two readers stopped to think about signatures, the reading it meant to rule out.
  Saying "owned" is enough; naming "signed" brings it in.
- **A second printing image fails, again.** M2's knockout and overprint was heard as the team's by no reader (0 of 3,
  rank 11.7): it "stops mid-sentence to explain a printing term" and "takes over the passage", "a specialist reaching
  for a clever comparison; the IPS's own images are simple and never need explaining". It is the third printing image
  outside pencil and ink to fail blind (the key plate, round 6; the tacky-ink smear, round 6). Its writer's own note
  said the printer's gloss sat between dashes "and a listener may hear the printing lesson before the reason".
- **"Our first pencil, rubbed out" passes, and bends the definition.** N2 was heard as the team's by all three readers
  and the image "mostly helped" ("a quick, vivid way to say the plan was dropped before anything was bought"), but all
  three said it stretches pencil from a figure to a whole plan. The working form, if a later round tests it, keeps the
  definition: the first plan's payments were in pencil, because no Treasury it owned would pay them. The passage lost
  most of its rank to a staged reveal ("The surprise was where the danger had been sitting. Not in a crash – on one
  date."), not to the image.
- **Across the readers:** the highest passages "sound like students talking to a client they like: quick, a little
  grand, carried by dashes and plain glosses, with a small clumsiness here and there that a careful editor would have
  smoothed". The lowest "lose the person", "tighten into an analyst's aphorisms", or "reach for a staged reveal or a
  specialist printing image that stops the sentence to explain itself".
