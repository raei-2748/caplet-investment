## Understanding (the check questions)

- **3e: all twelve answers right.** Every reader named a US default, a 2028 deposit that never comes or comes far
  smaller, and fund costs beyond the fee outgrowing the margin as what could make a payment short or late, and said a
  fall in shares could not; every reader gave "costs beyond the fees passing about 2% of what the two Treasury funds
  behind the top pay" for a stated figure. The failure-first shape carried the facts in every passage, plain or turned.
- **Laura's words: twelve of twelve right on the figure and the dates** ($115,000 booked; $95,000 held since January
  2028, the rest since January 2031) and on the condition (no co-sponsor pays if the total is not promised by December
  2032). One gap, in both of Q's passages: "mine still comes first" left two readers unsure whether her own figure is
  paid anyway. R's "her own $115,000 comes first either way" was read right by all three.

## What the readers heard

- **The plain 3e that won (R1) moves one risk per sentence and shuts the door with a short line:** "A US default could
  do that. So could ... No fall in share prices can." Two readers heard that line as the IPS's own "A fall in the market
  is not one of them." The passages that lost packed several conditions into one sentence, each with a qualifier
  ("costs beyond the funds' fees would need to pass about 2% of ..."), and broke their grammar mid-list ("since a
  downturn delays a writer's income first" wedged into the list, all three readers on Q1 and Q2).
- **The turn was understood and did not carry voice.** Five of six readings said the image helped, at least a little,
  because "put the shares at zero" glosses it at once; none said it made the passage sound more like the team. Where it
  came after the list it read as a recap; where it came first it interrupted the argument before the risks were said;
  "the range down to one figure, the bottom" took two readers a second read. Same pattern as rounds 3-4 and 7 (one turn
  below plain), against round 5-6's turns that were the only place the point was made.
- **Laura's words: spoken beats written, and contractions help.** The two spoken versions were heard as a person at a
  table ("Thank you for seeing me", "topped them up", "My broker can send you a statement", "either way"); the written
  notes as careful drafts for her to sign, stiff where they switch between "I'm" and "I am" or set one sentence alone as
  a paragraph. No reader heard a fundraising term this round.
- **What separates the top from the bottom (all three readers):** a "we" thinking aloud, dashes that turn the thought,
  a word that reaches a little far, the fact first and then a short line that shuts the door; at the bottom, a checklist
  turned into prose, with no one being spoken to.

## What changes (for SKELETON 3e and 3d, and for the Fri 27 Nov writers)

   can."; the dash for "nothing breaks – it's a limit"). Keep the facts SKELETON row 3e already holds; write each note as
   its own short paragraph; the "because a downturn delays a writer's income first" clause gets its own sentence, never a
   clause inside the list. Every reader got the two figures right, so the two-figure budget stands.
2. **3e's one turn stays in CONCEIT V2.2's place, and is tested once more before 27 Nov in a new spot:** as the gloss of
   the short closing line itself (shares at zero, the page still reads), in place of a separate sentence, so the image is

   conceit's store with this round as its reason (Caplet's call that the conceit stays is kept: it runs through the other
   parts, one turn per part).
3. **3d: the spoken version leads**, and the condition is said R's way, naming whose: "If the pledges aren't all made by
   December 2032, none of the co-sponsors has to pay, and my own $115,000 comes first either way." Contractions
   throughout; no one-sentence paragraph.
4. **Lengths:** every passage ran 5-15% over its band; the Fri 27 Nov writers get the same bands, and the main session
   cuts whole ideas, never squeezes sentences (Book 10).
