## Topic one (P1 written; P2 spoken): Laura's words to co-sponsors in 2031
Who reads it: a program officer at a foundation or a company's giving office, in Taiwan or the US, deciding whether to
take a meeting and what figure to put in a board paper. They read for thirty seconds. Checked facts, in the order the
words should follow:
1. **Her ten operating payments are already owned.** $50,000 each January, 2033 to 2042, for the residency's running
   costs, each paid by a US Treasury held for her that matures just before its January. Bought in January 2027 and
   completed in January 2028. Say "held for me", never "in my name". She is not asking anyone for these.
2. **Her facility contribution, one figure, then what it rests on.** One sentence that promises: she will give
   $115,000 to the facility (amount, purpose and when, in that one sentence; the money is paid out in December 2032,
   before the building's first draw in 2033). No "about" before it, and never "intend", "expect", "hope", "aim",
   "at least" or "up to" on it. Then one sentence of fact under it: $95,000 of it has been held for her in Treasuries
   since January 2028, and the rest since her shares moved into Treasuries in January 2031. Never "secured". At most,
   if it fits, "with a margin kept for the funds' running costs"; no rates, no percentages.
3. **It is fixed in US dollars.** It does not rise if the building costs more. One dated size in Taiwan dollars may sit
   beside it, labelled as today's size and not a promise: about NT$3.77 million at 32.768 Taiwan dollars to the US dollar
   on 6 November 2026 (written as "today", it would be the 2031 rate; keep the 2026 date if you use it).
4. **The ask, in two parts.** (a) Capital for the facility, pledged on the condition that the facility's total is
   reached by one close date; the residency sets that total in its budget before these meetings, and every pledge closes
   on one date, no later than when Laura's contribution is paid in December 2032, so all conditional pledges release
   together. Her own figure comes first and is not conditional. Do not invent the total; say it is set in the residency's
   budget. (b) Yearly programme money from 2033, which is how Taiwan's funders give (one year at a time).
5. **Who receives it.** The residency, its own body in Taiwan, which owns the site and runs the building.
6. **What she never asks for or offers.** Her ten payments are not on the table; she does not ask anyone to match her
   figure; the rest of her money is not part of the offer (do not name it or size it; she keeps it for her own reasons).
7. **Proof.** A statement from the custodian holding the Treasuries, on request.
Do not use finance terms a program officer would have to look up (no "ladder", "maturity profile", "net asset value",
