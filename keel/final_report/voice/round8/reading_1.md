# Reading 1, voice round 8 (blind reader 1)

I read the IPS twice, the second time slowly, then the thirteen passages.

### Passage 1

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** The same story as the IPS's opening turn, told by people looking back with dates and their own notes in hand, and it has human moments (Eric asleep before a 00:30 open, "luck, not skill"). But it opens with a stiff disclosure line, packs a lot of facts into each sentence, and closes on a neat "two changed and one held" summary that sounds more like a report template than the IPS's writers.
- **Meaning:** The team learned three things: the first deposit only just bought the ten payments, so the 2028 deposit's first job is to finish them; it dropped a mostly-shares plan because Laura's income falls when shares do; and it kept the world fund after a challenge, now for reasons specific to Laura.
- **Check question (reflection, first plan):** They dropped it because a writer's income is "lumpy" and comes later and smaller in the same recessions that knock shares down, so shares would fall just when her income did. The first model only priced the change: the first plan missed at least one payment in about 1 in 31 runs, the Treasury plan none once the 2028 deposit arrives, and it showed the danger was one date, 1 January 2033, when all ten payments had to be bought.
- **Check question (reflection, world fund):** They kept VT. Before, it was a sensible default (cheap, spread wide); after, they could say why it suits Laura: it is the one holding allowed to be wrong and only wrong with the whole market, it needs no opinions so it never becomes a third reason to change the plan, and a Taiwan fund would add more Taiwan to a plan whose building is already there.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 2

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It moves like the IPS does: "the usual answer to a six-year wait" echoes the IPS's "6 years to wait would usually own shares", and asides like "luck, honestly" sound like a person talking. It is still smoother and more measured than the IPS, and the closing three-part "is why ... is why ... left VT where it was" sentence is a tidy wrap the IPS never uses.
- **Meaning:** The tight fit of the first deposit gave the 2028 deposit its first job, Laura's lumpy income is why every payment is in Treasuries, and Darren's "copout" challenge left VT in place for Laura-specific reasons.
- **Check question (reflection, first plan):** They dropped the shares plan before any trade because her income comes later and smaller in the recessions that pull shares down. The model that day only priced the change (first plan 1 in 31 runs missing a payment, the Treasury plan none once the 2028 deposit's first dollars arrive) and showed the danger sat on one date, 1 January 2033.
- **Check question (reflection, world fund):** They kept VT; it went from a cheap, wide default to a fund kept because it is the one holding allowed to be wrong, can only be wrong with the whole market, needs no opinions, and does not add to Taiwan risk the building already carries.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 3

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It sounds like a speech written to be said aloud ("a hundred and fifteen thousand dollars", "before the builders need a cent"), clear and confident. But it is clipped into fragments ("Bought in 2027, finished in 2028." "Then two asks." "Mine has no condition.") in a way that feels scripted and compressed, a long way from the IPS writers' looser, chattier sentences.
- **Meaning:** Laura tells a co-sponsor the running costs are already paid by Treasuries, she gives $115,000 for the building in December 2032, and she asks for conditional building capital and yearly programme money, with no matching and her other money off the table.
- **Check question (Laura to a co-sponsor):** The figure is $115,000, paid December 2032; $95,000 of it has been in Treasuries since 2028 and the rest since her shares moved over in January, so all of it is held now. She does not ask anyone for the running costs, does not ask anyone to match her, and the rest of her money is not part of this.
- **Image:** Not used.

- **Meaning:** Laura says the ten running payments are paid by Treasuries, gives $115,000 for the building in December 2032, and asks for conditional capital and yearly programme money, with no matching and nothing else of hers on the table.
- **Check question (Laura to a co-sponsor):** $115,000, paid December 2032; $95,000 has been in Treasuries since 2028 and the rest since her shares moved over in January. She does not ask for the running costs, does not ask anyone to match her, and the ten payments and the rest of her money are not on the table.

### Passage 5

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** A calm, single-paragraph account that keeps the IPS's idea of her career as something the portfolio should balance ("fallen with her career instead of balancing it"). It is more even-tempered than the IPS and lands on a careful concession rather than a quip, but the reasoning runs the way the IPS's does.
- **Meaning:** The team dropped a three-quarters-shares plan because a writer's income falls in the same recessions as shares; the model then showed that plan's real danger was one date, and that 30 in 31 was not enough for this client.
- **Check question (reflection, first plan):** Because her advances, fees and licences come later and smaller in the recessions that pull shares down, so a share-heavy portfolio would fall with her career. The first model only priced the change: about 1 in 31 runs missed a payment against none for the Treasury plan once the 2028 deposit arrives, with the danger on 1 January 2033.
- **Image:** Helped a little: "in pencil" told me at once that nothing owned would pay those payments yet, though the sentence spells that out straight away, so the word mostly signposts rather than adds.

### Passage 6

### Passage 9

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** It is a compressed explainer in the third person, built around two polished maxims ("a number said aloud can't quietly be halved", "her spare money shrinks before her promise does"). It names a weakness in the ladder and says "the whole range comes down", which cuts against the IPS's "immune to markets" and "the bottom will remain constant", so it does not sound like the people who signed that letter.
- **Meaning:** In 2031 Laura's range runs from the owned floor (rounded down) to that plus half her shares moved into Treasuries; because the ladder relies on reinvesting interest, any shortfall is priced and covered from her kept half first, and if that is not enough the range falls.
- **Check question:** None fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 11

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** This is the IPS's sound: eager, a little grand ("near-ironclad", "the restless markets might decide to do"), dashes and "Notably –", and the same move as the IPS's "take that one step further" in "we take ... to mean ... and we provide exactly that". - **Meaning:** The 2037 Treasury will not grow wealth but removes uncertainty by matching a payment; the team reads "high degree of certainty" as a near guarantee, and since the payments are fixed in dollars a nominal Treasury fits and TIPS are not needed.
- **Check question:** None fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 12

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** The most natural of the reflections: "Then we thought about how a writer is paid" is the IPS's own turn ("until we stopped and considered") told in the past tense, and "for most clients it would be" is a fair, human concession. It is calmer than the IPS but sounds like the same people thinking it over.
- **Meaning:** The team first answered Laura's taste for risk with shares, then dropped that plan because her income falls with shares; the model only priced the change, and 30 in 31 was not enough for how Laura is paid.
- **Check question (reflection, first plan):** Because her lumpy income arrives later and smaller in the recessions that pull shares down, putting her savings on the same side as her work. The model that day priced the change and nothing more: about 1 in 31 runs missed a payment, none for the Treasury plan once the 2028 deposit's first dollars arrive.
- **Image:** Helped: "in pencil" followed by the dash made the point that the danger was one date, not a crash, click faster than the bare explanation would.

### Passage 13

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** The IPS's writers all over: "thousands of companies" straight from the IPS, "T-Bonds and iBonds", one long run-on sentence that piles up clauses ("which means ... instead buying ..."), a grand phrase ("the vain illusion that we can pick winners"), "as per JPMorgan", and a closing question. Slightly overcooked in the way a keen student is.
- **Meaning:** VT is the one place the team can afford to be wrong, because no payment depends on it; it buys the whole market cheaply, so it decides how generous the facility contribution can be.

- **Image:** Not used.

### What separates the top from the bottom

The passages I ranked highest have the IPS's own unevenness: enthusiasm, dashes, a grand word or two ("ever-reliable", "restless markets", "vain illusion"), sentences that run on and then land on a question or a quip, and holdings talked about as things the team actually bought. The middle group (the reflections and the notes in Laura's voice) carry the same reasoning and facts in a calmer, more even hand, with dates and careful concessions and tidy summary endings. They are believable as the same team writing more carefully, but they are smoother than the IPS. The lowest read like explainers by someone outside the team: textbook framing (pension funds), compressed maxims, and in Passage 9 a mechanism the IPS never mentions that contradicts its promise that the bottom stays constant.
