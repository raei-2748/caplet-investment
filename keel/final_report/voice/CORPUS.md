## 3. The thinking voice: how the team talks when it is working something out

Caplet's messages to Keel, kept verbatim in the archived Bible ([`archive/bible_2026-11-08/BIBLE.md`](../../../archive/bible_2026-11-08/BIBLE.md)).
The longest, most characteristic are Books 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 17 (Ahaan's and the team's long messages) and
Ray's shorter, quicker ones quoted in Books 1-5 and the register. These are not the register of the report: they are
chat, full of capitals, parentheses and second thoughts. They are here because they show how these people reason out
loud, what they find funny, how they concede a point and then turn it, and how much they care. Writing that is true to
them carries that person, in a more formal register.

### The Trading Note reflections (23 Oct), as submitted

> **The Treasury maturing 15 Feb 2037.** We know Treasury bonds (including this one – maturing 2037) won't create
> wealth, but they will remove uncertainty, through duration-matching. In other words, we take Laura's requested 'high
> degree of certainty' to mean a near-ironclad guarantee, and we provide exactly that. Every year the bonds sit
> untouched is a good year – one less year of Laura's money being exposed to anything the restless markets might decide
> to do. Notably – Laura's payments are fixed in nominal dollars, so a nominal Treasury matches the liability
> perfectly, meaning we don't need TIPS, which would hedge against a nonexistent inflation risk.

> **IBTM.** Some years, WInS lists no Treasuries maturing – so we purchased iShares iBonds (like IBTM for 2032)
> instead. Defined-maturity, investment-grade, liquid, cheap and transparent about yields, these behave a lot like
> bonds when held to their end – so we'll simply ignore price noise in the interim. Together, our T-Bonds and iBonds
> ensure each of Laura's payments rests on a dedicated instrument (not merely a forecast). The catch is, their terminal
> payout is a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par – but in return we gain a level of liquidity which no
> odd-lot bond offers.

> **VT.** VT is where we allow ourselves to be wrong – because it's the only investment not standing between Laura and
> a payment deadline. This ETF owns thousands of companies, diversified across countries and sectors, which means we
> can avoid paying the price for the vain illusion that we can pick winners, instead buying the entire market's growth
> premium at an expense ratio of just 0.06%, for a forecast return of about 7.5% a year (as per JPMorgan). The T-Bonds
> and iBonds already cover the operating commitments, so VT answers a different question – how generous can the
> facility contribution be?
