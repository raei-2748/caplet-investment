# The voice programme, Sat 14 Nov to Thu 26 Nov 2026

Caplet, Book 17 (Sat 14 Nov): voice "IS what sank the IPS". "You cannot begin from the position of your own default
generic AI writing style and work from there by trying to make it sound more human; you must START with OUR voice and
style and tone, and move forward from there. Literally write like we do." And: "it cannot be resolved even with 1000
rules."

This folder is how Keel learns to do that before Fri 27 Nov, when the report is written. Content still comes first;
this runs beside it, in every run, as a real share of the work.

## What changed, and what is retired

The first study (`STUDY1_two_voices.md`, 8 Nov) set Keel's sentences against Caplet's, looked at word changes, and
closed with six rules. Caplet's verdict: rules at best take out the worst sentences and leave Keel's style as the
backbone. So:
- No rule lists come out of this programme. What gets written down after a round is a few honest sentences about how
  whole passages felt next to Caplet's, and then the passages are written again, whole.
- No word-level comparison, no counts, no "what the humans did that I didn't".
- The older rule-shaped voice files (`STUDY1_two_voices.md`'s rules, `keel/voice/04_patterns.md`,
  `keel/voice/06_voice_guide.md`, the "moves" in `keel/EXPECTATIONS.md` note 3.3) stay as history, not method.

## The method

1. **Read them whole, every time.** Each round starts by reading Caplet's own writing (`CORPUS.md`) from start to
   finish, the IPS aloud, before a word is written: the IPS, the Trading Note reflections, the team's own lines, the
   long messages in which the team thinks aloud, and Ahaan's essays for how the same people carry an argument over
   pages. The aim is to hear the people, not to collect their habits.
2. **Write forward from inside their writing.** Three exercises, all borrowed from how writers have long learnt a voice:
   - *Rebuilding (Benjamin Franklin's method).* Franklin taught himself to write by taking short notes on essays he
     admired, setting the essays aside, rebuilding them from the notes days later, and comparing (*Autobiography*,
     written 1771-1790). Here: a paragraph of Caplet's IPS reduced to a few words per sentence, rebuilt from the notes
     alone, then read against the original as a whole.
   - *Continuing.* Start from the last lines of a Caplet text and keep going into new material, as the same people
     would.
   - *Moving house.* The same people on a new subject: a part of the Final Report, from the plain facts
     (`FACTS_PLAIN.md`), never from Keel's working files.
3. **Test whole passages, blind.** Readers who have read Caplet's IPS get a mixed, unlabelled set: practice passages,
   real Caplet passages (as a check on the readers) and passages from Keel's old drafts (as controls). For each passage
   they say whether the people who wrote the IPS could have written it as part of a longer report, in a sentence or two
   about the passage as a whole, and they say what the passage means in one sentence, so comprehension is tested at
   the same time. The test is about whose voice it is, never about anything else.
4. **Two writers, side by side.** Each round has a fresh writer who has read only Caplet's texts and the plain facts,
   and Keel's main session, which carries all of Keel's working files. If the fresh writer comes out closer to Caplet,
   the working files themselves are pulling the voice away (see `WHY_OPAQUE.md`), and 27 Nov is planned around that.

## Results so far

- **Round 1 (Sat 14 Nov), `ROUND1.md`.** Read blind by three readers: Caplet's own reflections were heard as Caplet's
  9 times in 9; the fresh writers 13 times in 18; Keel's main session 0 times in 9, just above Keel's old IPS. The main
  session's passages were clear but "too finished": built set pieces, neat reversals, rounded endings. So on 27 Nov the
  report's sentences are written by fresh writers who have read only Caplet's writing and the plain facts, and the main
  session checks afterwards.

- **The conceit (Sat 14 Nov, evening).** Caplet chose "pencil and ink" for the report (`../CONCEIT.md`). From round 2
  every writer gets its plain-words summary in `FACTS_PLAIN.md`, and the readers judge it like everything else: by ear,
  blind. For the 2031 range passage the readers are also scored, not polled (card Q42: readers prefer metaphor even
  where it does not help them): each restates the range rule in their own words and answers one applied question ("in
  January 2030, may Laura tell a co-sponsor $115,000?"; no, the top is not yet owned). A restatement with "owned"
  and "barring a US default" counts; "committed", "promised" or "signed" is a miss. Trim, bleed and safe area is tried
  in one conceit version and stays only if its readers do better (`../CONCEIT.md` section 10). Round 1 warned that set-piece openings and rounded endings read as Keel, so an image sentence never opens or
  closes a passage.
