## 3. How round 1 went (so you can build on it; these are descriptions, not rules)
Three blind readers heard the team's real writing as: people "sure of their plan, warm towards Laura, and plainly
enjoying the idea: explaining in quick bursts, with asides and turns on a dash, reaching a little further than they need
to, and saving one flourish for the end"; "students pleased with their plan and keen to talk it up"; "teenagers keen to
sound professional and to reassure, not like someone landing polished lines". Writing that lost was "too finished":
"staged set pieces, neat reversals, rounded endings", "a teacher's guided-tour structure", "a rhythmic, essayistic
build-up that repeats one sentence shape for effect". One writer said it got closer by "keeping their order of thought
rather than any particular words"; another, once it let the writing have "a pulse: it gets excited, it reassures".
The team does not hedge: Treasuries may be called guaranteed, locked, set in stone or risk-free, and nothing needs
"barring a US default" attached (Book 19). Accuracy still matters: never state a false fact.

## 4. Warm up (write it in your output file)
- Franklin's method: take the IPS paragraph that starts "The building is a different matter". Write a few words of notes per sentence. Put the IPS away. Rebuild the paragraph from your notes alone. Compare in two sentences: where did yours drift?
- Continuing: start after "Yours, Caplet" as if the same people were now writing the next page of a longer report (not a letter) for a judge, and write about 80 words.

## 5. The four passages (about 300-350 words each)
Each is a part of a longer Final Report, which is not a letter: it is an in-depth analysis of the plan, written for
Wharton judges, in the team's voice. It evaluates and explains; it never just repeats the IPS.
- P1. How certain are the ten payments? What "a high degree of certainty" means here, how the plan meets it, and what the first $38,282 of the 2028 deposit buys.
- P2. Why half the shares are promised to no one (her flexibility), and what that choice costs and buys.
- P3. The 2031 range: how Laura states it ($95,000 to $115,000 on today's figures, by a rule), why nothing is stated before half the shares move into Treasuries, and how sure she can be.
- P4. For the Team Reflection: the 5 October "copout" argument, and why the team kept the world fund. Pick the reasons that land best; you need not use them all.
