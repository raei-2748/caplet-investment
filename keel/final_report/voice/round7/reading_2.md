# Voice round 7: blind reading, reader 2

I read the IPS twice, the second time slowly, then the thirteen passages in my pack. The IPS's writers sound like
this to me: warm, direct, a little excitable ("So we hedge it!", "But worry not"), fond of a confident, slightly grand
word ("immune to markets", "ever-reliable Treasuries"), dashes for asides, "simply" and "naturally", light on figures,
and one light image tied to Laura's work ("You take risks on the page").

### Passage 1

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It tells three episodes in a plain, wry first-person-plural voice ("which was generous", Eric falling asleep before the 00:30 open, "certain payments can't lean on luck twice"), which sounds like students recounting their own record. But it is far denser with dates and exact figures than anything in the IPS, and the last sentence summing up the three moments feels assembled rather than said.
- **Meaning:** The ten payments only just fitted inside $300,000, so the 2028 deposit was given the job of finishing them; the share-heavy first plan was dropped because a writer's income falls when shares do, and was only priced afterwards; and VT survived a test set in advance and Darren's "copout" challenge, kept for better reasons.
- **Check question (reflection):** They dropped it on 27 September, before any trade, because a writer's income shrinks and arrives late in the same downturns that sink shares. The first model came afterwards and only priced the old plan: at least one payment missed in about 1 run in 31 (40.8% if the 2028 deposit never came), with the danger sitting on one date, 1 January 2033, when all ten had to be bought.
- **Check question (world fund):** They kept VT. Before, it was a cheap, diversified default; afterwards they could say why it suits Laura: it is the one holding they let be wrong, it can only be wrong along with the whole market, it needs no opinions (so the plan keeps only two reasons to change), and a Taiwan fund would add to Taiwan risk the building already carries (TSMC was 21.89% of EWT).
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 2

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** The same story as passage 1, told more carefully and procedurally (weekday names, all four alternatives listed), so it reads like a conscientious student writing up the record. Contractions and the Eric aside keep it human, but it lacks the IPS's bounce and ends on a balanced three-part summary that sounds composed.
- **Meaning:** The ladder's tight fit made finishing the ten the 2028 deposit's first job; how a writer is paid took the payments out of shares, with the first model only pricing that change; and VT stayed after a pre-set test and a "copout" argument, for reasons about Laura.
- **Check question (reflection):** Dropped before any trade because her lumpy income comes later and smaller in the downturns that knock shares down, so more shares add to a risk she already carries. The first model only priced the change: the old plan missed a payment in about 1 in 31 runs, 40.8% without the 2028 deposit, because everything hung on 1 January 2033.
- **Check question (world fund):** Kept VT. Before, a sensible cheap default; after, the one holding allowed to be wrong and only together with the whole market, needing no opinions so it never adds a third reason to change the plan, and avoiding extra Taiwan risk (a fifth of a Taiwan fund was TSMC).
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 3

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** A clear, friendly walk through the table ("does double duty", "and that's on purpose", "it's worth saying what it leaves out") that a careful student could write. It is tidier and more even-tempered than the IPS, and the image paragraph reads like a definition dropped in rather than something the writer was enjoying.
- **Meaning:** Table 1 shows the plan's one rule (promise only what is already owned in Treasuries): ten Treasury rows for the ten payments, IBTM also holding the floor, VT at about 8.8% as the only market risk, 89.55% in Treasuries; built in October, it leaves out the 2028 additions and the 2031 move.
- **Check question (opening page):** The ten Treasury positions (five iBonds funds for 2033-2037, five Treasury bonds for 2038-2042, with IBTM also the floor) pay a set figure on a date; VT does not. The book is built so that no payment rests on shares and only money not yet promised takes market risk.
- **Image:** It helped a little: it gave me a quick handle on why VT differs from every other row, though "owned, not merely signed for" made me stop and think about signatures, which it was trying to rule out.

### Passage 4

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** Confident, slightly grand and a bit breathless: one long sentence piles up reasons, reaches for "the vain illusion that we can pick winners" and "as per JPMorgan", and the passage ends on a rhetorical question. That is the IPS's enthusiasm and phrasing.
- **Meaning:** VT is the one place the team lets itself be wrong, because no payment depends on it; it buys the whole market cheaply (0.06% a year, about 7.5% expected per J.P. Morgan) and answers how generous the facility contribution can be.
- **Check question:** None fits closely (it gives VT's purpose but tells of no decision or change).
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 5

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It moves in short, staged paragraphs towards a reveal ("The surprise was where the danger had been sitting. Not in a crash – on one date."), which is lively and close to the IPS's punchy lines ("A fall in the market is not one of them."), but the staging feels more deliberately crafted than the IPS's chatty flow.
- **Meaning:** The three-quarters-shares plan was dropped on 27 September because writers are paid later and less in the recessions that drag shares down; the model then showed it failing in about 1 run in 31 (40.8% without the 2028 deposit), the danger being the single date of 1 January 2033; the new plan was written down on 28 September and locked on 1 October.
- **Check question (reflection):** Dropped the same day, before any trade, because a writer is paid later and less in exactly the recessions that hit shares. The first model then priced it: the old plan missed at least one payment in about 1 run in 31, 40.8% with no 2028 deposit, while the Treasury plan missed none from January 2028.
- **Image:** It got in the way slightly: calling a whole plan "our first pencil" stretches a word defined for dollar figures, and I had to work out what "before a single trade had put anything in ink" meant.

### Passage 6

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Terse and aphoristic. Each sentence is a finished rule, and the passage closes on a neat reversal ("Her spare money shrinks before her promise does"). It sounds like an economical professional explaining a mechanism, not the warm, talkative writers of the IPS.
- **Meaning:** The 2031 range runs from the floor (rounded down) to the floor plus half the shares, which move into Treasuries before she names it; the other half stays hers as a cushion; any reinvestment shortfall on the ladder is priced before 2031 and covered from her kept shares, and if they cannot cover it the whole range comes down.
- **Check question:** None fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 7

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It opens with the IPS's own move, setting Laura's taste for risk against what the plan actually does ("and that's on purpose"), and quotes the IPS; the image is grounded in everyday examples from her life. It sags in a long last paragraph crammed with qualifications and brackets.
- **Meaning:** Table 1 is 89.55% Treasuries because nothing is promised until it is owned in Treasuries: ten Treasury rows (all ink, IBTM also the floor), VT the one pencil row, a little cash; built in October, before the 2028 additions and the 2031 move.
- **Check question (opening page):** The ten Treasury positions (iBonds funds for 2033-2037, Treasury bonds for 2038-2042, IBTM also the floor) pay on their dates; VT does not, because no Treasury pays its value yet. The book is built so every promised dollar is owned in Treasuries first and only unpromised money takes market risk.
- **Image:** It helped: the pencil examples (the 2028 deposit before it arrives, her shares, her next advance) and her own words about not knowing how a book will do made the distinction concrete before the table was described.

### Passage 8

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** The first paragraph sounds like the team: plain, warm, "a writer is paid in lumps". The middle then stops for a printer's lesson on knockouts and overprints, a technical, show-and-tell image the IPS's writers never reach for, and it takes over the passage.
- **Meaning:** The share-heavy plan was dropped on 27 September because a writer's lumpy income falls when shares fall; the first model only priced that decision (1 in 31; 40.8% without the 2028 deposit) and showed the danger was one date, 1 January 2033; the new plan was written down on 28 September and locked on 1 October.
- **Check question (reflection):** Dropped that same day, before any trade, because her income comes in lumps that arrive later and smaller in a recession just as shares fall. The first model came after the decision and only priced it: about 1 in 31 runs missed a payment, 40.8% without the 2028 deposit, all because of one purchase date in 2033.
- **Image:** It does not use pencil and ink; it uses a print-registration image (knockout against overprint), which I had to read twice to decode, and it got in the way.

### Passage 9

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Plain and well organised, with a "one rule" frame and the IPS line and Laura's own words tied together by a good short sentence ("Nothing in her plan asks her to promise a figure on those terms"). It sounds like a capable student, more measured than the IPS but in the same register.
- **Meaning:** Table 1 tests the plan's one rule: ten Treasury positions, one per payment, each already owned and paying on its date, IBTM also holding the floor, VT the one row no Treasury pays yet, 89.55% Treasuries on purpose; built in October, so without the 2028 additions or the 2031 move.
- **Check question (opening page):** The ten Treasury positions (iBonds funds for 2033-2037, Treasury bonds for 2038-2042) pay on their dates; VT does not. Built that way so Laura never has to promise a figure she cannot yet know, as with a book before it hits the shelves.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 10

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** It opens on how pension funds work and proceeds like a textbook summary: impersonal, Laura never named, no aside or humour. Clear, but with none of the IPS's personality.
- **Meaning:** Of the two standard ways to cover fixed payments, duration matching with rebalancing or one bond per payment held to the end, the plan uses the second: in January 2027 her $300,000 buys a ladder maturing just before each payment, so none depends on shares.
- **Check question (opening page, partly):** The Treasury bonds and fixed-end Treasury funds pay a set figure on a date; it does not name the holding that does not, only that no payment depends on shares. It is built this way because it needs no forecast or trading.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 11

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** A slightly bumpy, honest note: a string of adjectives, "so we'll simply ignore price noise in the interim", then "The catch is," owning the downside conversationally. It sounds like the same people who wrote "But worry not" and "we'll simply complete it".
- **Meaning:** Where WInS had no maturing Treasury bond, the team bought iBonds funds such as IBTM, which behave like bonds held to their end; their final payout is the fund's net asset value rather than par, a cost traded for liquidity an odd-lot bond lacks.
- **Check question:** None fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 12

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** The same text as passage 3, except that the third paragraph says the distinction in plain words, which keeps one even, friendly register throughout. Competent and clear, though more measured than the IPS.
- **Meaning:** Table 1 shows the rule of promising only what is owned in Treasuries: ten Treasury rows, IBTM also the floor, VT about 8.8% and the only row whose 2031 value is open, 89.55% Treasuries; it leaves out the 2028 additions and the 2031 move.
- **Check question (opening page):** The ten Treasury rows (five iBonds funds for 2033-2037, five Treasury bonds for 2038-2042) pay out on the dates needed; VT does not, since its 2031 value is still open. Built that way so no payment rests on shares and only unpromised money takes market risk.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 13

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** Bold, slightly over-the-top claims ("near-ironclad guarantee", "restless markets", "nonexistent inflation risk"), a "Notably –" aside, and the feel of someone pleased with the plan: the same emphatic voice as "immune to markets" and "ever-reliable Treasuries".
- **Meaning:** Treasury bonds such as the 2037 one will not create wealth but remove uncertainty by matching the payments; the team reads "high degree of certainty" as near-ironclad, and because the payments are fixed in dollars, ordinary Treasuries match them and TIPS are not needed.
- **Check question:** None fits.

### What separates the top from the bottom

The passages I ranked highest share the IPS's habits: confident, slightly showy words ("vain illusion", "restless
markets", "near-ironclad"), "simply" and a conversational "the catch is", dashes for asides, a little roughness, and
reasoning given in the team's own voice without a stack of dates and figures. The middle group has the right content
and a friendly voice with contractions and wry asides, but is much denser with exact dates and numbers than the IPS and
tends to end on neat three-part summaries that feel built. The bottom three sound like a different writer: textbook
framing that starts from what pension funds do (10), compressed rule-by-rule aphorism ("her spare money shrinks before
her promise does", 6), or an imported technical image (knockout and overprint, 8) quite unlike the IPS's one light image
of Laura taking risks on the page. Where pencil and ink appeared, it helped most when its examples came from Laura's
life (7) and least when it was stretched to cover a whole plan (5).
