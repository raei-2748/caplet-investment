## From memory

1. **How much, and when.** $115,000 towards the building, in US dollars, paid in January 2033. She shows the range as $95,000 to $115,000, but her signed letter fixes it at $115,000.

2. **Could it end up lower?** In US dollars, no, as far as she tells it. The letter fixes $115,000. The money has been in Treasuries since January 2031. If the fund holding them pays out a little less, she makes up the gap from money she kept back. The only way it falls short is if that kept-back money were not enough, and I was not told how much it is. In Taiwan dollars, yes: what we receive depends on the exchange rate in January 2033. A stronger Taiwan dollar would give us fewer NT$ than the figure on her letter in 2031. I could not say how much fewer.

3. **The $95,000 in her words.** The part held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028, the part that never depended on her shares. It is the bottom of her range.

4. **How sure, and why believe her.** She says that since January 2031 neither figure depends on her shares, so no market can change either one. The money sits in US Treasuries, which the US government guarantees and which pay exactly what they promise. Why believe her: she shows her broker's own statement of the account, with what it will pay by December 2032. She signs a dated pledge letter. Unlike Lehman's pledge to Spelman, her money already exists before she signs. She is committing without waiting for anyone to match her. And she has already bought the Treasuries for the ten yearly payments, finished in January 2028.

5. **What I would write down, and one thing to ask.** I would book **$115,000 (US dollars), committed, payable January 2033**. Before committing our own money I would ask her this: if the letter fixes $115,000, in what case would we ever see only the $95,000 she still quotes? My board hates surprises, and a lower number on the page is one waiting to happen. I would want that answered in writing, along with how much money she has kept back to cover a shortfall.
