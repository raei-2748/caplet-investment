## From memory

1. **How much, and when.** $115,000 towards the building, in US dollars, paid in January 2033. (She states a range of $95,000 to $115,000 but signs for the top, $115,000.)

2. **Could it end up lower?** In US dollars, not on the letter's terms: the letter fixes it at $115,000, and if the fund holding the Treasuries pays out less she makes up the difference from money she kept back. The only way it comes in lower is if that make-up money were not there; even then the $95,000 has been held in Treasuries since January 2028 and the rest since January 2031, so the realistic worst case sits somewhere between $95,000 and $115,000, nearer the top. In Taiwan dollars it can be lower: the NT$ amount depends on the exchange rate on the payment day in January 2033, so a stronger Taiwan dollar means fewer NT$ for the building.

3. **What the $95,000 is, in her words.** The part of her building money that has been held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028, the part that never depended on her shares; the bottom of the range.

4. **How sure, and why believe her.** She presents it as certain in US dollars: both figures sit in Treasuries she already holds (since January 2028 and January 2031), backed by the US government, and she signs a letter fixing $115,000 with her own money behind any shortfall. A funder should believe her because the money exists before she signs, which we can check on her broker's own statement (value today beside what it will pay by December 2032); that is the opposite of Lehman's pledge to Spelman, paid out of future money, which delivered $3 million of $10 million. She also commits first, in writing, without waiting for a match. The one soft spot: the Treasuries are held through a fund, whose payout is not exact, and what stands behind any gap is her own reserve, whose size I was not told.

5. **Figure I would write down, and one question.** **$115,000** committed (US dollars, payable January 2033), booked against the signed pledge letter. Before committing our own money I would ask her for the size and form of the money she kept back to make up any shortfall in the fund's payout, since that is the only thing standing behind the top of her range.
