## 3. Step two: the team's lead-in and Laura's words
Now the same people write their report. Its readers are the competition's judges, so in the lead-in Laura is "Laura"
or "she", and the team is "we". Figures are "on today's figures" (Treasury prices at the close on 6 November 2026).

**L (three or four sentences): the team's lead-in.** What Laura shows and gives co-sponsors: she shows them the
Treasuries held for her (her broker's own statement of the account that holds the building's Treasuries, with what
they will pay by December 2032 beside their value that day), and she gives them a signed pledge letter (amount,
purpose, date). Holding the Treasuries shows the money exists; the letter says it will be given, and a letter is what a
funder's gift office can count. One outside fact, in one sentence: in October 2007 Lehman Brothers itself pledged $10
million to Spelman College over five years, to be paid from the bank's own future money; when the bank failed in 2008
the college received $3 million of it. Laura's money is held for her before she signs.

**N (about 230 words): Laura's own words.** A short passage set apart, in her voice, the way she would say it to a
funder in 2031: full sentences, the year named each time, no finance jargon, one idea in each sentence, and every "it"
or "this" plainly pointing at one thing. In this order:
- two sentences of purpose from the case (who the residency serves, why Taiwan, why her);
- the range for her building contribution, with its two dates, each end in a sentence of its own: the bottom, $95,000,
  held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028; the top, $115,000, held since January 2031, when she moved half of
  her shares into Treasuries;
- what the bottom is, now that the top is held (the fact sheet says it): the part that never depended on her shares,
  and why neither end can now change;
- one plain sentence on how sure she is, with the Treasuries as its subject (strong words belong to the Treasuries;
  the fund that holds them is not said to pay a fixed sum);
- why she signs for the top rather than the bottom, in her own words (the fact sheet gives the reason);
- what her letter says: the sum is $115,000, and if the fund pays less she makes up the difference herself, from the
  money she kept back (no list of what could cause it);
- the currency: the letter is in US dollars, with its Taiwan-dollar value on a named day, and the Taiwan-dollar amount
  depends on the rate on the day it is paid;
- her whole commitment in two parts: $50,000 a year for the residency's running costs from 2033 to 2042, bought from
  January 2027 and complete since January 2028, and $115,000 for the building; if you give a total, give the exact sum
  the two parts make;
- its date and form: committed in writing now, in 2031, without waiting for anyone to match it; the $115,000 paid in
  January 2033.
