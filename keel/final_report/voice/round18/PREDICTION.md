# Voice round 18: Keel's prediction, written before anyone reads (Tue 24 Nov 2026, 19:20 Sydney)

**The question.** Round 17 (`../ROUND17.md`) found one fault in both orders of Laura's words: with the top held and
signed, every reader asked why a range is stated at all and what could make her gift $95,000. The morning's repairs
(`research/COSPONSOR_NOTES.md` section 8, C1-C9; `FACTS_PLAIN.md` version 8) keep the range first and add: one sentence
on what the bottom is now (the part that never depended on her shares; since January 2031 neither end can change); the
letter's undertaking (the sum is $115,000, and if the fund pays less she makes up the difference herself); the currency
of the letter; her whole commitment in two parts with an exact total; "without waiting for anyone to match it"; and a
lead-in that says she shows co-sponsors her broker's statement of the building's Treasuries and gives them a signed
letter. Does "what could make it $95,000?" go away, and does anything new appear?

**Design.** One fresh writer (brief `brief_r18_Y.md`, fact sheet version 8) writes the lead-in (L) and Laura's words (N).
Three readers, one in each of round 17's roles (the head of giving at a Taiwanese family company, a US foundation
programme officer, a grants officer at Taiwan's Ministry of Culture). Each is calibrated on version 61, reads Caplet's
own IPS paragraph C3 as a control, then L and N, marking Follow and Sense per sentence, then answers round 17's five
questions from memory, unchanged. The comparison is round 17's range-first arm (readers 1-3, the same roles, the same
calibration, the same questions). Three readers against three: a signal, never a measurement.

**Keel's prediction.**
1. **The bottom's question goes away:** no reader names "what could make it $95,000" as the one thing they would ask
   (two of six did in round 17), and on question 2 all three say it cannot end lower, with a reason from the page (the
   Treasuries are held, and the letter fixes the sum).
2. **Something new appears in its place:** at least one reader asks about the letter's undertaking itself (what "makes
   up the difference" is backed by, or how large the money she kept back is), because the words now promise a top-up
   from money the page does not size.
3. **Followed at least as well as round 17's R:** at least 80% of sentence readings of N followed first time (R: 85%),
   though N is longer.
4. **The total is heard as exact:** no reader stops at the commitment's total (five of six did at "about $615,000").
5. **The currency sentence costs a stop:** at least one of the three marks it 2 or asks what happens if the Taiwan dollar
   moves; no reader books a Taiwan-dollar figure.
6. **What they would book:** all three book $115,000; the one thing still asked for is the statement itself, the
   letter's terms if she dies, or the other funders' timetable; no reader asks for a probability.

**The rule, set now.** If prediction 1 holds and N is followed first time in at least 75% of sentence readings, the
repaired words stand as Branch 5's form for 27 Nov. If a reader still asks what could make it $95,000, the bottom's
sentence is rewritten before 27 Nov from that reader's words. If the letter's undertaking draws a new question from two
or more readers, the notes add one fact to the lead-in (not to her words) that answers it, from NUMBERS.
