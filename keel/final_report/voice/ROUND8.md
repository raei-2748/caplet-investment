## Comprehension

- **Laura's words: 12 of 12 answers right.** Every reader, on every note, said the board paper carries $115,000, that
  all of it is already held ($95,000 since January 2028, the rest since the move in January 2031), and that she does not
  ask for the ten payments, for a match, or for the rest of her money. One reader flagged that O2 and P2 say "since
  January" without the year (both spoken versions); two readers had to infer 2031.
- **The reflection: 12 of 12 gave the IPS's reason and the model as the check.** Every answer on O3, O4, P3 and P4 gave
  how a writer is paid (income later and smaller in the recessions that pull shares down) as the reason, and the first
  model as what priced the change: about 1 in 31 against none once the 2028 deposit's first dollars arrive, the danger one
  date, 1 January 2033. No reader gave the 3.2% as the reason.
- **The turn: helped 5 of 6.** On P4 all three said it helped ("made the point that the danger was one date, not a
  crash, click faster than the bare explanation would"); on O4 two said it helped and one that it "gets slightly in the
  way" because the same long sentence has to spell it out. P4 put the turn in its own sentence after the decision ("We
  dropped it before a single trade. The first plan's payments were in pencil – no Treasury it owned would pay them ...");
  O4 put it inside the middle of the argument.

## What the readers heard (sentences about how passages felt, never rules)

- **Laura's notes are understood perfectly and heard as drafted by someone older.** All four were heard as the team's
  bar one, but they ranked lowest of the practice passages. The readers named fundraising terms that no student says
  ("the building's first draw", "close date", "every conditional pledge releases together", "custodian") and, in the
  spoken versions, clipped fragments ("Bought in 2027, finished in 2028." "Then two asks.") that read as a speechwriter.
  The note that ranked best among them (O2 for one reader) opened with a question and put what is done first. P1's "They're
  guaranteed" was heard as the IPS's own confidence ("immune to markets") by two readers.
- **The lighter reflection gains.** P3 (mean rank 4.7) is the best whole reflection any round has produced (round 7: 5.0
  and 5.3). Readers heard a person giving a reason before each decision ("We didn't want the payments to depend on which
  week we bought in") and admitting things ("luck, honestly", Eric asleep before the 00:30 open). O3 lost ground at its
  two edges: an opening sentence about the advisor heard as "a declaration bolted on" and a closing tally ("Two
  recommendations changed and one held") heard as "assembled for a rubric".
- **The turn lands when the decision comes first.** P4 tells the story in order (answered the risk half, "Then we thought
  about how a writer is paid", dropped it), then gives the turn with its gloss after a dash, and closes on a concession
  ("and for most clients it would be"). Two readers heard "Then we thought about how a writer is paid" as the IPS's own
  turn, "until we stopped and considered", in the past tense.
- **Across the readers:** the highest passages (Caplet's own) have flourishes, run-on sentences that land on a question or
  a quip, and claims a little bolder than an editor would allow; the middle passages carry the same reasoning "in a
  calmer, more even hand, with dates and careful concessions and tidy summary endings"; the lowest are "correct and
  polished but without the explaining, the warmth or the small flourishes" of the IPS.
