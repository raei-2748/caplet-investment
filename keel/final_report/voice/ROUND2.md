# Voice round 2 (Sun 15 Nov 2026, night): four writers, three readers, read blind

Run the night Caplet asked for "a hell of a lot more" voice study (Book 19). Method as round 1: read Caplet whole, write
forward, read blind. No rules came out of it; what follows is what the readers heard.

## What was done

Four writers each wrote four passages of about 330-365 words for the Final Report, from the same plain facts
(`FACTS_PLAIN.md`): how certain the payments are; why half the shares are kept back; the 2031 range and how sure Laura
can be; why the team kept the world fund after the 5 October "copout" argument (Team Reflection). Every writer first
rebuilt an IPS paragraph from notes (Franklin's method) and continued the IPS past "Yours, Caplet".

| Writer | What it read before writing | Conceit |
|---|---|---|
| B | Caplet's formal writing only: the IPS, the Trading Note reflections, the team's own lines | Used (version 2) |
| E | The same, plus one team member's essays (a private file, read, not copied) | Not used |
| M | The same as B, plus the team's long messages in which it thinks aloud (the books) | Used (version 2) |
| Keel's main session | The IPS and the reflections, straight after round 1's verdict ("too finished"), with all of Keel's files in its head | Not used |

Three readers read Caplet's IPS twice and then got 21 unlabelled passages, each in a different order: the sixteen
practice passages, Caplet's three real Trading Note reflections, and two paragraphs from Keel's last IPS draft. For each
they said whether the IPS's writers could have written it, why, and what it meant; for range passages they restated
the rule and answered "In January 2030, may Laura tell a co-sponsor she will give $115,000?". Files: `round2/` (the
writers, the briefs, the three readings, the key in `passages.json` and `order_reader*.txt`, and `score.py`).

## What they found

| Writer | Heard as possibly Caplet's | Average place in the readers' rankings (1 = most like Caplet) |
|---|---|---|
| Caplet's own Trading Note reflections | 9 of 9 ("Surely" 8 times) | 2.0 |
| Writer E (no conceit) | 12 of 12 | 7.1 |
| Keel's main session (no conceit) | 11 of 12 | 8.0 |
| Writer B (conceit) | 0 of 12 | 16.1 |
| Writer M (conceit) | 0 of 12 | 16.7 |
| Keel's last IPS draft | 0 of 6 | 16.8 |

Every reader put all three real reflections in their top three. Keel's main session went from 0 of 9 in round 1 to 11 of
12. Both writers who used the conceit were heard as "a skilled adult writer working a conceit" in all 24 readings,
including their copout passages, which carry almost no image.

The range rule was understood every time. All fifteen answers to the January 2030 question were "no", for the right
reason (the top is not owned until half the shares move), and every restatement of the rule said "owned" or "already
held". Readers took "certain" and "completely sure" after the move at face value, with no safety clause, and none of
them marked it as an overclaim.

## How the readers heard the difference (their words, condensed)

- The top passages sound like "the IPS's writers on an ordinary day: earnest, a little eager to show what they know,
  comfortable sounding slightly naive or overstated, with asides tacked on after dashes". They "explain an idea rather
  than polish it", and use grand words earnestly ("restless markets", "vain illusion", "ever-reliable").
- The bottom passages either "stack images from printing and comics (bleed, cut lines, non-photo blue, erasers, Image
  Comics, royalties)" or turn "clipped, note-like" (Keel's old IPS). Several said the stacked references read "like
  research gems being displayed" and that "by the third image I was reading the references, not the plan".
- The tidy question, answer and honest-caveat pattern, repeated paragraph after paragraph, pulled even good passages
  down ("the more often a paragraph followed the same tidy pattern, the less it sounded like the students").

## What it says about the conceit (honest sentences, not rules)

The conceit is Caplet's decision and it stays intricate (Book 19). What the readers showed is how it sounds when it is
not yet carried in Caplet's voice:

- The core image worked. Pencil and ink, defined as owned or not yet, "gives a clear test", and "one eraser across three
  pencil lines" was called "the clearest statement in the pack of why her advances, shares and co-sponsors fail
  together". Ink only goes one way also helped.
- What hurt was several turns stacked in one passage (trim, bleed and non-photo blue on top of pencil and ink, with an
  Image Comics fact and a royalties reserve as asides), and turns used where the passage had not set them up. That is
  exactly what version 2 already plans against: one turn per part, developed across the report as Donne develops the
  compass across stanzas, never piled into one paragraph. Round 2's writers were given the whole arc and tried to show
  most of it in four passages.
- Two things changed at once in this round (the conceit and the reading diet), so round 3 separates them: the writers
  who were heard as Caplet's (E's diet, and Keel's main session) write conceit passages, each carrying one turn, set up
  where it is used and told the way the team brings in an idea ("with a bit of relish", writer B's own note).

## Small things to carry

- One passage called $115,000 both "the top" and "the central contribution" and two readers stumbled; the report says
  "the top of the range" for the stated figure and keeps "contribution" for what is paid.
- Readers noticed when a passage addressed "judges reading this report": the team writes to Laura or about her, never
  to the judges.
- Model statistics in quantity (0.07%, 0.27%, 11 of 100) made even well-heard passages "a stretch from the IPS's
  lightness"; one headline number a part (SKELETON's density cut) fits what they heard.
