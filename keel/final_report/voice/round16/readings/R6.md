## From memory

1. "In ink" means there are Treasuries held for her that will pay that exact amount on that date, so it can't fall with the market. "In pencil" is anything not yet backed that way, such as a future book advance or the value of her shares. She only states a figure to others once it is in ink.

2. She gives a range for the building: $95,000, backed by Treasuries held for her since January 2028, and up to $115,000, backed by Treasuries held since January 2031 after she moved half her shares into them. She signs for the top figure. Separately, $50,000 a year for running costs from 2033 to 2042 is also held in Treasuries, about $615,000 in total.

3. Yes. Owning means the money exists and is held for her. Signing is the promise to give it. A funder cares because owning alone doesn't commit her to anything, so they can't count on it. A signed letter with amount, purpose and date is what their gift office can record. The Lehman and Spelman story shows a pledge without the money behind it.

4. "Held" is used for the Treasuries throughout, but "range" and "up to" felt slightly shifted: first it is a promise to co-sponsors, then the floor and top of what is held. "Pledge" is also used both for Lehman's promise and Laura's letter. "Guaranteed" appears in her letter, though she does not use it before. The "ink" word never comes back in Laura's own words, which seemed right, but the plain "held for me" wording meant the same thing and helped. "Commit" in the letter took on the legal sense, a signed promise, and that helped.
