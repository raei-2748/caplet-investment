=== C ===
So, 2031. By the time you meet your first co-sponsor, the hard part will already be done. That January we'll move half of your shares into the same Treasury fund that holds your floor, so whatever figure you name will be sitting in Treasuries before you say it, waiting for December 2032. On today's figures, that's up to $115,000 – and $95,000 of it you'll have held since January 2028, three years before anyone thought to ask. You can tell them exactly that, and sign for it. The other half of your shares stays yours, promised to no one: for the building that runs over, the Taiwan dollar that climbs, or the co-sponsor who doesn't quite come through. As for the ten payments, every one of them will have been in Treasuries for three years by then, so there won't be much to say about them – which, at a fundraising meeting, is no bad thing.
=== D ===
Laura draws comics for a living, and a comic page is drawn in pencil, which can still be rubbed out, then inked, and only the ink is printed. We borrow her two words for her money. A figure is in ink once Treasuries held for her will pay it on its date, and in pencil while none will yet: her next book advance, say, or even the value of the shares she owns, since no Treasury will pay it. As on her pages, only the ink goes to print: Laura states a figure only once it is in ink.
=== P ===
Holding the Treasuries shows that Laura's money exists, not that she will give it, so we recommend a signed pledge letter too (the amount, the purpose and the date): that is what a funder's gift office can count. In October 2007 Lehman Brothers itself pledged $10 million to Spelman College over five years, to be paid from the bank's own future money; when the bank failed in 2008, the college received $3 million. Laura's pledge runs the other way round, with the money held for her before she signs; on today's figures, she would say this.

> In 2033 I am opening a small residency in Taiwan where artists, writers, designers and educators can live, work and teach together. I tell stories for a living, and I want the people who tell them to have somewhere to work.
>
> For the building I give a range: $95,000 held for me in US Treasuries since January 2028, and up to $115,000 since January 2031, when I moved half my shares into Treasuries. Those Treasuries are guaranteed by the US government, and no market can now change what they pay. I am signing for the top, $115,000, because whatever figure I choose, the money for it is already mine. With it come $50,000 a year for the residency's running costs from 2033 to 2042, held in Treasuries since January 2028: about $615,000 in all. I commit to it in writing now, in 2031, and pay the $115,000 in January 2033.

=== NOTES ===
I put the added sentence straight after the lead-in's first sentence, where the pledge letter appears, because that is the moment a reader who met ink in D asks why money already in ink still needs signing; placed after Lehman or at the end, it would recap rather than answer. Ending it on "the kind of ink a funder can count" gives the plain meaning without repeating the first sentence's gift office, and Lehman's signed pledge with no money behind it then reads as that second kind of ink on its own. The hardest thing was why she signs for the top rather than the bottom without ever calling a figure owned, so ownership sits on the money, as in the IPS. The case gives no reason for Taiwan, so Laura's purpose names Taiwan as the place and gives why her (she tells stories for a living) without inventing one.
