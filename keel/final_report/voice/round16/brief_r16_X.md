## 2. Step one: carry the letter on (about 150 words)
The IPS ends: "You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that's your job. With these promises, ours is to take none."
Start straight after that line, as the same people in the same letter to Laura, and keep going: what she will be able
to tell her co-sponsors in 2031. This is a warm-up to get their voice in your ear.

## 3. Step two: three short pieces for the Final Report
Now the same people write their report. Its readers are the competition's judges, so Laura is "Laura" or "she", and the
team is "we", doing things in the sentence the way it does in the letter. Write new sentences in their voice rather
than reusing the IPS's own. Figures are "on today's figures" (Treasury prices at the close on 6 November 2026).

**D (about 90 words): where the report first uses pencil and ink.** Introduce the image from Laura's own trade (comic
pages drawn in pencil, then inked; only the ink is printed) and say, in the same sentences, what each word means for
her money: a figure is in ink once Treasuries held for her will pay it on its date; it is in pencil while none will
yet. Then the rule that follows: Laura states a figure only once it is in ink.

**P (about 230 words): the team's lead-in, then Laura's own words to co-sponsors.**
- The team's lead-in, two or three sentences: why her words carry a signed pledge as well as money held. Owning the
  Treasuries shows the money exists; a signed pledge letter (amount, purpose, date) says it will be given, and is what
  a funder's gift office can count. One outside fact belongs here, in one sentence: in October 2007 Lehman Brothers
  itself pledged $10 million to Spelman College over five years, to be paid from the bank's own future money; when the
  bank failed in 2008 the college received $3 million of it. Laura's money is held before she signs.
- Then Laura's words, as a short passage in quotation marks or set apart, in her voice, the way she would say them to
  a funder in 2031 (full sentences, the year named, no finance jargon):
  - two sentences of purpose from the case (who the residency serves, why Taiwan, why her);
  - the range with its two dates: $95,000 held for her since January 2028, and up to $115,000 held since she moved her
    shares (January 2031);
  - one plain sentence on how sure she is, with the Treasuries as its subject;
  - why she signs for the top: whatever figure she chooses is money she already owns (the IPS's own promise);
  - her whole commitment: $50,000 a year for the residency's running costs from 2033 to 2042, held in Treasuries from
    January 2028, and $115,000 for the building, about $615,000 in all;
  - its date and form: committed in writing in 2031, paid in January 2033.

**T: the same as P, word for word, with one sentence added to the team's lead-in (never inside Laura's own words).**
The added sentence carries the phrase "a signature is the other kind of ink", and in the same sentence says plainly
what it means here: the ink of D is money that Treasuries held for her will pay; a signature is a different kind of
ink, her written promise to give it, which is what a funder can count. Put it where it reads most naturally in the
lead-in, and change nothing else between P and T.
