# Blind reading, voice round 10 (Thu 19 Nov 2026, morning)

You are a careful reader. Six Sydney high-school students (team "Caplet") wrote an investment policy statement (IPS) to
their client, Laura Gao, for the Wharton Global High School Investment Competition. They are now writing a longer Final
Report. Your job is to judge, passage by passage, whose voice you hear.

1. Read the team's IPS twice, the second time slowly, as writing by people: `/home/user/caplet-investment/keel/final_report/FINAL_IPS_as_submitted.txt`.
   Do not open any other file in that repository.
2. Three things about the Final Report you need before you read:
   - The report has a part on the plan's assumptions and limits: what could make each of Laura's promises fail, and
     what could not. Some passages may come from that part.
   - The report has a part that recommends Laura's contribution to the building (the "facility contribution"), says
     why it is meaningful and responsible, and what she keeps unpromised (her "flexibility"). Some passages may come
     from that part.
   - The report defines one image early on, and some passages may use it. Here is the definition, as the report would
     give it: Laura is a graphic novelist, and comic pages are drawn in pencil, which can be rubbed out, then inked; only
     the ink is printed. In the report, **ink** is a dollar figure Laura already owns in Treasuries that will pay it on
     its date, and **pencil** is a figure no Treasury she owns will pay yet.
3. Then read your pack of passages (path in your brief). They are mixed: some may be the team's own writing, some written
   by others trying to sound like the team, some by others. You are not told how many of each.
4. For EACH passage write:
   - **Verdict:** could the people who wrote the IPS have written this as part of a longer
     report? One of: Surely / Probably / Probably not / Surely not.
   - **Why:** one or two sentences about the passage as a whole (how it moves, how it sounds, what kind of person it
     sounds like), not a list of words.
   - **Meaning:** what the passage says, in one sentence.
   - **Check question** (answer only the one that fits the passage, if any):
     - Assumptions and limits: "What could make one of the ten $50,000 payments fall short or arrive late, and what
       could not? What would have to happen for a figure Laura has already stated to co-sponsors to fall short?"
     - The facility contribution: "What figure is recommended for Laura's contribution to the building, why is it
       meaningful and why is it responsible, and what is the part she keeps unpromised for?"
   - **Image:** if the passage uses the pencil-and-ink image: did it help you understand, or get in the way? One sentence.
5. Then rank ALL the passages from most like the IPS's writers (1) to least.
6. Then, in three or four sentences: what separates the passages you ranked highest from those you ranked lowest?

Write your answers to the output file named in your brief, starting each passage's block with `### Passage n`, and the
ranking under `### Ranking` as a numbered list of passage numbers. Be honest and specific; do not try to guess which
are "real". Do not commit or edit any repository file other than your output file.
