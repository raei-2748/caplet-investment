# Voice round 10, reader 2

Read first: the team's IPS, twice (second time slowly). What I hear in it: a letter to one person, warm and a little
eager. It sets up what most people would do, then turns ("You'd think ... until we stopped and considered"). It uses
spoken asides ("So we hedge it!", "But worry not", "Fair enough; that's your job"), the odd showy word ("ever-reliable",
"immune to markets", "definitely not a contributor") and dashes that land a point. It gives one hard figure and lists
plain things in a row ("the advances come, and the speaking fees, and the licences"). There are small seams in it, and
those make it sound like real people wrote it.

### Passage 1

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** The logic is the IPS's and so is the closing beat ("No fall in share prices can."). But the passage moves in
  clipped, compressed clauses that need insider knowledge ("fund costs beyond the fees ... past about 2% of what the
  late-2032 fund behind the top pays out"). It sounds like a careful technical editor working through a checklist,
  where the IPS sounded like students talking to Laura.
- **Meaning:** Only a US default or a missing or much smaller 2028 deposit can break a payment. A stated figure breaks
  only if its Treasuries fail or fund costs run past about 2%. Her flexibility is a limit, not a promise.
- **Check question (assumptions and limits):** A payment could fall short or arrive late if the US defaults, or if the
  2028 deposit never comes or comes far smaller (her income is paid on contract dates, and a downturn delays it first).
  A fall in share prices could not. A stated figure falls short if the Treasuries behind it pay less or late (the same
  default), or if fund costs beyond the fees go past about 2% of what the late-2032 fund pays out.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 2

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** It sounds like a student explaining a trade, a bit pleased with its own vocabulary ("in the interim",
  "terminal payout", "as opposed to a promise of par"), and it owns the trade-off honestly ("The catch is ..."). The
  fronted list and the dashes have the IPS's own energy and its slight unevenness.
- **Meaning:** Where WInS lists no Treasury for a year, the team bought iBonds funds instead. Held to the end they act
  like bonds and pay out net asset value rather than par, in exchange for liquidity that small bond lots don't have.
- **Check question:** Neither question fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 3

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** It has the same mix as the IPS: a confident claim made with flair ("near-ironclad guarantee", "anything the
  restless markets might decide to do"), a slightly awkward hinge ("Notably –") and a little lesson tacked on at the
  end about TIPS. It reads like a keen teenager defending a choice, a bit rough at the joints, the same hand as "Held to
  the end, these pay what they say".
- **Meaning:** The 2037 Treasury is there to remove uncertainty, not to make money. The team reads "high degree of
  certainty" as close to a guarantee, and since the payments are fixed in nominal dollars, TIPS aren't needed.
- **Check question:** Neither fits fully. In part (assumptions): inflation cannot make a payment fall short, because
  the payments are fixed in nominal dollars.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 4

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It is the same audit as Passage 1, but a person shows through ("It's the risk we'd point to first", "her
  word that breaks"), and it pauses to let the reader see the result, which is closer to the IPS's habit of reassuring
  Laura. Some phrases are still shorthand I couldn't unpack ("the room under the figure", "even if cash waiting earns
  nothing").
- **Meaning:** Only a default or a missing or much smaller 2028 deposit can break a payment. Shares at zero leave all
  ten paid. A stated figure fails only through a default or fund costs past about 2%. Her flexibility, a limit, covers
  about a 25% cost rise.
- **Check question (assumptions and limits):** A payment could fall short or arrive late if the US defaults or the 2028
  deposit never comes or comes far smaller. A share fall could not, even to zero. A stated figure falls short if the
  Treasuries she owns for it pay less or late, or if fund costs beyond the fees go past the room under the figure,
  about 2% of what the late-2032 fund pays.
- **Image:** It helped. "Rub out every pencil line ... and the page still reads" made the share-price point stick
  better than the bare sentence, though the clause in the middle ("since from January 2028 they're the only pencil
  left") breaks the flow.

### Passage 5

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** It is well organised and clear, but it reads like a polished report template: "It's meaningful because ...
  It's responsible because ...", a slogan ("a size, not a promise"), an exchange-rate conversion and a long sentence
  carrying two citations. The IPS's warmth and its addressing of Laura have gone, and it sounds like a professional
  adviser rather than the letter-writers.
- **Meaning:** Recommends about $115,000 (about NT$3.77 million), held in one late-2032 Treasury fund bought in 2028
  and 2031 before any co-sponsor meeting. It is meaningful because it comes first from her own means, and responsible
  because nothing is promised before it is owned. About $21,200 stays unpromised.
- **Check question (facility contribution):** About $115,000 on 6 November 2026 rates. It is meaningful because it is
  her own money ($115,000 of the $450,000), owned before she asks anyone and given first, which is how practice judges
  a founder's gift (BoardSource, Kresge, Levati et al.). It is responsible because nothing is promised before it is
  owned, the figure is rounded down, and a share fall lowers only what she can still state. The unpromised half, about
  $21,200, is for building-cost overruns, a stronger Taiwan dollar or a co-sponsor falling short.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 6

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It is plain and quick, and it explains the plan the way the IPS does, in Laura's terms, with one human
  reason given ("a number said aloud can't quietly be halved after a bad year"). The last line is a neat reversal
  ("Her spare money shrinks before her promise does"), a little more polished than the IPS, but the passage sounds
  like the same people explaining themselves at greater length.
- **Meaning:** The 2031 range runs from the owned floor (rounded down) to a top that adds half her shares, moved into
  Treasuries first. Any shortfall from reinvesting the ladder's interest at low rates is priced and covered from her
  kept shares before she names the range. This differs from the other assumption passages, which name only a default
  or a missing deposit.
- **Check question (assumptions and limits, in part):** A payment could fall short if rates fall and stay low, since
  the ladder counts on reinvesting its interest. The team covers the gap from her kept shares before she names the
  range, and if they can't, the range comes down. Nothing is said about a figure already stated failing, beyond the
  point that a stated number is never halved.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 7

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It does the IPS's move of setting out what others do and then choosing ("We do the second"), in clean,
  short sentences with a closing reassurance much like "Held to the end, these pay what they say". It is more
  textbook-neutral than the IPS and has none of its personality, but nothing in it is out of the team's reach.
- **Meaning:** Of the two ways pension funds cover fixed payments, the team chose one bond per payment held to
  maturity. In January 2027 the first deposit buys Treasuries and dated Treasury funds that mature before each
  payment, so no payment depends on shares.
- **Check question:** Neither fits fully. In part (assumptions): a fall in shares cannot affect a payment, because
  each payment is met by a Treasury held to maturity.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 8

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** The middle has a natural spoken turn ("What makes it meaningful is whose money it is and when it shows
  up"). But "We'd recommend" sounds hesitant next to the IPS's confident "we'll", and the last paragraph piles up
  clauses until it is hard to follow. It sounds like a capable student working hard to fit the whole argument into one
  breath.
- **Meaning:** Recommends about $115,000 on 6 November 2026 market rates, paid by one late-2032 Treasury fund bought
  in 2028 and 2031. It is meaningful because it is hers and first, responsible because nothing is promised before it
  is owned. The other half of the shares (about $21,200) is her flexibility.
- **Check question (facility contribution):** About $115,000. It is meaningful because it is her own money ($115,000 of

- **Image:** It mostly helped. "Ink only goes one way" with its gloss explains well why the figure is rounded down, but

### What separates the top from the bottom

The passages I ranked highest have a person in them. They take a position with some feeling ("where we allow
ourselves to be wrong", "restless markets", "The catch is"), use the dash to land a point, and are a little uneven at
the joints, as the IPS is ("worry not", "ever-reliable", "definitely not a contributor"). The middle passages keep the
IPS's plain logic and a few spoken turns ("a number said aloud can't quietly be halved", "We name that deposit
because") but are drier. The lowest-ranked read like a professional editor's report: insider shorthand ("fund costs
beyond the fees past about 2%", "the room under the figure"), a "meaningful because ... responsible because" template,
stacked citations and slogans ("a size, not a promise"). They are clear and correct, but they no longer sound like six
students writing to Laura.
