# Reading 1 (voice rounds 5 and 6, blind reader 1)

I read the IPS twice, the second time slowly. What I hear in it: a warm, slightly showy letter by young writers who are
pleased with their idea. It makes a punchy claim, then a looser sentence explains it, with dashes and asides. It reaches
now and then for a grand word ("ever-reliable Treasuries", "immune to markets"), uses exclamation marks, and reassures
the reader in conversation ("But worry not", "we'll simply", "Fair enough"). It is uneven in a human way. The Final
Report talks about Laura rather than to her, so I weighed how a passage moves and sounds more than whether it says "you".

### Passage 1

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It sounds like someone explaining their own spreadsheet to a teacher. It owns the awkward parts ("The catch is",
  "Yes, ... that's on purpose") and talks a little loosely ("got bought and tested", "forced on us"), which is the
  IPS's mix of plain explanation and a confident shrug. It carries far more figures than the letter, but a person is
  still behind the sentences.
- **Meaning:** The competition book is Laura's whole plan scaled to $300,000, so it holds the floor fund and VT. It was
  built before the reserve was sized, which is why VT is overweight. IBTM does two jobs, the WInS listings forced the
  split between funds and bonds, the 89.55% in one issuer is deliberate, and the buying met targets set in advance.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** Because the book is Laura's whole plan, both deposits, scaled down to $300,000,
  every instrument the strategy needs (the 2028 floor fund and VT included) was bought and tested, not just assumed.
  IBTM also stands in for the floor fund.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 2

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Flat and textbook-tidy. It gives a general fact about pension funds, then a choice, a mechanism and a
  guarantee, each sentence doing one job, with no aside, no warmth and no turn. It reads like the IPS's third
  paragraph with the personality taken out, and could be anyone's competent summary.
- **Meaning:** Like a pension fund that buys one bond per payment and holds it, the plan buys a Treasury maturing before
  each January payment with the first $300,000, so no payment depends on shares.
- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 3

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It catches itself first ("at first glance it doesn't look like the IPS"), puts the reader's question and then
  answers "Because". That is how the IPS's opening paragraph moves ("that was our plan – until we stopped").
  It stays conversational and level, though the middle turns into a run of exact figures and maturity years.
- **Meaning:** The book holds a floor fund and VT because it is the whole two-deposit plan at $300,000 scale. VT is the
  only line not yet settled, the Treasury concentration is deliberate, the WInS listings mostly forced the
  maturities, and the trades hit pre-set targets.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** It's the whole plan, both deposits, shrunk to $300,000 of $450,000, so the
  strategy's instruments were bought rather than assumed. The passage asks this exact question and answers it
  directly.
- **Image:** Not used, though "the one line not yet settled" comes close to it without the words.

### Passage 4

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** A careful, honest walk through each assumption. The one image is used as a stress test, not as decoration,
  and the passage sounds like a thoughtful student who has been edited hard. Some phrasing is a little stylized ("Two
  safety margins we chose on purpose"), and the closing list of limits is tidier than anything in the letter.
- **Meaning:** The 7.5% share return is the most exposed assumption but barely moves the top; what shares actually do
  before 2031 does. Even with shares at zero, the payments and a $95,000 range hold. The passage then sets out the
  deposit, outside-money and inflation/Taiwan assumptions, two deliberate safety margins and the remaining limits.
- **Check question (assumptions):** Not much. Between 4% and 10% the top before rounding moves only from about $116,000
  to $119,500, less than one $5,000 step, because the top is set by what shares actually do before the 2031
  meetings, not by the long-run figure. The passage states this clearly but leaves the reason to me. I take it that
  only a few years on a fairly small fund count before the top is fixed.
- **Image:** Helped. "The shares gone to zero" is explained inside the image itself, so erasing the pencil and seeing the
  inked page still standing makes the worst case concrete.

### Passage 5

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Crisp and confident: short sentences, one sharp line ("a number said aloud can't quietly be halved after a
  bad year") and a neat reversal at the end. It sounds like a skilled, economical writer more than the chatty
  letter. It also describes a ladder that counts on reinvesting interest and a shortfall moved out of her kept
  shares, which sits uneasily with the IPS's promise that no payment depends on anything but Treasuries she owns.
- **Meaning:** The 2031 range runs from the owned floor to the floor plus half the shares, moved into Treasuries first,
  with the other half kept as a cushion. Any reinvestment shortfall on the ladder is priced and covered from her
  kept shares before she names the range.
- **Check question:** Neither fits closely. It implies the top depends on the shares' actual value in 2031, but it says
  nothing about an assumed return.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 6

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** The same ground as 4, compressed and more passive ("are taken as", "is assumed to land"), with the limits
  crammed into one long last sentence. It reads like an editor tightening a student draft. It is clear and
  competent, but the person behind it has mostly gone.
- **Meaning:** The share-return assumption barely changes the top, and shares at zero still leave the payments and a
  $95,000 range. The passage then states the deposit and outside-money assumptions, treats Taiwan cost and currency
  risk as a stated limit, and names two margins and the remaining limits.
- **Check question (assumptions):** Not much: about $116,000 to $119,500 across 4%-10%, inside one $5,000 step, because
  the shares' actual path before 2031 sets the top. As with 4, the passage asserts this more than it explains it.
- **Image:** Mostly helped. The explanation after the colon makes it clear, though I waited a beat to learn what
  "erase" meant here.

### Passage 7

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** This has the IPS's exact temperament: a bold claim, "In other words", a slightly grand turn ("restless
  markets", "near-ironclad guarantee"), a "Notably –" aside and a confident overstatement about inflation. It sounds
  like an eager student writing a note about one bond they have just bought.
- **Meaning:** Treasuries don't build wealth but remove uncertainty by matching the payments' timing. The team reads
  "high degree of certainty" as a near-guarantee, and because the payments are fixed in nominal dollars, ordinary
  Treasuries match them and TIPS aren't needed.
- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 8

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Same content as 4, but with the letter's bounce: an exclamation mark, "simply", "after all", "Outside
  funding: none". It ends on a run of short fragments ("The model is a model."). That ending is a little mannered,
  but overall this sounds like the IPS writers talking.
- **Meaning:** The 7.5% assumption barely matters to the top, while actual share performance before 2031 does. The
  passage then states the deposit and outside-money assumptions, treats Taiwan building costs and the currency as a
  limit, not a reason to change, and lists two margins and the remaining limits.
- **Check question (assumptions):** Hardly: 4% to 10% moves the unrounded top from about $116,000 to $119,500, under one
  $5,000 step, because what shares actually do before the 2031 meetings sets it. The "why" is stated, not shown.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 9

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** Practical and a little breathless. A list of adjectives runs into "so we'll simply ignore price noise", then
  comes "The catch is". That is the same move as the IPS's "But worry not ... we'll simply complete it" paragraph,
  in the same "T-Bonds and iBonds" vocabulary. Uneven in a human way.
- **Meaning:** Where WInS listed no Treasury for a year, the team used iBonds funds. Held to their end they behave like
  bonds, so each payment still rests on its own instrument; they pay net asset value rather than par, but they are
  more liquid than odd-lot bonds.
- **Check question:** Neither fits closely. It explains why funds stand in for bonds, not why the book holds a facility
  fund and VT.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 10

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It voices the reader's worry ("So is the top of Laura's range resting on the hopeful end? In our model,
  hardly."), and "The facility is another matter" echoes the IPS's "The building is a different matter". That
  sounds like students defending their own work. Elsewhere it goes passive and dense.
- **Meaning:** Investment performance looks the most fragile assumption but barely moves the top, and shares at zero
  leave the payments and a $95,000 range intact. The passage then states the deposit and outside-money assumptions,
  treats Taiwan costs as a stated limit, and lists two margins and the remaining limits.
- **Check question (assumptions):** No, the unrounded top moves only from about $116,000 to $119,500 between 4% and 10%,
  because actual share performance before the 2031 meetings sets it. Its question-and-answer opening made this the
  easiest of the four to take in.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 11

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Polished and assured, with a writerly frame ("in miniature", "at a real price rather than assumed") and the
  image used to sort the table. It reads like a strong writer on a good day, a notch smoother than the IPS, though
  "WInS made most of that call for us" keeps a human shrug.
- **Meaning:** The book is the whole plan in miniature, which is why it holds a facility fund and VT. Every Treasury
  row is owned and VT is the one unsettled line, so the concentration is deliberate. IBTM does two jobs, the
  listings forced most maturities, and the trades met pre-set targets.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** Because the team bought every instrument of the two-deposit plan at a real
  price at $300,000 scale, rather than assuming it. That includes the 2028 floor fund and VT.
- **Image:** Helped. Treasuries as ink and VT as pencil sorts the table at a glance, though "the way Laura reads a page
  before it goes to print" stretches the picture slightly.

### Passage 12

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** It opens with a punchy line much like the elevator pitch. Then one long, eager sentence runs through
  diversification, a borrowed phrase ("the vain illusion that we can pick winners"), cost and expected return, and it
  ends on a question. This is the IPS's enthusiastic, slightly over-full rhythm.
- **Meaning:** VT is the one holding allowed to be wrong because no payment depends on it. It is cheap, broad and
  expected to earn about 7.5%, and it decides how generous the facility contribution can be.
- **Check question (portfolio as built, partly):** It tells me VT is there to set the facility contribution, but not why
  a $300,000 book that mirrors the 2027 deposit would hold it.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 13

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Casual and clear ("shrunk to", "pulls double duty", "it turns out to be sorted already"). The image arrives
  naturally and is explained at once. It sounds like a student who has learned to write a report without losing
  the IPS's ease.
- **Meaning:** The book is the whole plan scaled to $300,000, hence the facility fund and VT. Read as ink and pencil it
  is already sorted, so the concentration is deliberate. IBTM does two jobs, the listings forced the split between
  funds and bonds, and the buying met pre-set targets.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** The team wanted every instrument of the two-deposit plan actually bought, not
  just described, so the $300,000 book is that plan in small.
- **Image:** Helped. "Sorted already" makes the image do real work, splitting the table into what is owned and what is
  not yet.

### What separates the top from the bottom

The passages I ranked highest move the way the IPS moves. They make a short bold claim and then explain it in a looser,
slightly over-full sentence with dashes and brackets. They reach for a grand word now and then, reassure the reader
with "simply" or "the catch is", and their unevenness sounds like eager students. The middle passages (the
portfolio and assumption pieces) keep some of that through contractions, a "Yes," or a "!", or a question put to
themselves, but they carry dense, exact figures in tidy order, as if the same writers had been edited hard. The lowest
passages are compressed, passive or textbook-plain, with each sentence doing one job and no warmth, aside or turn.
Passage 5 also rests on a plan that seems to differ from the IPS's.
