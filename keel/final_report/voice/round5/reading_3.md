# Reading 3, voice rounds 5 and 6 (blind)

I read the IPS twice, the second time slowly. What I hear in it: people talking to Laura, warm and a little eager.
Their sentences swerve on a dash ("So, naturally, that was our plan – until we stopped and considered..."), and they
throw in an exclamation or a "worry not" or "simply". They allow themselves a showy word ("ever-reliable") and one or
two clumsy joins, small numbers come as digits ("10 Treasuries", "2 possible reasons"), and the letter closes on a
neat turn. It takes positions and doesn't hedge, but it isn't clipped.

### Passage 1

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Cool, clipped and aphoristic. Every sentence is load-bearing and two of them are built to be quoted ("a number said aloud can't quietly be halved", "Her spare money shrinks before her promise does"), with none of the IPS's dashes, asides or warmth. It also brings in a mechanism the IPS never mentions (kept shares spent to cover a reinvestment shortfall), which sits oddly beside the IPS's "half ... promised to no one" and its "only 2 possible reasons" to change the plan.
- **Meaning:** The 2031 range runs from the owned floor to the floor plus half the shares (moved into Treasuries first), and any shortfall in the ladder from reinvesting its interest is priced and covered from the kept shares before the range is named, or the range comes down.
- **Check question (assumptions, partly):** The passage implies the top would fall, since it is half the shares' value when they are moved, but it gives no size and doesn't say whether the assumed return matters.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 2

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Some of it has the team's ease ("WInS made most of that call for us", "we picked IBTR"), but most of it is compressed ("it holds what the 2027 deposit alone never would", "The execution held to targets set before the first order"), and some joins don't follow: "That's how 89.55% of the book ends up with one issuer" doesn't come from the line before it. It reads as someone packing an inventory tight, not talking it through.
- **Meaning:** The competition book is the whole plan scaled to $300,000, so it holds the facility fund and VT. It predates the reserve sizing, is almost all Treasuries on purpose, was shaped mostly by what WInS listed, and was bought to pre-set targets.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** It holds them because the book is Laura's whole plan, both deposits, scaled to $300,000 of $450,000, so every instrument the strategy depends on was bought at a real price; the 2028 share of the money is split about 73% floor fund and 27% VT.
- **Image:** It got in the way a little. Calling the Treasury rows ink and VT pencil is clear enough, but the passage then treats the image as the reason for the one-issuer weight, and that logic doesn't hold.

### Passage 3

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Careful and correct, but tight-lipped and often passive ("is assumed to land", "Rates are taken", "Two margins are chosen, not forecast", "the model being a model"). Clauses are squeezed together ("nothing by 31 March 2028 is the first of the IPS's two reasons"). It sounds like an editor's condensed version of a section, not the people who wrote the letter.
- **Meaning:** The 7.5% share return barely moves the top of the range; what moves it is actual markets before 2031. The passage also sets out the deposit, funding and inflation assumptions (the 1-in-20 cost-and-currency jump slightly exceeds her flexibility), the two deliberate margins, and the remaining limits.
- **Check question (assumptions):** No. From 4% to 10% the top before rounding only moves from about $116,000 to $119,500, inside one $5,000 step, because the top is set by what shares actually do before the 2031 meetings, not by the long-run assumption. Actual markets give anywhere from $95,000 to $140,000.
- **Image:** It helped. "Erase every pencil line and the page still reads" made the shares-to-zero test easy to picture and tied it straight to the definition.

### Passage 4

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** This is the IPS's voice almost exactly: an opinion, a dash-swerve, a slightly grand flourish ("near-ironclad guarantee", "the restless markets might decide to do"), and "Notably –" used as a hinge. Most telling is the habit of taking Laura's "high degree of certainty" and raising it, just as the IPS "decided to take that one step further". The small over-eagerness ("a nonexistent inflation risk") sounds like students.
- **Meaning:** Treasuries matched to dated, fixed-dollar payments remove uncertainty without needing growth or inflation protection, so holding them untouched is the point.
- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 5

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It keeps the IPS's tics (an exclamation mark after a number, "simply", "after all", contractions) and moves conversationally through a dense section. The ending, a run of short fragments ("The model is a model."), is a little staged, but the IPS also ends on short sentences.
- **Meaning:** The assumed share return hardly affects the top; actual markets do. It also covers the deposit timing, no outside funding, inflation and Taiwan costs (a limit, not a reason to change), the two deliberate margins, and the plain limits.
- **Check question (assumptions):** No. From 4% to 10% the top before rounding runs only from about $116,000 to $119,500, less than one $5,000 step. What sets the top is how shares actually perform before the 2031 meetings ($95,000 to $140,000 across the markets tested).
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 6

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Smooth and readable, with a friendly turn or two ("shrunk to the $300,000 we had to trade", "it turns out to be sorted already"), and it explains its choices rather than just listing them. It is more polished and even than the IPS, with none of its rough edges.
- **Meaning:** The book is the whole plan scaled down, which is why it holds the facility fund and VT. It is almost all Treasuries on purpose, the fund/bond split was forced by what WInS listed, and the buying hit pre-set targets.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** Because it is Laura's entire plan, both deposits, shrunk to the $300,000 the team could trade, so every instrument the strategy relies on was actually bought, not just described.
- **Image:** It helped a little. "Sorted already" into ink rows and one pencil line gave me a quick way to read the table, though it adds little beyond "Treasuries vs shares".

### Passage 7

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Even, confident and conversational ("Of everything we assume", "What's left are the limits"), and it uses the image cleanly once. A couple of sentences are a touch crafted ("Two safety margins we chose on purpose, and neither is a forecast"), more polished than the IPS writers usually are.
- **Meaning:** The long-run share return barely matters to the top; actual markets before 2031 do, and with shares at zero only the range collapses to $95,000. It also states the timing, funding, inflation, margin and limit assumptions.
- **Check question (assumptions):** No. Between 4% and 10% the top moves only from about $116,000 to $119,500 before rounding, not even one $5,000 step, because the actual path of shares before 2031 sets the top, not the assumed rate.
- **Image:** It helped. "Erase every pencil line – the shares gone to zero" glosses itself, so the stress test lands at once.

### Passage 8

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** It reads like the team explaining to a reader: it names the puzzle ("at first glance it doesn't look like the IPS"), asks the question outright and answers it, with digits for small numbers as in the IPS. It is plainer and less playful than the letter, but the way it moves (question, "Because...", "Most of that was forced") sounds like people.
- **Meaning:** The book holds the facility fund and VT because it is the whole plan scaled down. It is mostly Treasuries on purpose, its shape was largely forced by WInS listings, and the buying met pre-set targets.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** Because Table 1 is Laura's whole plan, both deposits, scaled to $300,000 of $450,000, so every instrument the strategy needs was bought rather than assumed; that is also why VT is 8.84% of the book but about 6.6% of the plan.
- **Image:** Not used by name; "the one line not yet settled" gestures at it without the words.

### Passage 9

- **Verdict:** Probably not
- **Why:** Clean, neutral and textbook-like, a competent explainer with no opinion, swerve or flourish. The IPS's own version of the last sentence ("Held to the end, these pay what they say – no price fluctuations for us to fear") has a voice this one lacks.
- **Meaning:** Of the two ways to cover fixed payments, the plan buys one Treasury per payment and holds it, so no forecast, trading or share price is involved.
- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 10

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** An opinionated opening ("VT is where we allow ourselves to be wrong"), a long sentence that overloads itself with enthusiasm ("the vain illusion that we can pick winners", "(as per JPMorgan)"), and a dash into a closing question. It has the same eager, slightly showy student voice as "So we hedge it!" and "But worry not".
- **Meaning:** VT is the one place the plan accepts risk, buying the whole market cheaply for growth, because the Treasuries already cover the payments and VT only decides how generous the facility gift can be.
- **Check question (portfolio as built, partly):** It explains why a world share fund is held at all (to set how generous the facility contribution can be while the Treasuries cover the payments), but not why the $300,000 book holds it when the IPS spends the 2027 deposit on the payments.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 11

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** The liveliest of the long assumption passages. It asks itself a question and answers it ("In our model, hardly."), turns with "The facility is another matter" much as the IPS turns with "The building is a different matter", and uses digits for small numbers as the IPS does. It is denser with figures than the letter, but it moves like the same people thinking aloud.
- **Meaning:** The 7.5% assumption looks fragile but barely moves the top; actual markets set it, and with shares at zero only the range shrinks to $95,000. It then covers the deposit, funding and inflation assumptions, the margins and the limits.
- **Check question (assumptions):** No. From 4% to 10% the top before rounding moves only from about $116,000 to $119,500, less than one $5,000 step; what sets it is what shares actually do before the 2031 meetings.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 12

- **Verdict:** Surely
- **Why:** The slightly loose grammar of the opener ("Some years, WInS lists no Treasuries maturing"), a pile-up of adjectives, "so we'll simply ignore price noise" (the IPS's "we'll simply complete it"), and "The catch is, ..." with a dash-turn. It sounds like a student defending a choice, keen and a bit wordy, just like the letter.
- **Meaning:** Where WInS had no matching Treasury, the team used iBonds funds, which behave like bonds held to maturity; the trade-off is a payout at net asset value rather than a promised par, in exchange for better liquidity.
- **Check question:** Neither fits.
- **Image:** Not used.

### Passage 13

- **Verdict:** Probably
- **Why:** Conversational and owning its choices ("The catch is", "Yes, 89.55% of the book is in one issuer, and that's on purpose", "Hence", "And the buying..."). It answers an objection before it is raised, the way the IPS does with "But worry not". It is a little more tidily built than the letter.
- **Meaning:** The book is the whole plan scaled down, so it holds the facility fund and VT. Its mix was mostly forced by WInS, its single-issuer weight is deliberate, and the buying met pre-set targets.
- **Check question (portfolio as built):** Because Table 1 is Laura's whole plan, both deposits, scaled to the $300,000 the team could trade, so every instrument the strategy relies on got bought and tested.

### What separates them

The passages I ranked highest sound like people with an opinion talking it through. They swerve mid-sentence on a dash and allow themselves a slightly grand or over-eager word ("near-ironclad", "vain illusion", "simply ignore"), and they leave a join or two a bit loose, just as the IPS does with "worry not" and "ever-reliable". The middle group (5, 6, 7, 8, 11, 13) keeps the team's habits (contractions, a question answered, "another matter", "Yes, ... on purpose"), but it is more even and more measured than the letter, as a longer, number-heavy report would be. The lowest are either compressed and passive (2, 3), textbook-neutral (9), or built from quotable, tightly balanced lines (1). In all of those every clause is controlled and nothing is loose, and that, more than any word, is what made them sound like someone other than the IPS's writers. The pencil-and-ink image helped when it was used to picture the shares-to-zero test ("erase every pencil line"). It added little when it only relabelled the table's rows, and once (Passage 2) it was made to carry an argument it couldn't.
