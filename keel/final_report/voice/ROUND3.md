# Voice round 3 (Mon 16 Nov 2026): the operating reserve and monitoring, plain and with one turn

Run in the Final Report day 9 run, on round 1's method (read Caplet whole, write forward, read blind), to separate the
conceit from the reading diet as round 2 asked. No rules came out of it; what follows is what the readers heard.

## What was done

Two writers each wrote four passages of about 305-345 words from the plain facts (`FACTS_PLAIN.md`) and the checked
facts in `round3/brief_F.md`: the operating reserve twice (P1 plain; P2 with one conceit turn, "the one line that
waited", with the Authors Guild's reserve against returns as its parallel) and monitoring as 2031 and 2033 approach twice
(P3 plain; P4 with one turn, printers' registration marks, which are checked on every pass and trimmed off the page).

| Writer | What it read before writing |
|---|---|
| F | Fresh: the IPS twice, Caplet's other writing (CORPUS section 5) and one team member's essays (a private file, read, not copied), writer E's diet from round 2, which was heard as Caplet's 12 of 12 |
| Keel's main session (K) | The IPS and the reflections, with all of Keel's files in its head (11 of 12 in round 2) |

The common brief (`round3/writer_common_r34.md`) asked each writer to warm up by rebuilding an IPS paragraph from notes
(Franklin's method). It named a paragraph the IPS does not have ("We'll start with the 10 payments"); both writers used
the January 2027 paragraph instead. The next brief quotes its anchor from the file.

Three fresh readers read the IPS twice, then got 13 unlabelled passages in different orders: the eight practice
passages, Caplet's three real Trading Note reflections (C1-C3) and two paragraphs of Keel's last IPS draft (X1-X2). For
each they said whether the IPS's writers could have written it (surely, probably, probably not, surely not), why, what
it meant, whether any image helped, and answered a check question ("If interest rates fell to zero in 2029, would the
payments still be paid?"; "In 2030 world shares fall 30%. Does Laura change the plan?"). Files: `round3/` (briefs,
writers, readings, the key in `passages.json` and `order_reader*.txt`, `build_packs_r34.py`, `score_r34.py`).

Every check question on a practice passage was answered right: zero rates in 2029 leave the payments paid, because the
extra Treasuries meet them even if waiting cash earns nothing; a 30% fall in 2030 changes nothing, because it lowers a
top not yet stated. The readers also noticed what Caplet's own passages leave open: on the reflections and Keel's old
IPS they answered "yes" but added that the passage "never mentions coupons or waiting cash". That is the question 3a of
the report answers, so 3a evaluates rather than repeats the IPS.

## How the readers heard the difference (their words, condensed)

- Top: "people talking to someone: a 'we' with opinions, one idea per sentence, a turn that comes out of their own
  reasoning, and small human roughness or enthusiasm", "a little loose and eager ... the odd clumsy join, only a few
  numbers, and one idea pushed with conviction by a 'we' that acts".
- Bottom: "compressed into noun stacks and lists of figures", "crafted fragments and aphorisms ('Most, not all.')",
  "images from outside the plan (printing plates, undefined 'ink') that the reader has to decode", "an extended
  borrowed image ... over material the IPS explained plainly, and close on a polished aphorism".
- Monitoring was the hard topic even plain: F's plain monitoring passage (0 of 3) was "the same dense checklist", every
  watch in one paragraph. Keel's plain one (3 of 3) opened on an expectation and reversed it, "the same move as the IPS
  letter", and ended flatly on "We'd carry on."

## The one turn, passage by passage

- **The one line that waited (reserve).** It helped understanding in every reading that commented: "'one line is still
  in pencil' made the reinvestment risk concrete"; "pencil and ink helped me see which single part was not yet secure".
  Keel's version was heard as possibly Caplet's twice in three ("the conversational push and the turn at the end feel
  within reach of the same writers"): it kept the loose "we" ("It waits more than you'd think", "And it isn't a small
  line, either"). F's version, which opened on "Laura's pages begin in pencil and end in ink" and closed on "a deposit,
  not a fee", was "epigram throughout". The Authors Guild reserve, a second reference beside the turn, "needed a second
  read" in all three readings.
- **Registration marks (monitoring).** It got in the way in all six readings: "ink" was used without a definition ("the
  ink itself, whether the last of it ever goes down"), the image arrived mid-list, and the point it made (watches, not
  triggers) "was already clear without them".

## What it says about the conceit (honest sentences, not rules)

The conceit is Caplet's decision and it stays intricate (Book 19). One turn per passage did better than round 2's
stacks (2 of 12 heard as possibly Caplet's, against 0 of 24), and the pencil line did real explaining work. What still
costs is how a turn is told: a turn the passage stops to build and then names, an aphorism to close it, a second
reference beside it, or "ink" used cold. The passage that came closest kept the team's loose voice around the image and
let the image do one job.

## Small things to carry

- Keep one parallel per passage. The Authors Guild reserve and the pencil line together were one reference too many.
- A monitoring passage is not a list: the plain version that was heard as Caplet's said what would not change first,
  then the few watches, then "We'd carry on."
- Never define "ink" by using it. The report defines it once at 2.0 (CONCEIT V2.2); every practice pack from round 5
  carries that definition in its preamble, as the report will.
