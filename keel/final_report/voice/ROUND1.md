# Voice round 1 (Sat 14 Nov 2026): three writers, three readers, read blind

## What was done

Three writers wrote the same three passages for the Final Report, about 350 words each, from the same plain facts
(`FACTS_PLAIN.md`): how certain Laura's payments really are, why half the shares are kept back, and the 5 October
"copout" argument for the Team Reflection.

- **Writer A** read everything Caplet has written: the IPS, the Trading Note reflections, the team's own lines,
  Ahaan's essays and the team's long messages (Books 12, 13, 16 and 17). Nothing of Keel's. (`round1/writer_A.md`)
- **Writer B** read only the IPS, the Trading Note reflections and the team's own lines. Nothing of Keel's.
  (`round1/writer_B.md`)
- **Keel's main session** wrote the same passages with all of Keel's working files in its head, straight after reading
  the IPS, the reflections and the essays. (`round1/keel_main.md`)

Both fresh writers also rebuilt two IPS paragraphs from notes (Franklin's method) before writing; that work is in
their files.

Three readers then read Caplet's IPS twice and got fourteen unlabelled passages in a different order each: the nine
practice passages, Caplet's three real Trading Note reflections, and two paragraphs from Keel's last IPS draft. For each
they said whether the people who wrote the IPS could have written it as part of a longer report, why, and what it
meant. The readers were not told how many passages were real.

## What they found

| Writer | Read as possibly Caplet's | Average place in the readers' rankings (1 = most like Caplet) |
|---|---|---|
| Caplet's own Trading Note reflections | 9 of 9 readings ("Surely" every time) | 2.0 |
| Writer B (Caplet's formal writing only) | 7 of 9 | 5.6 |
| Writer A (all of Caplet's writing) | 6 of 9 | 7.9 |
| Keel's main session | 0 of 9 | 10.6 |
| Keel's last IPS draft | 0 of 6 | 13.5 |

Every reader put all three real reflections in their top three. Every reader put Keel's old IPS paragraphs last. The
main session's passages came just above them in every reading.

All fourteen passages were understood in one reading, except one of Keel's old IPS paragraphs: a reader noticed that
it refers to "that fund" without ever saying which fund.

## How the readers heard Caplet

They heard people who are sure of their plan, warm towards Laura, and plainly enjoying the idea: explaining in quick
bursts, with asides and turns on a dash, reaching a little further than they need to, and saving one flourish for the
end. One reader put it as "students pleased with their plan and keen to talk it up". Another: "they sound like
teenagers keen to sound professional and to reassure, not like someone landing polished lines".

## Why the main session lost

Its passages were clear, and the readers understood them. They were not heard as Caplet because they were too
finished. "A staged drama, attacking the promise to test it, with short punchy sentences and quotable turns." "A
teacher's guided-tour structure, a neat reversal that sums up the point, and a measured 'honest warning'." "A
rhythmic, essayistic build-up that repeats one sentence shape for effect, and closes on a 'what it taught us about
ourselves' reflection." Each passage was built, and you can hear the building.

So this round answers part of the question in `WHY_OPAQUE.md`. The opaqueness Caplet saw in the IPS drafts did not come
back here: the main session wrote clearly when it wrote from the plain facts. What stayed is the performer. Keel still
writes as someone presenting an argument well, and Caplet writes as people telling you what they did and why they like
it.

The fresh writers came closer in every case, and each said, in its own words, what got it there. Writer A: their
warmth "comes from being sure of the plan, not from trying to be liked", and its second rebuild came closer by
"keeping their order of thought rather than from any particular words". Writer B: its first tries came out "correct
and quiet", and it only got closer once it let the writing have "a pulse: it gets excited, it reassures".

## Two things worth testing, not yet known

- Writer B, who read less, came out ahead of writer A, who read more. A reader called writer A's Team Reflection "the
  measured, balanced cadence of a mature essayist drawing a moral at the end". Ahaan's essays are long arguments
  written for a different purpose, and reading them right before writing may pull a report towards an essay. This is
  one round with three readers, so it is a question for round 2, not a finding.
- Nothing scored like the real reflections. The best practice passages were "Probably", never "Surely". The gap that
  remains is the bounce: the eagerness and the slight overreach that every reader named first.

## What changes

**For 27 Nov.** The main session does not write the report's sentences. A fresh writer who has read only Caplet's own
writing and the plain facts writes each part. The main session's job that day is everything around the writing: the
plan, the facts sheet each part is written from, and the checks on every figure and claim afterwards, as a separate
pass on finished text, with any fix sent back to the writer to make in the voice. This is now written into
`README.md` and the 27 Nov plan.

**For round 2 (Mon 16 Nov).** The same three topics again from blank pages, plus the 2031 range. Four writers: writer
B's reading again; Caplet's formal writing plus Ahaan's essays only; Caplet's formal writing plus the team's long
messages only; and the main session again, writing after the readings above. The readers again get Caplet's real
passages mixed in. If Caplet has ten minutes, the most useful reader of all would be one of the team, reading the
passages blind.
