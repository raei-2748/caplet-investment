**Stops named by all three readers** (each is a name or a reason missing, as in round 20):
1. "a decade of payments whose dates and amounts are not": the elided word is heard as "not owed" or "not uncertain"
   (Caplet's own line). Say it: "whose dates and amounts are fixed".
2. "facility", "the floor" and "co-sponsors" arrive (Table 1's roles, then the IBTM paragraph) before the reader knows
   there is a building, who the other funders are, or who the ten payments are owed to. Section 2 needs one sentence
   on Laura's two commitments before the table.
3. The parenthesis "(We built the account in October, before sizing one more set of Treasuries ...)" made no sense to
   any reader, even after decoding. It needs its numbers (the x1.5 reconciliation under Table 1) or moves to the
   table's note.
4. "Eric fell asleep before it": a name never set up, and no purpose in the paragraph. Out.

**Named by two:** why a bond paying out ten and a half months early is worse (the 2037 payment's date is never said);
"one too high could have meant a bond nobody was trading" (no reason given). **By one:** "our risks" in the title
question; the WInS gloss mid-sentence; "former ... latter"; "shortly before" is wrong for the two early bonds (judge).

**Voice.** All three heard the opening (Caplet's) as polished and literary and the continuation as plain and
procedural. The join shows. Friday's writers are told the register of Caplet's own Section 2 opening is the one to
continue (`CORPUS.md` plus Caplet's v2 text first in their diet), and the blind compare in rounds 10-12 checks the join
by name.

**Evaluation.** The judge found Section 2 mostly describes: it never weighs the 90% against an alternative or names
what it costs. Caplet's map puts the price in Section 3 ("What certainty costs"), so Section 2 needs one forward line
naming it, or the judge's Portfolio Analysis mark stays at 3.
