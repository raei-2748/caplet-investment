=== C ===
So, before you meet anyone in 2031, here is what happens – and what you'll be able to say.

In January 2028 we spend your second deposit in order of what can't wait. First the payments: whatever the ladder still lacks, plus some extra Treasuries, so that all 10 are met even if the interest they pay along the way earns nothing more. Next, the Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, which sets the bottom of your range – $95,000 on today's figures – and from that day no market can move it. Whatever's left goes into VT, which then has three years to do what shares do.

For those three years we change nothing, on purpose. Then, in January 2031, we sell half the shares and buy more of that same fund. Only after that do you name a figure, because only then is every dollar of it in Treasuries held for you.

On today's figures, the sentence you take into the room is short: "$115,000, all of it held in Treasury funds before I say it, $95,000 of it since January 2028." Bring a signed pledge letter too – amount, purpose, date. The Treasuries show the money exists; your signature says it will be given.

The other half of the shares stays yours, for whatever the building throws at you.

Yours,
Caplet
=== P3 ===
In 2031 Laura will give her co-sponsors a range for her building money: on today's figures (Treasury prices of 6 November 2026), $95,000 to $115,000. In January 2028, once the ten payments are covered, we put most of what is left of her second deposit into a fund that holds US Treasuries maturing in 2032 and pays out their value that December, and the remainder into shares. That first purchase sets the bottom, and from that day no market can move it. In January 2031, before any meeting, we sell half the shares and buy more of the same fund; together the two purchases set the top. We count both ends cautiously and round them down, so each sits a little below what the fund is set to pay.

Seen from today, neither end is settled. The bottom depends on Treasury yields until January 2028, the top on those and on shares until January 2031: in the futures our model simulates, the top reaches at least $100,000 in about 91 of 100, and $115,000 in about 57. So Laura names nothing until the shares are moved: a top named a year earlier would exceed what the move could buy in about 11 futures in 100. Once they are moved, we are certain of the range she states: the Treasuries that will pay it are already held for her.

Why a range at all, then? Because its two ends were made safe at different times, by different money. The bottom she could have stated had every share gone to nothing; the rest is what half the shares added when we moved them. A co-sponsor can see exactly how much of her word ever rested on shares.
=== P5 ===
Laura's building money comes from one fund, bought twice, that holds US Treasuries maturing in 2032 and pays out their value that December, just before the residency opens. In January 2028, once the ten payments are covered, we put a little over half her second deposit into it; on today's figures (Treasury prices of 6 November 2026), that alone sets the bottom of her 2031 range at $95,000. We put the rest into VT, a world share fund: no promise rests on it yet, so it can afford a risk for growth.

In January 2031 we sell half the shares and buy more of the same fund, so that whatever figure Laura names is paid for before she names it; the other half we keep in shares, promised to no one, for what nobody can price yet. The move lifts the top to $115,000 on today's figures; left in shares, that top would rest on a share price that could fall before it is paid. The half we keep is her flexibility, for building costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar (the building is priced in it) or a co-sponsor falling short. It stays in shares because building costs have risen in about four years out of five since 1970, in Taiwan and five other economies, so money kept for them should grow too – though it can also fall.

Half is our choice, not the answer to an equation. Keep only 30% back and the top rises about $5,000, but her flexibility then covers a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar in about half our model's futures, not 93 in 100. Either way, her total barely moves: the split decides which of her money is promised, not how much there is.
=== NOTES ===
Carrying the letter on put the steps back in the order the money arrives and made "we" the one who buys and moves, so in both passages each step came with its reason in the same sentence rather than in a sentence after it. It also kept me from defining things the letter never stopped to define, which meant I had to add only the glosses a cold reader truly needs (what the 2032 fund pays and when, what "today's figures" means). P3 was harder: the reader's natural question is why a range is needed once both ends are held, and the only honest answer is that the two ends were made safe at different times by different money, which takes the whole sequence to set up before it means anything.
