## The clearest passage

It gives a reason for each step, including the order: the bottom is bought straight after the payments because nothing comes in after that deposit; shares come last because no promise rests on them; and the kept half stays unpromised because promising it would mean promising a forecast. It also uses real dollar amounts ($81,244, about $102,000, $95,000, $29,594), so I could follow the money. Passage 1 comes close and is the only one that says why the kept half stays in shares (building costs keep rising). Passage 3 was the easiest to read through once, but it left "why half" and "why so stingy" open.

## Any pattern

- The same reason went missing again and again: why half. Passages 2, 3, 4 and 5 move half the shares in 2031 without saying why half, or what the other half does. Only 1 and 6 give the trade-off.
- Reasons stated as effects: "count cautiously ... so each sits a little below" (5) and "stingily" (3) say what happens, not why it matters to me. Passage 2's "so that she never states more than the fund delivers" is the version that satisfies.
- Words that point at something off the page or earlier in the sentence: "it" in the letter (L09-L11), "a different matter" (P404.1), "a year earlier" (P405.8), "finished" (P406.3). Each cost me a reread.
- Packing several figures or facts into one sentence (P402.7, P402.10, P405.7) meant rereading; short sentences that each did one thing (most of Passage 3) were understood the first time.
- The letter has the most failures: an undefined name, a referent I still can't fix (L09), and one step (L14) that undoes an earlier claim (L09's "sets the bottom").
