## Steps I could not give a reason for

1. Letter R9: why the purchase is made in 2028 and not 2031. The reason given (co-sponsors, grants and fees shrink in recessions) shows why her contribution should rest on Treasuries, not why the timing; it is also unclear whether "it" is the Treasury holding or the share fund.
2. Letter R4: why no rung is ever sold before it pays out, and what the X and R each mean. Neither is given.
3. Letter R12: why a shortfall in the extra reserve is taken from the 2028 holding (which was said to set the bottom) rather than the reverse. The priority of the payments over the bottom is implied, never said.
4. Passage 2 R1 and R3: why the building is "a different matter", and why the rest of the deposit goes into shares at all. No reason for either.
5. Passage 3 R8: why the Treasuries are counted stingily. The rule is given, but not its purpose; I filled in "so she never overstates" myself.
6. Passage 7 R5: counting both ends cautiously. The only "because" is the step restated (each sits a little below what the fund pays).
7. Passage 5 R2 and Passage 6 R2: why that size goes into the Treasury fund ("a little over half", "$81,244"). In Passage 6 the cautious count from $102,000 to $95,000 is also never explained.
8. Passage 1 R1: how the team chooses between a bond and an ETF for each year ("whichever works best" is not a reason).

## The clearest passage

Passage 4. Every step comes with its reason in the same sentence or the one after it, including the one the others skip: the cautious count is given its purpose up front ("so that she never states more than the fund delivers"). Its answer to "why a range at all" gives both the case's ask and what each end separately says. Passage 6 gives the best reasons for the order of purchase (bottom straight after the payments, shares last, and the unpromised half because "promising it would mean promising a forecast"), but it stumbles on "once the ten payments are finished" and on a counting rule it never states.

## Any pattern

- **Counting rules without purposes.** "Cautiously", "stingily" and "less a margin" appear in most texts; only Passage 4 says why (so she never states more than the fund pays). Elsewhere the step is either unexplained or explained by restating it.
- **Reasons attached to the wrong step.** In the Letter (L11) the recession argument justifies resting on Treasuries but is offered for the purchase date. In Passage 5 (P305.7), building costs rising justifies a growth asset, not shares in particular, and covers one of three uses.
- **Sizes never justified.** How much goes into the Treasury fund in 2028 ("a little over half", "$81,244", "most") is never argued, while the 50/50 split in 2031 is argued carefully in Passages 5 and 6.
- **References that have to be held.** Most Follow-2 scores come from "it", "that half", "after this", "finished" or "a year earlier" pointing back to something earlier, and from "seen from today" versus "from that day" seeming to contradict each other (Passage 7).
- **Probability sentences.** Packing two model figures with a condition into one sentence (91 and 57 in 100, half against 93 in 100) cost a second reading every time, though the logic held once unpacked.
