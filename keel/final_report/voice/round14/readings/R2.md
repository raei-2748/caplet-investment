## The clearest passage

Passage 6. It gives a reason for where each dollar goes in order: the Treasury purchase comes straight after the payments because nothing arrives after 2028; the shares go last because no promise depends on them; one half is moved to turn share growth into money Treasuries will pay; the other half stays unpromised because promising a share price would be promising a forecast. It uses exact dollar sums, and it says why half rather than less. The others leave at least one of these reasons out. Its one slip is "once the ten payments are finished" (P206.3).

## Any pattern

What failed was mostly words pointing back at something the page had not pinned down: "it" with no clear noun (Letter L09-L11), "that half" or "the other half" after "up to half" (Letter L14-L18), "the payments" used to mean the purchases for them (P206.3, P206.5), and openers that point off the page (P205.1). Next came compressed phrases that needed unpacking: "covers a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar", "interest earned nothing more", "spend the uncertainty". The one reason most often left out was why the ends are counted cautiously. Several passages also say the bottom is fixed or "no market can move it" next to "seen from today, neither end is settled", and the reader has to work out the dates to see they don't contradict. The letter is the one text where the bottom really is undermined: the 2028 holding "sets" it, then may be drawn down in 2031.
