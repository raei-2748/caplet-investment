## Steps I could not give a reason for

1. Letter R5: why five of the ten rungs are funds rather than bonds; the funds' drawback is stated, their reason for being chosen is not.
2. Letter R11: why the extra reserve is paid for by selling shares, why "up to half", and why it waits until 2031 when the risk (R10) exists from 2027.
3. Letter R9: the reason given (recessions hit co-sponsors and shares together) is clear, but I could not tell what "it" was, so the step it justifies is lost.
4. Passage 1 R4: why half the shares are moved, and what the unmoved half is for; the passage never says.
5. Passage 4 R4: same gap, why half, and nothing on the half left in shares.
6. Passage 6 R6: same gap, why half is sold, and what the rest of the shares do.
7. Passage 1 R2 and Passage 6 R2: why "most" of the 2028 money goes to the Treasury fund and not some other share of it; no reason for the size of the split (Passage 7 gives one: promises first, the rest last).
8. Passage 2 R1 and R5: "a different matter" from what, and why half the shares are kept back.

## The clearest passage

Passage 7. Every purchase carries its reason, including the order of purchase (the bottom first because nothing arrives after the 2028 deposit; shares last because no promise depends on them) and why the kept half cannot be promised (its value is a share price, so promising it means promising a forecast). It gives real dollar amounts for each slice, and it alone explains both halves of the 2031 split and why the split is half. Passage 5 comes close, but its reason for keeping the flexibility in shares does not hold up.

## Any pattern

- The step most often left without a reason is "half": Passages 1, 4 and 6 move half the shares but never say why half or what the other half is for, so the range looks complete while a quarter of the 2028 money disappears from the story. Passages 5 and 7, which name the kept half as flexibility and price the 30% alternative, have no such gap.
- Following broke on pointers rather than on hard ideas: "it" with two possible antecedents (Letter L09-L11, Passage 7 P107.5), "a different matter" with no referent (Passage 2), "finished" for payments that run to 2042, and a metaphor ("spend the uncertainty") that needs the next sentence to mean anything.
- The letter packs conditional steps into chains (up to half, if that half falls short, after this, whatever of the first half), so each step can only be understood by holding the previous two; the passages that do one step per sentence read in one pass.
- Two claims did not hold up for a finance reader: a term fund does not "pay what it says" (Passage 3), and how often building costs rose is not a reason to hold shares rather than Treasuries (Passage 5).
