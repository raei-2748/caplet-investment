Readers read short texts about one client (Laura Gao, planning an artists' residency in Taiwan, with $300,000 in 2027 and $150,000 in 2028, ten $50,000 payments 2033-2042 that must be met with a high degree of certainty, and a contribution to the building that she states to co-sponsors as a range in 2031). After reading each text once, each reader retold from memory every step the text takes and the reason it gave, for a friend who keeps asking "but why?".

Your job: mark every retelling line with one letter, judging the **reason** ("Because") against the **step**, with the text beside you:
- **E**, explains: the reason does more than say the step again, it is what the text gave (or a fair reading of it), and a sceptical friend would accept that it produces the step.
- **R**, restates: the reason is the step in other words, circular, empty, or a name or label offered as a reason ("because that is the rule", "to set the bottom" for buying what sets the bottom).
- **M**, missing: the reader recalled no reason ("none that I recall"), or only a guess the reader marks as their own.
- **W**, wrong: the reason is not what the text said, or contradicts it.
Judge each line on its own; you are not told who wrote the texts, and it does not matter. Do not be charitable to a reason the reader supplied from outside the text: mark it M (if the reader says it is a guess) or W.

Write a Markdown file named in your instructions with one table per reader and text: columns `reader | text | line | mark | why (a few words)`, one row per retelling line, every line marked. Nothing else is needed.
