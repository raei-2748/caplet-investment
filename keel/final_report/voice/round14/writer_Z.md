=== C ===
So here's what happens between now and your first meeting with a co-sponsor.

When the 2028 deposit arrives, we spend it in a strict order. First we finish the ten payments, because they're fixed. Then we buy more of the 2032 Treasury fund for your floor, so that the least you'll be able to promise is held in Treasuries three years before you promise it. Shares come last, because nothing depends on them – and then, for three years, we leave everything alone.

Please don't name a figure before January 2031, however nicely you're asked. Name a top a year early and, in about 11 of our 100 simulated futures, the 2031 move would buy less than you'd said.

In January 2031, before your first meeting, we'll sell half your shares and put the money into that same 2032 fund. After that, whatever figure you name is paid by Treasuries already held for you. On today's figures you could tell them: $115,000, all of it held before you said it, $95,000 of it since January 2028. Say it in US dollars, give them the Taiwan-dollar value on the day, and offer a signed pledge letter – the Treasuries show the money exists, and your signature says it will be given. The other half of your shares stays out of the conversation; that half is yours.
=== P3 ===
In 2031 Laura gives her co-sponsors one figure to plan around. On today's figures it is $115,000 towards the building, every dollar of it paid by Treasuries held for her before she says it, and $95,000 of it held since January 2028. That is her range, $95,000 to $115,000, put the way a co-sponsor will use it.

Seen from today, nobody can tell her what that top will be. It depends on what her shares do until January 2031. Across 200,000 simulated futures, our model gives a top of at least $100,000 in about 91 of every 100, and of at least $115,000 in about 57. That is a forecast, and a forecast is the one thing Laura should not hand to people deciding how big a building to fund.

So we spend the uncertainty before she speaks. In January 2031, before the first meeting, we sell half her shares and put the money into the fund that pays the $95,000, bought with part of her 2028 deposit: a Treasury fund holding Treasuries maturing in 2032. From then, nothing about the figure she states is uncertain, because the Treasuries that will pay it are held for her. We count them stingily, too: as if none of their interest earned anything more, less 0.5% for the fund's costs, rounded down to a $5,000 step.

Why give two ends if both are paid for? Because they answer two questions. The top is what she gives. The bottom is what she would still have given had every share she owned become worthless, which shows a co-sponsor how much of her figure never rested on a share price.
=== P5 ===
Laura's facility contribution is paid by one Treasury fund, IBTM, which holds Treasuries maturing in 2032 and pays out their value in mid-December that year. We buy it twice.

The first time is January 2028: once the ten payments are finished, we put $81,244 of her second deposit into IBTM. On today's figures that pays about $102,000, which, counted cautiously and rounded down, is the $95,000 bottom of her 2031 range. It comes straight after the payments because nothing arrives after that deposit, so whatever Laura will promise must be bought from it, and buying the bottom then settles it three years before she states it. We put the last $29,594 into VT, a fund of world shares. It goes last because no promise depends on it, which is why it may be shares: they should earn more than Treasuries, and can fall.

In January 2031, before Laura meets a co-sponsor, we split the shares into two halves with two jobs. We sell one half and put the money into IBTM, turning three years of share growth into money Treasuries will pay: her top, $115,000 on today's figures, is held before she says it. The other half stays in shares, promised to no one, because its value is a share price and promising it would mean promising a forecast. It is her flexibility, for the three ways a building asks for more: costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar, a co-sponsor who falls short.

Half is our choice, not a calculation. Keep only 30% back and her top rises about $5,000, but her flexibility alone would cover a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar in about half our simulated futures, not 93 in 100.
=== NOTES ===
Carrying the letter on put "we" back in charge of the verbs and made me give each step its reason in the same sentence, the way the letter does, so in the passages money never acts on its own: we buy, we sell, we count. It also gave me the order of spending as the spine of P5, since the letter had already taught me to explain a step by what comes before and after it. P3 was harder: a reader who knows only the case expects a range to mean doubt, so the passage has to show both that the doubt is real from today and that it is gone by the time she speaks, and then still give a reason for two ends. I answered that with the bottom as the part that never rested on a share price, but it is the sentence I would test first.
