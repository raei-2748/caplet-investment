# Voice rounds 12 and 13: Keel's prediction, written before any writer's text was read (Sun 22 Nov 2026, morning)

## Round 12 ("we" and the why; three passages by fresh writers W and X on FACTS_PLAIN version 3; four blind readers)

- **Calibration with the new Why column.** Round 11's readers flagged a mean 8.7 of the 17 version 61 sentences Caplet
  could not follow, and none flagged V12, V13, V16, V20 or V21 (clear as grammar, empty as reasons). With a per-sentence
  "could you tell a friend why, from this page alone?" column: at least three of those five get Why N from at least
  half the readers, and each reader's flags rise to 11-13 of 17. If fewer than three of the five are caught, the
  column does not do its job and round 14 needs another test (a reader asked to retell the passage's reasons cold).
- **Fresh writers.** Rereads fall from round 11's 24-32% to 15-22%; "no sense" stays 4-8%; Why N on 10-20% of
  sentences that take a step or make a claim. Caplet's own paragraphs (the controls): rereads about 10%, Why N about
  15%, since the IPS is a letter that asserts more than it argues ("only 2 possible reasons").
- **By topic.** P3 (the range in two layers) fails Why most often, because "why give a range at all" has an answer that
  needs both layers held at once; P1 (the ten payments) fails it least.
- **"We".** Caplet's IPS uses "we", "our" or "us" about 2.3 times per 100 words. Both writers land between 1.5 and 3.5.

## Round 13 (one piece of 2,000-2,400 words; writer L studied long-form explainers first, writer M did not; six blind readers, three per piece, each reading one piece once)

- **Recall.** L's readers state the plan's argument in three sentences correctly more often (2 or 3 of 3 against 1 or
  2 of 3 for M) and name at least one "so that is why" moment each; M's readers name fewer.
- **Losing the thread.** Both pieces lose readers around the 2031 range (the two layers); M loses them at three or
  more paragraphs per reader, L at two or fewer.
- **The risk for L.** Set-ups heard as signposts (round 11's sixth kind of failure): at least one of L's readers names a
  sentence that promises a point instead of making it. If two or three do, set-up and payoff must be done with
  questions the facts raise, never with announcements.
