## The clearest passage

## Any pattern

- **Fixedness by label.** Passages that spell out the mechanism (1 and 4: a Treasury held to maturity pays exactly its stated interest and face value, guaranteed by the US government) carry their claims. Passages that skip it (6, 8, 9) put a label in its place ("pay what they say", "ever-reliable", "bought as Treasuries"), and the claim floats.
- **The reinvestment condition is never tied to the price paid.** Three passages say the ten reach $50,000 "only if" interest is reinvested at implied rates, but none says the purchase was sized on that assumption, so each time the condition seems to contradict the "pays exactly" sentence just before it.
- **Things named as if already known.** "The Treasury fund" (P302.1), "more of IBTM" (P303.2), "the $115,000 top" (P302.6), "$11,500 more" (P305.11), "a different matter" (P308.1). A page turned to cold cannot resolve them.
- **"Half" is the most common unexplained quantity.** No passage says why half, and in passages 3 and 10 the other half disappears.
- **"So" and "which is why" with a premise left out**: "could never be closed", "building should wait".
- **The letter fails by compression rather than by missing reasons.** Two-step mechanics (L09-L10) and back-references ("that half", "after this", "the first half") make the reader hold several sentences at once. Its reasons are mostly present but have to be rebuilt.
