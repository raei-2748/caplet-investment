## From memory

1. **How much, and when?** $115,000 towards the building, paid in January 2033. She states a range of $95,000 to $115,000 but signs for the top, $115,000.

2. **Could it end up lower?** I am not fully sure, and that is the problem. Her own words say no: the $115,000 is already held in US Treasuries, which the government guarantees, and she has signed for it. But she still gives a range down to $95,000, and I don't remember being told what would make it $95,000. The longer letter said the bottom has a margin taken off because a fund's payout isn't fixed, and that she gives "what those Treasuries pay, up to the top", so I suppose a fund paying a little less could take it slightly under $115,000. I would not expect it to go below $95,000.

3. **What is the $95,000 in her words?** The bottom of her range: the part held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028.

4. **How sure is she, and why believe her?** Very sure. The money is already held in US Treasuries before she signs, so the letter is backed by money that exists, unlike Lehman's $10 million pledge to Spelman, which was to come from future earnings and paid only $3 million when the bank failed. The running-cost payments were bought in 2027-28, and she puts the whole commitment, about $615,000, in writing in 2031.

5. **What I would write down, and the one thing I'd ask.** I would book $115,000 from Laura towards the building, due January 2033, with $95,000 noted in the file as her stated bottom. Before committing our own money I would ask her to say in writing what, if anything, could make her pay less than the $115,000 she signs for. A board that hates surprises needs to know why a range still exists.
