## From memory

1. **How much, and when:** $115,000 towards the building, paid in January 2033.
2. **Could it end up lower?** By her own words, probably not: all $115,000 is already held in US Treasuries before she signs, and she says no market can change what they pay. But she also called it a range of $95,000 to $115,000, so $95,000 seems to be the floor. Nothing in her words told me what would push it down towards $95,000. The earlier letter suggested two causes: a Treasury fund's payout isn't fixed, and the 2028 holding may have to cover a shortfall in the reserve if waiting money earns little. Neither appears in what she says to us.
3. **The $95,000 in her words:** the part of her building gift that has been held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028, the bottom of her range.
4. **How sure, and why believe her:** Very sure. She has signed for it in writing, and the money already exists in US government-guaranteed Treasuries bought before she signed. The Lehman/Spelman story was her contrast: a pledge backed only by future money failed, and hers is not like that.
5. **What I would write down, and the one thing I would ask:** I would book $115,000, payable January 2033, and note the ten $50,000 running-cost payments separately. Before committing our own money, I would ask her to state plainly in the letter whether $115,000 is firm, or under what circumstances she would pay less, down to $95,000. My board hates surprises, and "the one figure you can plan around" sitting next to "my range is $95,000 to $115,000" is exactly the kind of gap they would query.
