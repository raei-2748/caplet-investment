## From memory

1. **How much, and when?** $115,000 towards the building, paid in January 2033. (Plus $50,000 a year for running costs, 2033-2042.)

2. **Could it end up lower?** As she puts it, no: she says the whole $115,000 is already held in US Treasuries that no market can move, so short of a US default it will be there. From the first letter I half-remember that some of the holdings are Treasury funds whose payout isn't fixed, that the bottom carries a margin for that, and that she gives "what those Treasuries pay, up to the top". So if anything lowers it, it would be a fund paying a little less than planned (or a US default), and the floor I'd hold in mind is $95,000. Her own words don't say it could fall, which is part of why I'm unsure what the range is for.

3. **The $95,000 in her words:** the part of the $115,000 that has been held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028; the bottom of her range.

4. **How sure, and why believe her?** She sounds fully sure: the money is held in Treasuries before she signs, guaranteed by the US government, and she contrasts herself with Lehman's pledge to Spelman, which rested on money that didn't exist yet and paid only $3 million of $10 million. She also signs a letter with amount, purpose and date. That is a better basis than most founder pledges I see. What weakens it slightly is that she gives a range and then signs for the top without saying what the range is protecting against.

5. **What I'd write down, and one thing to ask.** I would book $115,000 as a signed pledge receivable in January 2033, and in the board memo note $95,000 as the part held since 2028. Before committing our own money, I would ask for a current statement from her broker or custodian showing the Treasuries held for her, with their maturity dates and the amounts they are due to pay by December 2032, adding up to the $115,000.
