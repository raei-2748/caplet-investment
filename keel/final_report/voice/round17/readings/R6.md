## From memory

1. **How much, and when?** $115,000 for the building, paid in January 2033. (Plus $50,000 a year for running costs from 2033 to 2042, about $615,000 in all.)

2. **Could it end up lower?** The passage says no: she signs for $115,000 and all of it is already in US Treasuries that the government guarantees and no market can change. But it also gives a range of $95,000 to $115,000, and I do not remember anything that would bring it down to $95,000 once the money is held. I left unsure why a range is stated at all. (The earlier letter said a fund's payout is not fixed, so there is a small margin, and the bottom could fall if the half of shares sold in 2031 was not enough for the extra reserve; I do not think Passage 2 said this.)

3. **The $95,000 in her words:** the part of the $115,000 that has been held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028; the bottom of her range.

4. **How sure, and why believe her?** She is sure: the money is already in guaranteed US Treasuries before she signs, and she gives a signed letter with amount, purpose and date. The Lehman and Spelman story shows the difference: a pledge from future money can fail, hers is money already there.

5. **What I would write down, and what I would still ask:** I would book $115,000 as her own funding share for the building, due January 2033, on the strength of the signed letter. Before committing ministry money I would ask for proof of resources: a statement from the institution that holds the Treasuries for her, showing the amounts and the dates they pay out before January 2033.
