## From memory

1. **How much, and when?** $115,000 towards the building, paid in January 2033. The earlier letter said she would give "what those Treasuries pay, up to the top" in 2033. Her own words to co-sponsors say $115,000.

2. **Could it end up lower?** In her own words, no. She says the $115,000 has been held in US Treasuries since January 2031, that no market can change what they pay, and she signs for it. But she also gives a range down to $95,000, which leaves me unsure. The earlier letter explained what could pull it down: some of the holdings are Treasury funds whose payout isn't a fixed sum (hence a margin off the bottom). And if the shares sold in 2031 fell short of the extra reserve for the running payments, the bottom's holding would make up the gap. So the realistic low is about $95,000, and less only through those fund-payout and top-up effects, which happen before she names the figure.

3. **What is the $95,000?** The bottom of her range: money held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028.

4. **How sure is she, and why believe her?** She sounds certain. She gives no percentage, just "guaranteed by the US government". A funder should believe her because the money already exists in Treasuries before she signs. That is the contrast with the Lehman pledge to Spelman, which was promised out of money the bank did not yet have. She also offers a signed letter with the amount, purpose and date.

5. **What I would book, and the one thing I would ask.** I would book $115,000, a written pledge payable January 2033, with $500,000 of operating support alongside (about $615,000 in all). Before committing our own money, I would ask for one thing: a dated statement from her broker showing the Treasuries held for the building and when they mature, so our finance office can confirm the $115,000 exists. I would also want the letter to name one figure, not a range.
