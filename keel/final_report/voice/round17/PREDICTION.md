# Voice round 17: Keel's prediction, written before anyone reads (Tue 24 Nov 2026, 06:37 Sydney)

**The question (the day's job: how "how sure" is said so people hear it right).** Laura's words to co-sponsors must
state a range, because the case asks for one, and she signs for its top. Two orders are on file. The tree's Branch 5
(panel 5, Mon 23 Nov) has her state the range first, both ends with their dates, then the confidence, then why she signs
the top. The fact sheet (`FACTS_PLAIN.md` l.81) has her give the one figure to plan around first, "$115,000, all of it
held ... $95,000 of it since January 2028". Round 16's readers gave the range sentence a 2 (five of six) and asked why
the top (four of six). The risk this round tests is the one the RBA's review found in another setting: listeners keep
part of what is said and drop the rest. Said range first, a co-sponsor may hear that her gift could land anywhere from
$95,000 to $115,000, when after the 2031 move she gives $115,000 and no market can change it.

**Design.** One fresh writer (brief `brief_r17_X.md`, the fact sheet version 7 with round 16's repairs) writes the
team's lead-in (L, owned against signed, Lehman and Spelman) and Laura's words twice: R, the range first, and F, the
figure first, with every other sentence the same. Six readers in two arms, one of each role per arm: a co-sponsor (the
head of giving at a family company in Taiwan that has sponsored arts venues), a foundation programme officer (a US
foundation that funds artists' residencies), and a Taiwan arts official (a grants officer at the Ministry of Culture
who reads English well). Each is calibrated on version 61 (Caplet followed sentences 1, 5, 6 and 8 only), reads
Caplet's own IPS paragraph C3 as a control, then L and R (readers 1-3) or L and F (readers 4-6), marking Follow and
Sense per sentence. Then five questions from memory: what she will give and when; whether it could end lower and what
could lower it; what the $95,000 is; how sure she is and why to believe her; what the reader would book or plan on, and
what one thing they would still ask her for.

**Keel's prediction.**
1. **Heard right on whether it could end lower:** all three F readers say no market can now lower it; at most two of
   the three R readers do, and at least one R reader says it could land anywhere between $95,000 and $115,000.
2. **What they would book:** all three F readers book $115,000; at least one R reader books the bottom or the range.
3. **Nothing lost on the case's ask:** all three F readers still name both ends from memory, so figure first costs
   nothing on "a range".
4. **Round 16's repairs work in both arms:** the why-the-top sentence is questioned by at most one reader of six (four
   of six in round 16), and Laura's words are followed first time in at least 70% of sentences in each arm (about half
   in round 16), against Caplet's own paragraph followed whole.
5. **What a funder still asks for** will be a statement or proof of the holding, the letter's terms if she dies or the
   residency changes, or a timetable for the other funders; no reader will ask for a probability.

**The rule, set now.** If F is heard right at least as often as R on questions 2 and 5 and loses nothing on naming both
ends, Laura's words lead with the figure (the fact sheet's form) and the tree's Branch 5, WORDS and the notes change the
same morning. If the two arms tie, the tree keeps panel 5's order, which follows the case's own order (a range, then how
confident). If R is heard right more often, the fact sheet's l.81 changes to the range-first form.
