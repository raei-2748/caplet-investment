## 2. Step one: carry the letter on (about 150 words)
The IPS ends: "You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that's your job. With these promises, ours is to take none."
Start straight after that line, as the same people in the same letter to Laura, and keep going: what she will be able
to tell her co-sponsors in 2031. This is a warm-up to get their voice in your ear.

## 3. Step two: the team's lead-in and two versions of Laura's words
Now the same people write their report. Its readers are the competition's judges, so in the lead-in Laura is "Laura"
or "she", and the team is "we". Figures are "on today's figures" (Treasury prices at the close on 6 November 2026).

**L (two or three sentences): the team's lead-in.** Why her words carry a signed pledge letter as well as money held:
holding the Treasuries shows the money exists; a signed letter (amount, purpose, date) says it will be given, and is
what a funder's gift office can count. One outside fact, in one sentence: in October 2007 Lehman Brothers itself
pledged $10 million to Spelman College over five years, to be paid from the bank's own future money; when the bank
failed in 2008 the college received $3 million of it. Laura's money is held for her before she signs.

**R (about 180 words): Laura's own words, the range first.** A short passage set apart, in her voice, the way she would
say it to a funder in 2031: full sentences, the year named each time, no finance jargon, one idea in each sentence,
and every "it" or "this" plainly pointing at one thing. In this order:
- two sentences of purpose from the case (who the residency serves, why Taiwan, why her);
- the range for her building contribution, with its two dates, each end in a sentence of its own: the bottom, $95,000,
  held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028; the top, $115,000, held since January 2031, when she moved half of
  her shares into Treasuries;
- one plain sentence on how sure she is, with the Treasuries as its subject (strong words belong to the Treasuries;
  the fund that holds them is not said to pay a fixed sum);
- why she signs for the top rather than the bottom, in her own words (the fact sheet gives the reason);
- her whole commitment: $50,000 a year for the residency's running costs from 2033 to 2042, bought from January 2027
  and complete since January 2028, and $115,000 for the building, about $615,000 in all;
- its date and form: committed in writing now, in 2031; the $115,000 paid in January 2033.

**F: the same passage with the figure first.** Use R's sentences word for word, except the sentences that state the
building figures. In F, Laura first names the one figure she signs for and co-sponsors can plan on, $115,000, and then
gives it as the range the case asks for, in one or two sentences: $95,000 of it held for her since January 2028, the
rest since January 2031, when she moved half her shares, so her range is $95,000 to $115,000. Then R's remaining
sentences follow in R's order, unchanged except where a word must change for the sentence to read right after the new
opening (say which, in NOTES). Change nothing else between R and F.
