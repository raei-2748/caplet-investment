## The sentences that made least sense

1. **P204.12**: "the range is certain, and her contribution is the top". If she is sure to give the top, there is no range of outcomes, and a "certain range" means nothing. This undercuts the very thing she is meant to give co-sponsors.
2. **P204.14**: "Because the two ends say different things." It answers "Why not just say $115,000?" with where each end's money came from, which is history and no reason co-sponsors need two figures.
3. **L11**: "It is bought in 2028, not 2031". Grammatically "It" is the world index fund just named, and that makes the reason absurd (shares bought early because the residency can't depend on shares). It only works if "It" is the Treasury floor.
4. **P203.2**: brings in "the December 2032 Treasury fund" without introducing it and doesn't say why it would pay more than her stated top. It also adds Treasury money to a quantity defined as half her shares.
5. **P203.8**: "The honest limit is the order in which those things can come." It doesn't say what is limited, and the sequence that follows (shares slump, then costs rise) isn't an order of "those things".
6. **P202.10**: the ten cost about $301,500 against $300,000 (about $1,500 short), yet $2,388 is bought a year later with no explanation on the page.
7. **P202.12**: "Only a US default, or no second deposit, could stop one", straight after "From January 2028". By then the second deposit has arrived, so the two time frames are run together.
8. **P208.2**: "is spent that month, because nothing comes in after it". The lack of later money isn't a reason to invest it all at once.

## Any pattern

- **References to things the page has not set up, or set up ambiguously:** "it" in L09-L11, "the 2028 holding" and "after this" in L14-L15, "the December 2032 Treasury fund" in P203.2, "those things" in P203.8. When a sentence failed, it was usually leaning on something that wasn't on the page.
- **Figures that should reconcile but don't, or only half do:** $301,500 against $300,000 against $2,388 (P202.10, P206.8), and $102,000 to $95,000 "with room to spare" (P208.11).
- **Linking words claiming more than the logic gives:** "so" (P206.7) and "because" (P208.2), and a time frame that slips (P202.12).
- **Summary lines that announce instead of saying:** "The honest limit is the order...", "the two ends say different things", "the range is certain". These were the hardest to paraphrase, and the range ones raise a real question the passages don't answer: if both ends are owned and she gives the top, why give co-sponsors a range?
- The plain step-by-step sentences that carry one fact and one number each (most of Passages 6, 7 and 8) almost never failed, however long they were.
