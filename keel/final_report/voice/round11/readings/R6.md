## The sentences that made least sense

1. **L06**: "The X and R stand for those ten rungs." Two letters cannot stand for ten rungs as written, and R is never explained; it reads as a riddle about the plan's name rather than a fact about my money.
2. **L09**: "enough to return about all of it by December 2032": "it" has no clear referent. Only the next sentence let me rebuild that the fund is sized so its payout equals about all the leftover money.
3. **L01**: "Under Caplet XR" uses a name I have never been given, as the very first words, and "repay" is the wrong verb for money I am giving away.
4. **P601.8**: "the only other place for that money in the meantime is shares": not true as stated, since money can wait in cash or short-term Treasuries, so the reason for buying in January 2028 doesn't follow.
5. **P605.7**: "whatever figure you choose is money you already own": overstated. Only figures up to what the Treasuries will pay are owned; I could choose a figure above that.
6. **P608.8**: "$115,000 on today's figures": the top depends on shares from 2028 to 2031 (as the next sentence says), and the passage never states what share growth gives $115,000.
7. **L11**: "It is bought in 2028, not 2031": "it" could be the shares (the last thing named) or the Treasury fund, and the reason given only fits the fund.
8. **P607.11**: "The hard case is two at once": which two isn't said, and the example that follows begins with a share slump, which was not one of the three things just listed. I had to work out that the slump matters because it shrinks the flexibility.

## The clearest passage

**Passage 1.** It takes the $150,000 in the order it is spent, gives each part one figure and one purpose, and defines its one term ("the floor") at the moment it is needed, so I never had to hold anything in mind or go back. It is also the only passage that says plainly what can fall ("the only money that can fall"). Its one weak point is the reason for buying the floor in 2028 (P601.8). Passage 4 answers the "why then" question more fully, but asks more of me in two sentences.

## Any pattern

- **Words that point at something not on the page or not yet settled**: "Caplet XR", "the X and R", "it" in L09 and L11, "a different matter", "the ladder" in passage 2, "the point that matches her contribution". This was the main cause of re-reading, and the letter has the most of it.
- **The cautious counting stacked into one sentence**: "interest earns nothing", "0.5% for costs beyond its fee", "rounded down (once)". Each step is fine alone; three in one clause, with "costs beyond its fee" never explained, slowed me in passages 4, 6 and 8.
- **Claims stronger than what the page supports**: "the only other place is shares", "whatever figure you choose", "$115,000 on today's figures" with the share assumption left out. These are where I could follow the sentence but did not believe it as written.
- **Dates I had to translate myself**: rates of 6 November 2026 for a January 2027 purchase written in the past tense, and 2000-2003 mapped onto 2028-2031. Once a passage said the mapping outright, it worked.
