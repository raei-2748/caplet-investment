5. **P305.2**: "whatever the December 2032 Treasury fund pays beyond the top figure" is an unknown amount, yet the total is given as about $28,700. 6. **P304.12**: "the range is certain, and her contribution is the top". If her contribution is certainly the top, the bottom is not a possible outcome, so the "range" stops being a range. P304.13-16 explain what each end records, not why co-sponsors are told both.
7. **P307.7**: "whatever figure you choose is money you already own". This holds only for figures up to what the Treasuries will pay, and in 2031 she owns Treasuries, not the money.
8. **P301.8** (and **P303.10**, same fault): the first deposit is about $1,500 short ($301,500 against $300,000), yet the missing part costs $2,388 a year later, with only "with fees" (or nothing) to explain the gap.

## The clearest passage

**Passage 8.** Every sentence makes one claim, and each risk comes with its cause and a single figure. It spells out the link the other flexibility passage (5) leaves implicit: building costs rise "whatever shares do, so her shares will not reliably be up when the price is". It never treats a figure as something owned or bought, introduces nothing it does not use, and its terms ("flexibility", "co-sponsor meetings") are defined on the page before they are used. Passage 3 runs a close second: it says plainly why funds were used, and it names "one catch" before giving the numbers.

## Any pattern

- **A figure treated as money or as a holding.** "Both ends are owned" (L17), "the bottom is bought" (P304.3), "Bought in January 2028, that figure" (P306.12), "the figure she stated [is] already owned in Treasuries" (P305.11), "whatever figure you choose is money you already own" (P307.7). In each case the writer means the Treasuries that will pay the figure. A literal reader gets a number that is bought or owned.
- **Money that acts before it exists, or verbs doing the wrong job.** "Payments cost" (L03), "the difference ... goes into" a fund (L10), Treasuries that "repay" a sum she commits to (L01).
- **Pronouns and "the" pointing at nothing, or at the wrong thing.** "It" in L09 and L11, "the IPS" (P305.1), "the December 2032 Treasury fund" (P305.2), "the day trading froze" (P303.9), "the honest limit ... those things" (P305.8). These cluster in the passages that assume a page the reader has not seen.
- **"So" and "because" joining things that do not follow, or numbers that do not reconcile on the page.** P301.7 (two gaps from one overspend), P306.2 (spent in a month because nothing comes later), P301.8 and P303.10 ($1,500 against $2,388), P306.1 (buys ten, does not buy ten).
- The sentences that passed first time were the short, single-claim ones in the order things happen (most of Passages 2, 7 and 8). The failures were almost all compressed sentences carrying two steps of reasoning, or shorthand that names a number where it means the bonds behind it.
