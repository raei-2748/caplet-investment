## The sentences that made least sense

1. **L11.** Two faults at once. "It" most naturally means the world index fund just named, which the reason contradicts; read as the Treasury fund, the reason (co-sponsors' money falls with shares, so her contribution must rest on Treasuries) explains Treasuries rather than shares, not 2028 rather than 2031.
2. **P504.8.** The reason rests on a false premise: shares are not "the only other place" for the money until later. Cash or short Treasuries would do, and then the floor would depend on rates, not shares.
3. **P502.7.** "So whatever figure you choose is money you already own" does not follow: moving half the shares into Treasuries backs figures only up to what those Treasuries pay.
4. **P507.3.** The stated gap in bonds (February 2031 to February 2036) explains four of the five funds; it does not rule out a bond maturing later in 2036 for the January 2037 payment.
5. **L08.** "Only" does not follow from payments fixed in dollars: inflation still erodes what the payments buy and moves rates and share prices.

The reasons sit in the same or the next sentence ("only enough if ...", "because ..."), every new term (operating reserve, floor, VT) is defined where it appears, and the amounts add up to the $150,000 ($2,388 + $36,774 + about $81,000 + about $29,600), so a reader can check the chain rather than take it on trust. Passage 4's one flaw is the false premise in P504.8.

## Any pattern

- Hard words and long sentences were not what failed. The failures come where a reason is attached to a different claim from the one stated: L11 (why Treasuries, offered for why 2028), P504.8 (a false "only other place"), P507.3 (a gap that covers four years offered for five), P502.7 (a reason that backs some figures offered for "whatever figure").
- Absolutes outrun their reasons: "only" (L08, P504.8), "whatever" (P502.7), "pay what they say" for funds (P505.2).
