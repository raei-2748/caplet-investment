# Voice round 11: Keel's prediction, written before any reader saw a passage (Fri 20 Nov 2026, morning)

The round asks whether a writer working from the facts alone can explain the parts of the plan Caplet could not follow
in version 61, so that a reader who knows only the case follows every sentence first time. Two fresh writers (U, V)
write from `CORPUS.md` and `FACTS_PLAIN.md` (version 2) only; Keel's main session (K), which carries six weeks of
working files, writes the same four passages as the control. Readers are first calibrated on version 61 itself.

What Keel expects, before reading anything the fresh writers wrote:

1. **Calibration.** Readers will flag (reread or no sense) about 11 to 14 of the 17 version 61 sentences Caplet could not
   follow, and fewer than 2 of the 4 it could. No reader will follow all 17, so all six will be used.
2. **Fresh writers (U, V).** Reread about 25-30% of sentences, no sense about 6-10%: better than rounds 9 and 10 (35-48%
   reread), because version 2 of the fact sheet introduces each thing before naming it and the brief tells writers the
   reader knows only the case.
3. **Keel's main session (K).** Worse than the fresh writers by about 10 points of rereads, from names Keel has used so
   often they feel like ordinary words ("the late-2032 fund", "the floor", "the top", "counted cautiously"), even though
   K knows that is the failure under test.
4. **Caplet's controls (the IPS's third and fourth paragraphs).** Reread about 15%, no sense about 5%.
5. **By topic.** P3 (the 2031 range) fails most for every writer: the bottom is a derived figure (counted cautiously,
   cut 0.5%, rounded down), and "why a range at all" has no answer a plain reader finds natural once both figures are
   owned. P2 (the 2028 deposit) second: the late-2032 fund has two jobs (the 2033 payment and the floor) and readers will
   stall where it gets both. P1 and P4 read best; P4 best of all if the writer shows events rather than naming uses.
6. **Sentences that will fail.** Any sentence that names the floor or the top before saying what it is; "counted
   cautiously" and "rounded down once"; the 0.5% cut said without what it is for; "cash waiting" used as a term; the
   interest-reinvestment point (why ten Treasuries costing about $300,000 still need the extra Treasuries), which is
   version 61's "reinvestment risk" sentence in new words and the hardest single idea in the four topics.
7. **Comprehension questions.** Nearly all readers answer P1's and P4's correctly; on P3 about half say why there is a
   range, and fewer than half say the bottom is fixed in 2028.
