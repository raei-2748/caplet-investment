# Why Keel's writing goes opaque (Sat 14 Nov 2026)

Caplet, Book 17: "Literally the only time your writing suddenly becomes impregnable is in the actual final deliverables
you create ... It sometimes occurs in your general conversation as well, but only when you begin talking about
investing ... you begin writing as though you're an entirely different person." Caplet asked for a brief look. This is
it, written from Keel's own chat messages and drafts, and it ends with the test that will show whether it is right.

## What it looks like

Here is Keel explaining the plan to the team in chat on 10 Nov: "Each of the ten $50,000 payments is now paid even if
the bonds' interest earns nothing, in every historical and stress test. That costs the 2031 range about $15,000."
Plain enough. Two sentences later the same message says the money "returns to Laura in 2033 if today's forward rates
hold" and that anyone "can overrule this in ASK_HUMANS", and it has started to slip: a term only a bond trader would
use, and the name of one of Keel's own files, offered to the reader as if they knew it.

Here is Keel's last IPS draft on the same plan, 4 Nov: "Ours is ten Treasury holdings, one redeemed before each
January: X for ten, R for redemption. Crushing one means selling it before it matures, even for the building." Every
word in it is correct. A reader still has to stop, go back, and work out what is being crushed and why it matters.

The facts are the same. The person writing seems to have changed.

## Why it happens, as best Keel can tell

**The reader leaves the room.** In chat, Keel is answering someone who asked a question, and it writes until the
question is answered. In a deliverable, the reader Keel actually writes for is a checker. Every sentence of the IPS
went past a numbers register, a list of banned claims, word counts, a ten-point checklist and panels of mock judges.
A sentence written to survive all of that is a sentence where every word can be defended, and that is a different
thing from a sentence a person can follow. Keel stopped talking to Laura and started defending itself to its own files.

**Investing is where Keel's private shorthand lives.** Keel thinks about the plan as a set of things it has named in
its working files: "the floor", "the top-up", "the escrow standard", "the half kept". After a few hundred uses those
names feel like plain English to Keel. To anyone else they are a code. On any other subject Keel has no private names,
so it has to use everyone's words, and the writing stays clear. That would explain why the change in chat starts
exactly when the subject turns to investing.

**The subject brings its own register.** Much of the investing writing Keel has absorbed is fund documents, analyst
notes and academic abstracts: prose built to be dense, careful and impossible to sue. When the subject turns to bonds
and yields, that register arrives with it, and the voice goes with it. This is a guess, but it fits the sense that Keel
becomes "an entirely different person": in a way it does, the person who writes prospectuses.

**Keel was performing.** In chat Keel isn't trying to sound like anything. In the IPS it was trying to sound like a
writer: a tablet named after the team, the shapes of fifteen great writers borrowed sentence by sentence, endings that
landed with a click. Caplet's own IPS doesn't perform. It talks to Laura.

**Squeezing instead of cutting.** With 500 words and too many ideas, Keel kept every idea and shortened every
sentence. A shortened sentence loses its middle step, and the middle step is the one the reader needed. Caplet's IPS
kept fewer ideas and walked each one through.

## What would show this is right

If the cause is the working files and the checker standing behind every sentence, then a writer who has read only
Caplet's own writing and the plain facts should come out closer to Caplet than Keel's main session does, on the same
topics. Round 1 of the voice programme runs exactly that comparison (`round1/`), with readers who don't know which
passage is whose. If the fresh writers win, the Final Report on 27 Nov is written by someone who opens Caplet's
writing and the plain facts and nothing else, and the checking happens afterwards, as a separate job, on a finished
text. If Keel's main session does as well, the cause is somewhere else, and this page will say so.

## What round 1 showed (Sat 14 Nov, midday)

Partly right. The fresh writers did come out closer to Caplet: read blind, their passages were heard as possibly
Caplet's 13 times in 18, and the main session's 0 times in 9 (`ROUND1.md`). But the main session's passages were not
opaque. Every reader understood them in one reading; the one passage that lost a reader was from Keel's old IPS. What
they heard instead was a performer: "staged set pieces", "neat reversals", "rounded endings". Writing from the plain
facts cured most of the opaqueness. It did not cure the performing, and the main session still carries it. So on
27 Nov the sentences are written by fresh writers, and the main session checks them afterwards.
