reading) answered all four questions about the plan correctly but scored the notes for 3a and 3b clarity 2, because

Three rules:
1. **Caplet's own words first.** Where the frozen IPS or Trading Notes name a thing, the report uses that name
   (coherence with what was submitted, CANON). Keel's working labels never reach the report.
2. **One name per thing,** defined at first use in plain words, then never swapped for a synonym (a judge reads a new
   word as a new thing).
3. **Each pot of money says where it comes from and whose it is** in the same sentence as its size.

## Numbers in prose
- Prose rounds to about $500 or $1,000 with "about"; tables carry exact figures. The co-sponsor passage never puts
  "about" before a figure co-sponsors are to count (SKELETON 3d).
- Each modelled figure carries its basis once in the paragraph (today's market rates; no interest; in 2033 money), not
  after every figure: a lay reader heard "on today's market rates" four times across three parts and "stopped hearing
  it" (Thu 12 Nov evening). Say it once per part, or once at the head of Section 3 for every figure after it.
- One phrase, one quantity: "about a quarter" is the first $38,282's share of the 2028 deposit (the top-up and the extra Treasuries together) and nothing else.
- Never print $75,001, "just over half" or any label saying the floor's written minimum binds; the floor's share is said as "most of the 2028 deposit" (54.6% centrally, NUMBERS 4) (coherence reader, Thu 12 Nov evening).
- "If cash waiting earns nothing" (Fri 13 Nov evening; was "if interest earns nothing", which a lay reader and a bond reader both read as false, since each bond's own interest counts) means money already received earns nothing while it waits; every coupon and fund payout still counts. Say so once, in Figure 1's note (bond reader, Thu 12 Nov evening: a lay reader wonders how a 3.125% bond pays $42,469 with "no interest"). Never write "so no payment needs interest": each payment needs its own bonds' coupons.
- Prose carries one figure for the first $38,282 ("about $38,000") and says the top-up as "the first $2,400 or so"; $35,894 and $55,920 stay in Figure 1's note (lay reader, Fri 13 Nov evening: four figures for one price lost the reader).
