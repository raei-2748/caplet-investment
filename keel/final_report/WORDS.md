# Words: one plain name for each thing in the Final Report (from Wed 11 Nov 2026, evening)

Notes for the 27 Nov drafting session, not report prose. Why this page exists: a lay reader (an English teacher, one
reading) answered all four questions about the plan correctly but scored the notes for 3a and 3b clarity 2, because
"gift", "floor", "backstop", "overflow", "excess", "cap", "moved half", "forward rates", "111% funded" and "paths" piled
up undefined (`research/panel/2026-11-11_lay_reader_3a_3b_figure1.md`). The first-round reader that morning stumbled
on "escrow verifier's standard" (`2026-11-11_fresh_reader_exhibits.md`). Card Q31 found the label itself wrong for the
test the plan meets. The ideas are clear; the vocabulary is not.

Three rules:
1. **Caplet's own words first.** Where the frozen IPS or Trading Notes name a thing, the report uses that name
   (coherence with what was submitted, CANON). Keel's working labels never reach the report.
2. **One name per thing,** defined at first use in plain words, then never swapped for a synonym (a judge reads a new
   word as a new thing).
3. **Each pot of money says where it comes from and whose it is** in the same sentence as its size.

## Numbers in prose
- Prose rounds to about $500 or $1,000 with "about"; tables carry exact figures. The co-sponsor passage never puts
  "about" before a figure co-sponsors are to count (SKELETON 3d).
- Each modelled figure carries its basis once in the paragraph (today's market rates; no interest; in 2033 money).
