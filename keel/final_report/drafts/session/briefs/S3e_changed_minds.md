## 3. What this part must do for its reader

Its question for the reader: **how did the team come to this plan, and what did it learn?** Its answer: the team
changed its own first plan, for its own reason, before any trade; then it tested its decisions against its own
members' doubts and kept what held. The part's title says "changed our minds": make clear in the telling that what
changed was the first plan, and that the argument about the world fund tested a decision and kept it.

Tasks:
- **The first plan, and why it was dropped.** What it held (mostly shares, buying all ten payments' Treasuries in
  January 2033 with whatever the money had become) and why that was dangerous (no money comes in after January 2028, so
  a shortfall found in 2033 could not be closed; no crash was needed). The reason the team dropped it on 27 September,
  before any trade, is the IPS's own: "how a writer is paid" ("stopped and considered the actual nature of a writer's
  income"). The model run that day only priced the change: about 1 in 31 of its runs missed a payment, against none
  once the 2028 deposit's first part is spent. Pay off the IPS's "one step further" here, with the quotation, as what
  that step bought.
- **The copout, told as it happened (the longest piece).** On 5 October Darren argued that one world fund was a
  "copout" and proposed picking stocks and ETFs; a Taiwan fund (EWT) and gold (GLD) were raised. Say what was weighed,
  in the team's own reasons (the Trading Note reflection: "the vain illusion that we can pick winners"), and how it was
  settled: "We decided, in the end, to keep the one world fund." Then what the team learned from it, in its own words:
  judgement is worth using where the answer can be checked, so the ten payments were matched to printed dates and one
  world fund holds the shares.
- **One or two other moments, a sentence or two each**, only where the fact sheet gives the facts: the room under
  $300,000 shrinking to about $1,900 on 16 October, and what the IPS had already planned for it; the rule that only Ray
  places or cancels orders, if it serves the point.
- For each account, as far as the words allow: what the team believed, what it cost, the reason, one thing the team
  now does differently, and what did not change.
- Between them, the accounts should touch all five of Wharton's words: research, collaboration, disagreements,
  decision-making and learning.
- **The close (optional), inside the last paragraph:** one echo of the opening's pencil and ink, explained again in a
  clause, since a judge may read Section 3 alone. Pencil is her career income, which she cannot count on until it
  arrives; ink is a commitment that Treasuries held for her will pay on its date. The IPS ended "You take risks on the
  page"; the report may answer it once.
