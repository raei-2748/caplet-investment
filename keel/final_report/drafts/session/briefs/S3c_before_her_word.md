## 3. What this part must do for its reader

Its question for the reader: **what can Laura honestly tell co-sponsors in 2031, how sure can she be, and how does she
say it?** Its answer: by the 2031 meetings both ends of her range are held for her in Treasuries, so she states
them as facts; the uncertainty belongs to the years before, and she names no figure until it is held; then she signs
for the top.

The part has three pieces, in this order.

**A. The range and its confidence (about 150 words).**
- Two layers in two dated sentences, the held one first. By the 2031 meetings, what she states is held: the bottom,
  $95,000, held since January 2028, and the top, $115,000, held since the January 2031 move. Seen from today, which
  top she will reach is uncertain: at least $100,000 in about 91 of 100 simulated futures, $115,000 in about 57.
- So by 2031 nothing is left to estimate; the odds belong to the years before. Why she names nothing before the move,
  in a clause: a top named a year early would be more than the move buys in about 11 of 100 futures.
- Why a range at all, in the case's terms, and what the two ends mean once both are held: two dates, not two
  outcomes.
- How each end is worked out, one step to a clause: count as if the interest the fund pays out earns nothing more,
  take off 0.5% for the fund's costs beyond its fee, round down to a whole $5,000; and why so cautious (a co-sponsor
  will plan a building on it).
- The factors, by date: before the move, share prices move the top and never the bottom or a payment; after it, no
  share price or interest rate moves either end.
- Figure 2 sits in this part; point to it where it helps.

**B. The team's lead-in to her words (about 35 words, 60 at most).**
- What she shows co-sponsors and what she gives them: the Treasuries held for her, a signed pledge letter (amount,
  purpose, date) stating the sum and what holds it, and the broker's statement of the second account. Say what the
  statement shows and why it is a little under $115,000 on the day of the move, with what those Treasuries will pay by
  December 2032 beside it, in the team's words.
- One outside case, in a sentence: Lehman Brothers and Spelman College, and the one way Laura's position differs.
  Owning the Treasuries shows the money exists; a signed pledge says it will be given.
- If you have room, what the five iBonds Treasury funds that have already ended paid against their last price.

**C. In Laura's words (about 200 words, 250 at most), set off as a block under the sub-heading "In Laura's words".**
- Written as she would say it to a co-sponsor: first person, plain, warm, full sentences, the year named each time,
  no finance terms a funder would have to look up.
- What it says is in the fact sheet's "What the team recommends": two sentences of purpose from the case; the range
  first, each end in its own sentence with its date; what the two ends are (two dates, not two outcomes; the bottom
  never depended on her shares; since January 2031 neither end does); why she signs the top; her whole commitment in
  two parts, then the total; the letter's sum in US dollars, with its Taiwan-dollar value on a named day and that the
  Taiwan-dollar amount depends on the rate on the day it is paid; that the money will be given "without waiting for
  anyone to match it"; and what she asks of them.
- The letter states the sum and what holds it. It makes no other promise.

## 5. Names this part may use, in the order a reader first meets them

A judge may open this part without reading the ones before it, so each name gets its plain description at its first
use here, even if an earlier part gave it. Laura's own words use none of the team's names: she says what each thing
is.

1. **Co-sponsors; the 2031 meetings**: the funders she first meets in 2031 to back the residency's building and
   running costs.
2. **Her range; the bottom; the top**: the band of dollars she tells co-sponsors at the 2031 meetings, and nothing
   else; its lower and upper ends.
3. **Held for her**: Treasuries her broker holds for her, directly or through a fund.
4. **IBTM, the late-2032 Treasury fund**: a Treasury fund that holds Treasuries maturing in 2032 and, about
   15 December 2032, pays its holders what it then has, which is not a fixed sum.
5. **The January 2031 move**: in January 2031, before the meetings, half her shares are sold to buy more of that fund.
6. **Simulated futures**: the team's model's 200,000 simulated futures for share prices and interest rates.
7. **The 0.5% margin**: 0.5% taken off what the fund pays, for its costs beyond its 0.07% fee, such as the cost of
   trading.
8. **Figure 2**: the chart titled "In every market tested, all ten payments are paid in full".
9. **The pledge letter**: a signed letter with the amount, the purpose and the date, stating $115,000 in US dollars and
   what holds it: Treasuries maturing in 2032, held for her through one fund.
10. **The building's Treasuries; the second account; the broker's statement**: the late-2032 fund bought for the
    building in January 2028 and January 2031, held in a second account at the same broker that holds nothing else;
    the broker sends co-sponsors its statement (at least quarterly), at the 2031 meetings and again before the
    December 2032 payout.
11. **Her flexibility** (the lead-in only, never sized): money she has promised to no one.
12. **Lehman Brothers and Spelman College**: as the fact sheet tells it.
13. **The watch list (Table 2)**: the table that follows Laura's words, of what Laura or her asset management team
    watch as 2031 and 2033 approach.
14. In Laura's words: **her whole commitment** ($50,000 a year for the running costs, 2033 to 2042, and $115,000 for the
    building, "$615,000 in all"); **the residency's own planners** (the residency's own team, before co-sponsors are
    approached).
