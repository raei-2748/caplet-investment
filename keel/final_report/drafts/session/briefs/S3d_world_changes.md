## 3. What this part must do for its reader

Its question for the reader: **what could still go wrong, and what would the team do about it?** Its answer: only two
things change the plan, and neither is a market price; each promise has a limit, which the team names with one figure

Tasks, in roughly this order. This is a lot for the words: one risk to a sentence, and say who or what carries it.
- The IPS's two reasons to change the plan, "a missed deposit or a Treasury default", each with its test (nothing
  received by 31 March 2028; a Treasury payment more than five business days late). Neither is a market price, and the
  IPS's "only" rules out a rise in share prices as firmly as a fall. From January 2028 the ten payments ask one thing of
  anyone: that no one sells what is already bought. If you have room, the experiment in which executives broke a rule
  they had agreed in advance about half the time.
- How each promise could fall short, one figure each, with what covers it:
  - the ten payments: the 2028 deposit could arrive about three-quarters smaller and every payment would still be met;
  - the top she signs for: the fund's own costs taking more than about 2% of what it pays, or an early end, merger or
    moved end in 2031 (up to about $4,600 short if cash waiting earns nothing); say in the team's words that her
    flexibility is there if the fund pays a little less;
  - her flexibility: with anything the fund pays above the top, about $28,700 in the central case, enough for a rise of
    about 25% in the cost of the part of the building her $115,000 pays for, if shares earn the 7.5% a year assumed;
    about $9,600 or less in 1 simulated future in 20. Beyond it, the residency's other funders, a smaller design or a
    later start, and not building before the money is in hand.
- After the January 2031 move, no fall in share prices moves either end of her range; only the fund itself could make
  it pay a little less, for the reasons above.
- The hard case from history, told against her flexibility: world shares bought at the start of 2000 and the building-
  cost rise that followed. One calendar to a sentence.
- One outside case, in one or two sentences: the 2022 gilt crisis, and why it cannot happen to Laura.
- The case asks for assumptions in five areas (investment returns, the timing of cash flows, outside funding,
  inflation, flexibility). Name each in a sentence, or say where Figure 2's note lists them.
- Table 2 in one sentence: what Laura or her asset management team watch as 2031 and 2033 approach, and that each row
  says what to do. A change there moves a figure she can state, never a payment.
- Last: what no fall in share prices can change. Put the shares at zero, in the same plain words the report used at
  its start, and say what is still paid.
