  strikingly dissimilar, surprising and seemingly far-fetched parallels. It displays wit, intellectual ingenuity, and

- **What it overrides.** For the Final Report only, it replaces Keel's reading of 8 Nov (no long-running image; the

  and stays as it was. Ray's warning of 5 Nov survives as the conceit's guard, not its ban: "metaphors alone does not
  count as creativity". Every image sentence has to do an investing job (section 7).
- **Why it is the right image.** It comes from Laura's own trade (the case: "a bestselling graphic novelist,
  illustrator"; her first success was "a comic"). It continues the IPS's last line, "You take risks on the page." And
  it is the IPS's own governing rule made visible: "only money you haven't promised yet goes into shares" (l.11) and
  "whatever figure you choose is money you already own" (l.15). In the image, a figure is shown only once it is inked.

## 2. The rule, in the image

- **Ink** is a dollar figure already owned in Treasuries that will pay it, barring a US default. In comics only the
  inked line prints: the pencils are rubbed out once the ink is dry, and a pale "non-photo blue" pencil is dropped
  out before printing (section 5). The neatest wording, "The inked line is what's reproduced in the finished comic
  book, since pencil lines are erased after inking", is in a manuscript of Gary Martin's *The Art of Comic Book Inking*
  (Dark Horse; 1997, 2nd ed. 2005, 3rd ed. 2019) and is quoted only after a printed copy is checked and its page noted.
- **Pencil** is anything not yet owned: Laura's future income, the 2028 deposit before it arrives, the world share fund
  (VT) before half of it moves into Treasuries, interest not yet earned, every model result.
- **The rule:** Laura states a figure only once it is inked. Pencil can be rubbed out and redrawn; ink can only be
  painted over, and the correction stays on the original (section 5).
- **Ink carries its exceptions with it.** "Inked" never means guaranteed. It means "owned in Treasuries, barring a US
  default", and an iBonds fund pays what its bonds are worth when it ends, not a fixed amount (Trading Note 2: "a net
  asset value, as opposed to a promise of par"). That is why each stated end of the range is counted at what the
  fund's bonds pay if no more interest is earned, cut by 0.5%, and rounded down to $5,000 (`NUMBERS.md` 4).

## 7. The guards (Caplet's five, kept; Keel's additions, with the audit's)

1. **Every image sentence carries a number or a rule,** and the plain name of the thing (`WORDS.md`). The image is a
   verb or an adjective inside a sentence that already says what it means: never "the inked floor" as a new name,
   never "the pencil money". No new figure enters through an image: it uses its part's own headline number, inside
   SKELETON's density cut.
2. **Never in tables, figures, exhibit notes or the co-sponsor words.**
3. **At most one image sentence in a part, and never as a part's first or last line.** The image sits inside the
   paragraph whose number it explains. Round 1's blind readers heard set-piece openings and rounded endings as Keel,
   not Caplet (`voice/ROUND1.md`); an image saved for the last line is the same habit.
4. **Two words carry the conceit: pencil and ink.** Any other craft fact (finished before offered, trim and bleed and
   safe area, erasing the pencils, non-photo blue) is used at most once in the report, explained in plain words in the
   same sentence. No colour for VT. No comics jargon beyond that: no panels, gutters, splash pages, origin stories or
   sound effects, and no puns on "issue", "draw" or "drawdown".
5. **"Inked" never overclaims.** One definition, stated once in 3.0: a figure owned in Treasuries that pays it even if
   cash waiting earns nothing, barring a US default (for the top, add the funds' running costs within 0.5%). It always
   travels with "barring a US default" and with its date (January 2028, or before the 2031 meetings); never
   "guaranteed", "locked", "set in stone" or "risk-free". For an iBonds fund, "inked" means the stated figure counted
   at what its bonds pay with no more interest, less 0.5%, never "what IBTM pays". Dollars on dates only, never values:
   a Treasury's price still moves. Never for her flexibility, the 7.5%, any median, or anything before its date. Ink
   never means "bought", "held" or "in WInS".
6. **The IPS is never called pencil, draft or thumbnail,** nor are the Trading Notes or the WInS notes. They are frozen
   ink, and a judge reads "pencil" as provisional. The report inks what the IPS left open (how the ten are sized, the
   range rule, which bonds are bought first); it never redraws the IPS. A method note for the writers, not report text:
   inking is not tracing (the inker decides line weight and fixes what the pencils got wrong, inside the penciller's
   intent), which is Book 17's rule that the report evaluates and does not repeat the IPS.
7. **The report's own modelled figures are pencil, and say so** ("we modelled", "on today's market rates"). Never
   "only inked numbers are shown" about the report: it shows today's figures as the rule's value, never as a promise.
8. **The image never carries the thesis.** Pencil carries timing (not yet owned), not the IPS's correlation (her

    one because it fits the image. No craft fact card Q40 has not checked (process pages, fill-in stories) until it is

11. **Johnson's test.** Samuel Johnson defined wit as "a combination of dissimilar images, or discovery of occult
    resemblances in things apparently unlike", praised wit "which he that never found it, wonders how he missed", and
    warned that with forced conceits "the reader commonly thinks his improvement dearly bought, and, though he

12. **No outside words in Laura's mouth beyond what she has said publicly.** Her Climbing interview may be quoted once,

## 8. Where the image is honest, and where it is not (for the writers; the report says the limit where it uses the image)

- **Ink can be corrected, visibly.** The plan has two reasons to change; a change is a patch on the original, recorded,
  never a quiet erasure.
- **An iBonds fund is ink that is not quite dry until mid-December of its year.** Hence the cut and the rounding.
- **A US default is not a smudge; it is the paper.** The image has nothing to say about it, and the report names it
  plainly every time.
- **Inflation and the Taiwan dollar do not fade the ink.** The payments are fixed in dollars, so the ink holds; what
  changes is what the dollars buy in Taiwan (3e).
- **Her flexibility is not a bleed:** its size varies (about $9,900 to $41,300, 1 in 20 each way), it is about a third
  smaller in the years shares fall, it is not a natural hedge, it stays hers, and on its own it covers a 10% stronger
  Taiwan dollar in about 93 of 100 simulated futures, so it can show a white edge. The bleed is the excess above the
  top (section 5b, parallel 3).
- **The two-thirds image holds for the ratio only:** VT is 8.84% of the portfolio but 6.63% of the plan, and the
  portfolio holds no top-up, no extra Treasuries and no Treasuries for the moved half, because it was built before
  the extra Treasuries were sized.
- **"Redraw" overclaims a little:** a missed deposit changes sizes in the IPS's order, and the ten bought in 2027 stay.
- **What can still fade the ink after its date:** a US default; fund running costs above the 0.5% cut (a few hundred
  dollars); a fund's board ending, merging or extending a fund (Table 3 row 4); a fee on waiting money above the
  modelled 0.15% (unverified). Table 3 watches each.
