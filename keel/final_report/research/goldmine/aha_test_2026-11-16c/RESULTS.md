## What the readers caught (to apply before any of these lands)

1. **Item 1 (Neyman).** Two decimals look like false precision ("92.2%", "59.8%"): give whole numbers and name the model
   and the share-return assumption (B). Item 1 gives the top's odds from simulated futures and item 4 from 585 history
   replays: never put the two bases side by side without saying which is which (B). Item 1's "holds" rests on item 6's
   0.5% cut covering fund costs (C): say the condition once, in the same paragraph. B's line is the one to keep: "the
   percentages belong to the question asked before 2031; calling the range a confidence interval is a category error".
   Tonight's analysis of why a range is stated at all (`../../meta/WHY_A_RANGE_2026-11-16.md`) decides how 3c carries it.
2. **Item 5 (nobody can run).** SVB does not fit "broke through borrowing, not through the safe assets": its Treasuries'
   and agency bonds' losses were the trigger, and the deposits were funding that could leave (B). The true pattern for
   all three cases is funding that can leave before the assets mature; Laura's payments are fixed dates she sets, so the
   plan has no such funding. "Only two reasons to change ... the only two flows it does not own" is a non sequitur (B)
   and ignores Laura herself, who can always sell (A, C): say "nobody else can make the plan sell", never "only two
   reasons" as a consequence. A default is a failed flow she owns a claim to, not a flow she does not own (B).
3. **Item 2 (Lehman).** "The only promise anywhere in the chain is the US Treasury's" is false as said (a fund, an
   adviser, a custodian, securities lending): say "the only promise to pay is the US Treasury's" (A). "The risk above
   it is delay, not loss" is a record, not a guarantee: "in the large broker failures so far, delay, not loss" (A).
   Accounts moved within weeks; full satisfaction of the claims took years: keep the two apart (A).
4. **Item 6 (fuel).** Add final reserve fuel, "never planned to be burned", as the ten Treasuries themselves: the
   parallel gets four parts and the ladder becomes the fuel no one touches (B). Say whether "about 0.27%" is a year or a
   fund's whole life (B; card Q3c). Beside item 5, three margins and "only two reasons" read as a clash (A): they are
   different things (margins sized in advance against a reason to change the plan), so never print them together.
5. **Item 7 (Taiwan pledges).** "None by year seven" proves nothing, since the scheme asked for five years (C): drop it.
   137 payers in year one and 46 in year five is the number C "would quote to my own board".
6. **Item 9 (the 2001 gap).** Five payments rest on funds, not four: four were forced (2033-2036) and one chosen (2037)
   (A, B). "None exists" ignores the 57 notes that mature inside the gap (A); the platform listed bonds, not notes, so the
   list was the immediate limit and 2001 the reason no bond was there to list (A). Card Q93 row 27 holds the safe words.
   A: "A budget forecast that failed in Washington in 2001 decides how a novelist's 2033 payment is held. That chain is
   clever." It belongs in 2.2 as one clause of fact, not as a spine row.
7. **Repeats.** Items 2, 7 and 8 all end on "owned" (B); items 7 and 8 say one thing twice (A, C); items 3, 5 and 6
   repeat one point by analogy (A). One home each: "owned before stated" lives at spine row 4 (3.0); item 7 alone
   carries the Taiwan base rate; item 5 is the only one of 3, 5, 6 that answers a question a judge asks (why so few
   reasons to change).
