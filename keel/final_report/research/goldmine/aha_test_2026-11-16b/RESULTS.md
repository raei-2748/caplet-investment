## Fixes row by row

- **Spine 5 (item 3).**
  - "Short in none of the 585 replays" proves nothing (A, B): a standard that needs no interest cannot fall short by
    construction (NUMBERS 2 says so already: "not separate evidence for a design that passes at 0%"). Say instead that
    the standard needs no interest at all, and use history only for the market-priced version's failures.
  - Give the shortfalls' size (A, B, C): in the 585 replays the market-priced ten were short by a median $16,055 when short (the all-window
    median $1,592 counts 260 windows short by nothing; card Q64), $34,555 at 1 in 20 and $45,266 at worst (July 2007 start).
  - The IRS sentence was wrong as written (A, B, C). Rev. Rul. 77-374 tests whether a charitable trust's chance of
    running out is above 5%, assuming one rate (the section 7520 rate at creation) for its whole life; when rates fell,
    new trusts failed the test (store H3-066, H3-067). So: "the IRS once tested charitable trusts by assuming the rate
    at their creation would last their whole lives; when rates fell, trusts failed it".
  - Put the price and what comes back in the same year's money (C): the extra Treasuries cost $34,369 in January 2027
    money and are worth $45,456 on 1 January 2033 at forward rates (NUMBERS 8h), so "most of the extra comes back"
    reads wrong; say what they cost and what they are worth on the day.
  - Overlapping windows (A, B): 585 windows from one 64-year history are far fewer independent cases. Card Q64 (running
    today) sizes the effective sample and the term premium's part; until it lands, say "585 starting months", never
    "585 tests".
- **Spine 7 (item 5).** "Would not yet be owned in 2.9% ..." is worded wrongly (A): before the move the top is never
  owned; the point is that the move would then buy less than the figure named. Say it so: a top named 1, 3, 6 or 12
  months early would turn out larger than the move could buy in 2.9%, 6.2%, 8.7% or 10.7% of futures. Say "about
  $3,300" rather than "two-thirds of one $5,000 step" (C). The 14 of 15 houses repeat the 4%-10% test (B, C): use one.
  The appropriations law is a parallel, not "the same rule" (C).
- **Spine 8 (item 6).** The 92% is theatres, the worst kind of building, not a residency (B). Q51 (running today)
  gives shortfalls for small arts buildings; use its figure. Readers B and C read items 6 and 7 together (an overrun
  that size would swamp a cushion of about $20,000-$30,000), so 3d's campaign contrast and 3e's honest limit must not
  contradict each other: the cushion is sized against Taiwan's cost and currency risk, never against an arts-building
  overrun.
- **Spine 9 (item 7).** One year is an example, not a tendency (A, B, C). Use card Q5e row 6: after seven falls of more
  than 20% in Taiwan's market, building costs rose 6.1% that year against 3.5% after rises; and measure the shares as
  world shares (VT), not Taiwan's index (B, C). Link the Taiwan dollar in a clause (C): the joint US-dollar measure is
  card Q10e's and NUMBERS 8q's (running today).
- **Spine 10 (item 8).** Say why 3.2% fails "a high degree of certainty" (A, C): about 1 in 31 against none for the
  Treasury plan. "Down on one of ten fixed dates" misdescribes the risk (B): the shares are sold to pay each date, so
  a fall in the early payment years drains every later payment. The readers want people in "the plan's own test" (A,
  C); the reflection rule (Book 18) tells events truthfully without saying whose work they rested on, so Caplet chooses
