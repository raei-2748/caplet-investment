**Item L.** The big capital funders a building campaign needs most, public arts programmes and large foundations, write
their rules so that they pay last, once every other dollar is confirmed. Two funders that each insist on paying last can
wait for each other indefinitely. Field experiments say a lumpy project, one that is worth nothing until fully paid for,
gets built far more often when someone goes first: seed money announced at two-thirds of the goal, against one-tenth,
raised what others gave nearly sixfold (List and Lucking-Reiley, 2002). So someone has to go first without conditions,
and for most givers that is the over-promise the case warns about, a pledge from money not yet in hand. Laura can go
first without that risk, because by the 2031 meetings her $115,000 is already held for her in Treasuries that pay out in
December 2032. Her condition then lets every other pledge stay conditional without anyone waiting on anyone: if pledges
to the building's total are not all made by December 2032, no co-sponsor has to pay.
