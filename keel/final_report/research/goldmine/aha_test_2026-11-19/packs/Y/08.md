**Item K.** Illinois's prepaid tuition plan sold families a promise: pay now, and the state pays the child's university
tuition later. Its own disclosures named the three things the promise rested on: 'continued Contract sales within
projections', 'a projected annual net return on Fund investments', and 'tuition/fee increases being within projected
amounts'. All three stayed open for as long as it kept selling contracts; in 2024 it moved most of its money into
Treasuries matched to its payments. Laura's plan carries the same three: new money is her 2028 deposit, from her career;
returns are her shares; the bill is what the ten Treasuries cost before they are all bought, plus building costs. But
they land differently. Returns never reach a promise she has made: no fall in share prices can break a payment or a
figure she has stated. New money and the price of the ten both close for good in January 2028, when the last Treasuries
behind the ten are bought. Building costs reach only the money she keeps unpromised. What remains is what Illinois never
listed: a US default, and the funds' running costs beyond the small margin held for them.
