**Item H.** An investment bank that offers a company a fixed sum for its new shares in a 'bought deal' takes the shares
onto its own books and carries their price risk until it resells them. A university's gift office does the same from the
other side: shares given to it count at what they fetch when sold, and many offices sell them the day they arrive. The
two rules agree: a share counts only once it has been turned into something whose value is fixed, and the one exception
is whoever chooses to keep the price risk. Laura's plan cuts her world shares along exactly that line. In January 2031,
before any co-sponsor hears a figure, half of them move into the Treasury fund that pays out in late 2032, the fund that
already holds the bottom of her range. That half is the bought deal, and the gift office's sale done before the gift.
The other half stays in shares, promised to no one: the exception both rules name, money whose owner carries the price
risk, which is why it never counts in her range. After the move a 40% fall in world shares changes neither end of the
range; only that kept half falls.
