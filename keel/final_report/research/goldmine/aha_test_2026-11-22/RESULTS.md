## What it decides

1. **P takes the 3d slot (aha 8).** All three would put it in the part addressed to co-sponsors, all three remembered it,
   and it is the only candidate any reader called new in a gift office's own terms ("will they pay, and can they?").
2. **Two fixes to P, from all three readers.**
   - *The check is at a point in time.* Every reader asked the same question: what stops Laura selling the fund
     between the 2031 meetings and December 2032? Ownership proves she can pay on the day a co-sponsor looks, not on
     the day she pays. So 3d says how a co-sponsor keeps checking: the pledge letter names the fund behind her figure,
     and co-sponsors see a statement of it at the 2031 meetings and again before the December 2032 payout (a new line
     for Table 3, the watch list, and for 3d's letter; the IPS's two reasons to change, a missed deposit or a Treasury
     default, are why no sale is in the plan). Never "escrow" (no rated escrow may hold a fund, card Q54).
   - *The closing line's mapping.* Two readers saw that "owned is why her figure will be paid" gives "will" to
     ownership after the item had given "will" to the signature. The line becomes: owned answers whether she can pay;
     signed answers whether she will, and lets a funder count it.
   - Smaller: "failed on the second question" is true of Lehman (a bankrupt firm), not of every 2008-10 pledge (many
     donors chose not to pay after a wealth shock); say it of Lehman. The $115,000 is the top on today's figures,
     dated, and the fund pays what its holdings pay, near but not exactly a set sum (WORDS).
3. **F repeats P.** All three readers said F and P rest on the same sentence and would read as repetition. F's one new
   point (she can go first while every other funder keeps its rule of paying last) becomes a phrase inside P's
   argument, "the first to commit, in writing", never its own sentence. The funder reader added that "pay last" is too
   broad (many funders commit conditionally and pay on milestones) and that List and Lucking-Reiley was a mail appeal
   with small goals: say "commit conditionally", and keep the experiment out of 3d.
4. **V goes to the store.** No reader remembered it; two found it at odds with P (an amount never signed is an amount a
   funder should not count) and all three saw three calls on one pot (the same half of the shares covers overruns, a
   stronger Taiwan dollar and a co-sponsor falling short). The currency question stays in 3e and Table 3, without the
   offer.
5. **The control holds again.** B (3.7 / 3.7) matches or beats every candidate, as in test 8: the plan's own measured
   history is still its strongest aha. One reader reread its last clause ("even if cash waiting earns nothing unless
   yields fall about 3.6 points"): two conditions in one clause, which round 12 named as a reread fault, so the writers
   give the limit its own sentence (it is 3e's in the tree already).
