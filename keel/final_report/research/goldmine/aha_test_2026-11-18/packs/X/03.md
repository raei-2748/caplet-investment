Each payment rests on its own Treasury, and the team's own trade notes left one forecast in the plan: for the later
payments, "reinvested coupons fund the rest". The ten as first priced, $302,287, reach $500,000 only if the interest
they pay before the payments earns today's forward rates (the rates for future years built into today's Treasury
prices), or a flat 5.12% a year on every waiting dollar. Replayed over 585 starting months since 1962, the ten as priced
would have fallen short more often than not for start dates since 1981, the years rates fell, and for about 1 start in 6
from 1962 to 1980: when short, by a median $16,055; at 1 in 20 of all starting months, by $34,555; at worst, by $45,266
(a July 2007 start). So the plan takes the forecast out. Extra Treasuries, bought in January 2028, are sized so all ten
are paid even if cash waiting earns nothing, the arithmetic an escrow verifier applies to a bond escrow and a condition
history has met (the three-month Treasury bill paid under 0.15% a year in 90 of the 222 months from January 2008 to June
2026). The test still charges the 0.15% yearly fee of the Treasury-bill fund where waiting cash would sit, so waiting
cash loses money, and it holds back 0.5% of every fund payout for fund costs beyond the funds' own fee. The price is
about $39,000 of the 2028 deposit at today's rates, the first $2,400 or so of it completing the ten. What comes back is
worth about $46,600 in 1 January 2033 money if rates hold, all of it Laura's by the last payment in 2042; if cash
waiting earns nothing, none beyond that 0.5% margin.
