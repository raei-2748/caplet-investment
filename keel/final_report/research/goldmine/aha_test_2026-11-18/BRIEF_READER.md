## Context every reader gets

Laura Gao, a graphic novelist, has $300,000 at the start of 2027 and $150,000 at the start of 2028. She must pay
ten fixed $50,000 payments, one each January 2033-2042, toward running an artists' residency in Taiwan, "with a
high degree of certainty", and she may not rely on any outside money for them. In 2031 she tells co-sponsors (other
funders) a dollar range for her contribution to the building, with how confident she is; over-promising would damage
her credibility. A student team's plan starts from one premise: a writer's advances, fees and licences can come later
and smaller in the same slumps in which shares fall, so her career already rides on the share market, and her
portfolio should lean the other way.

What the plan holds. In January 2027 the first deposit buys ten US Treasuries (Treasury bonds, or Treasury bond funds
that end on a set date), one ending before each payment. In January 2028 the second deposit buys three things: more
of the same Treasuries for the payments, a Treasury fund ending in late 2032, and a world share fund (VT). The
late-2032 fund fixes the bottom of the range. Half of the shares move into Treasuries before the 2031 meetings, which
fixes the top; the other half is promised to no one (her flexibility, for building costs that overrun, a stronger
Taiwan dollar or a co-sponsor falling short). At market rates on 6 November 2026 the range would be about
$95,000-$115,000. The plan names only two reasons to change course: a missed deposit or a US Treasury default.
