## Context every reader gets

Laura Gao, a graphic novelist, has $300,000 at the start of 2027 and $150,000 at the start of 2028. She must pay ten
fixed $50,000 payments, one each January 2033-2042, toward running an artists' residency in Taiwan, "with a high degree
of certainty", and she may not rely on any outside money for them. In 2031 she tells co-sponsors (other funders) a
dollar range for her contribution to the building, with how confident she is; over-promising would damage her
credibility. A student team's plan starts from one premise: a writer's advances, fees and licences can come later and
smaller in the same slumps in which shares fall, so her career already rides on the share market, and her portfolio
should lean the other way. The first deposit buys ten US Treasuries (Treasury bonds, or Treasury bond funds that end on
a set date), one ending before each payment. The first $38,282 of the second deposit completes them and buys extra
Treasuries, so that all ten are paid even if cash waiting earns nothing. Most of the rest buys a Treasury fund ending in
late 2032, which fixes the bottom of the range; the remainder buys a world share fund (VT). Half of the shares move into
Treasuries before the 2031 meetings, which fixes the top; the other half is promised to no one (her flexibility, for
building costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar or a co-sponsor falling short). At market rates on 6 November
2026 the range would be about $95,000-$115,000. The plan names only two reasons to change course: a missed deposit or a
US Treasury default.

## What each reader writes, for each item

1. Before reading it, what did you assume about this, if anything? Name the assumption in a few words, and say how sure
   of it you were before reading, from 0 (a guess) to 100 (certain). If you named none, write "none".
2. What it means: one sentence, in your own words, saying what it shows you had backwards (or "nothing new").
3. Surprise, 1 (nothing new) to 5 (changed how I see the plan).
4. Conviction, 1 (I don't believe it, or it does not matter) to 5 (I am convinced it is true and that it matters for
   the plan).
5. Would you repeat it to someone tomorrow? Yes, maybe or no.
6. What made it land or not: one sentence.
7. Any claim that seems overstated, unclear or likely wrong: quote it and say why.
8. The one question you would ask the team.

When you have finished all nine items, do this short task without looking back at the items: list ten countries in
alphabetical order. Then, still without looking back, write down from memory each item's key figure: one figure per
item, with the item's letter, or "none" if nothing comes to mind.

Then: the three items you would still remember after reading 40 reports, and any two items that clash or repeat.
