**Item V.** Fundraising advisers tell a charity to announce a building campaign once 60 to 80% of its goal has been
raised, and a residency's own campaign can look just like that: MacDowell, the American artists' residency, announced a
$13 million campaign in 2010 when "more than $10 million has already been raised". An announced figure can also leave a
great deal out. In a University of Chicago study of America's new arts buildings from 1994 to 2008, the theatres ended
about 92% above their first budgets, while the rises the public heard of were about 19%, which the study put down to
managers who "were clever about when to announce their budgets publicly". Laura's figure is her own share of the
building, not its cost, and co-sponsors still raise theirs. But when she names it in 2031, all of it, the bottom and the
top, is already held for her in Treasuries, so a co-sponsor hearing her figure is hearing money that exists, not a
target.
