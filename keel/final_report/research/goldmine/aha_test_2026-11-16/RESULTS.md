## Corrections the readers forced (applied today)

1. **Spine 3 overclaimed.** Its last clause, "the 2028 top-up means the plan never needed the luck", is false as a
   general claim: reader A saw that on 4 Aug 2020 the ten cost $470,217, more than both deposits ($450,000), so no
   top-up could have finished them. What is true (NUMBERS 5): the top-up and the extra Treasuries keep the payments'
   standard unless yields fall about 3.6 points before January 2027, and the plan never needed the fill price (the
   $200 of commissions against the $37,625 swing within 2026). So the honest aha is sharper, not weaker: **the case is
   set in a rare year, and the plan is built to need the year, not the day.** The register row is reworded.
2. **"June 2007 to August 2026" reads as a chosen window** (reader A). Say why the window: June 2007 is the last run
   of days before 2026 when $300,000 was enough, and September 2026 the next (8e); or say it from 2000 ("on 10.9% of
   days since 2000, and on one day from June 2007 to August 2026").
3. **Spine 6 must answer the all-Treasury row in the same breath** (reader B): an all-floor plan states $130,000 but
   keeps about $9,000 of flexibility, less than the about $11,500 a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar would need, and leaves
   Laura less in total in about 63 futures in 100 (NUMBERS 4, C8; 8j). The register row already names C8; the 27 Nov
   writer must say the flexibility clause too, or the row defends the floor more than the plan.
4. **Spine 6, "why under $95,000 in 3 in 100 if the floor fixes the bottom?"** (reader A): those are futures in which
   yields fall before January 2028, so the plan's own stated bottom is lower; it never pays below its own stated
   bottom (8n). One clause if the 3 in 100 is used; otherwise leave the figure out.
