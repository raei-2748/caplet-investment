**Item T.** In 2031 Laura signs a letter fixing her building contribution at $115,000, paid in January 2033, and co-sponsors see her broker's statement of a second account holding the building's Treasury fund. On the day she moves the money, at 6 November 2026 market rates, that statement would show about $111,700, about $3,300 under the figure she has just signed. It passes $115,000 in late August 2031, and what the fund's Treasuries will pay by December 2032 is never under about $117,700, even if the cash they pay out earns nothing more. Nothing is missing: Treasuries are priced below what they pay until they pay it, so a promise is counted by what its Treasuries pay on their date, not by what they would sell for today. Co-sponsors are shown both figures side by side: the day's value and what the holding will pay by its date.
