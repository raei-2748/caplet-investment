## 1. The rare word in l.17 is "only" (3e)

- **Card rows:** Q137-034 (keep, 22) with Q137-033 (keep, 22); support Q137-041, -038, -035, -037 (19-20).
- **The aha in one plain sentence:** "Nearly every investment policy says a market fall is no reason to change; what almost
  none says is that nothing else is either."
- **Why it might surprise a judge:** a judge who has read IPSs expects l.17's "a fall in the market is not one of them" to
  be the bold line. Six of seven institutional IPSs read carry that sentence near word for word, so it is the profession's
  boilerplate. What they all keep is an open door: TRSL's policy "may be modified, in whole or in part, by the Board at any
  point in time"; Akron's list ends with "Other presently unknown significant structural issues". The closed list is the
  rare part, and the report can say what it gains (a list anyone can check, nothing to decide in a falling market) and
  what it gives up (a catch-all for the unforeseen). That evaluates l.17 instead of repeating it, which is Wharton's ask.
- **Evidence:** seven IPSs read at source (TRSL 2024, SCERA 2026, FIU Foundation 2025, CSULB Alumni Center 2026,
  University of Akron Foundation 2024, Stockton 2025, UNE Foundation 2026); REFERENCES 2j.
- **Guards:** "six of seven read", never "every IPS"; the two reasons are exits for when the plan cannot be carried out,
  never the ways a promise fails; Laura keeps her legal power to change (card Q7); test it cold before it takes a slot
  (rater's note on row 33).

## 4. Public-money law ranks diversification fifth (2.3)

- **Card row:** Q137-024 (keep, 22).
- **The aha in one plain sentence:** "Texas law tells public treasurers what to weigh in order, and diversification comes
  fifth: first comes whether the money matches what it must pay."
- **Why it might surprise a judge:** "ten Treasuries and one fund" reads as under-diversified to a judge trained on
  diversification. The statute that governs public money ranks suitability to the body's financial requirements first,
  safety of principal second, liquidity third, marketability fourth, diversification fifth and yield last
  (Tex. Gov't Code 2256.005(d)). For promised money, matching outranks spreading, which is register row 2's claim in a
  lawmaker's words.
- **Relation to the register:** support for row 2 ("diversifying the payments would make them less safe"), one clause at
  most in 2.3, which already carries its one extra and one number.
- **Guards:** say "ranks", never that the law approves the plan; public money, not Laura's; the money not promised is
  diversified by VT, and the statute still asks a policy to address diversification.

## 5. A late Treasury costs her a date, not a dollar (Table 3 row 2's note, or 3e)

- **Card row:** Q138-015 (keep, 20); support Q138-020 and Q138-013 (keep, 19 each).
- **The aha in one plain sentence:** "Because the payments are sized to be paid even if waiting cash earns nothing, a
  Treasury paid late but before 1 January costs her nothing the plan counted on."
- **Why it might surprise a judge:** a judge hears "a late Treasury payment" as the default scare. The plan's own standard
  already prices an uncompensated delay at zero (NUMBERS 2: the payments hold even if cash waiting earns nothing), so the
  only thing a short delay can move is a date. The Fed's 2013 plan rolls a late Treasury forward one day at a time and
  keeps it transferable on Fedwire, so nothing has to be sold, which is the fact behind Table 3's "Never: a forced sale".
- **Evidence:** NUMBERS 2's standard; Board of Governors (2013, October 16) transcript; 31 C.F.R. 356.30; REFERENCES 2j.
- **Guards:** no new row, rule or reason (CANON A34); the trigger stays five business days; a delayed coupon still owed
  after 1 January is a different case (the card's own caveat); a note's clause, never a spine row.
