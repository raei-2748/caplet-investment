## C. Aha candidates (a judge's view turned around)

Overlap with `AHA_REGISTER_candidates.md` (written tonight by another worker) is noted. Candidates 1, 2, 4 and 5 are not in
that register; 3 sharpens its rank 3.

1. **Market risk is ambition risk, not default risk (#1).** A judge expects "how risky is the plan?" to have one answer.
   Here it has two: no market can make Laura break a figure she has stated, and markets decide only how large her next
   figure is. NUMBERS 8p is the proof: a 40% fall before the move lowers the top to about $105,000 and moves nothing
   else, and after the move it touches only her flexibility. (The register's rank 29 has the second half only.)
2. **Concentration is the diversification (#15).** Ten borrowers, each 99 in 100 sure, miss at least one payment about
   one time in ten; ten Treasuries stand or fall together. For a ten-payment promise, putting every promised dollar with
   one borrower, the risk-free one, is the safe choice. A judge trained on "diversify" reads it backwards first.
3. **Laura's plan was possible only in a rare window, and was built not to need it (#11, #12).** In August 2020 the same
   ten payments would have cost $470,217, more than both of her deposits together. Between June 2007 and August 2026,
   $300,000 bought them on one day. The team's orders filled within two days of 2026's cheapest close, which was luck,
   and IPS l.13's completion clause exists for the year that is not lucky. (Sharpens the register's rank 3 with the
   2020 price.)
4. **She starts where pensions end (#22).** Pension funds match every payment with bonds only at the end of their
   lives, when they "hibernate". Laura does it six years before her first payment. A practitioner judge knows the end
   state and will not expect it first.
5. **A calendar of promises, not a glide path (#2).** Every judge knows the target-date fund, which cuts risk as time
   passes. This plan cuts risk only on the dates Laura gives her word, and on each of those dates by exactly the amount
   promised. It also gives the pencil-and-ink conceit its dates in finance's own terms (CONCEIT 5b rank 2), with no
   image word.

Runners-up: #31 (the client is a Wharton-trained statistician, so the confidence she states is ownership, not a
model's probability); #13 (the bonds made money in October and Laura was no richer, if the NUMBERS run bears it out); #5
(her caution is forced by arithmetic: willing, but not able, with money already owed).

**For the conceit (rule 3: intricate, strikingly dissimilar parallels).** Four crafts far apart keep the same rule as
the inker, "commit only what is already fixed": investment banks in a bought deal (#23), dividend boards (#24), pension

they make the one mapping intricate without spending CONCEIT's five-sentence budget. Each still needs a fresh reader's
