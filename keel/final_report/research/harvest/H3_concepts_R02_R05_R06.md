## Best ten in my files for Caplet's FINAL plan

1. **Human capital, and the recession that hits everything at once (rows 28, 94, 104).** Bodie, Merton and Samuelson (1992) is the academic root of FINAL's opening idea. Followed through, it also explains why the ladder uses the first deposit rather than the career-funded second one. Campbell, Sunderam and Viceira add the honest limit: Treasuries offset a deflationary recession, not a 2022-style inflation. The aha: a sentence the judge took as style turns out to be economics, with its weak spot stated.
2. **Universities stopped building in 2009, and UNC builds only on cash (rows 82, 83).** Harvard's endowment fell 30% and Allston "pauses indefinitely"; Yale and Princeton halted too. UNC requires 100% of a capital gift in cash before the award. The aha: FINAL's "money you already own" is the rule universities apply to their own donors.
3. **Farmers hedge only 20-31% of the expected crop (row 86).** In the 2012 drought, the farmers who sold forward more than they harvested had to buy back at high prices. The aha: it explains why Laura promises only half of the shares, using a world the judge does not expect.
4. **The range as a credible signal (rows 44-47, 93).** Crawford-Sobel predict that a credible message takes the form of an interval. Spence and Leland-Pyle explain why owned money is believed. List and Lucking-Reiley measured a nearly sixfold rise in giving after a larger seed. The aha: the case's demand for "a range and a confidence" is what the theory predicts a credible promise looks like.
5. **Three professions' test of "already paid" (rows 11, 90, 101, 120).** The test comes from accounting (defeasance allowed in 1983, then withdrawn), insurance law (Solvency II matching) and banking (SVB: the same bonds with the opposite liability). The aha: Laura's definition of certainty is borrowed from people who define it for a living, and it names the iBond funds' partial pass honestly.
6. **The IRS 5% exhaustion test, and its one-rate trap (rows 66, 67).** US tax law fails a sum-certain trust if the chance it runs out is above 5%, but its maths assumes today's rate forever, and trusts failed when rates fell. The aha: an outside number for "high degree of certainty", plus the same reinvestment trap TNA note 1 names.
7. **A US default really means a delay, and every rung matures early (rows 13, 88).** This uses the GAO's figure for the cost of the 2011 delay and the three downgrades, and the ladder is no longer "AAA" anywhere. The aha: "barring a US default" becomes a risk the design already absorbs, not a slogan.
8. **Time does not make shares safe (rows 29, 30).** Merton and Samuelson (1969) show horizon irrelevance, and Bodie (1995) shows the cost of insurance rises with the horizon. The aha: they answer the IPS's own first sentence ("6 years to wait would usually own shares") with two classic results.
9. **Rules with named exceptions (rows 48, 75, 77).** Kydland-Prescott give the rules-over-discretion idea. Norway's budget rule says value swings must not drive spending. Germany's and the EU's escape clauses each come with a repair plan. The aha: FINAL's "only 2 reasons" is how governments write credible fiscal rules, and it prompts the FR to say what happens after each of the two.
10. **What the flexibility is for (rows 111, 112, 18).** Megaproject overruns (Flyvbjerg), Baumol's cost disease for arts organisations, and the Taiwan dollar's jump of more than 6% in two days in May 2025 each have a named source. The aha: each reason in FINAL's flexibility sentence (overruns, a stronger Taiwan dollar) is backed by evidence.
