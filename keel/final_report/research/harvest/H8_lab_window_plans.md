## Best ten in my files for Caplet's FINAL plan
Every number here is MODEL (old design) unless marked VERIFIED. Each must be re-run before the Final Report quotes it.
1. **The same downturn hits income, co-sponsors and shares, and the plan puts that hit on the gift only** (rows 121, 107, 112, 122). Publishers cut advances to a quarter in 2009 while sales fell ~5%. In COVID, 71.4% of authors lost income (average 49%). Giving fell in 2008-09 (all VERIFIED). In Keel's model, with rates down 2 points for good and only half the deposit, 99.96% of futures still paid all ten; halving the shares too changed no payment. Aha: the FINAL's opening line is backed by evidence, and the design answers it.
2. **$300,000 bought all ten payments in only one month in four since 1871, and in no month from mid-2007 until autumn 2026. In 2020 it would have cost $469,000** (row 2). Aha: Laura's 2027 window is rare, which is why the ladder is bought at once and the 2028 deposit stands behind it.
3. **Deletion test: wipe out every share on naming day and 0 of 600,000 futures pay a payment worse** (row 123). Aha: one test anyone can follow proves "none of your 10 operating payments will depend on a share price".
4. **Where the $500,000 of payments comes from: 63% face value, 27% coupons, 8.5% interest on interest, 1% bill interest. About a tenth still depends on future yields** (rows 106, 105, 24). Holding the Feb-2037 and May-2038 bonds to their payments costs $3,108 and $3,158 if yields fall. Aha: Caplet named this gap in TNA note 1 and can show it understands the risk to the dollar.
5. **The floor's own interest pays for the shares** (rows 59, 117). $117,936 of Treasuries grows to $150,000, which frees ~$32,000 for VT. It works like the SEC's capital-protected note, but with no option to buy and the US government as issuer. Aha: "our stocks are paid for with the bond's interest, not with anyone's savings."
6. **The price of the floor is about $15,000 of average gift, and it buys a crash-year bottom of ~$140k instead of ~$95k** (rows 96, 139). The whole difference is the equity premium; the floor wins on average if shares beat Treasuries by less than ~3% a year. Aha: an honest price for certainty.
7. **Move first, then say the number** (rows 97, 98, 91, 100, 113, 116). Moving the shares the same day keeps the top 97.9% reliable; a year late, 90.9%. Skipping the move after a boom costs the most (85.8% against 98.5%). Delaying a year in a post-naming crash drops it to 0.2%. Harvard and Yale paused buildings when pools fell; UNC signs only on cash; farmers pre-sell part of the crop. Aha: the FINAL's "money you already own" is the rule serious fundraisers use.
8. **Three prices for the same ten payments: $500k added up, ~$300k in Treasuries, ~$250k on a 7% stock forecast** (row 124). The shares-first plan missed a payment 3.2% of the time, 13.7% with a smaller deposit, 40.8% with none. Aha: it shows why the team dropped its own first plan.
9. **Outside rule-makers already refuse to bet fixed obligations on recoveries** (rows 110, 141, 114, 115). Basel RBC30.23 says it is "not acceptable" to pay out on "future predictions of recovery". SVB failed the "no forced sale" test. GFOA says bond proceeds mature by the drawdown date. Orange County was a forced seller (VERIFIED). Aha: the FINAL's two reasons match regulators' rules.
