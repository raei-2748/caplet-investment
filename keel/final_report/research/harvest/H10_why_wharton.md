## Best ten in my files for Caplet's FINAL plan

1. **The range is already owned (rows 59-60).** Under FINAL's 2031 move, the range Laura announces is made of Treasuries she already holds, so the confidence once announced is very high and any surprise can only be upward, by her choice. The aha: the case's hardest question (how confident are you?) has an unusually strong honest answer, and the uncertainty is moved to where it belongs, before 2031.
2. **IPCC rule D fits the case almost word for word (rows 55-57).** Range, likelihood, basis, what sets the bounds, what happens outside. The aha: a method used by the world's climate scientists to talk to governments gives Laura a shape for talking to co-sponsors, with the number always beside the word.
3. **The bond desk reads after the first screen (rows 10, 21, 52-54).** The academic director teaches fixed income, an Aberdeen fixed-income or credit judge has sat on every finale since 2024, and a former head of J.P. Morgan's insurance investing judged the 2026 semifinal. The aha: precision on par versus NAV, cash-flow matching and the maturity gaps is what these readers notice first.
4. **The case nearly hands teams the ladder (rows 47-48).** $300,000 is about the price of the payments at 5.09%. The aha: the marks are in the judgement calls (defining certainty, the range, the confidence, the fundraising text), so that is where the words go.
5. **The first reader has a stack (rows 11-13).** About 76 reports per Wharton student reader in 2024-25, and Wharton's own words: "the hundreds we are reading". The aha: the first page and the case's own nouns decide whether the plan is even seen.
6. **Costs, benefits, risk (row 8).** The academic director's whole lens in one sentence. The aha: one line per move turns FINAL's six moves into a checklist a judge can tick.
7. **The two-sided warning (row 49).** Overpromise and lose credibility; promise too little and lose the signal. The aha: the floor's size matters as much as its safety, and the report should say so.
8. **Inflation lives on the facility side (row 50).** The payments are nominal, but the case asks about inflation in facility costs. The aha: the inflation section should be about Taiwan building costs and the Taiwan dollar, not about TIPS.
9. **Clients want to feel understood (rows 26-27, 44-45).** Every recent client's public reaction was about being understood; Laura is a numerate graduate who once ran a case competition. The aha: write the client section for her as a reader who can handle numbers and knows how judges read.
10. **The learning arc is already frozen in the IPS (row 62).** "That was our plan – until..." The aha: the Articulation section does not need a new story; it needs the dated, confirmed history of a turn the judges have already read.

Close runners: narrative over bullets and charts (rows 14-16); show, don't tell (row 17); the authenticity regime (rows 36-38); no contingency fund (row 51); returning judges (row 65).
