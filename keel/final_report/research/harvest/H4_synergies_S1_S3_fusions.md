## Best ten in my files for Caplet's FINAL plan

1. **One storm, three holes (row 2, with Fukushima, row 56).** The FINAL's best idea, that her career is a holding of shares she can't sell, has a mirror on the bond side that a judge won't expect. The recession that cuts her advances also cuts yields. That makes the ladder dearer and the reinvestment gap wider just when the 2028 deposit that completes the ladder might shrink. Naming that one shared cause defines "high degree of certainty" more sharply than counting safeguards.
2. **Solvency II's two boxes (row 19, inside Synergy B, row 3).** European insurance law values a cash flow that "can be replicated reliably" at the price of the copy. Laura's ten fixed payments are the clearest case there is, so the price of her promise is the price of the ladder. The aha: the only piece that can't be copied is coupon reinvestment (TNA note 1), and that is where any reserve belongs.
3. **Brady bonds, "the 1989 twin" (rows 25-26).** Faced with a dated claim and only part of the money, the US Treasury and the IMF locked what must not fail in dated Treasuries, let the rest float, and told buyers which was which. That is the FINAL's floor fund plus VT, done at national scale. Say that Brady used zeros, which Laura can't buy.
4. **P50/P99 sizing and "who is allowed to be wrong?" (rows 32, 9; Synergy C, row 4).** Project lenders size promises on the 1-in-100 case and hopes on the expected case. That gives the FINAL's "markets move the top, never the bottom" an institutional rule, and explains why the two ends of the 2031 range carry different confidence.
5. **The cost-overrun facility and the order of call (row 33; Synergy F, row 7).** The FINAL's "half promised to no one" becomes a standby, called last, after the contractor, insurance, contingency and co-sponsors. Laura's gift is a capped contribution, not a completion guarantee. "Flexibility" gets a role a judge can picture.
6. **Equitable Life (row 12).** A mutual about 200 years old (S1's phrase) closed to new business in 2000 after selling a promise with no price on it and then cutting it after the fact, which the court ruled out. That is the real-world case behind "whatever figure you choose is money you already own".
7. **Perry v. United States and the 1933 question (row 30).** It answers "what if the government changes the rules?". The only US bond term ever rewritten was a gold clause, and Laura's bonds are plain dollars. The risk the Court actually named, buying power, sits on the facility side, which is why the kept half exists. Use the corrected wording only.
8. **The Taiwanese hui and Civil Code 709-7/709-9 (row 51).** Taiwanese co-sponsors already ask "who stands behind the last payment?", because their law makes the organiser liable. "A hui whose organiser is the US Treasury" is client knowledge with a Taiwan edge that few teams will have.
9. **Japan's negative spread against Laura's sliver (row 11).** Insurers that promised 4% and earned 2% carried the gap on the whole book for a decade; Laura's version is the reinvested coupons only, once. It sizes the one honest soft spot (TNA note 1) instead of hiding it.
10. **Germany's Höchstrechnungszins (row 15).** Regulators cap what insurers may guarantee at a cautious expected yield less 40%, because sellers under competitive pressure can't be trusted to cap themselves. "Why promise only half the shares?" gets a one-sentence answer: it is Laura's own maximum guaranteed rate.
