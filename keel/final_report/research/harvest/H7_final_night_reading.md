## Best ten in my files for Caplet's FINAL plan

1. **A riskless asset needs two conditions (#1, #2; Damodaran, with Shiller L4).** The aha: "high degree of
   certainty" becomes a textbook test, and the one condition the ladder only nearly meets (no reinvestment risk) is
   exactly the weakness TNA note 1 already admitted. The team's own trading note turns out to be finance's own
   definition.
2. **Escrows must be "sufficient without reinvestment" (#10).** The aha: US cities that repay bonds early hold their
   Treasury escrows to a zero-reinvestment test checked by an independent accountant. Caplet's cautious pricing of the
   coupon gap is a professional standard, not a guess.
3. **Buy every promise at Treasury prices on the day it is made (#7).** The aha: US public pensions did the opposite
   (valued promises at hoped-for returns), and research measures the extra risk and worse results that followed. That
   gives Caplet's whole design (ladder 2027, minimum 2028, range 2031) one reason with evidence behind it.
4. **Laura's balance sheet is levered (#28).** The aha: the "moderate" 60/40 most teams pick puts about 1.8 times
   the facility money into shares, so the conventional plan is the reckless one for exactly the money Laura names to
   co-sponsors.
5. **A named number is a quasi-bond (#60).** The aha: Damodaran's "fixed dividends turn equity into quasi bonds"
   explains the 2031 move exactly. Once Laura names a top, the shares behind it are already a promise, so the plan
   turns them into the bond they have become.
6. **An operating budget and a capital budget, a defined benefit and a defined contribution (#20, #21).** The aha: the
   case's two pots are how governments and pensions already split fixed claims from projects. The payments never move
   and the gift does, which is Caplet's "the bottom will remain constant" in public-finance terms.
7. **Pledges fail when the donor's fortune falls, and beating a promise earns nothing (#70, #63, #65).** The aha:
   real campus buildings stopped in 2008-10 because pledges rode on donors' shares (Lehman to Spelman, Duke). That is
   why the top is bought before Laura names it, and why the surplus stays unannounced.
8. **Rules beat discretion, and the 1987 endowments show it (#58, #23).** The aha: "a fall in the market is not one
   of them" is a Nobel-grade principle (Kydland-Prescott), with a dated failure behind it. Endowments that sold after
   the 1987 crash took six years to recover their bond weight.
9. **Diversify the residency, judged as trust law does (#80, #81).** The aha: Caplet's own career insight extends from
   Laura to the whole residency. Co-sponsors, grants and fees all shrink in a recession, so her Treasury-backed
   contribution is the one source that doesn't. Trust law counts a beneficiary's "other resources" in exactly this way.
10. **Forecast ranges are too narrow, so confidence should rest on evidence (#32, #31, #16).** The aha: finance chiefs'
    "80% sure" ranges held the outcome only 36% of the time. Caplet can state the co-sponsors' confidence level as
    "bonds already owned" (the quality of the evidence) rather than a model's odds.

trade-off against all-Treasuries (#46), the ladder as the opposite of a bank (#19).
