## Best ten in my files for Caplet's FINAL plan
1. **Survivorship and Wald (row 1).** "The floor needs no history; only the top does." A judge sees that the team knows which of its own numbers depend on a historical sample and which don't. It is built on the NBER and Jorion-Goetzmann figures and answers the "stocks always win over ten years" sceptic before they speak.
2. **Common-cause failure across the defences (row 33, with R11 L4).** The FINAL's best idea (her career is a share she can't sell) gains a second step: the same slump that cuts her advances also cuts yields and shares. That is exactly why each payment rests on a bond already bought. The aha: the career point explains both why the portfolio leans away from shares and where the one remaining risk (reinvestment) sits.
3. **Studebaker 1963 (row 30).** "No payment waits on money not yet earned." It has a human count (3,600 paid, 4,000 cut to 15%, thousands under 40 zeroed) and a law that followed (ERISA). It also gives the FINAL's careful wording on the 2028 deposit a reason: completing a gap, never funding a payment from future income alone.
4. **Four ways fixed promises have failed (row 82, with rows 70-73).** PACT, Equitable, Bonilla and Japan's insurers turn "high degree of certainty" into a checklist a judge can test: three doors closed, one (reinvestment) open, measured and named in TNA note 1. 5. **Perry v. United States (row 41).** The only Treasury term ever broken was a gold clause that Laura's bonds don't contain. 6. **The 2031 move as a commitment, with Varian's warning (rows 16-17).** Moving the promised half into Treasuries isn't only safety; it turns Laura's figure from talk into a commitment co-sponsors can build on.
