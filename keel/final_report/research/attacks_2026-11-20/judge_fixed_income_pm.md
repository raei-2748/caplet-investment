## What survives

1. The deletion test (shares at zero: all ten payments unchanged and the range one figure, the bottom). It is the cleanest proof that the parts carry separate jobs, and it needs no model.
2. The order of purchase (the 2028 deposit buys the payments' completion and reserve first, then the floor and shares), so a deposit about three-quarters smaller still meets the payments' standard. This is a real waterfall, and the smaller-deposit risk falls on building money, as it should.
3. Stating no figure before it is bought (a top named a year before the move would exceed what the move buys in about 11 futures in 100). Matching the date of a promise to the date its funding is bought is the liability manager's first rule, and the numbers show it.
4. The stale-price rule from 29-30 September (every bond's yield within 0.25 points of the official curve before ordering). It is a genuine independent-price-verification control, born of a real error, and any bond desk would recognise it.
5. The half-kept trade-off as a division, not a creation (each $5,000 more of stated top costs about $5,300 of what she keeps; what she gives plus what she keeps moves about $1,100 or less across 30%, half and 70%). It is honest, measured, and makes no claim to optimality.
