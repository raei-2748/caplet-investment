## What a synthesis is
Two to four ideas from the store, from **different sources and ideally different fields** (a bond desk and a
fundraising guide; trust law and a residency's budget; a printer's practice and a pension rule), put together so they
say something none of them says alone, about Laura's plan. Not a summary of one tag; not one source restated; not a new
strategy (the strategy is frozen: a synthesis evaluates, explains or defends what the IPS already does, or prices what
it gave up). The best ones change how a reader sees the plan: "the plan is X, which is the same thing a Y does when Z".

## Build 6 to 8, then keep the best 5 to 7
For each, in this order (notes, plain words, no prose for the report):
- **S<letter>-<n>. Title** (one line, plain).
- **Parts:** each store id with its idea in one line and its source as the store cites it.
- **The new thing:** what the combination shows that no part shows alone (two or three sentences).
- **The aha, in one sentence:** the startling new perspective a judge would remember. Say what is surprising and to whom.
- **Why it fits Laura:** the IPS line(s) and the case's words it serves (case lines from
  `competition/official/2026_27/Laura_Gao_2026_Client_Profile.pdf` text as quoted in CANON or CASE_MAP).
- **The five questions:** what is it; did it work (evidence, with its source); how Laura's case is like it; how it is not
  (the disanalogy, honestly); what Caplet takes.
- **Against the frozen work:** any clash with the IPS, Trading Notes, TNA reflections or portfolio (CANON item), or "none".
- **Its weakness:** the strongest honest objection a judge or a bond desk would raise.
- **Source chain:** from each primary source to the claim, every link named (store id, card, URL as the store gives it).
- **Where it lands:** the SKELETON part (2.1-2.5, 3a-3f, an exhibit) and the Wharton criterion (Investment Strategy,
  Client Knowledge, Portfolio Analysis, Articulation, Creativity and Presentation).
- **Figures:** each with its NUMBERS section, or "none".
- **Evidence gap:** what a research worker should read next to make it stronger or kill it.
- **Verdict:** keep (ready for the aha register's cold test), adapt (needs the gap closed), or drop (and why).
