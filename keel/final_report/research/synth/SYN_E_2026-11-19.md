## SE-1. Table 3 sorts risks by warning, not by noise

- **Parts:**
  1. **H5-046.** An FMEA table can carry a "years of warning" column. A bond price fall is loud and seen every day. A

     never multiply the scores. Source: Wikipedia, FMEA (MIL-P-1629, 1949; AIAG-VDA 2019 Action Priority table) and
     BioProcess Online. Field: reliability engineering.
  2. **Q109-005.** A firm's stress measures must take account of "the time that the firm would have to react to these
     events". Source: SYSC 20.2.5G; PRA SS31/15 4.6. Field: UK bank regulation.
  3. **Q59-006 with Q111-015** (one idea, two rows):
     - MINIMUM FUEL is "not an emergency situation but an indication that an emergency situation is possible should any
       additional delay occur". MAYDAY FUEL is declared when predicted landing fuel is below final reserve. Source: ICAO
       Annex 6 Part I, 4.3.7.2.2-4.3.7.2.3; EASA SIB 2018-08.
     - MINIMUM FUEL is declared on a forecast, "even after receiving clearance from ATC". Source: EASA (2022) GM1
       CAT.OP.MPA.185(e).

  4. **Q2a-001** (GOLDMINE 21, "nobody can run on the plan"). In 2022 the UK's liability-matched pension books broke
     through their financing, not their gilts: leveraged funds could not meet collateral calls. Source: Cunliffe (2022a),

## SE-2. The ledger's rule for gains, run forward: costs taken out first, gains counted only when owned

- **Parts:**
  1. **Q106-019.** "Probability is not a factor in the recognition of gain contingencies. Therefore, gain contingencies
     are typically recognized later than loss contingencies." Source: KPMG (2025), Handbook: Contingencies, 6.3. Field:
     accounting standards.
  2. **Q43-028.** Lenders, whose claims are capped, want "the asymmetrical verification requirements for gains and
     losses". Source: Watts (2003). Field: accounting research. It already sits behind CONCEIT 5b rank 1's line for 3b.
  3. **Q106-025 with Q106-026.** Bookkeepers wrote each entry in ink and the running totals ("footings") in pencil.
     When the account was closed: "Rule and foot the account; write the footings in black ink". Source: Bookkeeping:
     Complete course (1912); Bookkeeping and accountancy (1911). Field: bookkeeping practice.
  4. **NUMBERS 4 with NUMBERS 0's haircut row.**
     - A top stated 1, 3, 6 or 12 months before the move would be more than the move buys in 2.9%, 6.2%, 8.6% or 10.6%
       of house paths.
     - The 0.5% margin is taken off each stated end for fund costs beyond the fee. The ended funds show about 0.27%.

- **The new thing:**
  - CONCEIT holds the ledger as a V2.5 candidate, and rank 1 uses Watts for the 3b turn (room only on the upside). Read
    against the register, the same asymmetry runs through three separate parts of the plan, not one:
    - A likely cost is taken out before anything is stated: the margin, sized from what the ended funds actually cost
      beyond their fee.
    - A likely gain on waiting cash is counted as nothing: every payment holds even if cash waiting earns nothing
      (NUMBERS 2).
    - The likeliest gain of all, the shares' growth, counts for nothing until the move buys more of the late-2032 fund
      before the 2031 meetings. That is the same fund the floor bought in January 2028. The move is the closing at which
      the footing goes into ink.
  - NUMBERS 4 shows what "probability is not a factor" means in practice. Even a top stated one month early would be
    more than the move buys in about 3 futures in 100.
  - Builds on CONCEIT V2.5 "the ledger", CONCEIT 5b rank 1, and AHA spine row 7 (the forecast barely touches the
    promise; speaking early does).

- **The aha, in one sentence:** Accountants have long booked likely costs early and gains only when realised, and
  Laura's plan keeps the same books looking forward. The margin takes fund costs out before any figure is stated, the
  payments count no interest on waiting cash, and the top counts the shares only once the move has bought Treasuries
  with them. It surprises a finance judge who reads the quiet years before 2031 as timidity and finds an old,
  respected accounting rule run forward.

- **Why it fits Laura:**
  - IPS l.15: "so whatever figure you choose is money you already own".
  - IPS l.11.
  - The case: "If Laura promises more than she can ultimately contribute, she could damage her credibility" (as quoted
    in CONCEIT 5b rank 8).
  - Wharton asks for the 2031 range with its confidence.

- **The five questions:**
  - *What it is:* the rule that a contingent gain is not booked until realised, however probable, while a probable loss
    is booked; and the bookkeeper's habit of pencilled running totals inked at the close.
  - *Did it work:* it is the standing US rule, as KPMG's handbook reads it, and lenders choose it in debt contracts
    (Watts 2003). Its limit is also known: standard-setters reject one-sided caution in financial statements (IASB 2018,
    2.17; card Q43). So it is a rule for what you promise, not for what you estimate.
  - *How Laura's case is like it:* the co-sponsors' claim is capped at the figure she states, as a lender's is. What they
    need is a figure anyone can check against holdings held for her, which is Watts's verification point.
  - *How it is not:*
    - A ledger's ink proves what happened. Laura's ink proves what will be paid (Q106-035).
    - A closing fixes a past total. The move buys a future payment, and only half the account is closed: her flexibility
      stays open and is stated to no one (Q106-026's critique). IAS 37 would even disclose a probable gain; Laura
      states none (Q106-017).
    - After the move, not every doubt is gone. With cash waiting earning nothing, an early end of the late-2032 fund in
      2031 would leave the top up to $4,589 short (NUMBERS 3). So say "the top is owned", never that every doubt is
      resolved (Q106-019's critique).
  - *What Caplet takes:*
    - One clause behind 3c's "inked before the 2031 meetings": the footing inked at the closing. Read it cold with the
      definition first.
    - Never the two accounting phrases CONCEIT 5b rank 1's limit bans; the asymmetry is described, never named as a
      virtue.
    - Never give "never erase" to Pacioli; the line is Manzoni's.

- **Against the frozen work:** none.
  - IPS l.15 sets the move before the 2031 meetings.
  - The margin is the register's range rule (NUMBERS 0).
  - The funds pay their net asset value, not par (Trading Note 2), and the top is what the move buys, rounded down, so
    no fund payout is called fixed.

- **Its weakness:**
  - The asymmetry may read as caution for its own sake. An accountant on the panel will note that modern standards
    reject one-sided caution. The answer is in the parts: Laura is not valuing an asset, she is making a capped promise
    to others, which is the debt-contract case Watts describes.
  - The parallel has three members (margin, interest, top), but the conceit allows one turn per part. The 27 Nov
    writers must pick one place for it.

## SE-4. A row for the debt limit, none for a shutdown: the law an Illinois lottery lacked

- **Parts:**
  1. **Q58-035.**
     - A lapse in appropriations shuts the government: 20 shutdowns of a full day or more FY1977-FY2019, and 43 days in
       2025.
     - Interest on the public debt is a permanent appropriation (31 U.S.C. 1305(2)), and "the Treasury can continue to
       pay interest on U.S. Treasury debt on time".
     - 31 U.S.C. 3123(a) pledges the faith of the United States to pay principal and interest.
     - Source: CRS R48930; GAO; GAO-26-107551 fn 48; Brookings (2026). Field: US public law.
  2. **Q2b-006 with Q2b-008.**
     - Illinois Lottery, 2015: prizes over $25,000 were delayed although "the Illinois Lottery has the money to pay".
       Nearly 3,900 claims, more than $112 million, were paid "within a month of receiving an appropriation".
     - It happened again in 2017. The bills for a continuing appropriation stayed in committee.
     - Source: S10 (Lottery statement reproduced by KWQC, 27 Jun 2017); S27. Field: a state lottery's payouts.
  3. **Q7-008** (GOLDMINE 20). "Although sovereigns sometimes excusably default, they never repudiate their debts."
     Source: Grossman & Van Huyck (1988), AER 78(5). Field: economics.
  4. **Table 3 rows 2 and 3.**
     - A default is a Treasury payment more than five business days late.
     - A debt-limit deadline within a month of a cash date means "look closer": nothing changes before it, and nothing
       is sold ahead of it.
     - Source: `exhibits/tables.md`.

- **The new thing:**
  - The Illinois case separates two things the plan's words run together: having the money, and being allowed to pay
    it.
  - Read with the US statutes, it explains a Table 3 design choice the report has not yet explained:
    - There is no row for a shutdown, the louder and more frequent event, because Treasury interest has the standing
      authority the lottery asked for and never got.
    - There is a row for the debt limit, because principal at maturity rests on borrowing.
    - The default the IPS names is the event sovereigns avoid at the price of their reputation, so it stays a named exit,
      said once and with nothing attached.

- **The aha, in one sentence:** Table 3 watches the debt limit and has no row for a government shutdown, because US law
  already gives Treasury interest the standing authority an Illinois lottery lacked in 2015, when it held the money and
  still paid prizes late. It surprises a judge who treats "Washington risk" as one risk and sees it split by statute into
  one that cannot reach Laura's interest and one the table dates.

- **Why it fits Laura:**
  - IPS l.11: the ten depend on nothing but the Treasury.
  - IPS l.17: a Treasury default is one of the two reasons.
  - IPS l.13: "Held to the end, these pay what they say".
  - The case's "high degree of certainty" (CANON A4).
  - Wharton's ask for assumptions, limits and what to monitor.

- **The five questions:**
  - *What it is:* a standing appropriation lets a payer pay without a fresh vote. The lottery lacked one; Treasury
    interest has one.
  - *Did it work:* in the 43-day shutdown of 2025, interest was paid on time (GAO-26-107551 fn 48, as Q58-035 cites).
    Illinois paid every delayed claim within a month of an appropriation, so the money was late, never lost.
  - *How Laura's case is like it:* her money is in place from January 2027, with the ten held for her. What could make a
    payment late is a rule about releasing money, not its absence.
  - *How it is not:*
    - The permanent appropriation covers interest. Principal at maturity rests on the pledge and on borrowing, which is
      the debt-limit question (Q58-035's critique). Most of each payment is principal, so the honest line is "a shutdown
      cannot stop the interest", never "nothing in Washington can delay her payments".
    - Illinois is a public body; Laura is a private payer with no appropriation step. Her own version is who can act for
      her each January (Q2b-006's use). That is a set-up note, never a third reason (Q2b-008's critique).
    - Sovereigns borrow again and again; Laura asks co-sponsors once (Q7-008's critique).
  - *What Caplet takes:*
    - One clause in 3e's note on IPS l.17's second reason: why the table watches the debt limit and not shutdowns.
    - Grossman and Van Huyck as the one sentence behind calling Treasuries risk-free (GOLDMINE 20), with nothing
      attached.

- **Against the frozen work:** none.
  - No trigger is added (CANON A34). Row 3 already exists and changes "nothing before it".
  - IPS l.11's "barring a US default" stays the IPS's own words, and the report adds no hedge to Treasuries (Book 19).

- **Its weakness:**
  - A bond desk would say finance people know shutdowns do not touch Treasury interest, so the clause spends words on
    the obvious.
  - The real risk to principal is the debt limit, which row 3 already handles.
  - A state lottery set beside a sovereign may read as a stretch.

## SE-6. Owning both ends ends the forecasting, not the watching

  2. **Q82-027.** "A PSI is not a quality monitoring of the entire run and therefore does not constitute approval of the

  3. **Q3c-007 with NUMBERS 8h.**
