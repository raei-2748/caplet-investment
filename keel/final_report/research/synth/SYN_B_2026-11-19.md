## SB-1. Three rules leave one method: the ten are dedicated, and the extra Treasuries are dedication's price without STRIPS

**Parts**

**The new thing**
- Each part speaks to one rule. Together they show that three rules leave exactly one method.
  1. **No trades.** Redington shows that immunisation is a standing order to trade. IPS l.17 allows no trade for a market move, so the IPS itself rules immunisation out.
  2. **Dedication is left, but its condition fails for Laura.** Ferguson's condition is that the interest goes straight to payments. For Laura it fails: the interest arrives for six years before the first payment (C14). That is where the $93,941 of interest on interest sits (NUMBERS 2).
  3. **No zeros.** The textbook fix is zero-coupon bonds (Ryan), and Wharton bans STRIPS.
- With trading, zeros and a forecast all ruled out, one way is left to meet the case's "high degree of certainty" with coupon Treasuries. It is Leibowitz's own classic test: size the book so it pays with no reinvestment. That is the standard, and the extra Treasuries are its price.

**The aha, in one sentence**
- A bond reader who sees extra Treasuries bought "in case cash waiting earns nothing" assumes a nervous amateur. In fact this is dedication's founding test in Leibowitz's 1986 words ("totally passive management ... no reinvestment"), and with STRIPS banned and IPS l.17 forbidding trades, buying extra Treasuries is the only way left to meet it.
- Who is surprised: a fixed-income judge, who expects "no rebalancing" to mean the team ignored reinvestment risk.

**Why it fits Laura**
- The case: "must be funded by the investment portfolio with a high degree of certainty" (CANON B5).

- TNA l.7: the payments are "fixed in nominal dollars" (CANON A14).
- TNA l.5 names the reinvestment risk itself (A15).

**The five questions**
- **What is it?** Pension finance has two answers to fixed-dollar liabilities:
  - immunisation: match duration, and rebalance as rates move;
  - dedication: match each cash flow, and hold.
- **Did it work?**
  - Leibowitz calls dedication the "least-risk asset" for nominal liabilities. Bader says its yield is "assured" because nothing is sold and nothing is reinvested.
  - Immunisation's need to trade comes from its own founder (Redington) and the curriculum's table (Q16-028).
  - What a forced sale costs is GIW Industries (Q63-016: $340,000 of Treasuries that had cost $396,312.50 were sold to meet a payout). GIW is already in spine row 2, so it is support only, never printed twice.
- **How Laura's case is like it.** Ten fixed nominal payments, on known dates, from Treasuries only. This is the textbook dedication case.
- **How it is not.**
  - Bader's and Ferguson's books paid from year one. Laura's first payment comes six years after the purchase, so the interest waits.
  - Five of her holdings are funds that pay net asset value, not par (CANON B23).
  - Leibowitz allowed corporate bonds.
  - Leibowitz also says reinvestment "at positive interest rates" lowers cost "without substantially increasing risk" (Q16-026). He did not insist on zero.
- **What Caplet takes.**
  - The method's name, for 2.5's one use of "dedicated portfolio" (WORDS).
  - The standard's pedigree, for 3a's one bond-reader clause.
  - Redington as the reason behind IPS l.17. This is GOLDMINE 29 with sweep B #17's reason: no forced sale in a bad January, and no exposure to the curve twisting. It is never "never needs rebalancing", which six of six readers called a convenience.

**Against the frozen work**

- **Pitch l.3, "immune to markets".** It stays. The report can show that it holds in dedication's sense: the book never has to trade with the market. The ten are never called "immunised".
- **The standard's name.** It is never labelled "sufficient without reinvestment" (WORDS). Leibowitz's "no reinvestment" is quoted as his words.
- **CANON D7.** No new rule: this explains the January 2028 purchase that IPS l.13 and l.11 already make.
- **CANON A12 and A17-A18.** Consistent. WN l.18's "reinvested coupons fund the rest" is the forecast the extra Treasuries take out (row 5).

**Its weakness**

## SB-3. Dated slices of someone else's debt: the shape American cities chose after their 1930s sinking funds

This extends "Owned, not owed" with a shape argument. The Q62 items were 3a aha candidates in GEM_LEDGER and have never been cold-tested.

**Parts**

| Store id | Idea | Source as the store cites it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q62-022 | "Partly because of the experience with sinking-fund assets during the financial distress of the 1930's, the term bond has been generally replaced by the serial bond." | Lent (1955), ch. 3; Woelfel via Poitras | VERIFIED (read at source, 16 Nov 2026) |
| Q62-014 | In 1940, 93% of large cities' sinking-fund investments were municipal bonds, "including 91 per cent in own-city bonds". By 1950: 74% (70% own-city) and 20% federal. State sinking funds went from 5% to 40% federal. | Lent (1955), ch. 3 | VERIFIED |
| Q62-018 | Selling new bonds to the city's own sinking fund "would make the sinking fund a debt-creating instead of a debt-paying scheme". | Kelly v. City of Minneapolis (Minn. 1895) | VERIFIED (read on CourtListener; reporter citation not confirmed) |
| Q62-027 | Trust-fund Treasuries "are legal claims on the Treasury", yet for the Government as a whole "These assets and liabilities cancel each other out". | OMB, Analytical Perspectives FY2025 ch. 23 and FY2027 ch. 13 | VERIFIED |

- **Frozen work it uses:**
  - NUMBERS 8s: the first plan's danger was one date, 1 Jan 2033, when all ten had to be bought.
  - IPS l.9 (the reason).
  - IPS l.13: "each maturing just before it's needed".
- **Supporting, not counted:**
  - Q62-001, Hamilton (1813/1816): "The excess of revenue above expenditure is the only real sinking fund".
  - Q62-037, Ireland's NPRF (used under the weakness).

**The new thing**
- Municipal finance learned two lessons in the 1930s:
  - a reserve held in your own paper is weak exactly when you are;
  - one pot for one date is weaker than a slice for each date.
- So cities moved from term bonds with sinking funds to serial bonds, and their reserves moved toward federal debt.
- Caplet's first plan had the old shape (NUMBERS 8s): shares to 2031, then all ten bought on one date, 1 Jan 2033. That is one pot, held in assets that IPS l.9 says fall in the same recession that shrinks a writer's income.
- The final plan has the shape the cities moved to:
  - dated slices (IPS l.13);
  - held in another party's debt (IPS l.11: "or on anybody else's money");
  - bought with money she already owns (Hamilton's test).
- So the reason IPS l.9 gives for Caplet's change of mind is, in kind, the reason municipal finance gave for its own.

**The aha, in one sentence**
- A judge who reads Caplet's change of mind (IPS l.9) as a team's private discovery finds that American cities made the same change after the 1930s, for the same kind of reason (their reserves sat in assets that fell when they did). Laura's ten dated Treasuries are the shape public finance chose after its own reserves proved weak.
- Who is surprised: a judge who expects a high-school plan's shape to be a retail habit, not a lesson a whole field paid for.

**Why it fits Laura**
- IPS l.9 and IPS l.11 ("or on anybody else's money").
- IPS l.13: "each maturing just before it's needed".
- The case's reserve question (CASE_MAP A1): "explain how its composition should change, if at all, as the annual payments approach and are made". A serial reserve changes only by running down (NUMBERS 8h).
- CANON B5.

**The five questions**
- **What is it?** A sinking fund is a pot built up to repay a term bond on one date. Serial bonds repay a slice each year.
- **Did it work?**
  - Lent's 1955 survey records the switch and gives the 1930s experience as "partly" its cause.
  - Own-city paper fell from 91% of large cities' sinking-fund assets (1940) to 70% (1950), while federal debt rose to 20%.
  - New York's $774 million cancellation (Q62-015) shows that a reserve in its own paper was never extra money.
  - Kelly (1895) named the circularity.
  - No outcome study of serial issues' safety has been read.
- **How Laura's case is like it.** Fixed dated sums over many years, and two choices:
  - one pot for one date, or a slice for each date;
  - assets that move with the payer, or claims on someone else.
- **How it is not.**
  - A city is a borrower, and serial bonds are its debt, not its reserve. Laura borrows nothing; the analogy is shape and correlation only (Q62-022 critique).
  - The first plan was never a sinking fund in law, and it would have held shares, not her own paper. The link is "assets that fall when the payer's income falls" (IPS l.9), not identity.
- **What Caplet takes.**
  - One clause in 3a on the reserve's shape (dated slices of someone else's debt), answering A1's composition question.
  - Whether 3f's change of mind carries it as a gloss is Caplet's call. It is an idea, never the experience itself (standing order 22).

**Against the frozen work**
- The first plan was dropped for IPS l.9's reason. This synthesis never uses the 3.2% (8s prices it, nothing more).
- The first plan's notes said "glide". Avoid the word.
- Never "endowment".
- "Pot" and "slice" are note words; prose follows WORDS and CONCEIT.
- Otherwise none.

**Its weakness**
- "The US government also borrows. OMB says trust-fund Treasuries cancel out. Isn't her reserve someone else's sinking fund?"
  - For Laura the Treasuries are legal claims on another party (Q62-027's first half). The cancelling holds only inside the issuer. That is the whole difference.
- Ireland's reserve (Q62-037) was real money in other people's assets, and its owner redirected it.
  - Laura's reserve rests on her keeping IPS l.17, and she can always sell ("Nobody can run": "Laura herself can always sell").
- The parallel needs two sentences of setup, which is costly in 3a's 280 words.

**Source chain**
- Lent (1955), ch. 3, and Woelfel via Poitras lead to Q62-022 and Q62-014 (card Q62).
- Comptroller (1932) via Lent leads to Q62-015.
- Kelly v. City of Minneapolis (CourtListener) leads to Q62-018.
- OMB Analytical Perspectives FY2025 ch. 23 and FY2027 ch. 13 lead to Q62-027.
- Hamilton, Proposition XII, leads to Q62-001.
- The NPRF and NTMA annual reports lead to Q62-037.
- NUMBERS 8s rests on `keel/library/verified_2026-09-27/strategy_mc.py` and the team notes of 27 Sep.

**Where it lands**
- 3a: the reserve's shape and composition (case A1).
- 3f only as Caplet's option.
- Criteria: Investment Strategy; Articulation (only if 3f takes it).

**Figures**
- None in prose.
- 8s is cited for "one date" without a figure. 8h is cited for "changes only by running down".
- Lent's shares are outside facts, not plan figures.

**Evidence gap**

**Verdict:** keep. Cold-test it beside "Owned, not owed".
