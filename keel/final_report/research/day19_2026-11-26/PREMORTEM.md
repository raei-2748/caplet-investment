**The premise.** It is Sat 28 Nov 09:00 Sydney. The session that started Fri 27 Nov 06:00 has handed the students a
draft, and the draft is not good enough. Why?

**What this rests on.** `WRITING_SESSION.md`; `CAPLET_DOC_2026-11-25.md`; `research/meta/HOW_STRATEGISTS_PLAN.md` 2b
and 2d; `LESSONS.md`; `voice/WHY_V61_FAILED.md`; `voice/ROUND20.md`; the review-loop rehearsal

## 2. Each cause: why it is likely, the early sign, the guard

**1. Names before they are set up.** Section 2 written whole on Wed 25 Nov was remembered perfectly and followed only
63% of the time on first reading; all nine stops were a name or a reason missing (WInS, "the floor", IBTM's quarter,
the extra Treasuries' job). Version 61 failed the same way 17 times in 21 sentences. Section 2 carries about ten names in
600 words, and Section 3 adds more (the top, the bottom, the move, the late-2032 fund, the letter).
- *Early sign:* in round 1 or 2 the fresh reader names a stop that is a noun, or the reader test marks a question PARTLY
  because a name was not understood.
- *Guard:* each writer's brief lists the names its part may use, in `NAME_ORDER.md` order, with the plain description
  that must come first. K10 (a name used before its part sets it up) is a killer item every round. A sentence two
  readers stop at is rewritten whole by its writer, never patched by the lead.

**2. The session's readers pass what the students fail.** Book 10 and standing order 25: readers built by the session
"understand your language". In voice round 11 simulated readers caught about half of what Caplet failed; in round 14 the
cold retelling caught 2 of 5. A draft that passes every fresh reader can still lose the students on page 2.
- *Early sign:* the fresh reader recovers the plan, range and lesson perfectly in round 2 while the draft still holds
  "what" clauses, money as the subject, or a "because" with no middle step (the WHY_V61_FAILED table).
- *Guard:* before its first verdict each fresh reader reads Caplet's seventeen failed version-61 lines mixed with
  Caplet's own IPS lines, and is used only if it marks most of the seventeen as stops. The reading reviewer runs the
  WHY_V61_FAILED table as a checklist (ten kinds of taken-for-granted). Count "followed first time" per part and hold it
  at 80% or more by round 9 (round 20 started at 63%).

**3. A figure on the wrong basis, or with two dates.** The register holds two bases for the payments (fee-only,
$444,080 + $55,920, and fee-and-margin, $442,930 + $57,070), two curves (6 Nov and 13 Nov), $2,287 against $2,388 for
the same gap a year apart, and "about" figures beside exact ones. Round 19 found a figure with two dates stops every
reader. In the rehearsal, whole-document reviewers caught the planted wrong number far less often than the register
check.
- *Early sign:* K1 fails on the same figure in two rounds, or one quantity appears in the draft with two values.
- *Guard:* freeze on Thu 26 Nov a one-file numbers extract: every figure the report may print, with its basis, its one
  date and its NUMBERS line (about 150 lines, not the 84,500-word register). The killer checker reads the extract; the
  numbers owner is called only on a miss. Figure 1's basis is settled Thu 26 Nov (rebuilt on fee-and-margin or labelled,
  as Caplet asked). One date per figure in prose, the basis in the exhibit's note.

**4. Rounds churn.** Plan 2d's phases (structure 2-5, evidence 6-9, sentences 10-12) only hold if structure is shut. With
eleven reviewers' worth of opinions and no reviewer authority, a paragraph can move back and forth for hours.
- *Early sign:* the same paragraph rewritten in three rounds for the same point; the blind compare prefers N over N+1
  twice running; section order still changing after the structure phase.
- *Guard:* at the end of the structure phase (round 4 in the nine-round shape of section 3, round 5 in plan 2d's), structure is frozen and logged; a later structural change needs a K8 failure (an ask of the
  case not answered) to reopen it. A point that has been taken and reversed once goes to the lead for one decision,
  logged, and is not reopened. At most five fixes per round, ranked by reviewer count with K1 and K2 on top. The stop rule
  stands (two rounds the blind compare cannot tell apart with the fresh reader recovering all three points).

**5. A tour of the research.** The store holds 11,362 ideas, 44 nuggets, ten ahas and a parallel for most parts. Plan 2b's
first cause was exactly this. Wharton's Investment Strategy and Creativity criteria reward one idea carried through.
- *Early sign:* the fresh reader's one-sentence plan names facts rather than the rule (buy before she states it), or the
  red team says "dense" or "a research report".
- *Guard:* every paragraph names its branch of `ARGUMENT_TREE.md` in the writer's brief; a nugget without a branch stays
  in the store. Nugget and aha placement is a checklist for round 6, never a killer item. Outside parallels at most one
  per part of Section 3, as the tree says. Cut whole ideas, never squeeze sentences (Book 10).

**6. It sounds like a machine.** Standing order 26 names both traps: "plain boring and generic" and a style "so OBVIOUSLY
Claude that it's worse". Keel's main session scored 0 of 9 in voice round 1; fresh writers who read only Caplet's writing
did best. Twelve rounds of review pull towards tidy, balanced, safe prose.
- *Early sign:* D35 patterns (slogan flips, two-word kickers, one-line endings, "That is X." runs); the same turn of
  phrase in three parts; "we" outside about 1.5-3.0 per 100 words in a part where the team acts (Caplet's IPS 2.4,
  Section 2 in round 20 2.29).
- *Guard:* every writer reads Caplet's IPS, Trading Notes Analyses and essay whole before writing and continues from the
  IPS's last line. K4 and K9 every round. In round 10 one blind check sets a paragraph of the draft beside one of
  Caplet's own: if a reader picks the draft out for its voice, that paragraph is rewritten. Dry wit is welcome where the
  team's own writing has it.

**7. Loose reasons in the fact sheet.** Round 20's two sense faults were version 12's own wording ("every dollar promised
is bought before any money goes into shares"; "nothing is rebalanced because each holding is matched to a date"). Writers
copy the sheet's reasons word for word, so one loose line spreads to every part.
- *Early sign:* the same wrong or half-true reason appears in two writers' parts in round 1.
- *Guard:* on Thu 26 Nov the fact sheet gets one sense pass as if it were report prose (standing order 25's second test:
  once followed, is it true?), and is frozen with its version number. A sense fault found in the draft is traced to the
  sheet and fixed in both, the same round.

**8. The weekly limit stops the session.** Last week the limit stopped all work from Fri 20 Nov morning to Sun 22 Nov.
This week's limit resets Sun 29 Nov 09:00, after the handover, and has already carried eight paced runs plus Thursday's
two. Section 3 below estimates about 25 million tokens for the full twelve-round shape.
- *Early sign:* the lead's running total after round 3 is more than 25% above the per-round estimate.
- *Guard:* the budget in section 3 below, with its caps. A whole draft exists by hour 4 and every version is saved and
  pushed, so the last good version is always a deliverable. Thursday's runs stay light. If the total passes the soft cap,
  rotating reviewers stop except when K1 or K2 fails; at the hard cap the session goes straight to final checks.

**9. A contradiction with what was submitted.** The traps are known: a make-up promise against the half IPS l.15 promises
"to no one" (C3, dropped by default); "fixed payout" for a fund (TNA l.13); "secures"; "nothing is rebalanced" for VT;
"duration matches too".
- *Early sign:* K2 fails, or the bond reviewer quotes CANON against a sentence.
- *Guard:* K2 every round against `CANON.md`; a K2 failure is fixed whatever its count and calls the bond reviewer that
  round. The checker's brief lists the known traps from `SECTION_NOTES.md` by part.

**10. The draft departs from the students' own map.** Caplet is writing its own report in its Google Doc (v2 map, five
pillars, six editorial tests, a restrained pencil and ink, its own opening question). Caplet answered "keep doing what
you're doing", which Keel reads as a full draft to the v2 map. If the draft follows Keel's tree where it differs, or
Caplet's doc moves on during Friday, the students get two reports and merge neither.
- *Early sign:* the draft's headings or order differ from v2; Caplet's doc changes during the day.
- *Guard:* snapshot the v2 map at hour 0 and give it to every writer as the outline. Each structure round checks the
  draft against it in a short table. Where Keel's choice differs (the opening question, "secures", C3), the draft uses
  Caplet's and the handover lists Keel's alternative with one reason. Read Caplet's doc again at hour 18; never edit it.

**11-20, in brief.**
- *11. Too long.* Caplet's map uses pages 2-12, Wharton's maximum. Guard: measure pages in the template from round 1
  (12-pt Times New Roman, double-spaced, tables included) and hold the draft to 11 pages, so the students have room.
- *12. Compression returns in rounds 10-12.* Guard: words per step and "we" per 100 words measured per part each sentence
  round; a part that falls more than a third below its round-9 figure is sent back.
- *13. A thin Team Reflection.* Guard: 3f written in round 1 by its own writer from the three settled moments; the
  Articulation reader in rounds 6-9 must say what the team thought before, what changed it and what it does now.
- *14. The lead drifts.* Guard: the lead never writes report sentences; a one-page decisions file per part (from
  NAME_ORDER's sixteen and SECTION_NOTES' traps) is re-read at the start of each phase.
- *15. Regression.* Guard: the regression log is part of the killer list; the blind compare runs every round.
- *16. A heavy handover.* Guard: one plain page on top (what the draft argues, what is still open, what to check first),
  then the claims-and-sources sheet and the cut list, on the portal and in the thread.
- *17. Pencil and ink overused.* Guard: one opening reference and an optional closing echo, as Caplet's doc says; a third
  use is a K4 failure. Never in tables or Laura's words.
- *18. A patchwork.* Guard: in rounds 10-12 one writer reads each section through and smooths the joins, using WORDS.md's
  terms.
- *19. Bland by consensus.* Guard: the paired measures (fewer problems counts only if the fresh reader still recovers the
  plan, the range and the lesson, and the reader test answers at least as many questions).
- *20. Seeded faults missed.* Guard: as planned, seeding in rounds 3 and 8; a miss puts the rotating reviewers on every
  round.

## 3. Token budget per round, and how many rounds fit

**Measured.** The rehearsal's eleven reviewers used about 1.1 million tokens on a 539-word text, 97,000-130,000 each,
mostly instructions and files read. The draft will be about 2,300-3,000 words (Keel's budget 575 for Section 2 and 1,650
for Section 3; Caplet's map up to page 12), so the draft itself adds little per reader. What adds most is the files a
reviewer opens: `NUMBERS.md` alone is about 84,500 words (over 100,000 tokens) and is re-sent on each step a reviewer
takes. Hence the numbers extract in guard 3.

**Estimated per review round** (rehearsal figures scaled; a writer reads Caplet's writing, its part of the fact sheet and
its part of the draft):

| Item | Tokens |
|---|---|
| Fresh reader (with calibration on the seventeen lines) | about 140,000 |
| Killer checker, split in two (K1, K2, K6 on the extract and CANON; K3-K5, K7-K10 on WORDS, D35, NAME_ORDER, CASE_MAP E) | about 500,000 |
| Reader test | about 130,000 |
| Blind compare (two versions) | about 90,000 |
| Core, every round | **about 860,000** |
| Rotating reviewers (three at about 150,000, alternate rounds; plus co-sponsor and Articulation readers in rounds 6-9) | about 230,000 a round on average, 330,000 in rounds 6-9 |
| Lead: consolidation, log, briefs | about 200,000 |
| Writers: two to four parts rewritten at about 150,000 each | about 450,000 |
| **One review round** | **about 1.7-1.9 million** |

Round 1 (seven to eight part writers plus the lead's set-up, then one core review) is about 2.4 million; the final
checks round (core plus every rotating reviewer, no rewrite) about 1.5 million; the handover pack about 0.3 million.

**Totals.**
- Plan 2d's full shape (round 1, eleven review rounds, final checks): about 2.4 + 11 x 1.85 + 1.5 + 0.3 = **about 25
  million**.
- Recommended shape (round 1, eight review rounds, final checks): about 2.4 + 8 x 1.85 + 1.5 + 0.3 = **about 19
  million**.

**What the week can carry (a rough proxy, not a measurement).** The repo records agents per run, not tokens. Last week
about 400-450 agents ran from Sun 15 Nov to the limit on Fri 20 Nov morning (`volume_runs.csv`); at the rehearsal's
100,000-150,000 per agent that is roughly 50-70 million. This week, Sun 22 to Wed 25 Nov, about 160 agents ran (the
rehearsal's 1.1 million included), roughly 20-25 million, and Thursday's two runs add perhaps 5 million. On that proxy
about 25-45 million remain for Friday. The full shape would use most or all of the low end; the recommended shape leaves
a margin of a third or more.

**Recommendation.**
1. **Nine rounds in all, then final checks:** round 1 (whole draft by hour 4), rounds 2-4 structure, 5-7 evidence and the
   co-sponsor text, 8-9 sentences, then final checks. The stop rule may end it earlier; nothing adds a round beyond nine.
2. **Soft cap 15 million after round 6:** above it, rotating reviewers run only when K1 or K2 fails, and the round 8 seed
   is dropped.
3. **Hard cap 21 million:** at that point stop rewriting, run final checks on the best version so far and build the
   handover.
4. **The lead logs agents and an estimated total after every round** in the round log, by its own clock and count.
5. **Thursday's runs** (Day 19) stay at the freeze, this pre-mortem and the numbers extract; no new research.
6. **Every version is pushed** when frozen, so a stop at any hour leaves a deliverable.

Wall clock is not the constraint: the rehearsal's review round took about seven minutes; at the report's length a round
with its rewrite should take 45-75 minutes, so nine rounds fit well inside the 20 hours plan 2d gives them.
