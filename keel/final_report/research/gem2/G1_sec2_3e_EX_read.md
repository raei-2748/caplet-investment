## SHOULD LAND HIGH, ranked

### 1. H7-081: prudent-investor law already judges the whole portfolio, counting her other resources
- **Part:** 2.3, one clause beside "Diversify Laura, not the portfolio". It can also answer EVALUATE S6 (why one issuer holds all ten payments) where 3e or 3a takes that question.
- **SKELETON note (22 words):** Prudent-investor law already judges 'Diversify Laura': each holding within the whole portfolio, counting her other resources and regularity of income (UPIA §2).
- **Why HIGH:** today 2.3's boldest line is Keel's own phrase, backed by finance papers. US trust law writes the same test into the duty of care for other people's money. Each investment is judged "in the context of the trust portfolio as a whole", weighing "other resources of the beneficiaries", "regularity of income" and "the possible effect of inflation or deflation". The duty to diversify gives way where "special circumstances" mean the purpose is better served without it. A judge learns that the plan's stance is the legal standard, not a team's invention. The "regularity of income" clause also names the ladder's job.
- **Evidence:** store H7-081 (harvest/H7_final_night_reading.md row 81); Uniform Prudent Investor Act §§ 2-3, as enacted in N.C. G.S. 36C-9-902/903; the official text with comments is card Q34 S9. Thesis 10; EVALUATE S6 and S10.
- **Guards:** use it as an analogy only, never as a claim that the plan complies with trust law, and never cast Laura as a trustee or beneficiary. Never "hedge" in Keel's own words and never "optimal". 2.3 is at its budget, so the clause replaces the second citation the density cut dropped (Leibowitz); it is not added on top.
- **REF:** yes (the UPIA is not in REFERENCES_APA; UPMIFA, row 322, is a different act).

### 2. Q1-022: Laura made the career switch that real households answer with fewer shares
- **Part:** 2.3, under "why about 7%?". If 2.3 is full, it goes in Book 17 rank 4 (3e) beside thesis 9 step 2.
- **SKELETON note (24 words):** Real households switching to volatile income cut risky assets, up to 35% in Sweden (Betermier et al. 2012); Laura made that switch (case l.17).
- **Why HIGH:** it answers "why about 7%?" with what people do, not with a model. Swedish households that moved into industries with volatile wages cut their share of risky assets by "up to 35%, or USD 15,575" (3,815 switchers among about 73,000 households, 1999-2002). Laura left a salaried technology product-manager career for a creative business (case l.17, p.1). A judge learns that Caplet's small share holding copies what people who made her exact move actually did. It is one clause more than thesis 9 step 2: Heaton and Lucas show that business owners hold fewer shares, and Betermier et al. show the switch itself.
- **Evidence:** store Q1-022; card Q1 row 22 and card 7 ("She made the Betermier switch"); Betermier, Jansson, Parlour & Walden (2012); Sveriges Riksbank WP 255. The 35% is the paper's estimate, not a plan figure, so it needs no NUMBERS entry.
- **Guards:** "fewer shares, not none" (card Q33). "Hedging" is the paper's word: quote it in the title only, and never call the plan a hedge. Sweden 1999-2002 is evidence about behaviour, not a forecast for Laura.
- **REF:** yes.
