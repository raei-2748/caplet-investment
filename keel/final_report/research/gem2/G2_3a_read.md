## SHOULD LAND HIGH, ranked

### 1. H7-011: zero is a rate waiting cash has really earned
- **Part:** 3a, in "why test at no interest". It answers the finalist's question the density row records ("the finalist asked why interest would ever earn nothing").
- **SKELETON note (25 words):** 3a, 'why test at no interest': one clause: waiting cash really earned nothing, the Fed's target 0-0.25% Dec 2008-Dec 2015 and Mar 2020-Mar 2022 (FRED).
- **Why HIGH:** today the reply to the finalist is a model ("a test, not a forecast"). This turns it into a dated fact: US policy cash earned next to nothing for seven years from December 2008, and again from March 2020. A judge learns that the bound is history, not an invented stress.
- **Evidence:** store H7-011 (harvest/H7_final_night_reading.md row 11); FRED DFEDTARU; Shiller ECON 252 (2011) L18 ("no one would lend at a negative interest rate"). **[V]** These dates are not in NUMBERS. ### 2. H7-001: the standard's two clauses are the textbook's two conditions for a riskless cash flow
- **Part:** 3a, next to the standard's sentence. Use 2.3 if 3a has no room.
- **SKELETON note (24 words):** 3a: Damodaran's riskless asset needs no default and no reinvestment; the standard's two clauses ('barring a US default', 'cash waiting earns nothing') answer each.
- **Why HIGH:** a finance judge sees that IPS l.11, as the report tests it, is the textbook's definition of a riskless cash flow and not a team invention. The definition says "riskless" belongs to a date, not an asset class. This is the strongest outside support the standard has, and it costs one clause.
- **Evidence:** store H7-001 (H6-004 is its duplicate); Damodaran (2008) *What is the riskfree rate?* p.6; Damodaran, Corporate Finance lecture packet 1 (Spring 2026) pp.108-109; Shiller ECON 252 L4.
- **Guards:** this is an outside check of l.11, never a second definition (CANON A4; never "high certainty defined" or "our definition"). Damodaran's instrument is a zero-coupon government bond, which the competition bans (STRIPS), so name his two conditions and never his instrument. It sits beside BOLD 10 step 2 (Campbell & Viceira, H3-016) without repeating it: thesis 10 is about which asset is safe for Laura; this row is about what the standard tests.

### 3. NUM-001: pricing at today's rates is itself a forecast, the forward curve's
- **Part:** 3a, at "a test, not a forecast".
- **SKELETON note (25 words):** 3a, beside 'a test, not a forecast': priced at today's rates the ten assume reinvestment at about 5.1%, the forwards' average (8a); zero assumes nothing.
- **Why HIGH:** the plan says zero is "a test, not a forecast" but never says what the alternative is. This row names it. Sized at today's prices alone, the ten need every waiting dollar to earn 5.12% a year, which is the average of the forward rates (4.67% for 2027-33, 5.43% for 2033-42). Doing nothing extra is a bet that the bond market's own forecast comes true. A judge learns the bold point in one number. It ties 3a to BOLD 3's "waiting is a bet on rates".
- **Evidence:** NUMBERS 8a, "Flat break-even" (6 Nov close, current); store NUM-001 (cards/NUM_extras_2026-11-09.md).
- **Guards:** use BOLD 1's frame, "sized at one assumed rate". Never "the ten as bought would have fallen short" (density row 3a, INTENT failure 7), and never "coupons" in prose. It is not 3a's headline number: the standard and the $38,282 stay the headline, so put it in one clause, or in Table 2's note if 3a is full.
