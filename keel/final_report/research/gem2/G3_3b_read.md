## SHOULD LAND HIGH (ranked)

**1. Q11b-025, part 3b (the three uses of her flexibility, listed once in 3b).**
- Row: Grantmakers in the Arts (Fall 2018): board-designated reserves "should be prerequisites for entering into a
  significant capital project"; campaign goals usually miss "the 'buckets of money' beyond project costs"; operations
  take "three to five years" to stabilise; even good plans "find it difficult to protect these funds from covering
  cost overruns, fundraising shortfalls"; raising reserves after opening "is often an exercise in futility".
- Proposed SKELETON note (20 words): "Arts funders warn that overruns and fundraising shortfalls drain operating reserves
  (GIA 2018); Laura's meet her flexibility, never the ten Treasuries."
- Why HIGH: a judge learns the field's own way of failing, and sees that two of the IPS's three uses (overruns, a
  co-sponsor falling short) are exactly the two raiders arts funders name, kept off the payments by IPS l.11. It judges
  the IPS's design against outside evidence instead of restating it, and it ties 3b to 3a and thesis 8 in one clause.
- Evidence: store Q11b-025; card Q11b row 25 (VERIFIED: fetched text, 9 Nov 2026); BOLD thesis 8; IPS l.11 and l.15.
- Guards: no "buffer", "cushion" or "contingency fund" (WORDS); no order of use; the article's "little more than half"
  post-recession capital-giving figure stays out (one data point, US foundations; CANON B4: co-sponsors here fall
  short through budget votes, not recessions). The "three to five years" clause may join 3a's Q9-016 line if words
  allow (ten owned years cover it twice); not counted here.
