## The eight that must land, now in SKELETON ("Book 17's amendments", second pass)

1. **3.0 and 3d, a correction (H9-119).** The plan said "ten years of operations owned ... so the facility is the only
   ask". The case says her $50,000 a year goes "toward" the residency's operating expenses and that other operating
   support may come from co-sponsors (case l.85-88), and 3d itself asks for yearly programme money. Say "ten years of
   her operating commitment", a base, not a budget; never "operations owned" or "the only ask".
2. **3a: zero is a rate cash really earned (H7-011).** The Fed's target was 0 to 0.25% from December 2008 to December
   2015 and from March 2020 to March 2022, so the test at no interest is history, not an invented stress. Dates to be
   entered in NUMBERS from FRED before use.
3. **3a: the standard meets a textbook's two conditions for a riskless cash flow (H7-001).** Damodaran: no default
   risk and no reinvestment risk; IPS l.11's "barring a US default" and the test at no interest answer each. An outside
   check of l.11, never a second definition; name his conditions, never his instrument (a zero-coupon bond).
4. **3a: pricing at today's rates is itself a forecast (NUM-001).** Sized at today's prices alone, the ten need every
   waiting dollar to earn about 5.1% a year, the forward rates' average (NUMBERS 8a). Doing nothing extra is a bet on
   the bond market's own forecast.
5. **3b: the field's own way of failing (Q11b-025).** Arts funders warn that overruns and fundraising shortfalls drain
   operating reserves (Grantmakers in the Arts, 2018); two of the IPS's three uses of her flexibility are exactly those
   two, and they meet her flexibility, never the ten Treasuries.
6. **3b or 3c: the real case behind "name a figure only once the money is a bond" (H8-113).** When Harvard's endowment
   fell about 30% in 2009, it slowed and then paused its Allston science building, citing the loss and the limits on
   borrowing that followed.
7. **2.3: prudent-investor law already judges "diversify Laura" (H7-081).** The Uniform Prudent Investor Act judges
   each holding within the whole portfolio, counting the beneficiary's other resources and the regularity of income.
   An analogy only: never a claim of compliance, never Laura as a trustee.
8. **2.3: Laura made the switch real households answer with fewer shares (Q1-022).** Swedish households that moved
   into industries with volatile wages cut their risky assets by up to 35% (Betermier, Jansson, Parlour and Walden,
   2012). Fewer shares, not none.
