## 1. The verbs

### 1.1 NESA's definitions, verbatim

Source: NSW Education Standards Authority, "Glossary of key words", HSC student guide,
https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/hsc/student-guide/glossary ("This page is managed by NESA"; read Sat
14 Nov 2026). NESA's notice of 4 April 2025 (NESA 11/25) updated two entries, Apply and Critically evaluate/analyse:
https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/glossary.

| Verb | NESA's definition (verbatim) | What it asks of a paragraph in this report (Keel's reading) |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | "Recognise and name." | Name a thing: a holding, an assumption, a risk. Wharton uses it once, for assumptions. |
| Describe | "Provide characteristics and features." | Say what the plan or the portfolio is. Mostly done by the IPS; in the report, done by the tables. |
| Explain | "Relate cause and effect." "Make the relationships between things evident." "Provide why and/or how." | Say why a choice was made, or how one thing drives another. |
| Analyse | "Identify components and the relationship between them." "Draw out and relate implications." | Take the plan apart (the payments, the floor, the shares, her flexibility), show how they move each other, and say what that means for Laura. |
| Evaluate | "Make a judgement based on criteria." "Determine the value of." | Name the yardstick, judge against it, and say what it cost and what the alternative would have given. |

Neighbours worth knowing, from the same page (verbatim): **Assess** "Make a judgement of value, quality, outcomes,
results or size." **Justify** "Support an argument or conclusion." **Recommend** "Provide reasons in favour."
**Critically evaluate** "add a degree or level of accuracy, knowledge and understanding, logic, questioning,
reflection and quality to evaluate." **Critically analyse** "use interpretation and reasoning to assess a range of
evidence and make judgements based on detailed analysis." **Recount** "Retell a series of events." **Summarise**
"Express, concisely, the relevant details."

NESA's own caution, same page: "It is crucial to avoid rigid interpretations of key words." And: "terms like
'evaluate' require distinct responses in Maths compared to History." So the verbs are a lens, as Caplet said, not a
rule.

### 1.3 What this means here (Keel's reading)

1. **Caplet's intuition is Wharton's own wording.** The report's purpose and Section 2 are "evaluate"; Section 3's
   bullets are "explain", "recommend", "provide" and "identify", but the mark is for "analysis, evidence, assumptions,
   judgment". In NESA's terms a recommendation is "provide reasons in favour" and the case asks to "justify" it, so a
   Section 3 recommendation without its reasons and its comparison is half done.
2. **Some description is required and is not repetition.** Table 1 ("present"), the recommended figures (new: the IPS
   never gave a reserve size, a contribution or a range), the co-sponsor passage ("provide language") and the list of
   assumptions ("identify") are all new. They should be stated once, plainly, and then judged.
3. **NESA's "evaluate" needs criteria.** Every judgement in the report should name its yardstick. The yardsticks on
   file:
   - **The IPS's own commitments**, never its adjectives: l.11 (no payment depends on a share price or anybody else's
     money, barring a US default), l.13 (not dependent on yields staying put; completed from the 2028 deposit), l.15
     (the bottom constant; half promised to no one; whatever figure she chooses is money she already owns), l.17 (two
     reasons only).
   - **The case's words:** "high degree of certainty", "responsibly", "meaningful", "credible", "preserving financial
     flexibility", "protect the portfolio's ability to fund the ten-year operating commitment".
   - **Professional standards:** a dedicated Treasury portfolio as the least-risk asset for fixed-dollar promises
     (Leibowitz 1986); the "full cash" refunding escrow test (MSRB); a founder's gift judged by her means and by coming
     first (BoardSource; Kresge); buy-and-hold against rules that trade as markets move (Perold and Sharpe 1988).
   - **Alternatives the register has already priced:** no margin and a 3-point margin (NUMBERS 2, 4); pooled designs
     (NUMBERS 2); all Treasuries (C8); more shares (8n); keeping 30% or 70% of the shares (8o); buying the floor in 2031
     (8c); the shares worth nothing (NUMBERS 4).
   - **Targets set before trading** (NUMBERS 8m) for Section 2.
4. **A paragraph that evaluates carries four things:** the judgement, the criterion, one measure or one comparison
   with a named alternative, and what it cost or where it breaks. A paragraph that analyses names the parts, how they
   move each other, and what that means for Laura. Under the density rule (SKELETON l.29: one headline number per
   part) the comparison is that number. Evaluation does not need more figures; it needs a yardstick beside the one
   figure.
5. **The evaluation shows the work.** Book 17 also says the judges look for effort. The alternatives Keel priced (seven
   sets, above) are the plainest evidence of it, and each one is an evaluation, not decoration.

1. **Evaluating the IPS's loose words.** The pitch's "immune to markets", l.13's "pay what they say", l.19's "ours is to
