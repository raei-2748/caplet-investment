## Problems, ranked

1. **Contradiction with the Trading Notes on the floor's payout.** Text: "a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, whose fixed payout is the bare minimum you'll be able to promise your co-sponsors without doubt" and "the bottom will remain constant, built on the ever-reliable Treasuries". The submitted TNA (Note 2, IBTM) says "the final payout is not fixed" and "their terminal payout is a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par". A fund is not a bond; the IPS calls it fixed and constant, the team's own note says it is not. This fails "maintains consistency with the team's IPS/trades" (descriptor: "Nothing contradicts the trades or TNA"; rubric A11, A12). Cost: the 2031 floor, the centre of the facility plan, rests on a claim the team already denied. Also the IPS says the 2028 money buys that fund, while TNA Note 2 has IBTM doing a payment job and a floor job; the roles differ (unsure how a judge will read it).

2. **Payout years and the 2033 decision are thin.** Text: "From 2033 the Treasuries are the operating reserve." That is the only sentence on 2033-2042. Missing: how the reserve changes as payments are made (the case asks "how its composition should change, if at all"), what rule sets the facility contribution in 2033, and what "rung gap to cover" means ("no price fluctuations for us to fear, only the rung gap to cover" is unexplained; TNA Note 1 explains it as reinvestment risk over the 10.5-month gap). Fails "disciplined planning across Laura's changing time horizons" (Q4, S2, S3 of part B/C). Descriptor 7-8: "one phase is thin (usually the payout years or 2031)".

3. **The thesis is muddled, and is not the one the pitch's logic needs.** Text: "your career is a large holding of shares, and one you cannot sell. And so, we believe, your portfolio should be a counterbalance to that" and the pitch "Laura's career is already a giant stock she can't sell. So we hedge it!" Treasuries are what protects the payments; they do not hedge a career, and the plan still holds shares ("only money you haven't promised yet goes into shares"), so it is not a counterbalance. The text also says "5 years to wait would usually own shares" (the case gives a payments start in 2033, six years from 2027, and the rest is unclear). The real reason, that promised money sits in Treasuries and only unpromised money takes market risk, appears late ("only money you haven't promised yet goes into shares") and the career idea competes with it. The TNA reasoned from duration matching and certainty, not career hedging. Fails "clear thesis" and "creative part is a name or image laid over it" (rubric A1, A11). Unsure whether judges will reward the career-as-stock idea as creative or penalise it as muddled.
