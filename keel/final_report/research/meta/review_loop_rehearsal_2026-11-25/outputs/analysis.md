# Portfolio Analysis review (Wharton criterion 3)

Started 06:44:23 Sydney, finished 06:44:46 Sydney.

**Score: 3 / 5.** The core design (match dated payments with dated Treasuries, keep unpromised money in shares, move half to Treasuries before 2031) is sound and the concepts are mostly used rather than named. It loses marks because the text states two properties of the iBond ETFs that the team's own Trading Notes say are false, and because "hedge" is misused.

## Problems, ranked

1. **The floor fund is called fixed and doubt-free, against the TNA.** Text: "Most of the 2028 deposit will fund a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032, whose fixed payout is the bare minimum you'll be able to promise your co-sponsors without doubt" and "the bottom will remain constant, built on the ever-reliable Treasuries". Trading Note 2 says "the final payout is not fixed" and "their terminal payout is a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par". A term-Treasury ETF pays its NAV at the end, after fees and reinvestment, so its payout is not fixed and the bottom is not constant. This is a wrong concept (fund treated as a bond) and a direct TNA contradiction, in the part that supports the co-sponsor range. Fails A11, A12, A10 and Wharton's "wrong concepts, certainty with no stated basis" band.

2. **The ladder is said to pay "what they say" although it may hold ETFs.** Text: "10 Treasuries (T-Bonds or iShares iBonds Term Treasury ETFs, whichever works best for each year)... Held to the end, these pay what they say – no price fluctuations for us to fear". Also the pitch's "immune to markets". Same TNA contradiction as problem 1: an iBond rung pays NAV, not par, and the TNA's own bond note names the reinvestment-rate risk. The one risk the text names ("only the rung gap to cover") is not explained, so the reader cannot tell what it is or what covers it. The text also never defines "high degree of certainty" (case asks teams to define it and say how it was evaluated; rubric C1), only asserts it. Fails A12, A11, C1, A2.

3. **"Hedge" is used wrongly, and the career-as-shares claim is not tested.** Text: "Laura's career is already a giant stock she can't sell. So we hedge it!" and "your portfolio should be a counterbalance". Holding Treasuries instead of shares avoids adding a correlated exposure (diversification); it does not hedge a stock-like exposure, which would need an offsetting short position. The claim that her income "is a large holding of shares" is also unquantified, and the same sentence says only that advances come "later and smaller", which is weaker than a stock. A judge who knows finance will mark the term as loose. Unsure on how hard a judge would bite, but it sits in the pitch. Fails A2 (applied, critiqued where it does not fit).

Minor, not ranked: "a Treasury default" as one of two reasons to change the plan has no stated response, and "5 years to wait" is unexplained (2027 to the 2031-33 decisions).
