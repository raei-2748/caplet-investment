## Would a judge still be thinking about it the next day?
Yes, but for one idea and one closing line, not for the plan. The idea is that a writer's income is a stock she cannot sell, so the portfolio must be the counterweight. That is a real reframing of the client, and it is why the plan is built the way it is. The ending is memorable and fits the letter. The middle is competent, and the case's central ask (a dollar range with a confidence level) is missing, so the judge remembers the voice and not the answer.

And so, we believe, your portfolio should be a counterbalance to that". Runner-up, the close: "You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that's your job. With these promises, ours is to take none."

## Problems, ranked
1. No numbers and no range, so the one thing the case asks for is absent. The text never states the dollar range, the confidence, or the size of the reserve, and "At current yields, this would cost roughly $300,000" is the only figure. "the bottom will remain constant" and "whatever figure you choose is money you already own" promise a range and then never give one. A judge cannot repeat the answer the next day.
2. The key reasoning sentence is tangled, and it carries the overclaim. "...which, since we decided to take your request one step further, also means that, barring a US default, none of your 10 operating payments will depend on a share price, or on anybody else's money, when they fall due." Four clauses sit between subject and verb, and "one step further" is never explained. "immune to markets" (pitch) is stronger than the letter's own "barring a US default" hedge.
3. A hole in the closing logic. "There are only 2 possible reasons we would change our plan – a missed deposit or a Treasury default." This contradicts "none of your 10 operating payments will depend on ... anybody else's money", because a missed deposit depends on Laura's own income. It also makes the hedged default look like a plan change, not a failure of everything. Elsewhere, "rung gap to cover" and "the ever-reliable Treasuries" are jargon and filler at the points where the argument needs plain steps.
