In January 2027 buy ten Treasuries (bonds or iBond ETFs) to pay the ten $50,000 payments; in 2028 put most of the second deposit into a Treasury fund maturing late 2032 (the floor of the building contribution) and the rest into a world share fund (VT), then move half of that share value into Treasuries before 2031, keeping the other half unpromised as Laura's flexibility.

## (2) What Laura can promise co-sponsors, and how sure
A band for the building: a bottom fixed by the late-2032 Treasury fund and a top that rises or falls with the world shares. The text says the bottom is "the bare minimum you'll be able to promise your co-sponsors without doubt" and that after the 2031 move "whatever figure you choose is money you already own". It gives no dollar figures, no probability, and no way to know the top. How sure: certain about the bottom and (after the move) whatever she names; no stated confidence for the top.

## (3) What the team believes about Laura
Her career income behaves like a big stock holding she cannot sell: advances, fees and licences arrive later and smaller in a recession, when shares fall and co-sponsor budgets shrink. So her portfolio should counterbalance, not add to, that risk, even though she is willing to take thoughtful risk. Also that she wants "high degree of certainty" taken a step further (nothing depending on share prices or anyone's money).

I then took "Most of the 2028 deposit will fund a Treasury fund" and the 2028 deposit also "completes" the ladder if yields fall: two uses for the 2028 money, with no sizes.

- "There are only 2 possible reasons we would change our plan – a missed deposit or a Treasury default." That sits oddly beside "barring a US default" and "Treasuries ... immune to markets"; and other reasons (a fund ending early, a cost rise, Laura's income) are not mentioned.

- "Markets move. Promises don't." and "You take risks on the page ... ours is to take none." are slogan-like closers; "take none" contradicts the shares in the plan.
- Tone: "worry not", "naturally", "Fair enough" read chatty.
