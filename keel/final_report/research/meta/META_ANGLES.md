# Meta angles: thirty ways to look at the Final Report from above

Research notes for the Final Report (Sun 8 Nov 2026). Ray asked for more angles like "why Wharton started this
competition, what they want from us, and what they reward the most", to build "meta-thinking" or "high dimension
thinking". Each angle below is a different place to stand when judging a piece of the report. The evidence for the
Wharton facts is in `WHY_WHARTON.md` (sources W1-W35); this file adds the angles and answers the best ones. T05, T07
and T14 are tabs 05 (co-sponsor brief), 07 (Laura Gao) and 14 (Winning Team Portrait) of the FINAL REPORT Google
Doc's Research section, saved in `keel/library/research_tabs_2026-11-07/doc_snapshot_2026-11-08/`; R1 and B7a are
named at the top of `WHY_WHARTON.md`. Every label is resolved, with its link, in `../SOURCES.md`.

Labels as in `WHY_WHARTON.md`: [Source] read at the page, [Inference] my reading, [Arithmetic] my calculation,
[MODEL] a model figure. Every quotation from Caplet's frozen texts is from `../../FINAL_IPS_as_submitted.txt` (FINAL)
or `../../../drafts/writing_room_2026-11-03/tna_as_submitted.txt` (TNA).

**How to use it.** On Fri 27 Nov each draft-review-rewrite round can stand in a different place: round 1 reads as
the fortieth report (angle 2), round 2 as the bond desk (angle 5), round 3 as a co-sponsor (angle 11), and so on.
The High angles are the ones most likely to move a score.

## Part 2. The best angles, answered as far as research allows

### Angle 2. The fortieth report

**What we know.**
- In 2024-25, "24 Wharton students reviewed competitor reports" from "1,818 teams" [Source W3]. [Arithmetic] About 76
  reports each if read once and shared evenly; about 150 if each report had two readers.
- Aberdeen's professionals also read at scale: "more than 1,400 investment strategy proposals" in 2022-23 (B7a
  section 3.2); in 2018 one Aberdeen manager and 13 colleagues read 160 reports for one region (B7a).
- 2025-26: about 2,300 final reports; the Top 50 was announced on 27 Jan 2026 [W12].
- Wharton's own guidance to teams: "You want your report to stand out from the hundreds we are reading"; "Make sure
  your report is readable" [Source W8].

**What I infer.**
- The first reader has a scoring sheet and a pile. They look first for the answer to each criterion and the case's
  required numbers, and they compare each report with the pile's norm.
- They will remember one idea per report, at most two. For Caplet that idea is already chosen: Laura's career is a
  large holding of shares she cannot sell, so the payments are bought with Treasuries first.
- The pile has a sameness (angle 3). A report that answers the case's questions in the case's words, in order, is
  easier to score well than a clever one the reader has to decode.

**What it changes for Caplet.**
1. The first page carries the whole plan: the one idea, the operating reserve's size and contents, the floor and
   ceiling of the facility contribution, the 2031 range and its confidence, and the two reasons the plan would change.
2. Section headings use the case's nouns (angle 13).
3. Every section opens with its answer in one sentence; the evidence follows.
4. Every chart carries a one-line title that states its finding.
5. Figures are the same everywhere they appear (one number sheet, checked on 27 Nov).

**Test to run on 27 Nov.** Give a reader who has not seen the plan the first page for 90 seconds. Ask for the central
idea, the reserve, and the range with its confidence. Any miss means the first page is not done.

**What would answer it better.** Wharton's 2026-27 Final Report instructions (Tue 10 Nov 01:00 Sydney) may give a
page limit or a required structure. No rubric or evaluator brief for the student readers was found.

### Angle 3. The modal report

**What we know.**
- 2025 finalists used "sentiment, SWOT and Pestel analyses, customized algorithms, AI innovations, portfolio
  optimizations, performance and readiness indices, and some clever house, tree and fashion metaphors" [Source W15b].
  The 2024 winner's efficient frontier and machine learning were noticed (T14).
- The 2025-26 participant badge still listed SWOT and Porter's Five Forces among the learning objectives [Source W7].
- A Wharton teacher blog advises teachers to "Chunk the final report" and notes students can reuse earlier SWOT work;
  it also says "Thesis isn't just for English class" and asks what the team's "guiding light" is [Source, Essential
  Educator blog, https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/essential-educator-blog/the-essential-educator-10-tips-for-teaching-the-wharton-investment-competition/,
  read 8 Nov 2026].
- About 6,300 teams registered in 2025-26 and about 2,300 submitted final reports (CLAUDE.md).
- No winning or typical written report is public (T14).

**What I infer: the likely shape of a typical 2026-27 report.**
1. A multi-asset allocation pie (bonds, shares, perhaps property funds or gold) with an efficient frontier.
2. A Monte Carlo simulation giving "a 95% chance of success" as the definition of high certainty.
3. Stock or sector picks with SWOT tables, a carry-over from earlier seasons' cases.
4. A 2031 range taken straight from simulation percentiles (for example the 10th to the 90th).
5. Inflation discussed as if the payments were inflation-linked, or TIPS proposed (banned this year).
6. A generic teamwork section ("we learned to communicate").

**Where Caplet is already unlike it.**
- Certainty by construction, not by simulation: each payment has its own Treasury (FINAL).
- The bottom of the range is money already owned; markets move only the top (FINAL).
- A reason from Laura's life, not from a risk questionnaire: her career is cyclical with the markets (FINAL).
- TNA note 1 already says nominal Treasuries match nominal payments, "meaning we don't need TIPS".

**Where Caplet is at risk of looking like it.**
- If the Final Report adds a long Monte Carlo section, an efficient frontier, or many charts, it slides back toward
  the pile. Simulation should appear only where the case needs it: the cost of the ladder under different yields,
  and what range Laura may be able to announce in 2031.
- A generic experience section would erase the difference everywhere else.

### Angle 4. What the case-writers built the numbers to force

**The arithmetic** [Arithmetic, my calculation; flat yield, annual compounding, valued 1 Jan 2027, payments 6 to 15
years away]:

| Flat yield | Cost today of the ten $50,000 payments |
|---|---|
| 3.5% | $350,118 |
| 4.0% | $333,328 |
| 4.5% | $317,478 |
| 5.0% | $302,509 |
| 5.5% | $288,365 |

- The ten payments cost exactly $300,000 at a flat yield of about 5.09%. Repo figures priced on real Treasury curves
  are about $292,000 to $303,000 (CLAUDE.md; MODEL, $302,163 on the 5 Nov 2026 curve).
- The two deposits total $450,000 against $500,000 of payments. Cash alone fails before any facility gift.

**What the case asks, in its own words** [Source W1]:
- "define what they consider a high degree of funding certainty, explain how they evaluated that level of certainty,
  and identify the assumptions supporting their recommendation."
- "If Laura promises more than she can ultimately contribute, she could damage her credibility" and also "A
  meaningful personal contribution may signal that the residency is financially viable".
- "Teams are not expected to determine the size or investment composition of a separate contingency fund or
  endowment."
- Teams should explain assumptions about "the effect of inflation on portfolio projections and facility costs", while
  "each payment is a fixed $50,000 and is not adjusted for inflation."

**What I infer about the design.**
1. **The first deposit was sized close to the price of the payments at 2026 yields.** Whether by intent or not, a
   team that buys the payments with Treasuries finds it can just about afford them. The ladder is close to given. It
   cannot be the main source of marks.
2. **The shortfall forces some growth.** $450,000 cannot pay $500,000 plus a facility gift without earning
   something. Treasuries bought in 2027 turn about $300,000 into the full $500,000 of payments, so the bonds, not the shares, close that gap; shares supply the gift's top.
   This lets the report explain that Caplet's plan is not timid: the Treasuries do real work.
3. **The warning is two-sided on purpose.** Overpromise and she loses credibility; promise too little and the gift
   fails as a signal. The case wants a bottom that is safe and not tiny. FINAL's FLOOR answers the first half; the
   report should show the floor's size answers the second.
4. **Inflation is a trap with two sides.** It does not touch the nominal payments (TNA note 1 says so), but it does
   touch building costs. The inflation discussion belongs on the facility side: what Taiwan building-cost growth and
   the Taiwan dollar do to what the gift buys.
5. **"Define certainty" is a test of judgement, not arithmetic.** Caplet's frozen definitions: "barring a US
   default, none of your 10 operating payments will depend on a share price, or on anybody else's money" (FINAL)
   and "a near-ironclad guarantee" (TNA note 1). The Final Report should say how this was evaluated: what risks are
   left (a US default, the iBond net asset value, the maturity gaps) and how large each is.
6. **No endowment is wanted.** The kept half of the shares should be described as flexibility, which the case names,
   not sized as a contingency fund, which the case says is not expected.

**What it changes.** Few words on proving the ladder works; most words on the four judgement calls (what certainty
means here, the range, the confidence, the fundraising text) and on inflation in building costs.

### Angle 6. Calibrated confidence

**What the case asks** [Source W1]: "Teams must recommend the dollar range Laura should communicate and state how
confident they are that her 2033 contribution will fall within that range. They should explain how favorable and
unfavorable market outcomes could affect the amount she can provide."

**A tested method that fits it almost word for word** [Source, IPCC AR5 Guidance Note on uncertainty,
https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf, read 8 Nov 2026]. Its rule for
a quantity: "A range can be given for a variable ... Assign likelihood or probability for that range when possible
... Explain the basis for the range given, noting factors that determine the outer bounds. State any assumptions made
and estimate the role of structural uncertainties. Report likelihood or probability for values or changes outside the
range, if appropriate." Its likelihood words: virtually certain 99-100%, very likely 90-100%, likely 66-100%, about as
likely as not 33-66%, unlikely 0-33%, very unlikely 0-10%, exceptionally unlikely 0-1%.

Two supporting points [Source]:
- IPCC AR6 Working Group I, chapter 1: readers tend "to systematically underestimate the probability of the IPCC's
  higher-likelihood conclusions" (https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-1). So put the number beside the
  word.
- Sherman Kent, "Words of Estimative Probability": "We promise to speak, not in discrete figures, but in ranges of
  figures and ranges of our uncertainty regarding them."
  (https://permanent.access.gpo.gov/lps19742/www.cia.gov/csi/books/shermankent/6words.html)

**How FINAL fits it** [Inference, my reading of FINAL; needs a model run on FINAL's design]:
- FINAL: "Before you meet co-sponsors in 2031, the other half will move into Treasuries, so whatever figure you choose
  is money you already own." So by the time Laura announces a range, both its bottom (the floor fund) and its top (the
  floor plus the moved half) are held in Treasuries.
- That splits the case's question into two honest numbers:
  1. **Before 2031**, what range will Laura be able to announce? This depends on VT between 2028 and 2031. It is
     genuinely uncertain and should be given as a modelled spread with its source and assumptions.
  2. **Once announced**, how confident is Caplet that the 2033 gift falls inside it? Because the range is then made
     of owned Treasuries, the honest answer is very high. The remaining risks are the net asset value of the
     Treasury funds at maturity and a US default, both small and both nameable.
