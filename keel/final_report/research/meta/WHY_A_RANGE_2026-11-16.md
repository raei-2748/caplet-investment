## Verdict in one screen

1. **The professor's "in substance one figure" holds after the move, more strongly than he put it.**
   - The contribution equals the stated top in all 200,000 simulated futures, all 1,770 US stock starts since 1871
     and all 585 rates-and-stocks starts since 1962 (NUMBERS 4, 8l (iii)).
   - The "$120 for each tenth of a percent" is a worst-case slope, not what happens (bond desk 4, H1). Centrally the top
     is missed only if the funds' costs beyond their fees pass about 3.1% of value with cash waiting earning nothing,
     or about 6.9% at the interest the market expects (bond desk 4 check, 3.09% and 6.86%; the same by Keel arithmetic
     on NUMBERS 3 and 4: $118,666 and $123,476 against $115,000).
   - The register's "6% of futures, at most $402, at 0.75%" (8l) needs two stresses stacked: costs of 0.75% and cash
     earning nothing. With the funds paying their modelled interest, costs of 0.75% or 1% miss the top in 0 of 200,000
     paths, 2% in 6, 3% in 469; the smallest cover in any path is 1.53% (bond desk 4 check).
   - What the cut actually has to carry is about 0.15% over five years (the ended funds' worst year beyond the fee,
     repeated; bond desk 4, M3), or 0.27% on NUMBERS 0's wider measure.
   - To bring the contribution down to the bottom, costs would have to take about a fifth of the funds' value: 19.9% at
     zero interest, 23.1% at expected interest (Keel arithmetic on NUMBERS 3 and 4; bond desk 4, M2: "about 20%").
   - Every other channel (a US default, a fund ending early or merging, the broker, Laura herself) hits both ends
     alike, because both sit in the same kind of late-2032 Treasury fund in one account (section 1b). Nothing after the
     move separates the two ends.
2. **It does not hold for the view from today, which is the view the report is written from.** From December 2026 the
   range Laura will be able to state is unknown: the bottom turns on rates to January 2028, the top on shares to the
   move (stated top at least $100,000 in 92.2% of simulated futures, at least $115,000 in 59.8%; NUMBERS 4). The
   contribution lands between $95,000 and $115,000 in only about half of simulated futures from today (51.2%, bond desk
   4 check, never printed; M1). So "certain" belongs to whatever range the rule gives in 2031, never to "$95,000 to
   $115,000".
3. **So the error is not the range. It is "count the bottom".** After the move there is no risk to pay for between the
   two ends, so telling co-sponsors to count $95,000 gives up $20,000 of counted credit (17.4% of the contribution,
   Keel arithmetic) and buys no safety. That is the professor's point, and the first funder reader made it on 11 Nov
   (section 3e).
4. **Recommendation: option (b).** Keep the range in the case's form (about $95,000 to $115,000 on today's figures, by
   the rule). Name the top as her facility contribution and the figure co-sponsors count. Name the bottom once as the
   part held since January 2028, counted from the Treasury fund bought that month alone. Both ends are owned when she
   speaks. The range records how long each part has been held; it is not a margin for error (coherence reader 7, Tue 17 Nov, LOW 1:
   never "secured" or "before shares had any say").
5. **The one sentence for 3c** (the gist; the 27 Nov writers re-voice it, no figures, no "certain" in it; reworded
   Tue 17 Nov in 3c's own "held since", coherence reader 7, Tue 17 Nov, LOW 1): "Both ends are Treasuries Laura already owns: the bottom is the part held since January 2028, the rest has been held since half the shares moved into Treasuries before the 2031 meetings, and she gives the top."
6. **Nothing the IPS froze changes.** Holdings, dates, the move, the half promised to no one, the rule and the stated
   figures are all unchanged; only which figure co-sponsors are told to count changes. (b) is the reading CANON D1, D12
   and D14 already give: the moved half is "the promised half", and the contribution is "floor plus the promised half"
   (section 4f).
7. **One test before 27 Nov:** a fresh funder reader on (b)'s notes, asked only "what figure goes in your board paper,
   and would you take the meeting?" Both 11 Nov funders read (a)'s framing (section 3e).

### 1c. The contribution after the move (the distribution)

- In every tested future and starting month: exactly the stated top (NUMBERS 4; 8l (iii)). There is no spread to
  describe; there is one point and its conditions.
- Inside the range at any cost up to about a fifth of the funds' value centrally (Keel arithmetic; bond desk 4 M2);
  the only place a 0.75% stress reaches the bottom is the 247-in-200,000 case where the range is already one figure
  (8l).
- Below the top only with costs past the rounding cushion (centrally about 3%) and cash earning nothing, or past about
  7% at expected interest; then by at most about $119 per further 0.1% (NUMBERS 0; bond desk 4 H1).
- What does still vary after the move is what the funds pay above the top ($8,476 centrally) and her flexibility's
  shares (half kept $21,347; 1 in 20 $9,881, median $20,739, 1 in 20 high $41,258; NUMBERS 4, 8o). All of it lands on
  Laura's side, none on what she has promised.

### 1d. Is the professor right?

- **After the move, yes.** His either-or ("either the bottom is the IPS's constant that carries the period before the
  move and the range narrows to its top at the move, or there is a real risk below the top that I could not find") has
  a clear answer: the first. No risk below the top is large enough, or separates the two ends, to justify telling
  co-sponsors to count $95,000. Bond desk 4 makes the case stronger than he did: the $120-per-0.1% slope only starts
  after a cushion of about 3% (zero interest) or about 7% (expected interest) is used up.
- **The plan's own reason for the bottom is wrong as written.** SKELETON 3c's "the bottom is the figure that holds
  whatever the funds' costs" is false (bond desk 4 M2: about 20% breaks it centrally; 0.75% where the range is one
  figure) and, as the desk says, gives the wrong reason for stating a bottom. What is true by construction: the bottom
  rests on the floor alone, owned since January 2028, and it would hold had the half moved in 2031 paid nothing (M2).
  That fact matters before the move (shares to zero: one figure, $95,000); after the move, with both parts in the same
  kind of fund, no channel makes the moved part pay nothing while the floor pays (1b).
- **Where he goes too far, if he does:** "one figure" does not make the range wrong. The case and Wharton ask for a
  range, the IPS defined a band with two ends, and the frozen TNA gives co-sponsors "a minimum figure in 2031". What
  must go is the instruction to count the lower one.

## 2. Why the case and the IPS still call for a range

### 2a. The words

- **The case.** "In 2031, however, the value of her portfolio in 2033 remains uncertain" (l.112-113). "If Laura
  promises more than she can ultimately contribute, she could damage her credibility" (l.114). "Rather than promising
  one exact amount, she wants to communicate a credible range for her potential contribution" (l.115-116). "Teams must
  recommend the dollar range ... and state how confident they are that her 2033 contribution will fall within that
  range ... how favorable and unfavorable market outcomes could affect the amount she can provide" (l.117-119). "A
  meaningful personal contribution may signal that the residency is financially viable" (l.110-111). "Laura must decide
  how much of the remaining portfolio she can responsibly contribute" (l.101); flexibility (l.103, l.145).
- **Wharton's instructions.** "Provide a credible dollar range that Laura could communicate to potential co-sponsors in
  2031"; "Explain the level of confidence associated with that range and the factors that could affect it"; "Provide
  language Laura could use" (l.113-115). A range is a required item; a judge with the checklist looks for it.
- **The IPS (frozen).** Pitch (l.3): a Treasury fund "will decide the floor of her facility contribution, and a world
  stock ETF will determine the ceiling". l.15: the floor's payout is "the bare minimum you'll be able to promise your
  co-sponsors without doubt"; "markets will be able to increase or decrease the top of your planned band as we get
  closer to 2031, but the bottom will remain constant"; "half of the value of the shares will be promised to no one";
  "the other half will move into Treasuries, so whatever figure you choose is money you already own".
- **The TNA (frozen).** "provides a floor for her facility contribution, giving co-sponsors a minimum figure in 2031"
  (l.13; CANON A24). "They only set how far her gift rises above her promised minimum ... a rise before 2031 lifts the
  top of the range co-sponsors hear" (l.21; CANON A29).
- **CANON's readings.** D1: the ceiling is "floor plus the promised half". D12: "the facility contribution is what is
  promised (floor to floor plus the promised half)". D14: "In 2031 Laura announces a range inside the owned band; any
  figure inside it is owned money."

**How the IPS's single chosen figure became a two-figure statement (the professor's question).** The band always had
two ends (l.15: a top that markets move until 2031, a bottom that stays constant). "Whatever figure you choose" lets
Laura pick any owned figure up to the top. The case asks for a range, so she states the band she owns and names her
choice inside it. By the IPS's own split, the half that moves is the promised half (l.15's other half is "promised to
no one"), so her choice is the top. One clause in 3c says this.

|---|---|---|

| The TNA's "minimum figure in 2031" and "her promised minimum" (l.13, l.21) | | Yes: frozen words; the bottom must still be said to co-sponsors in 2031 |

So the bottom's risk job is real only before the move; its record job, its frozen-word job and its form job are real in
2031. None of these is a reason to count it in place of the top.

### 2c. Where the case's uncertainty went (what the confidence is about)

- The case says the portfolio's 2033 value "remains uncertain" in 2031. It does: her flexibility is still shares to
  January 2033, and what the funds pay above the top still varies (1c).
- The design moves all of that uncertainty to Laura's side of the line. What she has promised has none left after the
  move, and before the move the uncertainty is which range she will be able to state (Table 2; NUMBERS 4).
- So the confidence answers the case's own sense of the word (card Q74 row 36: "how confident ... that her 2033
  contribution will fall within that range"): the event (the contribution, paid by the funds in mid-December 2032,
  inside the two figures she states and at the top), how sure (certain, after the move), because (both ends Treasuries
  held for her, counted with nothing earned while cash waits), and the one condition (section 4b).
- Neyman (card Q74 row 2; candidates rank 1): one worked-out interval holds with probability zero or one; Laura's is
  the rare one known to be one. The aha survives (b) and is stronger under it, because under (a) a sharp judge asks
  why co-sponsors should count less than a range known to hold at its top.

## 3. The options for 3c and 3d

### (a) Keep $95,000 to $115,000 with "count the bottom, intend the top" (the plan now; BOLD_THESES 11)

- **For.** Funders book the bottom of a bare range by habit (analysts put 73-77% of the weight on it, card Q35 row 18;
  US GAAP books a range's minimum when no point is better, Q71 row 21). The finalist and the LDI judge liked the 3d
  line ("how funders behave"; "something a person would say"). It cannot break even in the costs stress.
- **Against.**
  - After the move no event separates the ends (Q71 row 32), so the count buys no safety (1d).
  - Gneezy and Epley: a kept promise scores 9.88, an exceeded one 9.77; exceeding a promise earns d .06-.14, exceeding
    an expectation d 1.04 (Q25 rows 1-3). Under (a) co-sponsors book $95,000 at the ask, and the $20,000 delivered
    later buys about nothing. The professor's point stands.
  - The case's signal is the counted figure (l.110-111): $95,000 is 21.1% of the $450,000 she puts in, $115,000 is
    25.6% (Keel arithmetic). (a) signals the smaller one.
  - 3d's own ask is "capital pledged toward one total by one close date, her figure first". A total set against $95,000
    asks other co-sponsors for $20,000 she will in fact give: the campaign is mis-sized by the width (Keel inference).
  - IPS and CANON: the moved half is "the promised half" (D1, D12). Calling the top "intended" treats promised money as
    unpromised.
  - The conceit: "intends" is a pencil word on a figure that is ink after the move.
  - Beside "certain", "count $95,000" tells a judge the team does not believe its own word (finalist, smaller points).
- **IPS and TNA fit.** Consistent with l.15 and TNA l.13 and l.21, uneasy with D1 and D12.
- **A co-sponsor hears:** "$95,000, maybe more." The board paper carries $95,000 (the Taiwan funder did exactly this,
  because the plan labelled the top "intended").

### (b) The range, with the top as her contribution and the bottom as the part owned first (recommended)

- **What it says.** In 2031 Laura states her range, both ends owned; the top is her facility contribution and the figure
  to count; the bottom is the part held since January 2028, counted from the Treasury fund bought that month alone. The range
  records how long each part has been held (LOW 1).
- **For.**
  - True to the money: the contribution is the top in every tested future (NUMBERS 4).
  - Gneezy and Epley: promise what you will deliver, keep it, and get full credit at the ask (Q25 rows 1-4). The AHA
    register's own reserve already says "Laura states the top and anything above stays hers".
  - Q71 rows 21-22: once one figure inside a range is named as the better estimate, both US GAAP (ASC 450-20-30-1) and
    IFRS (IAS 37.39-40) book that figure, so a careful treasurer books $115,000, not $95,000 or $105,000.
  - Q71 rows 12 and 19 (the UK ASA, EFSA): a range should say where in it the reader falls and what lies behind it.
    (b) does both.
  - Card Q43 (Watts 2003): a lender whose claim is capped wants a verifiable lower bound. The stated top is already one:
    zero interest, after fees, less 0.5%, rounded down, against about $123,500 the funds pay at today's rates (NUMBERS
    4). Counting the bottom would put a floor under a floor.
  - Card Q43 (Antideficiency Act): promise only what is in the account. After the move $115,000 is in the account.
  - IPS l.15 read whole: the band (bottom constant, top moving until 2031) plus "whatever figure you choose", with the
    choice at the top because that half is the promised half (D1, D12, D14).
  - TNA: co-sponsors still get "a minimum figure in 2031" (l.13), and the contribution still "rises above her promised
    minimum" by what the shares set before the move (l.21).
  - The conceit: both ends are ink; each line has its own inking date (CONCEIT row 2: January 2028, then the move).
    This turn answers "why a range" without print-shop vocabulary, which two judges asked to drop.
  - It answers the finalist ("why $20,000 wide if she gives the top?"): the width is the half of the shares moved into
    Treasuries before she spoke, not doubt.
- **Against.**
  - Professionals put most weight on a range's bottom (Q35). Answer: the labels. Name the top as the figure to count;
    the better-estimate rule does the rest. Untested on a funder (4g).
  - The top can in principle be missed by a little, and a missed promise costs trust whatever its size (Gneezy and
    Epley's concert pilot, Q25 row 4; Teigen and Filkukova, Q71 row 5). Answer: it needs costs past about 3% of value
    with cash earning nothing, or about 7% at expected interest, against about 0.15-0.27% measured (1b). That is
    smaller than any risk the case asks Laura to carry.
  - A judge may still say "then it is one figure". Answer in 3c: yes, after the move, by design; that is what owning
    both ends before the meetings means, and the range is the case's form and the record of when each part was owned.
  - The case's "rather than promising one exact amount" (l.115). Answer: the case wants a range because the value is
    uncertain in 2031; the uncertainty is real and sits in her flexibility (2c), so the contribution needs no hedge,
    and the case's credibility test (l.114) is met because nothing promised is unowned.
- **IPS and TNA fit.** Full; nothing frozen moves (4f).
- **A co-sponsor hears:** "$115,000, all of it already in Treasuries: $95,000 since 2028, the rest since the shares
  moved in January 2031." The board paper carries $115,000.

### (c) The top alone as her contribution, the range shown only as history

- **For.** The plainest for a funder ("one firm figure I can book is better, every time", funder reader 11 Nov); Gneezy
  and Epley; card Q52 (if one figure, the bare figure, never "at least").
- **Against.** It drops Wharton's required item and the case's stated wish (l.115-116); it breaks the TNA's "giving
  co-sponsors a minimum figure in 2031" (l.13, frozen) and l.21's "the range co-sponsors hear"; it loses the trust a
  bounded statement adds (Howe et al. 2019: both bounds raised high trust by 7.9 points, Q71 row 17). Not worth the risk
  of a missing checklist item. Its strength (one countable figure) is carried into (b).

### (d) Other framings weighed
