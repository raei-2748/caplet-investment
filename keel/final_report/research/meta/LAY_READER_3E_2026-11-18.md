## 1. Each promise or limit in my own words

**The ten $50,000 payments.** A payment "breaks" if, on one of the ten January dates, Laura can't hand over the full
$50,000 on time. For that to happen, one of these would have to happen:
- the US government stops paying on its bonds (a default);
- the second deposit (the $150,000 in 2028) never arrives, or arrives far smaller than planned;
- the funds she holds turn out to cost more to run than the team set aside for (the 0.5%), *and* the cash sitting
  between dates earns almost nothing;
- the broker holding her account fails, which would make a payment late but, in the past, not lost.

A fall in share prices can't break them.

**Figure: about three-quarters.** At today's interest rates the 2028 deposit could arrive about three-quarters
smaller (about $39,000 instead of $150,000) and all ten payments would still be paid, even if the cash waiting
earns nothing.

*Unsure:* what "cash waiting" is. I guessed it's money that has come back from one bond and sits there until the
next payment date. I also guessed "owned" means "already bought, in Treasuries that will pay it". The 3.57-point
"fall in yields" was the line I understood least. I worked out, without being told, that lower interest rates make
bonds dearer to buy. The notes keep it out of the prose, which is the right call.

**A figure she has stated (the bottom, about $95,000, and the top, about $115,000).** This breaks if, after Laura has
told co-sponsors a figure, the Treasuries she holds for it pay out less than she said. Then her word is broken, not
just her hopes. For that to happen:
- the same US default;
- fund running costs beyond the fees, big enough to eat through the room left under the figure;
- the team forgetting a step: not parking the funds' early payouts in a short-term Treasury fund, or not moving half
  the shares into Treasuries before the 2031 meetings.

A missed 2028 deposit can't break it, because she would never have stated a range in the first place. A fall in
share prices before the move only lowers what she *can* state. It doesn't break anything.

**Figure: about 2%.** Those extra costs would have to pass about 2% of what the two funds pay, if cash waiting earns
nothing.

*Unsure:* this was the one I struggled with. "Beyond the two fees": which two? (8u mentions a 0.07% and a 0.15% fee,
so probably those.) "The two funds": which two? I took them to be the fund bought in 2028 for the bottom and the
Treasuries the shares move into for the top. I also couldn't tell how 2% relates to the 0.5% "margin" without 8u's
line that the 2% is "the 0.5% held back plus the rounding down" (from about $117,000 to $115,000). The SKELETON row
says both "larger than the margin under the figure" and "never called 'the margin'". To me that reads as a
contradiction.

**Her flexibility (about $28,700 on 1 Jan 2033; half the shares plus a little extra).** It's promised to no one, so
it can't break a promise. It can only run out. It runs out if what she uses it for comes to more than it holds:
- building costs in Taiwan running over budget;
- the Taiwan dollar getting stronger, so her US dollars buy less there;
- a co-sponsor paying less than expected.

It also shrinks if shares fall before 2033.

**Figure: about 25%.** It covers a rise of about 25% in the US-dollar cost of a building budget set at the $115,000
top, but the worst two-year spans since 1991 rose by more.

*Unsure:* "a budget set on the top" took a moment. I checked that 25% of $115,000 is about $28,700, the size of
her flexibility, and then it clicked. A lay reader in the final report won't do that sum, so the sentence has to.
