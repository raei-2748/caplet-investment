# Q126. When US states promised families future tuition and invested the money partly in shares, what broke in 2008-09, who paid, and which promises held?

- Claims tested:
  1. "State prepaid tuition plans are the institutional version of the plan Caplet rejected: fixed future payments, invested partly in shares; when shares fell in 2008-09 while tuition kept rising, several could not keep the promise as made."
  2. "Alabama's PACT cut what it paid and was sued."
  3. "Washington's GET and Illinois's College Illinois ran large deficits."
  4. "Many states closed their plans to new buyers."
  5. "The plans that kept their promises did so because of full faith and credit, matched assets or mostly bonds."
- Problem class and the field that solved it first: a fixed promise paid later from money invested now (asset-liability matching). Insurers' and actuaries' cash-flow matching came first; among prepaid plans, Florida's board ran a matched-bond book with shares capped at the size of its surplus from 2006, and Illinois, South Carolina and Alabama moved to matched bonds or Treasuries only after they had failed.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** The structure is the same: a fixed promise, a bill due years later, shares in between. Alabama held a 72% share target, against the first plan's 75% in 2027-2030 (NUMBERS 8s). Three differences matter:
  - The prepaid plans' bill was tuition, which rose in the recession that sank their shares. Washington's actuary modelled a correlation of -0.45. Laura's ten are fixed dollars (TNA l.7).
  - They had new buyers to charge premiums to, and states that paid in.
  - Their gap showed slowly, term by term. The first plan's was settled on one date, 1 Jan 2033, with no money coming in after January 2028 (NUMBERS 8s).
  So say "a fixed promise backed by shares, broken in the recession IPS l.9 names", not "the institutional version of our first plan".
- **Claim 2: KEEP, now sized at source.**
  - Alabama's PACT was 97.6% funded in Sep 2007, 67.2% in 2008 and 62.3% in 2009.
  - A settlement signed 4 May 2011 and upheld 7 May 2013 caps payments at Fall 2010 tuition. Any amount above that is "the exclusive responsibility of the PACT Contract purchaser or beneficiary".
  - It was sued in three ways: a parent's suit against the investment consultant, a federal class action and the state-court class action that ended in the settlement.
- **Claim 3: KEEP the deficits, but neither plan paid families short.**
  - Washington's GET fell from 109.5% to 84.2% funded (2008 to 2009). It was backed by the state's full faith and credit. When lawmakers cut tuition 5-20% in 2015, it held its payout value for two years and later added units.
  - Illinois was about 70.5% funded in 2011 (Keel arithmetic on the Auditor General's figures) and 69.6% in 2019. Its state paid in $280m (FY21-22). It then adopted a matched Treasury portfolio in 2024.
- **Claim 4: KEEP.** Of 22 states that once offered prepaid plans, 11 were still open to new buyers in 2018. Alabama closed in March 2009, Texas in 2003, Mississippi in 2012, Washington in 2015 (it has since reopened) and Illinois in 2017.
- **Claim 5: CHANGE.**
  - Full faith and credit kept the promise for families but moved the loss to taxpayers: Texas about $813.6m (Keel arithmetic), Kentucky and Washington's own guarantee.
  - Taxpayers also paid where there was no guarantee: Alabama $547.6m from its Education Trust Fund, Illinois $280m, Mississippi $35m in FY22.
  - Only Florida kept every promise through its assets. Its rule: bonds matched to the contracts, and shares limited to "the lesser of 15% of the total fund, or the actuarial reserve". In the crash its reserve fell from 12.9% of liabilities to about 4.9%, and no promise was cut.
  - "Mostly bonds" came after the failure, not before, at Alabama (100% short fixed income), South Carolina (100% duration-matched, 2015) and Illinois (about 79% Treasuries, 2024).
- **Judge's note (3a):** Florida's rule is IPS l.11's sentence in an institution's words. "Only money you haven't promised yet goes into shares" is "the lesser of 15% of the total fund, or the actuarial reserve". The one big prepaid plan built that way kept every promise through 2008-09 without a cut or a rescue.
- **Judge's note (3a, 3e):** A smart reader thinks prepaid plans broke because shares fell. That is half of it. Washington's actuary put a number on the other half: tuition rose when returns fell (2008: a 60/40 mix -23.1%, then 2009 tuition +13.1%). The bill grew in the same recession that shrank the money. Laura's $50,000 cannot grow, so that door is shut for the ten (TNA l.7).
- **Judge's note (3f, 3e):** Alabama's funded ratio went 102.3% (2011), then 56.4% (2012), then 110.1% (2013), and no market moved it. Courts approved, struck down and re-approved a cut to what was promised. The ratio was measuring the promise, not the money. A dollar promise owned in Treasuries cannot be redefined.
- **Judge's note (3f):** Alabama was short even at the top of the market (92.5% in 2006, 97.6% in 2007). A promise that needs growth to catch up starts the recession already behind.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 36 (3a):** Florida limited shares to "the lesser of 15% of the total fund, or the actuarial reserve". IPS l.11's rule appears in an institution's own words, and that plan kept every promise through 2008-09 without a rescue or a cut.
2. **Row 19 (3a, 3e):** Washington's actuary modelled tuition rising when returns fall (-0.45; a 60/40 mix -23.1% in 2008, then tuition +13.1%). A fixed promise priced in a real cost grows in the same recession that shrinks the money. Laura's ten are fixed dollars (TNA l.7). The facility's costs are not, which is a 3e limit.
3. **Row 31 (3a):** after a deficit and $280m of state money, Illinois adopted "a structured U.S. Treasury bond portfolio" to "match future expected benefits" (2024). Alabama and South Carolina ended in bonds too. The cure arrived 15 years late at the design Laura buys in January 2027.
4. **Row 15 (3e, 3f):** Alabama's funded ratio went 102.3%, then 56.4%, then 110.1% on court rulings alone. The promise was redefined three times and families paid the difference above Fall 2010 tuition. A dollar figure owned in Treasuries cannot be redefined.
5. **Row 45 with rows 5 and 20 (3f):** the outside example for the reflection. A share-led fixed promise was already short at the top (97.6% in 2007) and was settled by new buyers or a state. The first plan had neither, and its one date was 1 Jan 2033 (NUMBERS 8s). This is told as context only; the team's reason stays IPS l.9's (CANON A3).
