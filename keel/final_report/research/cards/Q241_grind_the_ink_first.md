## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** The text exists, but it is about thinking first, and its own words differ from the ones on file.
  - **It exists.** The afterword titled 王右軍《題衛夫人〈筆陣圖〉後》, in Zhang Yanyuan's Tang anthology 法書要錄 卷一, reads: 「夫欲書者，先乾研墨，凝神靜思，預想字形大小偃仰、平直振動，令筋脈相連，意在筆前，然後作字」. Huang Tingjian's Song copy of the passage is in the National Palace Museum, Taipei (故書000398).
  - **Its four characters are 意在筆前, not the 意在筆先 that CONCEIT V2.2 quotes.**
    - Taiwan's Ministry of Education dictionary uses 意在筆前 as the headword and lists 意在筆先 and 意存筆先 as later forms.
    - The 意在筆先 most often quoted is Zheng Banqiao's, from the Qing dynasty: 「意在筆先者，定則也；趣在法外者，化機也」.
  - **"Grind the ink" is not settled.**
    - 先乾研墨 is read three ways.
    - The only English translation read at source renders it "concentrate and meditate quietly while pretending to grind the ink" (Shi 2017, pp. 125-126, "Based on Gao Jianping's translation").
    - So the grinding is a pause for thought, and what the text asks for is a settled idea.
  - **Map test.** It maps only the order (prepare, then write), not the plan's substance (own, then state). In the conceit's own terms a settled idea is still pencil, not ink.
  - **Placement: store only. Never a 3.0 clause:** the clause would need a sentence explaining that the preparation is thinking, which every planner does.
  - **If Caplet keeps a title-page epigraph (Caplet's call since V2.2), use Chinese only.** Either:
    - 意在筆前, "traditionally attributed to Wang Xizhi"; or, better,
    - Sun Guoting's 意先筆後 from the 書譜 of 687 (a scroll in Taipei, whose author is not doubted).
  - **English only as Shi's, with the translator named.** No English wording may be invented.
  - **Judge's note:** the line the West quotes as "grind the ink before you write" is rendered by its translator as "pretending to grind the ink". The grinding was how the writer bought time to think.
- **Claim 2: KEEP.**
  - **The doubt is at least 1,300 years old.**
    - Sun Guoting (687) on the seven-line 筆陣圖: 「疑是右軍所製。雖則未詳真偽」 (some think Wang made it; whether it is genuine is unclear; Keel gloss).
    - On another text passed down under Wang's name: 「殊非右軍」 (not Wang at all; Keel gloss).
  - **Modern scholars agree.**
    - Usami Bunri (2010, as cited by 王俊鈞 2018) holds both the 筆陣圖 and the afterword to be later forgeries, though older than Zhang Yanyuan.
    - Theobald: "long been identified as apocryphal works under assumed names".
  - **Wording.** Say "traditionally attributed to Wang Xizhi". That is the English for 傳, the mark Taiwan's own museum and scholars print on a doubted attribution.
  - **The other side.** Taiwan's Ministry of Education dictionary names Wang plainly, so a Taiwanese reader will know it as his.
- **Claim 3: KILL as stated; CHANGE to two narrower true claims that do map the plan's timing.**
  - **"Took time that could not be skipped" is false.**
    - The Smithsonian wants an inkstone "abrasive enough for grinding the ink quickly".
    - Practitioners grind for 20 seconds (a test strength) up to about an hour (a practice session).
    - Ready-made ink has existed since 1950 (paste) and 1961 (liquid) in Japan.
  - **"Visible before any writing" is half true.** The ground ink fills the inkstone's well before the first stroke, but only the writer sees it.
  - **What holds, narrower claim 1: grind enough for the whole work before the first stroke.** "Better to have too much Ink than to run out mid-character!" This maps the ten payments bought in full before any is due, with the extra Treasuries and the 0.5% margin.
  - **What holds, narrower claim 2: the strength of the line is set at the stone** (the Met; the Smithsonian). This maps the top's size, settled at the January 2031 move before the meetings.
  - **Use.** Facts behind an existing working word (2.2 or 3c), never a new turn.
  - **Judge's note:** how long a calligrapher grinds tells you how much they mean to write. Laura's purchases are sized to the ten payments, not to a forecast.
- **Claim 4 (Keel's own): KEEP, with changes.**
  - **What the curriculum says.** Taiwan's national curriculum for Chinese (NAER 2018) lists Ab-Ⅱ-11 「筆墨紙硯的使用方法」 (how to use brush, ink, paper and inkstone) for learning stage 2, grades 3-4. That comes after hard-pen writing, which stays the main tool (「硬筆為主，毛筆為輔」).
  - **Taiwan's own inkstone.** Changhua County registered the 螺溪 inkstone as a traditional craft in 2015 and ran a transmission plan in 2025.
  - **Not shown.** Whether pupils grind sticks or use bottled ink was not found.
  - **Use.** The conceit's image is ordinary to a reader educated in Taiwan. Laura's words to co-sponsors (3d) stay plain anyway.
- **New finds beyond the four claims:**
  - **Two schools of calligraphy.** The tradition sets "idea first" (意在筆先) against "deciding as circumstances change" (臨陣決機). Laura's promises sit wholly in the first and her flexibility in the second (row 10).
  - **Counting rods.** The same text says strokes "alike, like counting rods" are "not calligraphy" (Keel gloss). In her promises, ten identical rungs are the point (row 15).
  - **Ink is meant to be used up.** The most famous ink in Taipei survives because it was never ground (rows 24-26).

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 10 (two schools: idea first against deciding as circumstances change), for 3b or 2.0.**
   - Calligraphy's own split maps the plan's split exactly.
   - The promises are decided once, in advance (IPS l.17), and her flexibility is where she decides later.
   - It is a strikingly dissimilar parallel with a sourced English wording (Shi 2017).
2. **Row 15 (counting rods), for 3a.**
   - In calligraphy, ten identical strokes are "not calligraphy"; in her promises, ten identical rungs are the point (IPS l.19).
   - It is the sharpest contrast this card found.
3. **Rows 2, 3 and 13 (the epigraph fixed), for the title page.**
   - The text's words are 意在筆前, and 意在筆先 is the later form.
   - Q47's citation is wrong.
   - Sun Guoting's 意先筆後 (687, in Taipei) gives the same order without the doubted attribution.
   - This prevents an error a Chinese-reading judge would catch.
4. **Rows 24 and 26 (ink is meant to be used up; Taipei's ink that was never ground), for 3a.**
   - A true, Taiwan-held fact behind "never an endowment": the reserve exists to be spent rung by rung to January 2042.
5. **Rows 22 and 23 (grind enough for the whole work; the strength is set at the stone), for 2.2 and 3c.**
   - They are claim 3's true core.
   - They map the order of purchase with its margin, and the top settled at the January 2031 move, as facts behind existing words.
