- **Problem class:** a promise made by an office-holder whose successor, and whose budget, are decided after the promise and before the money moves. **Field that solved it first:** political science and public budgeting (a government cannot bind the next one; only money already voted or already held is safe from the change). Taiwan's constitution, election laws and Budget Act set the dates; the Constitutional Court's Interpretations 387 and 419 set what a change of government does to the cabinet.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP the substance, CHANGE the words.** The law fixes windows, not "January".
  - **National.** Legislators are elected "於每屆任滿前三個月內" (in the three months before the term ends) and the vote must be done ten days before it ends. For the legislature sitting 1 Feb 2028 to 31 Jan 2032 the window is 1 Nov 2031 to about 21 Jan 2032. The presidential vote must be done 30 days before the term ends on 19 May 2032, so by about 19 Apr 2032 (Keel arithmetic on the Additional Articles, Arts. 2 and 4, and the two election acts).
  - **Practice.** One combined January vote every time since 2012 (14 Jan 2012, 16 Jan 2016, 11 Jan 2020, 13 Jan 2024). Before that, legislators were elected in December and presidents in March. The Central Election Commission fixed the 2020 and 2024 dates in March of the year before, so the 2032 date will probably be set around March 2031, when Laura meets co-sponsors.
  - **Local.** The Local Government Act itself names 25 Dec 2030 as the day the next councillors take office. Votes must be done ten days before (by about 15 Dec 2030). Recent local votes were in late November (29 Nov 2014, 26 Nov 2022, 28 Nov 2026). So the mayors and councils Laura meets in 2031 will be months into new terms.
  - **What a pledge crosses (Keel arithmetic).** A central pledge made in 2031 crosses the national vote, the new legislature (1 Feb 2032), the cabinet's resignation, the new presidential term (20 May 2032) and the new government's first budget, all before Laura pays (about 18 Dec 2032, Q30 row 31). A city or county pledge crosses no local election: councils elected in late 2030 sit until 25 Dec 2034.
  - **Judge's note:** on every precedent since the Ministry of Culture opened in 2012, the minister who hears Laura's range in 2031 will have left office by 20 May 2032, seven months before she pays. All five changes of minister came on an inauguration day (2016, 2020, 2024) or within ten weeks after local elections (2014, 2022).
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** After each change, money for local culture building moved to a new channel within months, but in different directions. Rules also change without elections.
  - **2008.** Two days after the 20 May inauguration, the cabinet passed a local construction plan. It was voted in July and promulgated 1 Aug 2008: NT$58.3bn for local projects, NT$4,338m of it the culture council's "補助地方文化建設" (grants for local culture construction), 94.04% spent. Each county council then had to pass its own advance-payment motion (22 of 25 by 8 Sep 2008). By the end of September only 8.48% had been spent.
  - **2016.** The cabinet dates its forward-looking infrastructure work from "去（105）年5月20日後" (after 20 May 2016). Its special act was promulgated 7 Jul 2017, about 14 months after the inauguration. It kept the ordinary local rules, and cities spend its money only after their councils vote.
  - **2024.** A legislature in which "no party has an absolute majority", the first in 16 years, changed the revenue-sharing law on 20 Dec 2024, the first change since 1999. The Finance Ministry's first estimate moved NT$375.3bn more to local governments. Then the cabinet cut FY2025 general grants to cities by NT$63.6bn after the budget vote. The law changed again in 2025.
  - **Rules on their own clock.** Of the art-village grant's 13 versions (Q10d row 7), only 3 fell within a year after an inauguration, about what chance gives (Keel arithmetic).
  - **Neutral words for the report:** "after each change of government the channel for public building money moved"; never which party cut or added.
  - **Judge's note:** an election is not the thing to watch for rule changes. The Ministry's grant rules changed about every 16 months whoever governed, while the money's channel moved after every change of government.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE.** "Late 2032 at the earliest" is the law's date: Budget Act Art. 51 asks for a vote by 30 Nov 2032 (Q49 row 9). On the record, the vote comes later.
  - **Who votes it.** The 2033 central budget will be the first budget of the legislature elected in January 2032, drafted by a cabinet formed on 20 May 2032. Ministries' estimates are due to the cabinet in late May (Q49 row 1), days after that cabinet takes office.
  - **When.** The first budgets of the last three newly elected legislatures were all passed in January, after the year had begun (19 Jan 2017, 29 Jan 2021, 21 Jan 2025; Keel grouping of Q49 row 10's dates). On every precedent, public 2033 money is passed in January 2033, after the floor fund pays (about 15 Dec 2032, CANON C8) and after Laura pays.
  - **What an election does to a budget.** It delays public money; it does not erase it. A budget bill does not lapse at a legislature's end (立法院職權行使法 Art. 13), but a law does: revenue-sharing bills died at term end five times. A cabinet change can pull a budget already sent (17 Sep 2009, re-sent a week later).
  - **Judge's note:** the money a public co-sponsor promises for 2033 will be voted on by a legislature that does not exist yet when Laura states her range.
- **Claim 4: KEEP.**
  - **A watch, not a reason.** It is a Table 3 watch or one clause in 3e area (3), never a reason to change; the IPS's only two reasons are a missed deposit and a Treasury default (FINAL l.17). A shortfall from an election-year delay is the "co-sponsor falling short" that her flexibility already covers (FINAL l.15).
  - **Her figure.** Laura's stated figure is owned in Treasuries and crosses the election unchanged. No clash with the IPS or TNA.

## Best five for the report

1. **Rows 12-13 (every change of culture minister since 2012 came on an inauguration day or within ten weeks after a local election).** 3d. The official who hears Laura's range in 2031 is, on every precedent, gone before she pays. A judge sees why a pledge is weaker than money she owns, without any adjective.
2. **Rows 6, 16-17 (the 2033 money is the first budget of a legislature not yet elected; such budgets passed in January 3 of 3).** 3e area (3) and Table 3. It answers the brief's "voted in late 2032 at the earliest" with a date and a reason: January 2033, after Laura pays.
3. **Rows 19-20 (budgets survive a change of legislature; laws die).** 3e. The risk an election brings is delay, not erasure. The co-sponsor test that follows: is the money in a budget bill, or does it need a new law?
4. **Rows 22-24 and 26 (after the 2008 and 2016 changes, new money for local buildings still waited for each council's vote).** 3d and 3b. Even a generous new government's money needs two more votes before it is spent. Laura's money needs none. It is a strikingly dissimilar parallel that the conceit pages (CONCEIT V2.2) can weigh.
5. **Row 31 with row 28 (grant rules change about every 16 months whoever governs, while the revenue-sharing law changed at least three times in a year).** 3e. A smart reader would watch the election. The record says to watch the Ministry's notices and the revenue law instead.
