# Q102. What will a fixed $50,000 a year (January 2033 to January 2042, "not adjusted for inflation", case l.90) buy in Taiwan, counted in people's time rather than in prices?

- **Date:** Tue 17 Nov 2026, Sydney (every source read Tue 17 Nov 2026; data run to September 2026 for pay and December 2025 for full-year prices).
- **Report parts:** 3.0 (the payments as "a base for running the residency, not its whole budget"), 3e area (4) (inflation "decides what they and the contribution buy, never whether they are paid"), 3a (the ten-year commitment: what each payment buys), Table 3 (a candidate watch line: each September's minimum-wage decision). Most rows are for the store only.
- Notes for the Final Report store, not report prose. The case puts the facility's cost and the residency's budget out of scope: every staff-month figure below is a yardstick for what a dollar buys, never a budget, a forecast or a plan figure.
- **Not repeated:** NUMBERS 8d (US frame: at 2.4% the 2042 payment buys about 70% of what $50,000 buys today) and 8j (the "10% stronger" convention, 32.768/1.10 = 29.79); card Q10 rows 13, 15-17 and 23 (US and Taiwan price frames; NT$1.64m; 77% by 2042 at 1.8% Taiwan inflation; Taiwan CPI 1.47% a year over ten years); card Q9c rows 2 (NT$800 a day resident allowance) and 17 (Treasure Hill's 19 staff, NT$13.878m); card Q10c (building costs led by construction wages); store H7-088 (with H1-019 merged: "Inflation can make $50,000 buy less. It cannot make $50,000 cost more", Baumol named but not sized), H3-112, H4-175, H9-119 (a base, not a whole budget; US residency budgets), Q9-035, Q10d-018, Q49-036. The store held no row on Taiwan's minimum wage, arts-sector pay or the rent index (`grep -ci "minimum wage"` on insights.jsonl: 0).
- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Inflation decides what the payments buy, never whether they are paid." (keep; size it)
  2. "Wages, not goods prices, drive a residency's running costs" (Baumol's cost disease; store H1-019 / H7-088).
  3. "By 2042 the tenth payment buys about X% fewer staff-months than the first."
  4. "The register's US frame (2.4%, about 70% of today's buying power by 2042) understates what a wage-heavy Taiwan residency loses, because it ignores Baumol."
- **Problem class:** the real value of a fixed nominal stream spent on labour-heavy services. **Field that solved it first:** cultural economics (Baumol and Bowen's 1966 study of the performing arts; Baumol 1967), with labour economics on wage floors (Taiwan's Minimum Wage Act and its committee) and nonprofit cost-structure studies (Form 990 data, arts-agency salary surveys).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Yardstick (Keel arithmetic).** $50,000 is NT$1,638,400 at 32.768, NT$1,489,455 if the Taiwan dollar is 10% stronger (29.79). A "staff-month" is one full-time month of pay (about 173 hours in Taiwan's arts sector, 2025).
  - Today one payment buys about **55.5 staff-months at the 2026 minimum wage** (NT$29,500), 53.0 at 2027's (NT$30,900), 49.9 at the arts sector's median regular pay (NT$32,810, 2025), **40.3 at its average** (NT$40,634) and **32.5 at its full employer cost** (NT$50,355 a month incl. bonuses, insurance and pension). With a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar: 50.5, 48.2, 45.4, 36.7 and 29.6.
- **Claim 1: KEEP, now sized.** The ten payments are fixed in dollars and owned in Treasuries (IPS l.11, l.13), so none can be missed for inflation; what moves is what each buys.
  - In arts-pay terms the first payment (2033) buys about 34.7 staff-months and the tenth (2042) about 29.4. At the minimum wage, 42.0 and 29.6 (Keel arithmetic on the paths below). The dollars are certain; the staff-months are not.
  - Wording guard: TNA reflection 1's "nonexistent inflation risk" is about funding, and stays true there; the report keeps "for the payments' funding" attached.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** Wages are about a third to two-fifths of a small residency's running costs, not the whole of them. In Taiwan the pay that runs fastest is the legal minimum, not arts pay.
  - **Share.** Millay Colony's "other salaries and wages" were 30.7% and 37.3% of expenses in two filings (ProPublica); salaries and benefits 38.3% in 2023 (990finder, secondary). US local arts agencies: "one third ... (35 percent)" (AFTA FY2001). Treasure Hill: staff 31% of the unit's revenue plus outsourced cleaning (card Q9c row 17).
  - **Pace.** Taiwan's arts average regular pay rose 1.88% a year 2015-2025 against consumer prices 1.49%: only about 0.4 points a year faster. It lagged the whole economy (2.24% a year, 2016-2025) and even fell 2.7% in 2021 (DGBAS Yearbook 2025 Table 19; Keel arithmetic).
  - **The floor.** The monthly minimum rose 3.96% a year 2016-2026, eleven rises in a row, +54.4% from 2016 to 2027. By law its rise is set from the CPI (Minimum Wage Act Art. 9), and in 2026 every committee member agreed the CPI "應全數反映" (should be reflected in full). The bottom tenth of all pay in 2025 was exactly the minimum (D1 NT$28,590).
  - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan, Baumol's disease shows up at the bottom of the pay scale, set by statute each September, not in artists' pay: arts pay barely beat prices for a decade, while the legal floor beat them by about two points a year.
- **Claim 3: X is about 15% (arts pay) to 30% (minimum wage); 12.5% at the pace of prices (Keel arithmetic).**
  - Minimum-wage path (2027 known, then 3.96% a year): NT$39,000 in 2033, NT$55,300 in 2042; 42.0 then 29.6 staff-months: **the tenth payment buys 29.5% fewer than the first**, and 47% fewer than a payment would today.
  - Arts-pay path (1.88% a year from 2025): NT$47,150 in 2033, NT$55,700 in 2042; 34.7 then 29.4: **15.4% fewer**; 27% fewer than today.
  - Prices path (1.49%): 12.5% fewer. Baumol and Bowen's old 2-to-1 ratio of arts costs to prices, applied to Taiwan's CPI (about 3% a year): 23% fewer.
  - A stronger Taiwan dollar cuts every year's figure by the same 9.1% if it holds steady, so it lowers what each payment buys but not the first-to-tenth gap. A currency that keeps strengthening through the 2030s would widen the gap.
  - The two wage paths meet near 29-30 staff-months in 2042 because the floor overtakes the arts average there: a sign the straight-line paths stop being consistent by then. Use the band (about 29 to 42 staff-months per payment across 2033-2042), never a single figure.
  - **Judge's note:** a payment fixed in dollars loses its people faster than its prices: a sixth to three-tenths of its staff-months between the first and tenth payments, against an eighth of its goods.
- **Claim 4: KILL.** A residency whose costs are a third staff at the minimum-wage pace and two-thirds at the CPI pace keeps 67%-69% of today's buying power by 2042, and 77% if staff follow arts pay. The register's US frame at 2.4% gives about 70% (NUMBERS 8d; 68.4% from end-2026 by Keel arithmetic). So the report's figure already covers a wage-heavy Taiwan budget; no second figure is needed (Keel arithmetic, steady exchange rate).
  - **Judge's note:** the cautious US-price frame and a Baumol-adjusted Taiwan wage frame land in the same place, so the one figure the report prints holds from both sides.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 26 (3e area (4), with NUMBERS 8d).** A wage-heavy Taiwan residency and the register's US frame both keep about 70% of today's buying power by 2042, so the one printed figure holds from both sides. Aha: the cautious figure already covers Baumol.
2. **Rows 6-9 (3e area (4); Table 3 candidate).** Taiwan's minimum wage is indexed to the CPI by statute (Art. 9). The committee agreed in 2026 that the CPI "should be reflected in full". The new rate takes effect each 1 January, the day a payment lands. Aha: the residency's lowest costs are inflation-linked by law, while Laura's payments are fixed by design. The September decision is a free three-month warning of what the next payment buys.
3. **Rows 22-25 (3a, 3e).** Answers claim 3 with a band: the tenth payment buys about a sixth (arts pay) to three-tenths (minimum wage) fewer staff-months than the first, against an eighth fewer goods.
4. **Rows 14 and 11 (3.0, 3e).** The bottom tenth of Taiwan's pay is the minimum wage itself, and the arts median has fallen from 1.27 to 1.15 times the minimum since 2020. Aha: by the first payment the legal floor may set the pay pace for half the arts workforce.
5. **Rows 30-31 with 32-35 (3.0, 3d's co-sponsor language).** Staff are a third to two-fifths of a small residency's costs. History shows a fixed budget shrinks quietly (fewer residents, smaller casts, fewer programmes), never by a missed payment. That is why the payments are "a base for running the residency, not its whole budget".
