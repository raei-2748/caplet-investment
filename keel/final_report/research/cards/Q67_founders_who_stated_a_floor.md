- **Problem class:** a first mover's credible commitment under uncertainty: how a lead party states what it will put in so that others act on it, and what makes the statement believed (signalling and commitment in fundraising). **Field that solved it first:** American institution-building philanthropy of the 1880s-1910s (Rockefeller's conditional pledges, Carnegie's library formula), later written into university gift-counting rules (CASE standards as applied in gift acceptance policies) and tested in public inquiries (London's Garden Bridge, 2016-2019).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KILL as stated.** In about 17 more founder, lead-donor and co-funder cases read today, none states its own share as a range with a likelihood (Q18 row 39 holds). Six forms appear instead:
  - **An exact sum, conditional and paid from future income.** Rockefeller, 1889: "$600,000 ... providing four hundred thousand dollars ($400,000) more is pledged", paid over five years, pro rata, "otherwise this pledge to be null and void" (rows 1-7).
  - **An exact sum in a signed contract.** 麗明營造, Taichung, 2025: "承諾投入2億元" (row 35).
  - **"Up to", a capped match.** Broad and MOCA, 2008: "match up to $15 million raised and received by MOCA". By 2012, $8.75m "remains untapped" (rows 36-37). Also Droom en Daad at Boijmans, "up to 80 million euros" (row 41).
  - **A budget that turned into a floor.** Hong Kong Jockey Club at Tai Kwun: "the project budget of HK$1.8 billion" (2010), then HK$3.8bn (2018) (rows 16-18).
  - **A "first phase" with more to follow.** 河仁 at Fuyao University: "首期捐资100亿元". Its basis was shares ("按2亿股福耀玻璃股票对应100亿元出资"), paid as building went on (rows 31-33).
  - **A share of wealth, not dollars.** Li Ka-shing, "one-third of his assets" (row 26); 曹德旺, 60% of his Fuyao shares, cut to 14.98% (row 29).
  - So Laura's statement is new in form: a bottom and a top, both owned in Treasuries before she speaks (NUMBERS 4, range rule: "No figure is stated before the move"). Among the founders read, it has no precedent.
  - **Judge's note:** founders state one figure because one figure is what they own or can earn. Laura states two because by 2031 she owns both.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** A founder's figure became a ceiling only when the founder had no more to give. Where the founder had an income stream, the figure became a floor:
  - HKJC's HK$1.8bn became HK$3.8bn plus HK$0.6bn for programmes and running costs. Hong Kong's Chief Executive said: "I hope nobody will label this as a cost overrun!" (2.1 times; Keel arithmetic).
  - 河仁's 100億 is still being paid: about 43億 by 31 Aug 2025, with "未来 ... 还会付满100亿元".
  - Perelman's $75m stayed fixed while the building's cost rose to $500m, "more than twice what was projected in 2016". Others filled the gap: Bloomberg gave $130m.
  - **Laura's top is a hard cap by rule.** The 2033 contribution is "the lower of what the two funds pay and the stated top", and it equals the stated top in every modelled path (NUMBERS 4). A building overrun is met from the half promised to no one, which the IPS keeps "for building costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar or a co-sponsor falling short".
- **Claim 3: KEEP, with the price named.**
  - **曹德旺.** He announced 60% of his Fuyao shares in 2007 and gave 14.98% in 2011. The press: "和2007年曹德旺最初所宣称的60%有很大缩减". His reason was that regulators wanted him to stay the controlling shareholder (row 29).
  - **曹興誠.** He said he would give NT$5,000萬 of an antique's sale to Cloud Gate, then that the money "不會捐給雲門了". The headline: "捐雲門5000萬？曹興誠：不是我" (row 34; UNVERIFIED at the original).
  - **Carnegie in Canada.** Bertram cut a $40,000 grant by $10,000 after a census, and "everything collapsed" (row 12).
  - **Garden Bridge.** "£50 million of public funds ... produced no demonstrable public benefit ... risks undermining public trust" (row 25).
  - **In policy.** A pledge not completed loses its acknowledgement (William Paterson), and forgiving a pledge "could be interpreted as gifts back to donors" (Pomona) (row 44).
  - **The counter-case.** Perelman's wealth fell and people wondered "if he made good on his pledges". The answer was "He's paid in advance" (row 13).
- **Claim 4: KILL.** Co-funders and auditors look through the announcement to what holds it.
  - **Hodge on Garden Bridge:** "over half of the pledges were anonymous which significantly contributes to the fragility of the commitments" (row 20).
  - **The NAO:** the department "didn't really hold any information" and relied on "verbal updates" (row 22).
  - **University counting rules** count only "a specific dollar amount according to a fixed time schedule", where "the donor may not prescribe contingencies or conditions" and "must be considered to be financially capable of making the gift" (UVM). A pledge that hangs on "some future event over which neither the university nor donor may have control" is not counted (EWU) (rows 43-44).
  - **Judge's note:** by a gift officer's own rules, a top that hangs on a share price is not yet a gift. That is why Laura states no figure until the shares have moved into Treasuries.
- **Claim 5: KILL.** In the failures read today, the running money killed the project, not the building money:
  - **Carnegie.** The town had to pledge running costs "not less than ___ Dollars a year" (10% of the grant). Towns that could not, like Parkston and Tripp, never built, and 225 US communities never took up their offers (rows 8-11).
  - **Kroc.** Long Beach: "we cannot build a center nor can we be confident that adequate funds can be raised to keep it open" (row 39).
  - **Garden Bridge.** The Trust closed after the Mayor refused "the guarantee for the annual maintenance costs", "a condition of planning consent" (row 23).
  - **Tai Kwun.** It opened because its founder promised "all operational deficits" until it paid its own way (row 16).
  - **Judge's note:** the plan does first what killed these projects when it was left until last. Ten years of the residency's running money are owned in Treasuries before co-sponsors are asked for a building (SKELETON 3.0).

## Best five for the report

1. **Rows 8-10, 23, 38-39: running money killed Carnegie offers, Kroc centres and the Garden Bridge (3.0, 3a).** Three unrelated failures over a century turned on running costs, not bricks. The plan owns the running money first. This is the proposition SKELETON 3.0 says no other founder can make, now with a record of what happens to those who leave it last.
2. **Rows 2 and 4: "the thing more than half done at the start" (3d).** Gates told Rockefeller that co-funders give when the founder's part is already done; Chicago then raised $549,000 against $400,000. It shows why a co-sponsor's first question is how much is already done. Never framed as the founder's share of the whole.
3. **Row 43: a gift officer would not count a figure that hangs on a share price (3c).** EWU's rule ("some future event over which neither the university nor donor may have control") explains in a fundraiser's terms why Laura states no figure before the move. It reads as practice, not caution.
4. **Rows 20-21: anonymous pledges were "fragile", and "raised" meant "pledged" (3d's words).** The strongest case read for one plain word: Laura's money is "owned". It is named and listed, and can be checked by a co-sponsor's auditor.
5. **Rows 16-17 with row 26: a founder's budget became a floor in Hong Kong, and money "no longer counted as my wealth" once moved (3b, the conceit).** Tai Kwun shows what an overrun does to a founder's figure. Laura's top cannot follow it up, and the half kept meets it. Li's line gives the conceit a real founder's words for the moment money moves into ink.
