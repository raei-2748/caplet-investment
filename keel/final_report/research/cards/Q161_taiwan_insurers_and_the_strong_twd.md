- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** a promise fixed in one currency, paid from assets held in another, protected by a buffer set aside ahead instead of a full hedge (self-insurance, store Q87-027). Insurance supervision met it first at national scale: Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC, 金管會) created the life insurers' currency reserve (外匯價格變動準備金) by a rule issued on 7 Feb 2012 and sizes its cap as a one-year 1-in-20 loss. International finance explains why the currency can jump when such holders rush to hedge (the hedging channel, Liao and Zhang, 2025).

**Convention.** "x% stronger" means x% more US dollars for the same NT$ budget (start rate / end rate - 1), as NUMBERS 8d and 8j. The FSC uses the same convention: its "9.53% stronger than end-2024" at end-May 2025 is 32.781 / 29.929 - 1 (Keel arithmetic check, exact). Amounts in NT$ are the FSC's own (億 converted to billions: 2,647億 = NT$264.7bn).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, sharpened.**
  - **The size came in two months, not two years.** On the FSC's own month-end figures the Taiwan dollar went from about 33.18 per US$ (end-March 2025) to 29.929 (end-May): 10.9% in two months (Keel arithmetic on FSC 2025a, 2025c).
  - **Taiwan's own yardstick.** The FSC caps the insurers' reserve at a one-year 95% value-at-risk: exposure times the 1-in-20 one-year rise of the NT$, computed "根據中華民國七十九年一月至前一年度十一月美元兌新臺幣匯率歷史月資料" (from monthly data since January 1990) (FSC, 2023; 2025e). On that window (monthly averages, windows ending by Nov 2024) Keel arithmetic gives a 1-in-20 one-year rise of about 8.0% and a 1-in-20 two-year rise of about 11.0%; a two-year rise of 10% or more came in 7.8% of 395 windows. The plan's 10% two-year test sits just under the two-year 1-in-20 on the regulator's own data window.
  - **The industry's design size is about 10-12%.** The life insurance association: a reserve above NT$1tn "1年內匯率波動在1成內都可抵禦" (absorbs moves within 10% in a year) (CNA, 2025f). Cathay Life: "新台幣升到28元也沒有問題" (no problem even at 28), 11.4% stronger than that day's 31.18 (CNA, 2025i; Keel arithmetic). Taipei Fubon Bank: NT$1.08tn of reserves and special surplus "估計可抵禦台幣匯率升值12%的風險" (Taipei Fubon Bank, 2026).
  - **Judge's note:** the holders with the most at stake in a stronger Taiwan dollar, Taiwan's own life insurers, build their buffer for a 10-12% move. That is the move Table 3 row 7 tests, and her flexibility plus anything above the top covers it about 2.5 times (NUMBERS 8j: $28,682 against $11,500).
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.**
  - **Their answer has three parts, not one:** about half hedged (66.39% at end-2024, 50.23% at end-2025, 45.1% in Feb 2026), a currency reserve, and rule changes by the regulator when the reserve ran short.
  - **The reserve nearly ran dry.** It stood at NT$283.6bn at end-March 2025, NT$164.8bn at end-April and NT$18.8bn at end-May, after NT$63.9bn of one-off top-ups (FSC, 2025a, 2025b, 2025c). In April-May hedges took 66% of the gross exchange loss and the reserve absorbed 61% of the loss left after hedges. NT$213.9bn still reached results (Keel arithmetic on the FSC's cumulative tables).
  - **The supervisor's own verdict:** at a record NT$384.5bn in October 2025, the reserve was "其實還不夠" (still not enough); "外價金要上兆元才是安全水位" (over NT$1tn is the safe level) (CNA, 2025h).
  - **Keep the parallel in a corrected form:** the large holders of the same exposure chose money set aside ahead over a full hedge, because a hedge costs about 3% a year. May 2025 showed such a buffer works only if it is large next to the move. Before the move the insurers' reserve was about half (52%) of what the two-month move cost them after hedges. Her flexibility plus anything above the top is about 2.5 times what a 10% move costs her.
  - **Four differences to keep in mind:** theirs is an accounting reserve that the regulator refilled by changing rules (30% of 2025 profit, released policy reserves), and nobody can rewrite her rules. Hers is money in shares, which can fall. They convert nothing and can wait. She converts once, in December 2032.
  - **Judge's note:** "the insurers' reserve" is the right picture only with its 2025 ending. A buffer built for currency moves lost 93% of its balance in two months.
- **Claim 3: KILL.** The insurers hold US-dollar assets against NT$ promises; Laura holds US-dollar money against a NT$ building. That is the same exposure, not its mirror image: a stronger Taiwan dollar hurts both (FSC, 2025f: "以外幣資產對應新臺幣負債"). Q10b card 2 already said "same sign"; this records the kill of the word.
- **Claim 4: KEEP.**
  - **The swing ran both ways within one year.** In Q1 2025 the NT$ weakened 1.21% and the reserve grew NT$64.0bn. By end-May it was 9.53% stronger than end-2024. It ended the year only 4.27% stronger (FSC, 2025a, 2025c, 2026c).
  - **The insurers' year turned the same way.** Their foreign investments lost NT$138.4bn over January-May and earned NT$518.5bn over the full year, FX and hedging included (FSC, 2025c, 2026c).
  - **The forecasters split.** The FSC (2024) and the insurance association (2025) say the rate mean-reverts. CTBC, Taipei Fubon Bank and a CFR economist expect long-run strength. CTBC's call of "about NT$30" by end-2025 missed: the year closed at 31.438.
  - **No one names a level.** The central bank says it "從未預設匯率目標水準" (has never preset a target level) and "無法亦不會預設新台幣升值防線" (cannot and will not preset a line against appreciation) (CBC, 2026a).
  - **Judge's note:** the insurers' defence is that the rate mean-reverts and their losses are on paper until they sell. Laura has neither: her dollars are converted once, in December 2032, and that day's loss is real NT$ building. That is why her buffer is sized against a move, not against a forecast.
- **Clash with the FINAL IPS or the TNA: none.** IPS l.15 names "a stronger Taiwan dollar" as a use of her flexibility. Nothing here sizes a contingency fund (CANON A31) or proposes a hedge. Two risks to record:
  - **(a) A jump can come in a slump.** The hedging channel (row 32) says a creditor country's currency can jump in global distress, when shares, and so her flexibility, are low. That is the short-run exception to the register's two-year pattern (8j, 8q: since 2001 a stronger NT$ came with rising shares).
  - **(b) Her exposure is joint.** Laura faces building costs as well as the currency, which the insurers do not (row 35, 8q).

## Cards for the strong rows

**Card 1. The reserve that nearly ran dry (rows 13-15, 24; FSC monthly releases for March, April and May 2025).**
- *What is it?* Taiwan's life insurers keep a currency reserve, written into rules since 2012, that stores part of their currency gains and pays out against currency losses. It stood at NT$283.6bn at end-March 2025. Over April and May the Taiwan dollar rose 10.9% on the FSC's month-end figures. The reserve paid out NT$328.7bn and was at NT$18.8bn by end-May, even after NT$63.9bn of one-off top-ups.
- *Did it work?* Partly. Hedges, covering about 63% of the exposure, took 66% of the gross loss. The reserve absorbed 61% of what was left. NT$213.9bn still hit results, and May 2025 was the industry's worst month on record (NT$106.6bn pretax loss). The supervisor then refilled the reserve by changing rules, and its official said a safe size was over NT$1tn.
- *How is Laura's case like it?* The same exposure: US-dollar money, Taiwan-dollar needs. The same choice: money set aside ahead instead of a hedge (which she cannot buy inside the plan anyway, Q10b row 31). IPS l.15 names "a stronger Taiwan dollar" as a use of her flexibility.
- *How is it not?* Size. Their reserve was about half of what the two-month move cost them after hedges. Her flexibility plus anything above the top is about 2.5 times what a 10% move costs her (8j). Also, theirs is an accounting reserve that a regulator can refill by rule. Hers is money in shares that can fall, and no one can change her rules.
- *What Caplet takes:* 3e or 3b, one sentence of prior art at most. Taiwan's own life insurers met a 10.9% two-month rise in 2025 with a currency reserve that lost 93% of its balance. The lesson the plan takes is size, which is why her flexibility is tested against the move itself.

**Card 2. Taiwan's own design size (rows 9, 22, 23; FSC rule text; CNA; Taipei Fubon Bank).**
- *What is it?* The FSC sizes the reserve's cap as a 1-in-20 one-year loss, using monthly NT$ data since January 1990. On that data Keel arithmetic puts the 1-in-20 rise at about 8% over one year and about 11% over two. The insurance association's goal is a reserve that absorbs "moves within 10% in a year". Cathay says it can take 28 (11.4% stronger). A bank estimates the 2026 buffers absorb 12%.
- *Did it work?* It is a target, not yet a record. The 2025 move hit before the buffers reached it.
- *How is Laura's case like it?* Table 3 row 7 tests 10% from the 2031 rate. That is the size Taiwan's biggest holders of the same exposure build for. Her flexibility plus anything above the top covers it about 2.5 times centrally, and in 99.74% of house paths (8j).
- *How is it not?* Her window is about two years, not one. Her need also grows with building costs (8q's 1-in-20 joint rise, 28.0%, leaves her $3,509 short), and their need does not.
- *What Caplet takes:* the T3r7 note or 3e. The 10% test is the size Taiwan's own life insurers and their regulator build for. Never call it a forecast.

**Card 3. Paper losses and mean reversion: the two defences Laura does not have (rows 7, 11, 26, 27).**
- *What is it?* The insurers' case for hedging less rests on two things. First, they hold bonds for 20-30 years, so a currency move is a "paper change" until they sell. Second, by historical experience the rate mean-reverts (the FSC's 2024 slides; the association: 28 in May, back to 31 in November).
- *Did it work?* For 2025 as a whole, yes: the NT$ ended only 4.27% stronger and their foreign investments earned NT$518.5bn. For May 2025, no: the reserve nearly emptied and the regulator stepped in. Fitch warns that a persistent move or mass surrenders would make the paper losses real.
- *How is Laura's case like it?* The exposure is the same and the rate is the same.
- *How is it not?* Laura converts once, in December 2032, to pay for a building. Her loss on that day is cash. She cannot wait for reversion and has no regulator to smooth her books.
- *What Caplet takes:* 3e, one clause. Unlike a life insurer, Laura cannot wait for the rate to come back, so the plan sizes her flexibility against a move, not against a forecast. A judge who knows the 2025 insurer story will see the team knows why it does not transfer.

**Card 4. No one names a level (rows 27-29; CBC 30 Jan 2026; Setser).**
- *What is it?* The central bank says it has "never preset a target level" and "cannot and will not preset a line against appreciation". It smooths both ways. Forecasters split between mean reversion and long-run strength, and one public call (CTBC's "about NT$30 by end-2025") missed.
- *Did it work?* Smoothing slowed May 2025 and did not stop it. Setser argues that years of smoothing had made unhedged dollars look safe.
- *How is Laura's case like it?* A US-dollar figure stated in 2031 promises dollars, not NT$ building.
- *How is it not?* Policy may change before 2031.
- *What Caplet takes:* 3e. The plan states a dollar range and keeps her flexibility for the currency, because neither the central bank nor the forecasters will name the 2032 rate.
