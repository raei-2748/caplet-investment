# Q84. Taiwan and Japan make companies change their forecasts by rule: what do those rules really say, and does Laura's range need one?

- **Date:** Mon 16 Nov 2026 (Sydney).
- **Report parts it serves:**

- **Claims tested:**
  1. Taiwan's rule makes companies correct a forecast that misses by 20%, a range is allowed, and there are penalties.
  2. Japan's exchange rule makes companies revise when sales move 10% or profits 30%.
  3. Forecasts in Taiwan and Japan are often revised.
  4. "Laura's stated range needs no correction rule because each end is money she already owns, so a co-sponsor in Taiwan, used to forecasts that must be corrected by law, meets a figure that cannot miss."
- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** This is securities disclosure regulation in East Asia: how a company states a figure about its own future, labels it, and when it must change it.
  - Taiwan's securities regulator made forecasts compulsory for many listed firms from 1991, with a fixed band (10%, widened to 20% in December 1993). It made them voluntary in 2005.
  - Japan's exchange built a near-universal habit on request, not law: over 90% of listed firms gave forecasts by 1980. It tied revisions to a fixed 10% / 30% test and to the 1989 insider-trading rules.
  - Both fields answer "when must a stated future figure be changed?" Neither has to answer Laura's case: a figure with nothing left to change.

## Verdicts in one screen

1. **Claim 1, Taiwan's "20% correction rule": CHANGE.**
   - The 20% is real, but it triggers an *update* (更新), not a correction. An update is due when changed assumptions move comprehensive income by "百分之二十以上" **and** by at least NT$30 million **and** by at least 0.5% of paid-in capital (Regulations Art. 20).
   - A *correction* (更正) is a separate duty, for an error that "可能誤導使用者之判斷" (may mislead users; Arts 11 and 21).
   - Forecasts are voluntary: a company "得" (may) publish one (Art. 5).
   - A range is allowed only in the summary form ("單一數字或區間估計", Art. 9). The complete form takes single amounts (Art. 13).
   - Every forecast must carry the label "本資料係屬預測性質，將來未必能完全達成" ("this is a forecast and may not be completely achieved"; Arts 10(6) and 17(5)).
   - Penalties are a fine of NT$240,000 to NT$4.8 million (Securities and Exchange Act Art. 178(1)(11)) and TWSE deficiency records, with NT$30,000 to NT$5 million (TWSE Arts 26-27).
   - The real bite is access to money. The FSC may return a firm's offering filing (FSC Q&A Q12). In the compulsory era, a firm more than 20% off could not raise new capital without special permission (Hsu et al., 2012).
   - **Judge's note:** Taiwan priced a missed forecast as lost access to future funding. That is the case's own warning for Laura: she could "lose the confidence or participation of co-sponsors".
2. **Claim 2, Japan's 10% / 30%: KEEP, with the wording fixed.**
   - A company must disclose "immediately" when a new forecast or result, set against the last published forecast (or against last year's actual if there was none), is "not less than 1.1 or not more than 0.9" for sales. For operating profit, ordinary profit and net income the test is "not less than 1.3 or not more than 0.7" (Securities Listing Regulations Rule 405(1); Enforcement Rules Rule 407(1)).
   - A zero base always counts.
   - The same ratios make a new forecast an insider-trading "material fact" under the Cabinet Office Ordinance, Art. 51. That ordinance adds net-asset floors and a 20% test for dividends.
   - JPX's FAQ treats each end of a range as its own forecast.
   - **Judge's note:** in Japan a range gives a company no shelter: each end is judged alone. Laura's range after the move is the reverse case. Read alone, each end is already held for her (NUMBERS 4).
3. **Claim 3, base rates: NEW, now measured.**
   - **Japan:**
     - 69.88% of 20,783 firm-years made a timely revision (Nagata & Nguyen, 2017).
     - 70.6% of 36,023 firm-years revised at least once (SAA Journal, 2024; Keel arithmetic).
     - The first net-income forecast equalled the result in 74 of 32,814 firm-years, which is 0.2% (Tazawa & Tashiro, 2015).
   - **Taiwan, compulsory era:** 1,538 of 2,628 forecasts (58.5%, Keel arithmetic) started more than 20% off and were brought inside (Hsu et al., 2012).
   - **Taiwan, after 2005:** forecasters fell to 0.58% of listed firms by 2009 (Chung & Chiu, 2021).
   - **Judge's note:** in a market where about 95% of firms forecast, the first number is exactly right 2 times in 1,000, and the exchange says revision is "naturally expected" (TSE, 2012).
4. **Claim 4: CHANGE. The core is right; two parts are wrong.**
   - **(a) "A co-sponsor in Taiwan, used to forecasts that must be corrected by law" is false today.**
     - Since 2005 forecasts are voluntary. By 2009, 7 of 1,200 listed firms issued one. Complete forecasts "驟減至幾乎為零" (fell to almost zero).
     - What Taiwanese business readers get is actual revenue each month, filed by the 10th (Securities and Exchange Act Art. 36(1)(3)).
     - What they do know: the compulsory label "may not be completely achieved". And, if they run a listed firm, the TWSE criteria that turn a revenue range told to the press into a full forecast that must be kept current.
   - **(b) "A figure that cannot miss" holds only after the 2031 move, and only for the stated ends as held.** After the move NUMBERS 4 supports "certain". The IPS's two reasons can still move an end. A missed deposit is behind her by 2031, since both deposits are in by January 2028 (Keel's reading). A Treasury default is the other, said once as the IPS says it.
   - **(c) Fund costs beyond the two fees.**
     - Costs up to 0.5% of value are covered everywhere.
     - At 0.75%, an end breaks in about 1 house path in 17.
     - Each 0.1% of excess cost leaves the funds at most 0.1% of both funds' value short of the stated top, about $119 centrally (NUMBERS 0, 4).
     - The 0.5% carries about 0.27% of costs outside the 0.07% fee (Keel arithmetic, card Q3c).
   - **(d) Before the move the top is a forecast in Taiwan's sense.** Stated 1, 3, 6 or 12 months early, it is not yet held in 2.9%, 6.2%, 8.7% or 10.7% of house paths (NUMBERS 4). Taiwan's own rule makes a forecast stale after one month (Art. 19).
   - **(e) It is certain in US dollars, not in Taiwan dollars.** Laura's range is in dollars, but a Taiwan forecast must test exchange rates and raw-material costs (Art. 15). A 1-in-20 two-year rise of 28% in the building's US-dollar cost needs $32,191 on the $115,000 top. Her flexibility is $29,823, so she would be $2,368 short (NUMBERS 8q). That is her flexibility's job, not the stated range's.
   - **Changed claim (notes, not wording):** "Laura's figure needs no correction rule because, once she states it after the move, nothing is left to correct: the Treasuries that will pay each end, maturing late 2032, are already held for her. A Taiwanese co-sponsor knows company forecasts as figures that carry a legal warning that they may not be met; hers carries none, because it is not a forecast."
   - **Judge's note:** Taiwan forbids forecasts while a company is raising money (FSC Q&A, citing the offering rules). Laura states her figure at exactly the moment she asks co-sponsors for money. She can do so only because, after the move, it is not a forecast.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 16 (3d): Taiwan forbids forecasts while a firm is raising money.** Laura states hers at exactly the moment she asks co-sponsors, and can do so only because after the move it is not a forecast. This is a regulator's reason for the plan's timing.
2. **Row 15 (3c, 3d): in Taiwan the price of a missed forecast was lost access to new money.** It was never mainly the fine. This is the case's own credibility warning, with real teeth behind it.
3. **Row 3 with row 39 (3d): every Taiwanese forecast must say it "may not be completely achieved".** Laura's ends after the move are the rare future figure that needs no such label. It also suits the conceit, since Taiwan's law makes every forecast wear a pencil label.
4. **Rows 30 and 36 (3c): Japan's exchange says a forecast "is not a commitment" and revision is "naturally expected".** The first figure is exactly right 2 times in 1,000. This gives a measured contrast with a figure already held.
5. **Row 20 (3d, and it changes claim 4): given the choice, Taiwan's listed firms stopped forecasting.** They went from 59.47% (2003) to 0.58% (2009) of firms. A Taiwanese co-sponsor knows monthly actuals, not forecasts, so Laura's words should sound like a reported fact.

## Gaps

- **Current count of Taiwan forecasters.** TWSE's quarterly summary of listed firms' forecasts (115年第3季上市公司財務預測彙總資訊, 15 Oct 2026, a .docx on dsp.twse.com.tw) could not be opened by either fetch tool. The latest count found is 2009 (0.58%).

- **MOPS lists.** MOPS's quarterly lists of firms 10% or 20% off forecast were not read.
- **Fines in practice.** No case was found of an Art. 178 fine imposed for a forecast. Whether the fine is used is unknown.
