## Verdicts in one screen

1. **CHANGE.** Under Secretary Peter Fisher announced on 31 Oct 2001: "We are suspending issuance of the 30-year bond: there will be no auction of 30-year securities in February 2002", and "we plan no further auctions of either 30-year nominal or inflation-adjusted bonds." His reasons: "We do not need the 30-year bond to meet the government's current financing needs, nor those that we expect" to face, and the bond "no longer maintains a position of significance in the financial markets." The surpluses were a forecast, not the moment: FY1998-FY2001 were "the surplus years" (CRS), but the same statement planned for "a unified budget deficit for this fiscal year and, perhaps, the following fiscal year as well", after "the attacks of September 11th". The last nominal 30-year sale was 9 Aug 2001 (a reopening of the 5-3/8% bond of 15 Feb 2031). Treasury announced "the reintroduction of the 30-year Treasury Bond" on 3 Aug 2005, with "regular semi-annual auctions", and sold the 4-1/2% bond of 15 Feb 2036 (CUSIP 912810FT0) on 9 Feb 2006: $14bn at a 4.530% high yield. That is the team's "4.5% Feb-2036 bond".
   Judge's note: the gap was dug by a forecast. Treasury expected surpluses to come back, and they did not. Laura's plan was built so that no payment rests on a forecast (TNA l.15).
2. **KEEP, exact.** Every Treasury bond auctioned that matures 2029-2038 (Fiscal Data, 22 auction records) gives the same answer. The nominal bonds run 15 Feb 2029, 15 Aug 2029, 15 May 2030, 15 Feb 2031, then nothing until 15 Feb 2036. The hole is exactly five years, the same to the day as the team's 29 Sep record ("none mature between 15 Feb 2031 and 15 Feb 2036"). The one bond of any kind inside it is the 30-year inflation-indexed bond of 15 Apr 2032, auctioned 10 Oct 2001, three weeks before the suspension. It is a TIPS, so Wharton bans it for Laura this year.
3. **KEEP for 2031-2036; KILL elsewhere.** On 31 Aug 2026, 57 Treasury notes ($4.48 trillion) and 12 TIPS mature inside the window, and not one nominal bond does (MSPD, Keel count). The team saw none of those notes in WInS, so WInS lists bonds, not notes (inference, matching the team's Session Rules record: "bonds limited to US Treasury bonds"). Because no nominal bond exists in the window, even a complete bond list would show this hole. The team's other gaps were the simulator's: WInS lists 43 bonds of the 113 outstanding, and it did not list the 5% May 2037 or the 1.375% Nov 2040 bond.
4. **CHANGE.** The funds were forced for four payments (2033-2036), not five. The fifth (2037, IBTR) was a choice made over the very bond that ended the gap. Two decisions made the hole: the 30-year's suspension (Oct 2001 to Feb 2006) and the 20-year bond's long absence (last sold 8 Jan 1986, back 20 May 2020). A 20-year bond sold in 2011-2016 would have matured in 2031-2036. And the cause was a forecast of surpluses, never "a budget surplus".
   Judge's note: IBTR, the fund chosen over the Feb-2036 bond, itself held that bond (3.03% of the fund on 24 Sep 2026; one "TREASURY BOND" at 4.11% on its 30 Jun fact sheet). IBTM to IBTQ hold only the Treasury notes that WInS does not list. The funds are how Laura owns, at one remove, the Treasuries the bond list was missing.
5. **KILL.** Nothing in the plan moves. IPS l.13 ("whichever works best for each year") and TNA l.15 ("Some years, WInS lists no Treasuries maturing") both stand. The finding explains them one level deeper and changes no holding or figure.
