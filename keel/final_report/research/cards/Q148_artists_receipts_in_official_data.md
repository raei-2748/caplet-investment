- **Claims tested:**

  2. "The arts fell more than the economy." KILL for independent artists in the national accounts for 2008-09. CHANGE for 2009 in tax and census data. KEEP for 2020.

- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** how labour income moves with share returns, at the scale of an occupation (human capital as an asset; the finance papers are already on card Q1b). The measurement problem was solved first by official business statistics. Census Nonemployer Statistics and the IRS Statistics of Income count one-person businesses. BLS QCEW counts employers. BEA's satellite accounts measure the industry inside GDP.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE. The fall came in 2009, not 2008, and again in 2020.**
  - **Census, one-person businesses (NAICS 7115).** Receipts +1.8% in 2008, -6.2% in 2009, +4.6% in 2010, +5.6% in 2011. 2020: -12.4% (Q1c-010).
  - **IRS tax returns (NAICS 711, the finest level the IRS publishes).** Receipts +2.8% in 2008, -10.0% in 2009 (Q1c-005). Arts sector 2020: -23.4%, with profits -26.2%, "the largest percentage decrease" of any sector.
  - **BLS QCEW, employers in 711510.**
    - 2008-10: jobs -0.2% in 2008, -7.2% in 2009, -1.2% in 2010. Wage bill +5.65%, then -4.71%, then +2.98%.
    - 2020: jobs -18.1%, wage bill -8.76%.
  - **Shares (S&P 500 with dividends).** -36.55% in 2008, +25.94% in 2009, +18.02% in 2020.
  - **Judge's note:** the occupation's bad year after the crash was 2009, the year shares rose 26%.
- **Claim 2: KILL for independent artists in BEA's accounts for 2008-09; CHANGE elsewhere.**
  - **2008-09, the national accounts.** BEA's real value added for independent artists rose 3.0% in 2009 (Q1c-008), while real GDP fell 2.6%. "Fell more than GDP" (Q1b-023) is true only for the whole arts economy.
  - **2008-09, tax and census data.** Artists fell 3 to 5 times as much as nominal GDP (-6.2% to -10.0% against -2.0%). But they fell only a little more than all one-person businesses (-6.2% against -4.1%). Their share of all such receipts went 1.51%, 1.48%, 1.50% (2008-2010).
  - **2020: KEEP.** BEA real value added -18.2% against -2.1% for real GDP and -1.7% for all arts. IRS arts receipts fell 11 times as much as all sole proprietors' (-23.4% against -2.1%).
- **Claim 3: KEEP "later" for the dip. The recovery was quick.**
  - **2008-09.** Census and IRS receipts were back above 2008 in 2011. The S&P 500 regained its end-2007 level in 2012 (Keel arithmetic). Employer jobs were back above 2007 in 2013. Taiwan's publishing was back above 2008 in 2010.
  - **2020.** Census receipts and the QCEW wage bill were back above 2019 in 2021. IRS arts receipts and QCEW jobs were back in 2022.
  - **Judge's note:** the occupation's dip started a year after shares' fall and ended a year before shares recovered.
- **Claim 4: KEEP for publishing, CHANGE for comics.**
  - **Taiwan's publishing.** Revenue fell 7.2% in 2009 in the 2010 annual report, which says the industry "尚未從 2008 年全球金融大海嘯的衝擊中復原" ("had not yet recovered from the 2008 crisis"). The wider measure in the 2013 report fell 5.7%. It was back above 2008 in 2010 (+10.2%).
  - **Taiwan's comics.** Publishers' revenue fell every year from 2016 to 2022 (-25.5% in all), including -1.9% in 2019, before COVID. It was back above 2019 only in 2023. Comics for 2008-2010 were not found.
- **Claim 5: what the IPS can take.**
  - **2008-09.** "Later and smaller" is the record in four official series. Smaller means the occupation's worst year (2009) cost 6-10% of receipts, about a sixth to a quarter of shares' 36.55% fall. "During that same period" holds only if 2008-09 is read as one episode.
  - **2020.** The occupation fell 12-26% while shares rose 18.02%.
  - **2022.** Shares fell 18.04% and the occupation boomed (wage bill +9.55%, arts receipts +24.4%).
  - **Judge's note:** in 20 years of BLS employer data (2006-2025), the occupation's wage bill never fell in a year shares fell. In each of the five years it fell, shares rose 18-32%.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 11 (2.3, CANON B4).** The occupation's dip started a year after shares fell and was over a year before shares recovered (back in 2011; shares in 2012). This turns "later" from a vague word into a dated fact.
2. **Row 14 (2.3, 3e area 2).** In 20 years of BLS data, the occupation's wage bill never fell in a year shares fell. In each of its five down years, shares rose 18-32%. This is an aha that corrects the easy reading of "your career is shares", and it shows why the risk is two losses in back-to-back years.
3. **Row 2 (2.3).** "Smaller", sized: 6-10% in the worst year (2009) against shares' 36.55%. These are official figures a judge can check, and they are industry figures only.
4. **Rows 16 and 21 (3e area 2, CANON B4).** 2020 ran the other way: arts receipts fell 23.4%, with "the largest percentage decrease" in profits of any sector, while shares rose 18.02%, and fewer artists filed rather than each earning less. This keeps the report honest about l.9's "during that same period".
5. **Row 26 (3e, Table 3 if a career watch is wanted).** BEA has stopped regular production of the artists' satellite account. A watch to January 2028 uses IRS and Census tables, and after the 2028 deposit her income no longer touches the plan.

## Gaps

- **Census Nonemployer Statistics 2016-2018 and 2023.** The historical files from 2016 are zip archives (404 as .txt). data.census.gov tables did not render values. The Census API needs a key. 2022 has a method break (Q1c-010).
- **Census receipt-size classes.** Codes 111-1411 are in the 2009-2015 files, but their meanings were not read, so the share of artists under $10,000 or over $100,000 is not known.
- **IRS 2019 NAICS 711 line.** The TY2019 bulletin (irs-prior p1136--2022.pdf) is a PDF portfolio and could not be read. The SOI spreadsheet Table 1 stops at NAICS 711, with no 711510 line.
- **IRS TY2022 bulletin.** Read only as a verbatim excerpt. The full table was not opened.
- **BLS QCEW establishments for 2005-2018.** Not fetched. The open-data CSV for 2009 was a 404, and data.bls.gov is closed to one of the fetch tools.
- **BEA's latest-vintage values for 2007-2010.** Not read, so 2008-09 and 2020 cannot be put side by side in one vintage.
- **Taiwan comics revenue for 2008-2010.** Not found. The 2010 and 2013 annual reports give publishing totals and sub-industries but no comics line in the parts read. Taiwan's stock market for 2008-2010 was not read for this card.
- **Cause of the 2023 fall** (QCEW -8.42%, BEA -12.1%). Not read at source.
- **Tools.** Firecrawl was out of credits. The WebFetch hourly budget ran out mid-run, and Parallel hit a rate limit once. The next round can fill the Census 2016-2018 zips and the QCEW establishment series.
