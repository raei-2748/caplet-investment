- **Claim 1: KEEP, with numbers.**
  - **Cost.** The middle overrun of the ten is about 31.6% and the average about 42% (Keel arithmetic). Eight of the ten overran by more than about 25%.
  - **Time.** Every building with dates opened late, by 3 to 9 years. The budget sometimes held; the opening date never did.
  - **The two that held their budget gave something up for it.**
    - The museum branch dropped its first design and opened seven years late (rows 12-13).
    - The library's price-surge money was handed back (row 14).
  - **Bias.** The class leans high because it comes from watchdog files (row 30).
  - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan's ten public arts buildings, the budget held twice and the opening date held never. Laura's figure has no date risk: the Treasuries that pay her contribution are already owned.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE. In Taiwan's own 2024 contract data, small jobs overrun less often but by more.**
  - **How often.** In 2024 an addition hit 15.66% of contracts of NT$1.5-50m, against 22.15% of contracts of NT$200m or more. The large ones were extended 1.93 times as often (LY Budget Center, Jul 2025, PCC data; row 23).
  - **How much.** Among contracts that had an addition, 35.14% of the small ones added 20% or more, against 7.43% of the large ones. For small contracts, "20% or more" was the most common size of addition (row 24).
    - The Budget Center itself says part of this is a smaller denominator ("分母愈小").
  - **Per contract (Keel arithmetic).** An addition of 20% or more hit 5.5% of all small contracts under construction that year, against 1.6% of large ones (row 25).
  - **This matches Q5d's English and Norwegian finding** (Torp; HLF), now in Taiwan's own data.
  - **Where Laura sits.** $115,000 is NT$3.77m at 32.768 (Keel arithmetic), inside the small band.
- **Claim 3a: CHANGE. About 25% covers 2 of the 10.**
  - **The counts (Keel arithmetic on NUMBERS 8u and 8q).**
    - About 25% (24.9%) covers 2 of the 10: the two that held at about 0%.
    - The half kept alone (18.4%) also covers 2 of 10.
    - On the stated bottom, her flexibility is 30.2% ($28,682 / $95,000) and covers 5 of 10.
  - **The cluster.** Five of the ten sit between 27.6% and 33.6%. On $115,000 that is $31,786-$38,663, which is $3,104-$9,981 more than $28,682 (row 3). The gap is a few points, not a multiple.
  - **This is the wrong class for her** (row 4).
    - **Size.** Each building cost 318 to 2,850 times NT$3.77m (Keel arithmetic).
    - **Kind.** They were public landmarks let at the lowest price, and a government could vote more money for each.
    - **The figure is generous.** The 24.9% is central (19 times in 20 the house model covers 15.8%, 8q), and it assumes her $115,000 is the whole budget (Q5d row 30).
  - **Judge's note:** the honest comparison is the PCC's small band (row 25), not a museum.
- **Claim 3b: CHANGE. An overrun falls first on the building's owner, not on "the other funders" as such.**
  - **What the case says.** It puts "any remaining facility cost" on "co-sponsors, grants, collaborators, program fees, continued business income, or other sources" (case l.84-86).
  - **What Taiwan's rules say.** A fixed grant does not top up, and the owner raises the difference: 「不得要求追加補助數額」 (the owner may not ask for a larger grant) (Q11e rows 15-16; Q5d row 28).
  - **Taoyuan in real life.** The central grant was fixed at NT$1.376bn, 44.8% of the first total. Held fixed, it is 23.6% of the new total (Keel arithmetic).
    - The city, as owner, carried the rest.
    - A larger central share needed a new decision; in January 2025 the culture minister promised only to seek it (row 20).
  - **For Laura.** Her stated contribution is a fixed sum in the same way. Her flexibility reaches the building only if she chooses (CANON D12).
    - If she is the residency's owner, the overrun reaches her as owner, and she meets it by scope, phasing, more fundraising or her own choice. Her promise to the co-sponsors does not change.
  - **Judge's note:** when Taoyuan's museum cost rose by about 90%, the central government's fixed grant fell from 45% to 24% of the cost. Nothing fell on the funder who had promised a fixed sum. That is what a fixed sum is for.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 20, with row 21 (3b, 3d): Taoyuan's fixed central grant.** When the museum's cost rose by about 90%, the fixed central grant fell from 45% to 24% of the cost, and only a new decision could add to it. This is CANON D12 in a real Taiwanese building: a fixed-sum promise does not grow with an overrun, and her flexibility adds only by her choice.
2. **Row 2, with rows 3 and 4 (3b, 3e area 4): the coverage count.** About 25% covers 2 of Taiwan's ten big public arts buildings, and 5 of 10 at the bottom. Five of them sit a few points above her flexibility, not a multiple of it. The class is the wrong size, which the report says plainly.
3. **Rows 24 and 25 (3e area 4): Taiwan's own PCC data by contract size.** Small contracts get additions less often, but by more. That repeats Q5d's foreign finding in Taiwan's own data and gives the nearest class to a small residency.
4. **Row 9 (3d, 3e): the Control Yuan's diagnosis.** Taiwan's watchdog blames overruns on a first budget set below the market. Laura's figure is built the other way round: what she owns, up to a capped top. One line in 3d can say why co-sponsors can trust it.
5. **Row 22 (3e; Table 3): delay is near universal.** Not one building in the class opened on time. Her payments and contribution are dated and owned, so a late building moves neither. The owner carries the delay's cost.
