# Q96. Is a creative career one asset or three: a bond (the backlist), a stock (new advances) and an option (licences and screen deals)?

- **Report parts:** 2.3 (diversify Laura: what kind of "giant stock" her career is), 3e area (2) (the 2028 deposit comes from the cyclical parts of her income), the report's opening idea (IPS l.3 sharpened, never reversed). Most rows are for the store only.

Store counts on 17 Nov: "backlist" 6, "royalt" 16, "Hipgnosis" 0, "catalog" 3, "option-like" 0.
- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Her career is a giant stock she can't sell" (IPS l.3): keep the analogy (Caplet's), but which part of a creative career behaves like a stock in a recession, which like a bond, which like an option?
  2. "A creator's back catalogue falls in value when interest rates rise, like a long bond."
  3. "The ladder counterbalances her career" (IPS l.9's "counterbalance"): where does the analogy break?
  4. "A steady backlist is a store of value that could stand in for Treasuries" (a judge's natural follow-up).
- **Problem class:** valuing human and intellectual capital: how a stream of creative income is split into parts with different risks, and how each part is priced when it is sold. **Field that solved it first:** music-royalty finance (catalogue valuation by discounted cash flow, with published discount rates and sensitivities, and royalty securitisation since the 1997 Bowie bonds); then publishing economics (BookScan's frontlist/backlist split, the 2022 Penguin Random House antitrust record) and human-capital finance (the stock-or-bond test, card Q1).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, sharpened into three parts.** A creative career is a bond, a stock and an option held together, and the case names only the stock and the option as the source of the 2028 deposit.
  - **The bond: the backlist.** Books more than 12 months old were 51% of US print units in 2004, 54% in 2009, 67% in 2020 and a record 70% in 2023 (NPD/Circana BookScan). In 2009 backlist units fell about 2.0% while frontlist units fell about 5.9%. In 2022, the year shares and Treasuries both lost about 18%, frontlist fell 10.5% and backlist 3.7% (Keel arithmetic and BookScan via Publishers Weekly).
  - **The stock: new advances.** An advance is priced from the publisher's sales forecast ("the driving force behind the advance offered"; the link is "extremely close") and can take "three to four years" to be paid (US v. Bertelsmann, 2022). At Hachette "About half the books we publish earn out their advances"; for those that do not, the advance is all the book pays. The frontlist also falls in good markets: -7.5% in 2019, when US shares rose 31%. It is a single stock, not the index.
  - **The option: licences and screen deals.** The author sells the option. She gets a small sure fee, about 10% of a purchase price that is usually "2.5%-5%" of the budget "with a floor and cap" (Authors Guild model agreement), paid only if the option is exercised. No one publishes how often that happens (guesses run from under 1% to about 10%). Adaptation deals fell 53% in 2023, a year of high rates and strikes (Vitrina).
  - **The case.** The $150,000 comes "using earnings from publishing advances, speaking engagements, licensing, and other entrepreneurial ventures" (case p.2). Royalties are not named. IPS l.9 names the same three: "the advances come, and the speaking fees, and the licences."
  - **Judge's note:** the "giant stock" is exactly the part of her career that pays the 2028 deposit. The steady part, the backlist, never enters the portfolio.
- **Claim 2: KEEP for the arithmetic and the market price; CHANGE "like a long bond" in timing and in what moved.**
  - **The arithmetic.** Hipgnosis Songs Fund's own valuer, Citrin Cooperman, disclosed that a 0.5-point higher discount rate cut the catalogue's fair value by 7.9% (31 Mar 2023) and 7.8% (30 Sep 2023), and a 0.5-point lower rate added 9.4%. Round Hill's valuer: +1 point, -14.37%; -1 point, +20.12%. Both behave like a bond of about 16-17 years' duration (Keel arithmetic). Laura's ten payments have a duration of 9.94 years and her holdings 7.76 (NUMBERS 1).
  - **The valuer did not move.** The discount rate stayed at 8.5% from at least 31 Mar 2021 to 30 Sep 2023, while the 10-year Treasury went from 1.52% (30 Sep 2021) to 4.59% (29 Sep 2023). The spread over Treasuries shrank from 6.98 to 3.91 points (Keel arithmetic on FRED). The 2023 report: Citrin Cooperman "has consistently taken a long-term view on interest rates".
  - **The market moved instead.** By September 2023 the shares traded near 50% below reported value, and Round Hill's 47% below. Round Hill's board blamed "the inflationary and higher interest rate environment". Shareholders voted 83% against continuing Hipgnosis (26 Oct 2023).
  - **Then all at once.** A new valuer (Shot Tower, 4 Mar 2024) used "a mid-point discount rate of 9.63%" and cut the value 26.3%. About three-fifths of that cut is the discount rate and the rest lower cash-flow forecasts (Keel arithmetic on the disclosed sensitivity). Blackstone paid $1.31 a share, 24.7% below the last Citrin-based value ($1.7392) and 10.3% above the new one ($1.19).
  - **Judge's note:** a song catalogue is a longer bond than Laura's whole ladder, and its valuer held its discount rate still while Treasury yields tripled. The price fell late and in one step: 26.3% in a single statement.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE.** In value terms the ladder sits beside her backlist, not against it, because both lose value when rates rise.
  - **When values moved together.** In 2022 the 10-year Treasury returned -17.83%, US shares -18.04% and frontlist units fell 10.5%. In 2008 Treasuries returned +20.10% while shares fell 36.55% (Damodaran). So the value counterbalance works in some downturns and fails in others.
  - **What holds every year is the cash.** Held to the end, each Treasury pays its dollars on its date (IPS l.13: "Held to the end, these pay what they say"). No part of a creative career pays fixed dollars. The Bowie bonds' rating fell three notches on falling record sales. Hipgnosis lost $155.0m of value to a royalty-rate ruling and $47.5m to creators reclaiming rights, in six months.
  - **The right reading** is CANON A2's: the portfolio "must not add risk that moves with her career". Never "the ladder rises when her career falls". IPS l.3's "hedge" is Caplet's own word; the report adds no adjective to it.
- **Claim 4: KILL.** A backlist's steadiness is in units, not in dollars or price.
  - The Bowie bonds (1997, $55m at 7.9%) were cut from A3 to Baa3, "just one notch above junk", in March 2004, though they were repaid in 2007.
  - Chorion, owner of the Agatha Christie, Enid Blyton and Mr Men backlists, broke its banking covenants in 2011 and sold them all for undisclosed sums.
  - Three valuers put Hipgnosis's songs at $2.62bn, $1.93bn and $2.36bn within eleven months.
  - **Judge's note:** the one asset in her life that looks most like a bond has no price until someone buys it. Her Treasuries have a price every second, and held to the end they need none.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 40 (the case names the stock and the option, not the bond).** 2.3 and the report's opening idea: the deposit comes from "publishing advances, speaking engagements, licensing, and other entrepreneurial ventures", exactly the cyclical parts IPS l.9 lists, so "a giant stock" is precise about the money the plan touches. Why: a judge who knows the textbook objection that most careers are bond-like (Q1 rows 3, 11 and 13) sees Caplet had already answered it from the case.
2. **Rows 14-16 (a song catalogue is a 17-year bond whose valuer held its rate still while yields tripled).** 3e or 2.3, one clause: Hipgnosis's own reports put its catalogue's sensitivity at about 8% per half point, a longer bond than Laura's ten payments (9.94 years). Why: the aha that the "safe" part of a creative career is long-duration, and that its marked value can sit still while Treasuries move.
3. **Rows 3 and 6 (frontlist fell about three times the backlist in 2009 and 2022).** 2.3: a measured split between the stock-like and bond-like parts of book money, in the two years shares fell hardest. Why: it turns "In a recession they come later and smaller" (IPS l.9) into the industry's own numbers without repeating Q1's advance and licensing rows.
4. **Row 41 (the counterbalance is in dollars on dates, not in value).** 2.3 or 3e: in 2022 Treasuries, shares and new-book sales fell together; held to the end each Treasury still pays its dollars on its date. Why: it stops the report overclaiming IPS l.9's "counterbalance" and ties the career premise to IPS l.13.
5. **Row 18 (the valuer prices a catalogue as 50% debt, 50% equity).** The opening idea or 2.3: even professionals price a creative catalogue as half bond, half stock. Why: a one-line, sourced reason to say "three assets" rather than "one stock", with the option part from row 36.
