# Q94. Guidance heard as a promise: when a central bank's conditional forecast was heard as a commitment, what did it cost, and what does that say about a figure Laura states in 2031?

- **Date:** Mon 16 Nov 2026 (Sydney).
- **Report parts:** 3c (why nothing is stated before the move; why each end is rounded down), 3d (what a co-sponsor hears in Laura's words), 3e (a limit: what an owned figure does not settle), 3.0 only if aha A or B is used. No table rows.
- Notes for the Final Report store, not report prose.
- **Not repeated:** Q15 row 40 (the Fed's Dec 2012 thresholds, Bernanke's "will not automatically trigger", the 2014 drop of numbers); Q71 (how a range is heard, "certain" heard as "at least", overstatements judged worse than understatements); Q74 (what confidence means); Q76 (regulators' rules on stated projections, the UK's rounding down); Q43 (the Antideficiency Act, "moral obligation"); store H3-046 (cheap talk), H3-048 and H3-049, cards Q7 and Q7b (Kydland-Prescott, time inconsistency, Elster's Ulysses, Ashraf, Karlan and Yin's "Tying Odysseus to the Mast"). This card adds three things: central banks' own record of a forecast heard as a promise, the Delphic and Odyssean pair as the field's words for it, and why a speaker who controls the outcome is heard as promising.
- **Claims tested:** (1) a central bank's conditional guidance was heard as a promise and cost it credibility when it "broke"; (2) the Fed's calendar guidance and the Delphic/Odyssean distinction, and which a co-sponsor hears; (3) so a figure stated in 2031 is heard as a commitment whatever its caveats, which is why Laura states only owned figures and rounds each end down; (4) which example serves the report best.
- **Problem class and the field that met it first:** central bank communication, where a conditional forecast of the speaker's own future action is heard as a commitment. Linguistics and the psychology of reasoning described the hearing first: "if" is heard as "only if" (Geis and Zwicky 1971, as reported in Horn 2000 and Boukendour 2025), and a conditional about the speaker's own action is heard as a promise (Evans and Twyman-Musgrove 1998; Haigh et al. 2011). Central banks paid for it in public (the Fed 2011-12, the Bank of England 2013-14, the RBA 2020-22) and wrote the lessons down (the RBA's two reviews in 2022, the independent Review of the RBA in 2023).

## Verdicts in one screen

1. **Claim 1: KEEP, quoted exactly.** The RBA's own review (15 Nov 2022) says: "The fact that many people interpreted the forward guidance as 'a promise' that there would be no rate raises until 2024 led to considerable reputational damage to the Bank. When the cash rate was increased in May 2022, many people saw the Bank as having broken 'its promise'." The guidance had been conditional in its own words ("The Board does not expect these conditions to be met until 2024 at the earliest", Feb 2021). The yield-target review (June 2022) found "some reputational damage" and said the experience "could lessen the effectiveness of any future commitments of this nature by the Bank". The independent Review (2023) heard the same thing at arm's length: "many in the public understood this to be a commitment". The Governor's apology at Senate estimates (28 Nov 2022) is known only from news reports, since the Hansard was not read (row 12, UNVERIFIED). His own words on ABC 7.30 in June 2022 are on the RBA's site (row 10).
2. **Claim 2: KEEP, with both terms defined.** Delphic guidance "publicly states a forecast". Odyssean guidance "does publicly commit the policymaker, just as Odysseus committed himself to staying on his ship by having himself bound to the mast". Having no rope, central banks "must rely on their reputations" (Campbell, Evans, Fisher and Justiniano 2012). Markets mostly read the Fed's dates as Delphic; much of the press read them as promises; the two readings sat side by side among forecasters (Andrade et al. 2019). **A co-sponsor hears Laura's figure as Odyssean**, for three reasons. The figure is her own action (reasoning studies define a promise as a conditional whose outcome is "a potential action of the speaker", row 34). The co-sponsor will act on it. And she says it once, with no years to teach a caveat. The plan makes that hearing true. The moved half sits in Treasuries before she speaks, so her statement only reports something already done. In Bassetto's words (2019), "actions speak louder than words".
3. **Claim 3: CHANGE.** "Whatever its caveats" is too strong for every listener. Markets mostly heard the Fed's dates as forecasts, and the Riksbank says years of repeating "a forecast, not a promise" made the caveat "widely understood". The claim holds for the listeners Laura has: people who budget on the figure, hear it once and know she controls it. Write it as: "a figure Laura states in 2031 will be heard as a promise by the people who budget on it, whatever caveat comes with it, so she states only figures she already owns, and rounds each end down." The rounding rests on NUMBERS 4 and Q71. This card adds why it matters: the RBA's "promise" broke in a direction its listeners had not planned for.
4. **Claim 4: the RBA, on the merits.** In no other case did both the bank's own review and an independent review find that a forecast was heard as "a promise", broken, at a cost to the bank's reputation. The Fed's minutes of 9 Aug 2011 add the warning written in advance (row 21), and Campbell et al. add the two words. The choice needs no mention of the team's home.

**Judge's notes (aha candidates):**
- **A. The RBA did what its condition said and was still found to have broken a promise.** Inflation came early, rates rose, which is what the condition allowed, and the public judged it against the year. Caveats did not protect the figure. Only owning the money behind it does that (rows 1-3, 11).
- **B. The cures run in opposite directions.** The central banks cured it by saying less: guidance that "would not be time-specific", and the RBA "does not intend to publish its own forecasts of the expected policy path" (row 8). Laura cannot say less, because the case asks her for a dollar range. So she owns first and speaks second. Central banks learned to promise less; Laura buys before she speaks. This adds to SKELETON 3c's aha "The forecast barely touches the promise; speaking early does".
- **C. Speaking early also bends the speaker.** The Review found the 2024 guidance "may have affected the openness of the RBA to respond to early signs of inflation by upgrading the inflation forecasts" (row 17). A figure stated before the move would give Laura a number to defend on the day she measures the top.
- **D. The Fed wrote down the risk on the first day.** From the minutes of 9 Aug 2011: "One member expressed concern that the use of a specific date in the forward guidance would be seen by the public as an unconditional commitment" (row 21).
- **E. Odysseus had a rope; central banks had only their reputation.** Laura's rope is the Treasuries she owns before the meeting (row 25). The report's conceit is pencil and ink, so the rope would be a second image. Use the words Delphic and Odyssean as vocabulary at most, or put it in the conceit's terms (row 39).

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 2 (with row 1).** The RBA's own words: a conditional forecast ("until 2024 at the earliest") was heard as "a promise", and raising rates earlier was seen as "having broken 'its promise'", with "considerable reputational damage". This is the case's warning, already lived through by a central bank. For 3c.
2. **Row 8 (aha B).** Central banks cured it by saying less ("would not be time-specific"; no published policy path). Laura cannot say less, so she owns first and speaks second. This adds to 3c's aha "speaking early does".
3. **Row 34.** Listeners treat a conditional as a promise when its outcome is "a potential action of the speaker". A central bank's rate and Laura's contribution are both the speaker's own action, so both are heard as promises. This answers which kind a co-sponsor hears: Odyssean.
4. **Rows 24 and 28.** Odysseus had a rope and central banks had only reputation. Laura's rope is the Treasuries she owns before the meeting, and in Bassetto's words, "actions speak louder than words". Use as vocabulary, in the conceit's terms (row 39).
5. **Row 17 (aha C).** Speaking early bent the RBA's own forecasts ("may have affected the openness of the RBA to respond to early signs of inflation"). Stating nothing before the move keeps Laura's own judgment free on the day the top is measured.
