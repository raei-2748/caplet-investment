## Part B. Where the image would mislead even with correct numbers

1. **Ink on an iBonds fund.** Ink is a fixed line, but an iBonds fund "does not seek to return any predetermined
   amount" (CANON B20, B23). Its last months are held in cash, and up to about 2% of a rung depends on the rate that
   cash earns (NUMBERS l.204). Its board can end it early, merge it or extend it (CANON C2). Five rungs, the floor and
   the moved half are all such funds. The design copes, because each is counted at zero interest and the stated ends
   are cut 0.5%. So the honest use is to ink the stated figure, not the fund's payout. *Verdict:* use ink only for
   figures counted that way, never for "what IBTM pays".
2. **Ink and price.** Ink describes what is paid on a date. A Treasury's price still moves, by about $300 per basis
   point on the ten (NUMBERS l.74). A bond reader will hear "inked" as "immune to markets" (CANON C1). *Verdict:*
   dollars on dates only, never values.
3. **Bleed for her flexibility.** A bleed is a fixed 1/8 inch, set wider than a known 1-2 mm blade wander, and it is
   cut off and thrown away (Q40 items 4a and 4c). Her flexibility fails that match in five ways:
   - its size varies ($9,881 / $20,739 / $41,258, NUMBERS l.263), and it is about a third smaller in the years shares
     fall (8f);
   - it is not a natural hedge: it can be smallest when building costs press (FACTS_PLAIN l.56);
   - one of its three uses, a co-sponsor falling short, is a missing panel, not a wandering blade;
   - it stays hers and sits outside the announced range (CANON D12, check 7), whereas a bleed belongs to the page and
     is discarded;
   - the mapping's "never shows a white edge" is false: on its own it covers a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar in 93.2% of
     simulated futures (NUMBERS l.17).

   The bleed also suggests a margin sized to a cost, and the case says teams need not size a contingency fund
   (case l.106-107). *Verdict:* the wrong money. Move the bleed to the excess (C3).
4. **Colour for VT.** "Goes on last" is true: in WInS, VT was bought last from the cash left, 167 units against 164
   (SKELETON 2.5), and in the plan it is the last use of the 2028 money. But colour never becomes line art, while half
   of VT becomes Treasuries before 2031. The mapping contradicts itself here ("only colour" against "she inks the
   top"). CONCEIT's own definition also makes VT pencil (l.30-31). *Verdict:* drop colour. "Erase the pencils" does the
   deletion test without a third term (C4).
5. **"Erased" for the first plan.** Erased pencil is gone. This record is evidence the report has to show, and the IPS
   already shows it. "Rubbed out" (WORDS l.53) is closer but still suggests gone. *Verdict:* pencils kept and shown on
   purpose (C11), or plain words.
6. **"Finished issues" for the ten.** Finished issues stand alone. The ten share the extra Treasuries at zero interest
   (NUMBERS l.167). *Verdict:* say "all ten owned", never "each stands alone".
7. **"Inked inside the line" for the bottom.** This is not a craft practice: an inker draws on the pencils (CONCEIT
   l.60). The craft fact that does fit is the safe area: text kept back from the cut (Q40 item 4a). *Verdict:* use the
   safe area for the bottom.
8. **"Inks the top before showing anyone the page."** Exact, and the best row in the mapping. The moved half goes into
   a fund that pays its net asset value again (CANON C22), so the top's ink carries the funds-cost exception (C3).
9. **"Redraw the page" for the two reasons.** Exact once said as the pencil never arriving (no 2028 deposit) or the ink
   failing (a Treasury default). "Redraw" overclaims a little: a missed deposit changes sizes in the IPS's order, and
   the ten bought in 2027 stay. A smaller deposit is not a reason at all (CANON D9).
10. **"Only inked numbers are shown."** True of what Laura says in 2031 (NUMBERS l.211). False of this report, which
    must show today's modelled figures. The report's figures are pencil and say so ("we modelled", "on today's market
    rates"). Otherwise the conceit's own rule convicts the report on every page of Section 3.
11. **Pencil for her career.** Pencil says "may change". The IPS's thesis says something stronger: her income falls
    when shares fall and co-sponsors cut (l.9), a holding she cannot sell. Pencil carries the timing, not the
    correlation. *Verdict:* the image must not carry the thesis. State the thesis in the IPS's words (CANON A1-A2).

## Part C. Better parallels, ranked by what each teaches a judge

Each entry gives the parallel, the plan fact it rests on, what it teaches, its risk, and one plain example sentence
(not report prose). The figures are NUMBERS figures. The craft verdicts are Q40's unless marked "to verify".

### 1. Ink only goes one way: lines can be added later, never taken off cleanly
- **Plan fact.** Each end is rounded down, never up (NUMBERS l.255, l.260). Anything the funds pay above the stated top
  ($8,476) joins her flexibility (l.262). Half the shares are promised to no one ($21,347, l.263). Each $5,000 more of
  stated top costs about $5,300 of what she keeps (NUMBERS 8o; FACTS_PLAIN l.53). Overpromising costs "credibility"
  (case l.114). Later spending from her flexibility is her separate choice, outside the range (CANON D12).
- **Teaches.** Why the design has room only on the upside. It predicts three facts a judge can check: every rounding
  goes down, every surplus goes to unpromised money, and she keeps the ability to give more. It ties the image to
  Klein (2001, a lead donor who "decides later") and to Arrow and Fisher (1974, a promise can be raised but never taken
  back), which SKELETON 3b already cites.
- **Craft.** Ink is fixed only by white paint or a patch, visible on the original (Q40 item 3, TRUE). That a line can
  always be added needs no source.
- **Risk.** It must never read as a promise to add money (CANON A30). The decision belongs to her.
- **Example.** "Ink can be added to a page but never taken off it cleanly, and the range is built the same way: each end is rounded down to the nearest $5,000 and anything the funds pay above the top joins her flexibility, so in 2033 Laura may choose to give more than she stated and, barring a US default, will not have to take any of it back."

### 2. Each line has its own inking date
- **Plan fact.** The ten payments are inked in January 2028 by the first $38,282 of the 2028 deposit (WORDS l.24;
  NUMBERS l.161). The bottom is inked when the floor is bought that same month, and the top when the shares move
  before the 2031 meetings (NUMBERS l.326-328). Her flexibility is never inked (IPS l.15). Before the bottom is inked
  it could land anywhere from $85,000 to $115,000, and the top from $95,000 to $140,000, at 1 in 20 each way
  (l.337); after, each is one figure.
- **Teaches.** Certainty has dates, and the confidence statement changes in kind on two of them. The inking also
  follows lines the IPS already drew (the 2028 order and the move are planned steps, CANON l.12-14), so nothing is
  decided at the inking stage. This corrects both "inked from 2027" and "fully inked by January 2028".
- **Craft.** Pencils, then inks, as separate passes (Q40 item 1a, TRUE).
- **Risk.** Low, provided each ink sentence carries its date.
- **Example.** "The ten payments and the bottom of her range are inked in January 2028, when the first $38,282 of the 2028 deposit and the floor are bought; the top is inked only when half the shares move into Treasuries before the 2031 meetings, which is why, barring a US default, Laura states no figure before then."

### 3. The top is the trim line and the excess is the bleed; the bottom sits in the safe area
- **Plan fact.** Each end is what the holdings pay with no further interest, less 0.5%, rounded down (NUMBERS
  l.208-210). Her contribution equals the stated top in every simulated future and every historical start (l.261),
  because the funds pay past it: $8,476 centrally, $4,340 to $11,218 at 1 in 20 (l.262). Fund costs up to 0.5% are
  covered everywhere. At 0.75% the top falls short in about 1 simulated future in 17, by a few hundred dollars (l.233;
  prose says "a few hundred dollars", since SKELETON 3c cuts $429 from the text). The bottom is the figure
  co-sponsors count, which holds under every exception but a US default (SKELETON 3c, 3d).
- **Teaches.** The whole range rule and its confidence, including its one exception, sized as a printer sizes a bleed:
  for small mechanical error, not for a mis-cut (a US default). It answers the lay reader's question in SKELETON 3c
  ("if the contribution always lands at the top, why state a bottom?"): the top is where the blade falls, and the
  bottom is the text kept back from it. One honest difference should be said: the strip cut from the contribution is
  not thrown away but joins her flexibility. This replaces the mapping's bleed.
- **Craft.** Bleed 1/8 inch against 1-2 mm of wander, and text kept inside the safe area (Q40 items 4a and 4c, TRUE).
  The width of the safe area varies (4d): never print one figure for it.
- **Risk.** Two terms in one sentence, both explained there. Use the bleed once in the report.
- **Example.** "Her contribution runs exactly to the top she states in every simulated future, because the funds are counted so that they pay past it, the way comic art is printed past the trim line so that a slightly wandering blade never leaves a white edge, and the strip cut off is not thrown away but joins her flexibility."

### 4. Erase the pencils and the inked page still reads (the deletion test)
- **Plan fact.** If VT went to zero, the ten payments would not change, the floor would still pay $103,178, the range
  would be one figure, $95,000, the contribution would be $95,000, and the $8,178 excess would be hers (NUMBERS
  l.311-315).
- **Teaches.** It tests Trading Note 3's "where we allow ourselves to be wrong" (TNA l.23): the shares add to the plan
  and support nothing. SKELETON's rank 6 asks for this test. It is better than CONCEIT's "colour on a scan", because
  erasing the pencils is the real production step and needs no third term.
- **Craft.** Graphite pencils are erased after inking, and only the inked line reproduces (Q40 item 2, TRUE).
- **Risk.** Low.
- **Example.** "Erasing a comic's pencils once the ink is dry is the test the plan passes if the world share fund falls to zero: the ten payments do not change, the floor still pays about $103,000, and, barring a US default, Laura states one figure, $95,000."

### 5. Her own trade pays on roughs and prints only accepted art
- **Plan fact.** The 2028 deposit comes from advances, speaking and licensing (case l.44-45). Illustrated-book
  advances are commonly paid in thirds, "on signature of the contract, delivery of roughs, delivery and acceptance of
  the artwork" (Association of Illustrators, VERIFIED in `research/cards/Q12b_lauras_working_life_gaps.md` row 16).
  For a deal signed in 2026, only the signing part is sure to land before January 2028 (Q12b rows 17-18). This is why
  a missed deposit is reason 1, and why Table 3 row 1 asks for written confirmation by 1 December 2027 (SKELETON's
  Book 17 gems).
- **Teaches.** Why the 2028 deposit is pencil until it arrives, and why the ten wait until January 2028 for their ink.
  It also shows that her co-sponsors get the terms her publishers give her: nothing counts until it is delivered. It
  is the most client-specific parallel available.
- **Craft.** Q40 item 7, TRUE.
- **Risk.** Never claim the terms of her own contracts, which are unknown (Q12 row 1).
- **Example.** "An illustrator's advance is commonly paid in parts, the last only on delivery and acceptance of the finished art, so the 2028 deposit is pencil until it arrives, and the ten payments are inked only when its first $38,282 is spent in January 2028."

### 6. Non-photo blue: the report's modelled figures are construction lines, dropped before printing
- **Plan fact.** No figure is stated before the move (NUMBERS l.211). A top stated 1, 3, 6 or 12 months early is not
  yet owned in 2.9%, 6.3%, 8.7% or 10.7% of simulated futures (l.344). The report gives "about $95,000 to $115,000 on
  today's figures" and the rule, "never the figures as a promise" (l.367).
- **Teaches.** It resolves the mapping's contradiction (B10). The judges see the construction lines, and the
  co-sponsors of 2031 see only inked figures. Printing early has a measured cost.
- **Craft.** The blue is dropped out on purpose before printing (Q40 item 2). Modern scanners can see it, so say
