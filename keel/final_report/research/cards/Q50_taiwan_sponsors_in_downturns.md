- **Problem class:** counterparty risk on money promised for later, when the promiser's ability to pay moves with the economy.
- **Field that met it first:** fundraising's own records of pledge collection. The credit field's "wrong-way risk" is the parallel: the promiser is most likely to fail when you need them most. This card uses Taiwan's records: NCAF's annual reports, the Ministry of Culture's 文化統計, Ministry of Finance tax tables, and surveys.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE. Some co-sponsors cut, in the same year, but the big named ones did not.** Keep the IPS line as a risk; never say every co-sponsor cuts.
  - **Big named sponsors.** Recognised company and private arts giving set its record in the window covering the crash.
    - The 9th Wenxin Award (2008) recognised "逾16億元" (over NT$1.6bn).
    - The 10th (2010, covering about 2008-09) recognised about NT$2.2bn, up to about 37% more (Keel arithmetic).
    - No later edition with a published total has beaten it (rows 1-2).
  - **Small pooled money.** It fell.
    - NCAF's fundraising was 19% under budget in 2008 and fell 17.2% in 2009.
    - It rose 42.7% in 2010, a year after shares turned (row 5).
  - **Company budgets track this year's profit, not last year's** (row 19, NCAF survey: p=0.007 against p=0.975). So when a firm cuts, it cuts in the same year, as the IPS says.
  - **Company giving in tax data** fell 3.3% in 2020 and rose 64.8% in 2021 (row 30).
  - **Judge's note:** Taiwan's record for recognised company arts giving was set in the two years of the global crash, and it has not been beaten since.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE. Neither crisis fits "public up, private down".**
  - **2008-09.** Public money rose: CCA spending was +29.1% in 2009 (Q10d row 20). Named private giving also rose (row 1). NCAF's regular grants dipped about 6% (Q4b row 29).
  - **2020-21.** MoC subsidies to arts groups nearly doubled in 2020, from NT$7.42bn to NT$13.32bn, mostly relief. In 2021 they halved, to NT$6.33bn in approved amounts (row 26). Named private giving held: "over NT$2bn" for 2019-20 and NT$1.87bn for 2021-22 (row 2).
  - **2020-21 was not a downturn for Taiwanese shares.** The TAIEX rose 22.8% and 23.66%, and GDP grew 3.1-3.4% and 6.5-6.6% (row 29). The fall came in 2022 (-22.40%), and Taiwan's only sponsorship count dropped its money bands before it could measure that window (row 3).
  - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan the arts' worst years (2020-21) were boom years for shares. The co-sponsor risk depends on the kind of shock (a shutdown, a disaster, a profit fall), not on a fixed link to share prices.
- **Claim 3: KILL as a general rule. Taiwanese givers renew a year at a time; the only multi-year money that reliably arrived was paid up front.**
  - **Firms.** The survey's words: "企業無法進行長期贊助" (firms cannot sponsor long-term) (row 20).
  - **NCAF's five-year pledge scheme (1996).** 141 honorary members pledged NT$200,000 a year. 137 paid in year one, 46 in year five and none by year seven. Year five's money was 32% of year one's (row 8).
  - **A law-backed ten-year capital promise.** NCAF's NT$10bn fund "在十年內收足" (to be collected within ten years) stood at about 60% of that target fourteen years later (row 18).
  - **The multi-year terms that worked:**
    - fixed-term programmes, such as Tung Ho Steel's three-year artist residency launched in 2009 (row 14) and partners serving in three- to four-year blocks (row 16);
    - money paid on day one, such as an anonymous NT$3m for three years in 2006, booked a year at a time (row 12).
  - **Conditions.** No Taiwanese multi-year capital pledge with written conditions was found (Gaps). The conditions on public grants are in Q11d.
  - **Judge's note:** Taiwan's national arts fund once collected a five-year pledge from 141 donors. 137 paid in year one, 46 in year five, and none in year seven. A pledge is pencil; only paid money is ink.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE.**
  - **The date and name.** The Act is the rewrite of 19 May 2021 (文化藝術獎助及促進條例), not a 2019 law. Its older name, 文化藝術獎助條例, is the one cited in NCAF's 1994 establishing act.
  - **What gets an uncapped deduction:**
    - a gift through NCAF or a city or county culture foundation, which counts as a gift to government (Art. 27; store Q11b-018);
    - gifts of artworks, heritage buildings and their land to government (Art. 28).
  - **A gift straight to a private residency** falls under the general 10%-of-income cap for firms (row 35).
  - **Scope.** The case puts Taiwan tax matters out of scope (case p.4), so this is one line of Client Knowledge at most.
- **Claim 5: CHANGE for Taiwanese firms.**
  - **Firms cut in the same year their profit falls** (row 19).
  - **Foundations under pressure cap their total budget first** (row 22).
  - **The US lag of a year or more (Q4b) belongs to endowed grant-makers.** Nine in ten Taiwanese foundations are operating foundations that raise money and run programmes, not grant-makers (row 23).
- **The use question: how big were the falls, and how fast the recovery? (Keel arithmetic on the sources' figures)**

  | Money stream | Years | Fall | Recovery |

  | Named sponsorship (Wenxin) | 2008-09 window | none | n/a |

  - **For the 2031 range:** none of this money can stand behind a stated figure. That is already the plan's rule.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 8, the five-year pledge that faded** (3d; 3c; the conceit).
   - 137 payers in year one, 46 in year five, none in year seven.
   - This is Taiwan's own record of why Laura states only money she owns. The pencil-and-ink contrast is the judge's to see, not hers to say to co-sponsors.
2. **Rows 1-2, the record set in the crash years** (2.3; 3e).
   - The judge expects co-sponsors to vanish in a crash. In Taiwan the big named ones gave a record, and the small untied money fell (row 5).
   - So the IPS's line is right as a risk, and the plan never needs to bet on which way it goes.
3. **Row 18, NCAF's ten-year NT$10bn promise at about 60%** (3c; 3d). Even a promise written into law stayed partly unpaid. NCAF's grants ran on the money it held, which is the plan's rule in another country's public life.
4. **Row 19, this year's profit sets this year's sponsorship** (2.3). It gives the IPS's "during that same period" an evidence base for Taiwanese corporate co-sponsors: no lag, unlike US foundations.
5. **Row 14, Tung Ho Steel's three-year residency, launched in 2009 and paid in kind** (3d). It is the nearest real parallel to Laura's facility. It shows the multi-year form that does survive a recession: a fixed-term programme built from what the sponsor already has.
   - **Runner-up:** row 29 (2020-21 was a boom for Taiwanese shares) as a Judge's note in 3e.
