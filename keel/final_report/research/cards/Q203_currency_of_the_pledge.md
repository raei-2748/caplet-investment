## The ideas, each in three plain sentences

### Idea 1. "Maintenance of value": the World Bank's fund for the poorest countries once made its donors keep their gift's worth, and then switched the rule off

- **(a) The parallel.** When the International Development Association (IDA), the World Bank's fund for the poorest countries, took a member's money in that member's own currency, its founding rules said that if the currency later fell, the member must pay "an additional amount of its own currency sufficient to maintain the value, as of the time of subscription". If the currency rose, IDA paid the difference back. The duty lasted only until IDA had spent the money or "exchanged [it] for the currency of another member".
- **(b) What it shows about Laura's plan.** That is exactly what the campaign director asks of her: the stated figure stays in US dollars, and her flexibility pays an extra amount on top to keep the gift's Taiwan-dollar worth, until the day the residency changes her dollars. IPS l.15's "a stronger Taiwan dollar" is that clause in four words. It also explains why card Q24 releases the currency part of her flexibility once the money is converted: IDA's duty ended at the same moment, for the same reason.
- **(c) Where it breaks, and what the break teaches.** IDA's members were governments that could tax or print their own currency, and the clause ran both ways. Laura's extra can come only from shares promised to no one, and it would only ever add. IDA itself now writes into each round that maintenance of value "will not be applicable" (IDA20, 2022), and fixes reference rates and hedges instead. So she can offer the top-up as an intention with a known size, never as a promise. The honest form is one-way, capped by what her flexibility holds, and announced as a separate gift when it is paid (CANON D12).
- **Decision it serves:** IPS l.15's second use of her flexibility, and the currency clause of the 2031 statement.
- **Report part:** 3d (one clause) and 3b. Under ATTACKS D6 it is a candidate for 3d's one outside parallel: it is finance's own practice, a reader can check it, and it does analytical work. Never in Laura's words.
- **Source:** IDA Articles of Agreement, Art. IV §2 (a), (b) and proviso; IDA Resolution No. 248 (31 Mar 2022), para 6(d). Read at source.
- **Pedigree or aha:** aha. A finance judge knows the World Bank. Few know that "maintenance of value" is the exact term for what the judges asked, or that the world's largest pool of donor money wrote it in and then switched it off. It shows the IPS's phrase has a precise and tested meaning, and where that meaning stops.

### Idea 2. Name the day: multi-donor funds count each pledge at one named day's rate, then carry the move themselves

- **(a) The parallel.** When dozens of governments pledge to the Global Fund in their own currencies, the Fund turns every pledge into US dollars at "the market exchange rate applicable on the date of the official announcement". Within days it hedges "75% or more of contributions that have been signed". IDA instead uses a six-month average fixed in advance, because the method "should mitigate short-term currency fluctuations" and avoids "selecting among different sets of foreign exchange rates".
- **(b) What it shows about Laura's plan.** Laura's co-sponsors are a small campaign of the same kind. So the honest statement is her figure in her own currency, US dollars, beside its Taiwan-dollar value at one published rate on a named day (the central bank's closing rate, store Q26-025). The residency, which counts in Taiwan dollars, then decides when to change the money.
- **(c) Where it breaks, and what the break teaches.** Those funds can hedge with forward contracts. The Global Fund even has a rule for a pledge made after rates moved: "a gain or loss greater than $20 million" triggers an averaged rate. Laura's plan holds no derivatives, so its only tool is a reserve, and that is what her flexibility is. The store's US Defense Department account (card Q69) is the same choice, made by a body the law bars from hedging. So the Taiwan-dollar figure is a translation on a named day, never a second promise.
- **Decision it serves:** the currency and the rate of the 2031 statement.
- **Report part:** 3d.
- **Source:** Muniu (2025), Global Fund Observer 465; Global Fund Comprehensive Funding Policy (2016), D.4 and E.2; IDA (2016), *Effective foreign exchange rates for use in the IDA18 replenishment*. Read at source.
- **Pedigree or aha:** pedigree for anyone in development finance; NICHE for a Wharton judge. Its job is to show that "US dollars plus a dated Taiwan-dollar value" is how the largest donor campaigns already do it.

### Idea 3. Whoever receives foreign money counts it on the day it lands, and Taiwan asks for the bank's exchange slip

- **(a) The parallel.** University gift offices write a pledge in their own currency, but count a foreign donor's money at its value on the day it arrives. CASE's UK and Ireland standard says gifts from overseas donors are "counted according to the value on the date they were transferred to your institution", and Carnegie Mellon, Fordham and Alberta say the same in their own words. Taiwan's Ministry of Culture tells its grantees to convert every foreign-currency sum into Taiwan dollars, note the rate, and attach the bank's exchange slip (「應附兌換水單」).
- **(b) What it shows about Laura's plan.** Whatever Laura says in 2031, the residency's books, and any report it makes to the Ministry, will count her gift in Taiwan dollars at the rate of the day the money is changed, in or after December 2032. The only figure that can be exact before then is the US-dollar one. That is why SD-1's "her side of the sum is exact" is true in US dollars only, and has to say so.
- **(c) Where it breaks, and what the break teaches.** None of the four gift-office policies read says what happens to a pledge written in a foreign currency: they count cash, not promises. So the currency of a cross-border pledge has to be written into the pledge itself. A recipient that writes the pledge in its own currency puts the move on the donor. Laura cannot honestly accept that, because nothing she owns pays Taiwan dollars: a promise is ink only in the currency its Treasuries pay (FAS 76, store Q173-003).
- **Decision it serves:** SD-1's wording, and 3d's currency clause.
- **Report part:** 3d.
- **Source:** CASE (2025), A.4.1 and A.7.8; Carnegie Mellon (2017); Fordham (2020); University of Alberta (2012); 文化部 (2023), settlement note 19. Read at source.
- **Pedigree or aha:** known practice; the Taiwan exchange slip is NICHE. It settles which of the two figures is fixed, with the funder's own paperwork.

### Idea 4. Ink in her ledger, pencil in theirs

- **(a) The parallel.** An accountant books a sum owed in a foreign currency at that day's rate, then rewrites it at each year-end's rate and puts the difference through that year's result (IAS 21, whose text Australia's AASB 121 repeats, paras 21, 23 and 28). Taiwan's Ministry of Culture makes the foundations it oversees keep their books and accounts in Taiwan dollars (Art. 5), with Taiwan's business accounting standards behind them (Art. 2).
- **(b) What it shows about Laura's plan.** If the residency booked Laura's US-dollar figure as money due to it, its Taiwan-dollar value would be rubbed out and rewritten every year-end until paid. In her own book the US-dollar figure never moves. The same figure is ink in her ledger and pencil in theirs, so the Taiwan-dollar number in 3d is a pencil line by its nature, and must carry its date every time it is printed.
- **(c) Where it breaks, and what the break teaches.** A 2031 statement may never be booked as money due at all: that takes a signed pledge (card Q200). So the image says what the Taiwan-dollar number is, not what any ledger will show. The conceit is never used in Laura's own words to co-sponsors.
- **Decision it serves:** how 3d prints the Taiwan-dollar figure (always dated).
- **Report part:** 3d, the report's own sentence beside her words; a candidate for 3d's one turn, competing with the turn SKELETON already holds there.
- **Source:** AASB 121 (compiled), paras 21-29; 全國性文化事務財團法人會計處理及財務報告編製準則 (2019, rev. 2025), Arts 2 and 5. Read at source.
- **Pedigree or aha:** the accounting is textbook. The turn is the aha: one figure is ink or pencil depending on whose currency you count it in.

### Idea 5. Running money in a currency that strengthens: the Global Fund's Swiss-franc costs and Laura's ten payments

- **(a) The parallel.** The Global Fund raises US dollars but pays much of its own running cost in Swiss francs. Between its funding cycles the franc rose "from 1.03 to 1.24" US dollars, and its board was told that a US$930 million running budget had a true value of "approximately $834 million", about a tenth less (Keel arithmetic).
- **(b) What it shows about Laura's plan.** Her ten payments have the same shape. The case fixes them in US dollars and the residency spends them in Taiwan dollars, so a stronger Taiwan dollar shrinks every year's running money. Nothing in the plan is held against that: the IPS names her flexibility in its paragraph on the building (l.15). In every run of ten Januaries since 2001, the best January's $50,000 bought between a tenth and a fifth more Taiwan dollars than the worst January's (Keel arithmetic, rows 25-26).
- **(c) Where it breaks, and what the break teaches.** The Fund can cut its own costs or move them; the residency's costs are in Taiwan by nature. Its programme funders carry the move, as grantees of a US-dollar funder usually do (store Q69 rows 19-23). So 3e owes programme funders one plain clause, and no remedy the plan does not have.
- **Decision it serves:** none changes; it answers campaign director 34.
- **Report part:** 3e.
- **Source:** Global Fund, *2026-2028 Allocation Period: Sources and Uses of Funds* (54th Board Meeting), Annex 2 point 6. Read at source. FRED EXTAUS monthly via the repo mirror (verified in full by NUMBERS 8l).
- **Pedigree or aha:** NICHE. A real institution's own board paper shows the payments' currency problem at scale.

## Best three for the report

1. **Rows 1-4: maintenance of value (Idea 1).** It turns IPS l.15's "a stronger Taiwan dollar" into a precise, tested clause: an extra amount, paid on top, ending the day the money is changed. It also shows where the clause stops. IDA's version was two-way and binding, and IDA now switches it off. Laura's is one-way, capped by her flexibility, and an intention, never a promise. A finance judge sees that the team knew the precedent and the reason for the difference.
2. **Rows 12, 18, 21 and 29-32: the named day, and the 3d wording.** Gift offices and Taiwan's Ministry count foreign money on the day it lands; Taiwan wants the bank's exchange slip. So only the US-dollar figure can be exact before December 2032. 3d says so in one sentence, with the Taiwan-dollar value at a named day's rate.
3. **Rows 11 and 25-27: the payments' value in Taiwan dollars.** The Global Fund's franc costs lost about a tenth of their value. Laura's payments, fixed in US dollars by the case, bought between a tenth and a fifth more Taiwan dollars in the best January of any ten since 2001 than in the worst. One clause in 3e tells programme funders this plainly.

**Verdicts on the named attacks:**

- **Campaign director 4: stands; the claim changes.**
  - The notes never put the currency in her words, and "her side of the sum is exact" holds in US dollars only.
  - The report should say, in 3d:
    - she states US dollars, because her Treasuries pay US dollars;
    - beside it, the Taiwan-dollar value at a named day's published rate ($115,000 was about NT$3.77 million on 6 November 2026, at 32.768);
    - the Taiwan-dollar figure becomes exact only when the residency changes the money;
    - if the Taiwan dollar is stronger by then, she means to make up the difference from her flexibility, as an extra gift announced when paid (CANON D12; never "will", never inside the stated figure).
  - SD-1 becomes "exact in US dollars".
- **Campaign director 34: stands (minor).**
  - One clause in 3e: "The ten payments are fixed in US dollars, as the case sets them, so what each buys in Taiwan moves with the rate that January. In every run of ten Januaries since 2001, the best January's $50,000 bought between a tenth and a fifth more Taiwan dollars than the worst's. Her flexibility is kept for the building, so funders of the residency's programmes should know this."
- **Finance professor 10: stands; the claim changes.** This agrees with card Q201.
