- **What is new here:** world shares (MSCI World and ACWI in US dollars) instead of TAIEX or the S&P 500; six economies instead of two; tender-price indices (what an owner pays at award) beside input-cost indices (what a contractor pays); and Laura's own window shape (VT bought, half kept valued five years later, building costs over the last two years and the build).
- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Building costs tend to rise in the years world shares fall."
  2. "So the money Laura keeps unpromised is likely to be smallest when a building costs more than planned."
  3. "A recession usually cuts tender prices" (the opposite claim).
  4. "Taiwan's building costs rose 14.0% in 2008 while its stock market fell 46.0%" is a fair example of the risk.
  5. "The inflationary falls (1974, 2022) are the cases where the two meet."
- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** the co-movement of a reserve and the claims on it (asset-liability matching; see card Q5e), with the building side answered by construction economics: the gap between tender prices (output) and building costs (inputs) over the business cycle. Construction economists named and modelled that gap first (McCaffer, McCaffrey and Thorpe, 1983, "The disparity between construction cost and tender price movements"; Runeson, 1988; Akintoye and Skitmore, 1993), and public cost offices measured it with separate indices (UK PUBSEC against NOCOS; Hong Kong's ArchSD tender index against its labour and material index).

## Verdicts in one screen

**Method (Keel arithmetic).** World shares: MSCI World, US dollars, gross, calendar years 1970-2025 (the table in `numbers/fall40.py`; 2001-2025 checked equal to MSCI's month-end levels in `cards/data/Q61/`; 1970-2000 from Wikipedia, UNVERIFIED at MSCI), and MSCI ACWI month-ends from Jan 1997 (price to Dec 2000, read at MSCI today; gross after, card Q61's read). Building costs, one series per economy for pooling: Taiwan DGBAS input index (yearly average; the repo copy in `numbers/taiwan_joint.py`, card Q10e's read of 15 Nov; DGBAS refuses curl, 403), US Turner selling-price index (card Q5e's read), Hong Kong ArchSD Building Works Tender Price Index (read today), UK PUBSEC tender price index (read today; 1970-79 and 1990-2006 only), Singapore BCA tender price index (read today; 2005-2023), Japan MLIT construction cost deflator (read today; fiscal years April-March, partial). Every series is in `cards/data/Q66_building_costs_world_shares.csv` with its source. "Above trend" = the year's change less the series' own average change over the previous five years (Q5e's M6 convention), a stand-in for "more than planned".

1. **Claim 1: CHANGE.** Building costs do rise in most years world shares fall, but they rise in most other years too, and no faster than their own trend.
   - **The eight falls.** MSCI World fell more than 10% in eight calendar years since 1970: 1973 (-14.5%), 1974 (-24.5%), 1990 (-16.5%), 2000 (-12.9%), 2001 (-16.5%), 2002 (-19.5%), 2008 (-40.3%), 2022 (-17.7%). More than 20% only in 1974 and 2008.
   - **Same year, pooled across six economies:** costs rose in **28 of 35** country-years (80%; mean +7.8%, median +5.5%). In years world shares rose: 140 of 172 (81%; mean +4.3%). The seven that fell are all 2000-2002 in Asia (Hong Kong, Japan, Taiwan).
   - **Against their own trend:** above it in **15 of 30** fall country-years (50%), against 86 of 178 (48%) in other years. By five points or more: 6 of 30 (20%) against 28 of 178 (16%). No tendency.
   - **The two big crashes (1974, 2008):** costs rose in the crash year in **all 9** country-years (mean +16.0%), then fell or stalled in **7 of 9** the next year (mean -8.6%: Hong Kong -30.3% in 1975, -15.9% in 2009; Singapore -15.5%; Taiwan -8.9%; US Turner -8.4%).
   - **The year after any fall above 10%:** costs ran below their own trend in **25 of 34** country-years (mean 6.5 points below).
   - **On Taiwan's own index, the sign depends on the dates.** In the five world-fall years since 1992 the yearly-average change was +4.4% against +2.7% in other years; December to December it was +1.4% against +3.2%. On matched months (ACWI, 12-month windows, 1997-2026), in the 20 windows where world shares fell more than 20%, Taiwan's costs fell on average (-1.4%; rose in 8 of 20; range -15.0% to +14.2%). Card Q5e row 6's +6.1% after TAIEX falls does not carry over to world shares.
   - **Judge's note:** a building costing more in a crash year is the normal state of building costs, not a link with shares: they rose in 80% of crash country-years and 81% of all others.
2. **Claim 2: CHANGE "is likely to be" to "can be".** On Laura's own window shape the link is U-shaped, not one-way.
   - **Taiwan, every start month Jan 1997 to Sep 2021 (297 windows):** five-year ACWI return (VT bought to half kept valued) against Taiwan's building-cost change over the last two years (the 2031 meetings to the December 2032 contribution). Bottom fifth of share returns (-27.3% to +3.1%): costs +9.6% on average, above 5% a year (NUMBERS 8d's band top) in **37%**. Middle three fifths: +4.5%, 15%. Top fifth (+77.9% to +159.6%): +10.3%, **44%**. Correlation +0.13 (and -0.13 if two years of build are added).
   - **January starts only (Laura's calendar), 25 windows:** world shares lost money over five years for VT bought in 1998, 1999, 2000, 2004 and 2007. Building costs from meetings to contribution beat 5% a year in **3 of those 5** (1999 +13.5%, 2000 +17.9%, 2004 +10.6%) and in **4 of the other 20** (2002 +11.1%, 2003 +24.0%, 2017 +15.8%, 2018 +14.5%).
   - **That rests on two episodes, and the mechanism is a sequence, not a coincidence.** Shares bought near the 2000 peak were still below cost five years later, while Taiwan's costs rose 17.9% from Jan 2003 to Jan 2005 in the world recovery (steel). Shares bought in 2004 met the 2007-08 cost boom before the 2008 crash. It matches NUMBERS 8q's -0.30 (five-year shares against the joint move).
   - **Outside Taiwan the shape is absent.** Same test on calendar years since 1974 (MSCI World five years to year t; costs t-2 to t): US Turner +0.13, US BEA -0.12, Hong Kong tenders +0.25. Bottom-fifth means against all years: Turner +7.0% against +9.2%; BEA +11.1% against +9.8%; Hong Kong +16.5% against +11.8% (its top fifth +27.7%).
   - **Judge's note:** the danger is not that costs jump in the crash; it is that they jump in the recovery, while shares bought at the peak are still under water.
3. **Claim 3: KEEP for tender prices, with a lag; KILL for input costs.** What an owner pays at award (a tender price) falls in a demand slump, usually a year or so after shares; what a contractor pays for materials and labour often keeps rising.
   - **Tender indices fall hard.** Hong Kong's ArchSD index fell 35.7% from Q2 1974 to Q3 1975 and 29.8% from Q3 2008 to Q2 2009; UK PUBSEC fell 20.4% from Q1 1990 to Q4 1992; Singapore's BCA index fell 15.5% in 2009; BCIS's UK index was 13% down on a year in Q2 2009 (Lowery, 2010); Gardiner and Theobald found 2009 "deflation averaging 8.3%" in 12 of 20 European countries (Reina, 2009).
   - **Input indices do not.** UK input costs (NOCOS, all four columns printed) rose 14-20% from Q1 1990 to Q4 1992 while PUBSEC tenders fell 20%; in Q2 2008 UK tender prices were flat while "costs of materials rose 5.5 percent whilst wage rates rose 4.4 percent" (BCIS, 2008). In the US, "Increases in material costs have outpaced bid prices for as long as 28 months", months that "coincided with periods in which the value of nonresidential construction was stagnating or shrinking" (AGC, 2021).
   - **The literature agrees on the mechanism.** UK tender prices rise with building costs and GNP and fall with unemployment (two quarters ahead) and with more firms competing; the model explains 97% of the variance, 1974-1987 (Akintoye and Skitmore, 1993). The gap between tender prices and costs is set by market conditions (McCaffer et al., 1983; Runeson, 1988; read through a 2014 UCL thesis). South Africa: tender prices "decelerate during the recessionary phase" and "In severe recessions the index dropped in absolute terms" (Snyman, 1989, read through Hindle, 1991).
   - **Judge's note:** in the 1990-92 UK slump the materials and labour for a building cost 15% more while the building itself was bought 20% cheaper; which index you watch decides the answer.
4. **Claim 4: KILL as the example.** The +14.0% is Taiwan's 2008 yearly average over 2007's; the -46.0% is TAIEX year-end to year-end. On the same dates (Dec 2007 to Dec 2008) Taiwan's index rose **1.2%** while world shares (ACWI) fell 41.8%. The index peaked in Jul 2008 (+20.8% on a year, while ACWI was down 9.8%) and fell 15.1% to May 2009. Better examples: 2022 (row 21), or the Laura-shaped window of VT bought in Jan 2000 (row 11).
5. **Claim 5: KEEP 2022, CHANGE 1974.** In 2022 every series rose while world shares fell 17.7% (US BEA +16.3%, Singapore +11.6%, Turner +8.0%, Taiwan +7.4%, Japan +6.0%, Hong Kong +5.5%), and US bid prices caught up with input costs only in May 2022 (AGC, June 2022). 1974 split: input and selling-price indices rose (Turner +17.2%, Japan +19.5%, UK tenders +16.1%), but Hong Kong's tender prices then fell 30.3% in 1975 and UK tenders were flat (0.0%) in 1975 with British inflation high.

**The honest form for spine row 9 (3e), in note form, not prose:** building costs do not reliably fall when shares fall, and they do not reliably rise either. Since 1970, in the eight years world shares fell more than 10%, building costs rose in 28 of 35 country-years, about as often as in other years, and beat their own trend about half the time either way. In an inflationary fall (2022) they rose everywhere; in a demand slump, tender prices fell a year or so later, sometimes by a quarter or more. So her flexibility can be smallest in the years a building costs more than planned: in an inflationary fall, or when a cost burst arrives in the recovery while shares bought near a peak are still below cost (Taiwan 2003-05). History shows it as a case to name, not a tendency; the plan claims no hedge in either direction. Never "smallest when it is needed" as a rule.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 2 (with row 3), 3e spine row 9.** The sized answer to the cold reader: costs rose in 28 of 35 country-years when world shares fell more than 10%, and in 140 of 172 when they rose; above trend half the time either way. It turns "an example" into a tested tendency, and the tendency is absent.
2. **Row 8, 3e.** The report's own example compares a yearly average with a year-end close; on the same dates Taiwan's costs rose 1.2% while world shares fell 42%. Fixing it before a judge does is the point.
3. **Row 11 (with rows 9-10), 3b and 3e.** The Laura-shaped example that does hold: shares bought in January 2000 still 6.6% under cost five years later while Taiwan's building costs rose 17.9% over the two years to the contribution. The aha: the danger is the recovery, not the crash.
4. **Row 18 (with rows 13, 26), 3e or a footnote.** UK 1990-92: inputs up 15-20%, tender prices down 20%. A judge who assumes "building costs" is one number learns the owner's price and the builder's costs part company in a slump.
5. **Row 21, 3e and Table 3 row 7.** 2022 is the year every economy's building costs rose while world shares fell 17.7%: the inflationary fall is the case to name, and the one Table 3 row 7 would see first.

## Gaps

- **World shares before 1997 at MSCI.** MSCI's level service refuses dates before 1 Jan 1997; MSCI World's 1970-2000 calendar returns rest on Wikipedia's table (UNVERIFIED). A primary annual series (MSCI's own history file, or Dimson-Marsh-Staunton world returns) would firm up rows 1-5 and 12.
- **VT's own index.** FTSE Global All Cap was not used; ACWI and MSCI World stand in.
- **No Taiwan tender price index.** DGBAS measures inputs; a national award-price series was not found (as Q5e). The Taiwan rows therefore test input costs only.
- **Series not built:** Korea (KICT construction cost index), Australia (ABS producer price indexes for building construction; Rawlinsons), Japan's full deflator years (only parts of the e-Stat table came through), UK PUBSEC 1980-89 and after 2006, BCIS's private all-in tender price index (paywalled), Singapore before 2005 (CEIC lists BCA quarterly from 1987).
- **Originals not read:** McCaffer, McCaffrey and Thorpe (1983); Runeson (1988); Snyman (1989); Wong and Ng (2010) beyond the abstract; the 2014 UCL thesis's author name was not read.
- **UK inflation in 1974-76** was not read, so row 17 states the nominal stall only.
- **Monthly world shares against US, UK or Hong Kong costs** were not run (only Taiwan monthly); the annual tests average away turning points.
- **Independent episodes are few.** About five non-overlapping five-year spans since 1997 for Taiwan; the counts describe the past, not odds for 2031.
