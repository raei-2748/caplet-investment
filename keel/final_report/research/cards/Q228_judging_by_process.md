- Notes for the Final Report store, not report prose.

- **Claims tested:**
  1. (a) "Before 2033 the team can only judge its choices by the process that made them, so its reflection says what it knew, what it assumed and what it would watch, never that the plan worked."
  2. (b) "The WInS gains are no evidence, and saying so is a mark of judgement" (Baron and Hershey's outcome bias; Wharton's own rule).
  3. (c) "Keeping score in advance (what would show the plan was wrong) is how a team avoids hindsight later": what IPS l.17 and Table 3 already do, and what Tetlock's scorekeeping adds, with no new rule or trigger.
- **Problem class:** judging a decision whose result is years away, from one unrepeatable draw of the future. **Field that solved it first:** decision analysis (Howard; Spetzler and colleagues) and judgement research (Baron and Hershey; Fischhoff; Tetlock).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim (a): CHANGE.** Keep the voice (what we knew, assumed, would watch). Drop "only" and "never that the plan worked" as stated.
  - Process is the right test now: Baron and Hershey's "evaluate the decision itself, the quality of thinking that went into it" (p. 571), and Fischhoff: "Good decisions, those which maximally utilize all available knowledge, may have bad outcomes" (1974 version, p. 11).
  - But "can only" overreaches. Some of the plan can be checked now by a fact, not an outcome: what Treasuries are held and what each pays on its date (rows 28-29). What cannot be said is that the shares, the 2031 range or the co-sponsors' pledges "worked". The sentence should split the two, not blanket both.
  - One draw will not settle it even in 2033. Duke: "very often, the quality of the decision doesn't reveal itself except over time" (row 15). A single 2033 does not repeat, so even then the reflection should say "this is what happened", not "this proves".
- **Claim (b): CHANGE.** Keep the rule, drop "a mark of judgement" as praise. Say why the gains carry no weight, in plain words.
  - Baron and Hershey: people rated the same decision better after a good result "although subjects who were asked felt that they should not consider outcomes" (p. 569); "Mere understanding that such confusion contaminates these evaluations is not enough to eliminate it" (p. 578). So refusing the gains is not a sign of a clever team; it is a rule that has to be written before the result comes. The team's IPS l.17 and Wharton's own rule are that kind of rule.
  - A better reason than "bias": outcome information "tells us nothing about the utilities of a decision maker" (p. 570), and one path of one market tells almost nothing about the probabilities (rows 6, 8).
  - **Judge's note:** the experiment found the bias with the chances shown on a roulette wheel (row 3). So a win on a wheel with known odds still raised the rating of the decision. A one-path book gain cannot be cleaner evidence than that.
- **Claim (c): KEEP the idea, CHANGE the words and the credit.**
  - What IPS l.17 and Table 3 already do: they say before any result what the team would act on, and when (31 March 2028; five business days late). Tetlock's scorekeeping adds only precision, a date and a record kept: vague forecasts ("might happen" can mean .1 or .9) cannot be falsified (row 21). No new rule.
  - Honest limit: l.17's two reasons are exits, not tests of whether the plan was wise. They show a promise unsupported (no deposit, a default), not a plan wrong. "What would show the plan was wrong" is therefore the wrong gloss. The right one is "what we said we would do, and when, before the market moved" (rows 31-32).
  - The plan's own thesis is that no market move changes the payments or the plan (l.17: "A fall in the market is not one of them"); Wharton: a plan "should not be rewritten simply because markets move" (guide). So the useful record is the dated statements the report already prints, not a Brier-style scorecard (rows 22, 30).
  - **Judge's note:** on 30 Sep, before any number was run and before the copout objection, the team wrote down a bar for leaving the world fund (RANKING_4). That is Tetlock's and Gardner's step (record first, check later) done by the team in its own record (row 33). Model figures of 30 Sep are on a superseded plan and stay dated.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 5 and row 6 together (3f, one sentence of method).** Knowing the bias does not remove it; outcome tells nothing about utilities. The plan's rule is written before the result, and the gains say nothing about a writer's income.
2. **Row 3 (3e or References).** The bias appeared even with the odds shown on a roulette wheel, so one WInS path is weaker still.
3. **Row 21 (3f / 2.5, method).** Vague statements cannot be scored; every figure the report states should carry its date and unit.
4. **Row 31 (3f).** l.17's reasons are exits, not tests of wisdom. Say "what we said we would do, and when", never "what would show us wrong".
5. **Row 33 (3f, moment 3).** The team wrote its bar for leaving the world fund before the objection came, on 30 Sep. That is process shown, not claimed.
