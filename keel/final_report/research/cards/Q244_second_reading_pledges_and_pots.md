## Verdicts in one screen

**(a) Kept, with the entity softened and the timing added.** Every element now rests on a source read today, and the five-year term rests on two. The pledge: the joint release says "Lehman Brothers has committed $10 million" (PR Newswire, datelined "NEW YORK, Oct. 17"; posted by Spelman on Newswise 17 Oct 2007, 8:40 a.m. EDT), while the next-day press says "said yesterday" (Sorkin, *New York Times*, 17 Oct 2007) and "announced yesterday" (*Chronicle of Philanthropy*, 17 Oct 2007), so Tuesday 16 October. A campus event followed on 17 October (Black College Wire). So "on or about 16-17 October" is right, and the report should say only "in October 2007". The five years: the Clinton Global Initiative's commitment record, written by the partners, says "$10,000,000 over five years in an outright grant", and Masterson (2008) says "a five-year, $10-million partnership". The $3 million: "The college would receive just $3-million of the original pledge" (Masterson, 2010). The timing: Lehman Brothers Holdings Inc. filed for Chapter 11 on 15 September 2008 (its 8-K), 335 days after the announcement, inside the first of the five years (Keel arithmetic). The entity: only the Chronicle (2007) and the Black College Wire write "Holdings". The release and the CGI record say "Lehman Brothers", and no source read names the entity that signed or paid. So the claim becomes "Lehman Brothers' own pledge (the bank's, not its foundation's)", which is what the tree already says.
- Exact words for 3d: "In October 2007 Lehman Brothers promised Spelman College $10 million over five years; eleven months later the bank filed for bankruptcy, and Spelman received $3 million."
- Judge's note: the pledge had the job the case gives Laura's figure. Its own success measures included "Increase in the ability of Spelman College to attract major individual, corporate and foundation donors". After it failed, the college was left "looking for new donors to step into the spot that Lehman Brothers left". A failed first promise takes the signal down with it (case l.111-115). Rows 4-6, 9-11, 14.

**(b) Changed: right words, wrong label, wrong page.** The sentence is word for word: "To record and report on a pledge, you should have a signed document detailing at least the amount, purpose and payment period for the donation." But it is CASE's page 58 (as cited in Taylor, 2021, p. 2). Card Q200 row 1's "p. 2" is Taylor's page. CASE's 2021 contents put "6.3.1 Pledge Documentation ... 58" in "SECTION III: Global Management Guidelines", not in Section II, the Reporting Standards, and its verb is "should have". So it is guidance, not a rule that "needs" anything. Edition: 1st (2021). CASE now sells a 2nd (2024) whose wording was not read, and Taylor's March 2025 revision still quotes the 1st edition unchanged.
- Exact words: "CASE's guidelines ask for 'a signed document detailing at least the amount, purpose and payment period for the donation' (CASE, 2021, p. 58, as cited in Taylor, 2021)." Rows 15-20.

**(c) Changed: the words are right, "define ... unconditional" is not.** FAS 116 para 6 (June 1993) defines a promise to give, any promise, as "a written or oral agreement to contribute cash or other assets to another entity". It then sets the test for booking one: "sufficient evidence in the form of verifiable documentation that a promise was made and received". Whether a promise is unconditional is a separate test. Since June 2018 that test is ASU 2018-08's: a condition needs a barrier and a right of return or release (958-605-25-5A), and FAS 116 para 22's "remote" rule is superseded (958-605-25-12). The Codification's own 25-8 is still behind FASB's login. Its wording is quoted in FASB's open ASU 2016-14 at 958-605-55-19, which adds that "verifiable oral promises, such as those documented by tape recordings" count. So tree l.280's "verifiable paper" should read "verifiable documentation". The "pledge" passage is FAS 116 para 89 (S4 had 90).
- Exact words for 3d: "a gift office books a promise only on 'verifiable documentation that a promise was made and received' (FASB, 1993, para. 6; FASB, 2016, para. 958-605-55-19)".
- Judge's note: neither CASE nor FASB asks whether the giver can pay. Both ask only for evidence that she promised, and a taped phone call can pass. That is why "held" must be shown on its own, and why Laura's plan can show it before she signs. Rows 21-25.

**(d) Kept, word for word.** The live page reads "In the fall of 2025 we completed an updated project budget ... $11,504,266 is the price to complete the project. This includes: ... $500,000 operating seed funds for first two years once open". Card Q204 row 14's "$400,000 operating support during construction" is, on the page, "$400,000 operational support during construction for lost parking and rental revenue, contingency, etc". The page prints no date (APA n.d.). New, for the store: the same page shows a donor slump plus "spiking construction material costs" leaving the building "unfinished", and a 9%-a-year cost escalation in the budget. Rows 26-31.

**(e) Changed: the quotation is cut one item short.** Oman's whole sentence reads: "Even those organizations with well-laid plans for raising working capital, facility reserves, and funds to cover planned deficits during early operations in a new facility often find it difficult to protect these funds from covering cost overruns, fundraising shortfalls, and the desire to 'get it done' when all eyes are focused on construction." The tree stops at "fundraising shortfalls". It is a consultant's essay (Katie Oman, principal of KO Projects, LLC) in Grantmakers in the Arts' journal, *GIA Reader* 29(2), Fall 2018, published October 2018, subtitled "Better Practices for Capital Projects". So "Oman, Grantmakers in the Arts" should read "Oman, writing in the GIA Reader".
- Exact words for 3a: even organisations with well-laid plans "often find it difficult to protect these funds from covering cost overruns, fundraising shortfalls, and the desire to 'get it done' when all eyes are focused on construction" (Oman, 2018).
- Judge's note: the third cause is a wish, not a market. The money goes because people want the building finished. Laura's ten are bought before any building money exists and move only for IPS l.17's two reasons, so "get it done" has nothing to reach. Rows 32-34.

**(f) Kept, with the pin cite and the context fixed.** The quotation is at 895 F.2d at 733 (not 732-733): "There is ample evidence in the record that such undue risk could have been avoided by staggering maturity dates." The risk was having to sell long bonds before maturity to meet a known cash need. The court said long bonds "exposed the fund to greater risk if the bonds needed to be sold prior to maturity". S3's open question resolves: the 70% and the 85% are both right. The opinion gives "70 percent in long-term government bonds, 15 percent in zero coupon bonds (strips)" at 731, and "70% ... 30-year maturity" at 733. Citation: No. 89-8262, decided March 1, 1990, Tuttle, J.
- Judge's note: the court did not fault Treasuries. It faulted a manager who bought 30-year bonds on a rate forecast, for every client alike ("not a decision which was specific to GIW"), without asking when the cash was due. In the court's words, a sound investment's prudence "cannot be analyzed in the abstract". Laura's ladder starts from her ten dates and makes no forecast. Rows 35-41.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 32, for 3a ("why the two pots are separate").** Oman's dropped third cause, "the desire to 'get it done' when all eyes are focused on construction", is the strongest reason for her two pots. The money is lost to a wish, not a market, and Laura's ten are bought before any building money exists.
2. **Rows 4, 11 and 14, for 3d (the one outside parallel).** "Over five years" now rests on the partners' own record as well as Masterson (2008), and "eleven months later" comes from Lehman's 8-K. The sentence shows a promise to be paid from future business failing before its first year was out.
3. **Row 39 (with 36 and 40), for 2.3.** The court that wrote "staggering maturity dates" punished a house view bought on a rate forecast, not Treasuries: "cannot be analyzed in the abstract". Her ladder starts from her ten dates, which is the Client Knowledge criterion made visible.
4. **Rows 22 and 25, for 3d (aha 8).** FASB books a promise on "verifiable documentation", and even a taped promise can pass. Neither CASE nor FASB asks whether the giver can pay. That is why "held" has to be shown on its own, and only her plan can show it.
5. **Row 8, for 3d (behind aha 8).** A gift adviser in October 2008 said a bankrupt company's foundation would "maintain current commitments" but get no "new money", because foundations live on "an annual money transfer from their parent company". This is held against expected, in a practitioner's words, within a month of Lehman's fall.
