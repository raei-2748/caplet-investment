# Card Q44: the craft facts card Q40 left open (Sat 14 Nov 2026)

Research worker's check of six open items for the conceit (`../../CONCEIT.md` sections 5, 5b, 7). Q40's verdicts stand
and are not redone here. Every page below was read on **Sat 14 Nov 2026** through the web search and page-fetch tools;
where the fetch tool served a cached copy, its cache date is in the source table. Quotes are copied from the text as
returned. Verdicts: TRUE / PARTLY / FALSE / UNVERIFIED. Notes for the 27 Nov writers, not report prose.

**Verdicts in one line each**
1. Process pages (pencils printed beside inks; corrections printed on purpose): **TRUE.** IDW's Artist's Editions are
   scanned in colour so readers see "corrections and blue pencils"; IDW's *Jack Kirby Pencils and Inks* prints Kirby's
   pencils "side-by-side" with the finished inks. Limit: these books show how a page was made, not a plan that failed.
2. Gary Martin's sentence in a printed edition: **TRUE.** Third edition (Dark Horse Books, 2019), page 6, Google Books
   snippet view: "The inked line is what's reproduced in the finished comic book, since pencil lines are erased after
   inking." The 1997 first edition matches the same phrase on page 6 (no snippet shown). Second edition not checked.
3. "Ink only goes one way" (cannot be removed, only covered; can always be added): **PARTLY.** Adding is easy and
   taking away is "a lot more complicated (and messy)" (Tom Richmond); ink is corrected with white paint or a pasted
   patch (Martin, Richmond, comic tools). But "cannot be removed" is too strong: some artists scrape ink off with a
   blade, and scans are cleaned digitally. Safe: "ink is not erased; it is painted over or patched".
4. Pencils and inks as separate passes; pencils erased after the ink dries: **TRUE** for graphite on paper (Richmond:
   "Once I get all the lines inked ... I erase the pencils ... give your piece a good 30 minutes to dry"; Martin 3rd
   ed. p. 69). Not universal: blue pencil and printed bluelines are never erased.
5. Non-photo blue: **TRUE.** Camera era: the film "sees light blue and white as the same" (a printer on Briar Press);
   "undetectable by film cameras" (illustrator Glen Mullaly). Scanning: scanners do pick it up and the blue is removed
   with threshold, levels or channels (Mullaly; cartoonist Becca Hillburn).
6. Laura Gao's tools, from her own words: **guard confirmed.** Only "I had an iPad" is sourced, said of the time she
   made her first comic. Procreate, paper, pencils or inks of her own: **UNVERIFIED** (not found). Nothing changes.

## Notes by item

### 1. Process pages
- IDW's own definition (Artist's Editions page): "AN ARTIST'S EDITION PRESENTS COMPLETE STORIES WITH EACH PAGE SCANNED
  FROM THE ACTUAL ORIGINAL ART. While appearing to be in black and white, each page was scanned in color to mimic as
  closely as possible the experience of viewing the actual original art—for instance, corrections and blue pencils. Each
  page is printed the same size as drawn, and the paper selected is as close as possible to the original art board."
- IDW product page, *Marvel Covers Artist's Edition*: "you are able to clearly see paste-overs, blue pencils in the art,
  editorial notes, and art corrections."
- IDW product page, *DC Covers Artist's Edition, Volume One*: "You can see blue pencil notations, corrections, margin
  notes, and all the little nuances that make original art so unique and special. The only better way to view comic
  art would be if you were standing over the artist's shoulder as they were laboring at their drawing table." (search
  tool's excerpt of the IDW page)
- The format spread: the Artist's Edition Index (secondary) lists the same format under other names (Dark Horse and
  Graphitti "Gallery Edition", Titan "Original Art Edition", Fantagraphics "Studio Edition", 2000 AD "Apex Edition")
  and quotes IDW's Artifact Edition text: "corrections, blue pencils, paste-overs, all the little nuances that make
  original art unique."
- Pencils beside inks: *Jack Kirby Pencils and Inks Artisan Edition* (IDW, 2016, ISBN 9781631407581, 160 pp.), the
  publisher's text above; the Kirby Museum-backed book pairs Kirby's pencils with Mike Royer's inks, "pencils on the
  left and inks on the right, page after page" (AE Index review). A second volume, *Jack Kirby's The Eternals Pencils and
  Inks Artisan Edition* (IDW; solicitation reproduced by Bleeding Cool, 5 Aug 2024, for 25 Feb 2025).
- "Process pages" as a phrase: not found in any publisher text read. Use plain words.
- What it supports: "the original keeps the record" (corrections and blue pencil stay on the board and are printed in
  these editions) and a 3f that shows its first plan beside the finished one on purpose (Martin p. 68, the portfolio
  rule, is the closest craft reason: the reader judges the inking by seeing the pencils). What it does not support:
  that the pencils were wrong, or that publishers print them to confess.

### 2. Gary Martin's sentence
- Google Books full-text search for "pencil lines are erased after inking" returns two Martin editions: the third
  edition (id `_EOwDwAAQBAJ`) and the 1997 first edition, "The Art of Comic-book Inking, Volume 1", Dark Horse
  Comics, 1997, 109 pages, ISBN 9781569712580 (id `CLLHjHB-qM8C`), "1 page matching ... Page 6".
- Third edition, in-book search for "The inked line is what's": "Page 6 ... The inked line is what's reproduced in the
  finished comic book , since pencil lines are erased after inking . So the excuse that a mistake was " in the pencils "
  just doesn't cut the mustard ! 3. The inker determines the look of the finished art". Google shows it as a snippet
  ("No preview available for this page"), from the printed book, with "Pages displayed by permission of Dark Horse
  Comics".
- The second edition (2005) was not found in Google Books' full-text index; not checked.
- The Internet Archive item "The Art Of Comic Book - Inking" (uploaded 26 May 2022) is **not** the printed book: its
  text begins "THE ART OF COMIC BOOK INKING (rewrite) Anina Bennett", the same manuscript Q40 read. Do not cite it.
- Title spelling: Dark Horse's product page and Google Books say "The Art of Comic Book Inking"; the book's running
  head reads "The Art of Comic-Book Inking". Take the APA title from the title page of the copy cited.

### 3. Correcting ink
- Martin (2019): p. 9 glossary, "white-out: a thinned white paint used for making corrections to inked art"; p. 13 tool
  list, "[FW white and] Pro White (opaque watercolors) work on most black inks; standard white-outs like Liquid Paper do
  not."; p. 48, "[You can use white ink to] correct mistakes as well as to add negative-space details" (bracketed words
  are the manuscript's; the printed snippet starts at "correct").
- Richmond is a working cartoonist (MAD Magazine caricaturist) writing about his own inking; Part I names Martin's book
  as "to date the best book on the subject".
- The one-way rule is a practitioner's rule of thumb, not a law of the medium: white paint, patches and blades take
  lines away on the board, and scanned pages are cleaned on screen. What stays true: the original shows every fix
  (Q40 item 3), and adding is always easier than removing.

### 4. Pencils, then inks, then erasing
- Richmond Part I opens the demonstration with "Let's Start with the Pencils" and notes "the pencils are not very tight";
  Part II inks over them and erases only at the end, after the ink has dried.
- Martin (2019) p. 69: "Erase your pages one at a time as you finish them. This will save you major armache. When I'm
  inking a page, I mark an X in ink on each area that will be solid black. I then fill in blacks after I erase the page."

### 5. Non-photo blue
- Mechanism in one sentence: process-camera film for line work was sensitive to blue light, so a light blue line
  reflected like the white paper and left no mark on the negative (Briar Press). Scanners record colour, so the blue
  is captured and removed by a threshold, levels or channel setting (Mullaly; Hillburn; Q40's LaFille and Wikipedia).
- The Briar Press commenter writes from memory ("It's probably been 20+ years since I've done this"); Mullaly's post
  and Wikipedia agree with him.

### 6. Laura Gao
- Searched her published interviews and her own site for tool names (iPad, Procreate, Clip Studio, Photoshop, tablet,
  paper, pencil, ink). Only the iPad line was found. Wharton Magazine (6 Apr 2022) quotes her: "You can get sneak peeks of
  my process on my social media accounts"; those accounts were not opened (log-in pages), so anything there is
  UNVERIFIED. She describes scripting ("Whenever I script out a graphic novel, I'll visualize the scenes in my head like
  a movie", Nerd Daily, 8 Mar 2025) and colour choices, not drawing tools.

## Do not write

- "Ink can never be removed" or "ink is permanent" as craft fact. Write "ink is not erased; it is painted over or
  patched", or "a line can be added later, never taken off cleanly".
- That comics publishers print pencils to show mistakes or failed plans, or that the pencils in these books differ much
  from the inks (Royer "faithfully inked"). They print them to show the making.
- "Process pages" as the trade's own term (not found); "Artist's Edition" for any publisher but IDW (Dark Horse calls its
  books "Gallery Editions").
- Martin's sentence with a second-edition page, or with any page other than 3rd ed. p. 6 (1st ed. p. 6 only after a
  look at the copy); never cite the Bennett manuscript PDF or the Internet Archive item as the book.
- "The camera couldn't see blue": the film was sensitive to blue and recorded light blue as white. Write "the camera's
  film saw light blue as white". Never "invisible"; never "scanners can't see it" (Q40).
- "Pencils are always erased": blue pencil and printed bluelines are not.
- That Laura Gao uses Procreate, works on paper, pencils and inks, or drew *Messy Roots* or *Kirby's Lessons* on an
  iPad; that she coloured *Messy Roots* alone. Nothing from her social accounts (not read). No personal details.
