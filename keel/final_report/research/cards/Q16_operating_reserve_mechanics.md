# Q16 research cards: how the operating reserve pays out (size, composition, how it changes)

**Date.** Mon 9 Nov 2026 (Sydney), which is Sun 8 Nov in the US. Every web source below was read at the source that day
(Parallel web_search and web_fetch), unless a row says otherwise.

**Notes for the Final Report store, not report prose.** The plan is frozen: a clash is a risk to note, never a change
to propose. Keel model figures come only from `../../NUMBERS.md` sections 1, 2, 3 and 8a; anything else marked
"Keel arithmetic" is plain date or yield arithmetic on the sources named.

**Why this card.** The case asks teams to "recommend its size and initial asset composition and explain how its
composition should change, if at all, as the annual payments approach and are made" (case l.95-97), and Wharton's
Final Report instructions ask teams to "recommend an appropriate operating reserve" (`../../WHARTON_NORTH_STAR.md`
section 0). The store already holds the analogies (defeasance, FAS 76, escrow sufficiency verification, pension
hibernation, the lottery jackpot priced by Treasuries, the IRC s.130 "obligation of the United States", NY7 asset
adequacy, the debt-limit buffer). This card adds the paying-out mechanics underneath them, so the report can state
the reserve precisely enough for a fixed-income manager.

**Claims tested.**
1. FINAL IPS l.13: the ten Treasuries are "each maturing just before it's needed".
2. FINAL IPS l.13: "Held to the end, these pay what they say".
3. FINAL IPS l.19: "From 2033 the Treasuries are the operating reserve."
4. TNA note 1: "The 10.5-month gap creates reinvestment-rate risk on the proceeds held in a short-term Treasury ETF."
5. TNA note 2: IBTM's "final payout is not fixed"; reflection: "their terminal payout is a net asset value, as opposed
   to a promise of par".
6. The case's "if at all": whether the reserve's composition should change as payments approach.

**Problem class.** Operations of a dedicated (cash-flow-matched) Treasury portfolio in run-off: wind-down of
defined-maturity funds, settlement and business-day timing, where early money waits, how matched portfolios are
governed and reported, and how a fixed nominal commitment is sized. Prior art comes from fund prospectuses, Treasury
and Federal Reserve rules, state lottery annuity funding, structured settlements, pension dedication (Leibowitz),
actuarial immunization (Redington; Fisher and Weil), insurers' reserve opinions, municipal escrows and nonprofit
reserve guidance.

**Verdicts.**
1. **KEEP** "From 2033 the Treasuries are the operating reserve" with no rebalancing. A cash-matched portfolio needs
   none (CFA curriculum: "Not required"; Leibowitz 1986); immunization is what needs "a steady process of selling
   shorts and buying longs" (Redington 1952). A state lottery funding the same kind of promise runs a written "Buy and
   Hold" rule. The composition changes only as each holding pays out.
2. **KEEP** the reserve's size stated as the promise itself: ten fixed $50,000 payments, $500,000 nominal, shown with a
   rung-by-rung sufficiency schedule (cash due from each holding against each payment) and the holdings' market value
   on 1 Jan 2033. That is how escrows, lottery funds and insurers state it. NUMBERS section 9 still has to add the
   2033 value.
3. **KEEP** the waiting place as TNA note 1 names it, "a short-term Treasury ETF", without a ticker (Keel's call in
   `../../../pm/ASK_HUMANS.md`, 25 Oct). SGOV and BIL are the obvious fits; whether WInS lists either is UNVERIFIED.
4. **CHANGE (wording only)** "Held to the end, these pay what they say" is exact for the five T-bonds (par plus the
   last coupon, paid by Treasury on the next business day if the date is a weekend or holiday). For the five iBond
   funds it means net asset value, close to the yield when bought, which is what the terminated IBTF delivered. The
   report should say which rungs are which.
5. **KILL** moving any rung into bills a year early. There is no liquidity need. GAO's 2011-2023 evidence shows that
   near a debt-limit date it is the bills maturing just after that date that are at risk, and such a move would be a
   trade on a view, which the IPS rules out.
6. **KILL** any line that gives the iBond funds a fixed end date or payout. The prospectus says "on or about"
   15 December, the board may extend the date, and the payout is net asset value. In the three terminations so far,
   cash reached holders 3 to 7 days after the last trading day.
7. **CHANGE (register, not plan)** NUMBERS should add three things: (a) the reserve's market value and sufficiency on
   1 Jan 2033; (b) the cost of the Feb-2037 and May-2038 waits on their own; (c) a note that the 2026 prospectus has
   the funds reinvesting maturing bonds "in investments already in the Underlying Index" during the last six months.
   That makes section 3's "earns nothing" a stress test, not the expected case.

## Rung-by-rung table (notes)

Each rung's coupons are reinvested into the same holding (design A, NUMBERS section 2), so each rung's whole value
arrives on one date. Weekdays and day counts are Keel arithmetic. Business-day rules: S12 (Treasury pays on the next
business day "without additional interest") and S13 (Federal Reserve holidays; a Saturday holiday means Reserve Banks
open on the Friday before, a Sunday holiday means closed on the Monday after). Day gaps match CANON B8.

Reading the table: the five T-bonds pay a fixed par amount by law, and the five funds pay a market net asset value.
No rung pays after its payment date. The two long waits (2038 and 2039) are where the short rates of 2037 and 2038
matter. Since 1 January is never a business day, "the payment on 1 January" in practice is a transfer on the last
business day of December or the first business day of January.

## Best five for the report

1. **IBTF, a fund that already ended (rows 6-9).** Its price fell 6.4% in 2022, yet from launch it returned about
   1.15% a year, roughly the 5-year Treasury yield on its launch day, and paid holders in cash 14 days before New
   Year. That is real-world proof of TNA note 2's "behave a lot like bonds when held to their end", from a fund the
   judges can look up.
2. **The California Lottery's rule (rows 18-22).** "mature ... on dates immediately prior, or as near prior as
   practicable, to prize payment dates", "Buy and Hold", extra bonds bought "to mitigate ... reinvestment risk", and
   a yearly "Sufficiency of Cash Flow" report. A public body paying fixed annual prizes runs exactly Laura's reserve,
   and its policy hands the report a reporting template.
3. **The rung-by-rung table with real cash dates (rung table, rows 12-13).** It shows par for the T-bonds and NAV for
   the funds, and dates down to Treasury's next-business-day rule (Tue 17 Feb 2037). No rung pays after its payment.
   This is the table a fixed-income judge checks first.
4. **Leibowitz's "no reinvestment" standard (row 26).** It lets the report place the ladder honestly. As written, it
   needs coupons reinvested at forward rates (5.12% break-even). Keel's margin covers reinvestment 3 points lower. The
   purist's version would have cost $337,836.
5. **Debt-limit evidence for "if at all" (rows 36-37 with rows 27-30).** Matched portfolios need no rebalancing. The
   one tempting change, moving a rung into bills early, would put the money into the very securities GAO found at risk
   near a debt-limit date. The early maturities are the buffer, so the composition changes only as each rung pays out.
