## The ideas, each in three plain sentences

### Idea 1. Bernstein sorts money by how far away the bill is; one sentence in the case makes Laura's plan sort it by what the bill is paid in

- (a) Bernstein splits risk in two, a "shallow" loss that recovers within several years and a "deep" one that never does, and he tells investors to guide money for near bills by shallow risk and money for very distant bills by deep risk, because over decades it is inflation, the likeliest deep risk, and not a crash that ruins someone who holds bonds.
- (b) By that rule Laura's 2042 payment, fifteen years after the ladder is bought, is a distant bill that should be guarded against inflation; but the case fixes every payment at $50,000 "not adjusted for inflation" (l.90), so inflation can change what the payment buys and never whether it is paid, and a Treasury that matures just before the date is the whole answer however far away the date is. In Laura's plan the dividing line is therefore not distance but unit: dollars for the ten payments and for any figure she states, Taiwanese building costs for the building.
- (c) It breaks because Bernstein's retiree has to absorb inflation himself, while the case hands it to the residency's other funders by name ("any operating support beyond Laura's commitment" may come from co-sponsors, grants, collaborators and program fees, l.85-86). On every US start year from 1913 to 2011, the last of ten payments fixed this way bought a median of about two-thirds of what it would have bought at the start, and at worst (fixed in 1967, 1968 or 1969) about a third, so 3e can say plainly that the plan makes the number certain and that the case, not the plan, decided who carries the rest.
- **Decision it serves:** nominal Treasuries for the ten payments (IPS l.13; TNA l.7's "nonexistent inflation risk", which holds for the payments, CANON C15); the building, not the payments, as the place inflation is answered (IPS l.15).
- **Report part:** 3e area (4), in words, with at most one history figure ("about a third").
- **Source:** Bernstein (2013) as quoted in Carlson (2014); Zweig (2013); case l.85-86, l.90; FRED CPIAUCNS, Keel arithmetic (rows Q197-003, -010, -011).
- **Pedigree or aha:** aha. "Nominal payments need nominal bonds" is pedigree (store H2-021); that the case's one sentence turns Bernstein's sort-by-distance into a sort-by-unit, and names who carries the inflation it removes, is new, and it gives 3e a measured limit instead of a reassurance.

### Idea 2. Bernstein files the Great Depression under shallow risk; a payment date files it under deep

- (a) Bernstein counts a loss as permanent only if it is still a loss after inflation thirty years later, so by his definition "even the Great Depression" is shallow, and he says the usual way a shallow loss becomes deep is an investor who panics and sells near the bottom.
- (b) A payment date does what panic does without anyone panicking, because money that must buy something on a fixed day has to sell at that day's price. That is why the IPS puts no promise on a share price (l.11), and it is how the 27 Sep model's first plan missed: every one of its misses was settled on one day, 1 January 2033, when all ten payments had to be bought at once, so a fall in 2031-32 that would have recovered by 2035 was permanent for the payments (NUMBERS 8s).
- (c) The plan still keeps one dated sale of shares, the move of half of VT into Treasuries on 1 January 2031, which turns whatever VT has lost by then into a lasting change in the top. The plan puts that date before Laura states anything, so a fall can lower the top she will be able to state ($110,000 after a 21.2% fall, $95,000 if VT is worthless, NUMBERS 8u) but cannot make a stated figure untrue; and after 2033 her flexibility is the one pot that a builder's bill could force to sell in a slump, which the plan accepts only because that money is promised to no one.
- **Decision it serves:** IPS l.11 (no payment depends on a share price) and l.15 (the move before the 2031 meetings, so that whatever figure she chooses is money she already owns).
- **Report part:** 3e (why "No fall in share prices can."); one clause for 3c (the move as the plan's one dated sale). The first plan was dropped for IPS l.9's reason (CANON A3); this row explains how the check that priced it worked, never why it was dropped.
- **Source:** *Financial Planning* (2014); Edesess (2013); Zweig (2013); NUMBERS 8s, 8u, 8q.
- **Pedigree or aha:** the general point (dated bills cannot wait for a recovery) is textbook (store H1-035). The aha is that the most famous crash in history is "shallow" on Bernstein's clock, and that the plan keeps his danger, a date that makes a fall permanent, in exactly one place, where it can only lower a figure not yet stated.

### Idea 3. Run Bernstein's four horsemen past the ten payments, add the one he leaves out for retirees, and the two that still get through are IPS l.17's two reasons

- (a) Bernstein names four causes of permanent loss (inflation, deflation, confiscation and devastation) and has no fifth for lost income, because his reader has stopped working; when he advises younger people to ride out falls in shares, he does so "assuming they keep their jobs through the hard times".
- (b) Once the ten Treasuries are bought on 4 January 2027, inflation cannot make a fixed $50,000 unpaid, a disaster in Taiwan cannot reach Treasuries held at a US broker until each 1 January, and deflation, which helps anyone owed dollars, reaches the payments only by shrinking the 2028 deposit (NUMBERS 8u). What is left is confiscation, which for an owner of Treasuries means a default, and the fifth horseman, a writer's income failing to deliver the deposit; those are the two reasons in IPS l.17, and IPS l.9's turn ("until we stopped and considered the actual nature of a writer's income") is Bernstein's own assumption about jobs failing for a writer.
- (c) It breaks in two places, and both teach something. First, IPS l.17's two reasons and the list of ways a promise can break are different lists (NUMBERS 8u adds fund costs beyond the margin and a broker's failure, which would make a payment late, not short), so the report must never say that only two things can break the plan; those extra items are plumbing, not horsemen. Second, the one time the US did confiscate from its creditors (gold in 1933, the gold clause in 1935), the bondholder was paid every dollar and lost only what a dollar was worth in gold, which is inflation's door again, the door the case's l.90 has already shut on the promise.
- **Decision it serves:** IPS l.17 (only two reasons to change the plan) and l.9 (the career as a holding of shares she cannot sell).
- **Report part:** 3e's two-reasons line (one sentence of lineage); 2.3 for the clause on l.9.
- **Source:** Zweig (2013); Edesess (2013); Bernstein (2013) via Carlson (2014); NUMBERS 8u; *Perry v. United States* (1935); Executive Order 6102 (1933).
- **Pedigree or aha:** aha. Human capital is pedigree (store H9-001); a finance judge will not expect the IPS's two exits, written for other reasons, to be exactly what a deep-risk audit of the payments leaves standing.

### Idea 4. Bernstein's two cures cancel each other, so the plan does not blend them: it gives each cure to the money it fits, and the building is where that leaves a gap

- (a) In Bernstein's account bonds protect against deflation but expose their holder to inflation, while shares protect against inflation over decades but "exacerbate shallow risk", so his retiree has to blend the two: about 25 years of basic spending in a ladder of inflation-linked Treasuries, and the rest in shares.
- (b) Laura's plan needs no blend, because each of its pots has only one kind of risk that matters. The dollar promises get Treasuries held to their dates, whose deep risk (inflation) lands on what the dollars buy, which the case has taken off the promise; money promised to no one gets shares, whose shallow falls break nothing (IPS l.11); so the only money that needs Bernstein's inflation cure is the building's, and it gets his cure, shares, in her flexibility (IPS l.15).
- (c) It breaks on the clock and on the price index. The building's money has about five years, not Bernstein's decades, and although world shares rose in 48 of the 50 two-year windows since 2001 in which a Taiwan building's US-dollar cost rose more than 20% (store Q10e-022), a slump before a cost burst leaves her flexibility small just when the bill arrives (2000-02, then 2002-04; NUMBERS 8q). Bernstein's other cure, inflation-linked Treasuries, is banned this year, and it would have hedged the wrong prices: from July 2006 to July 2008 US consumer prices rose 8.1% while the building's dollar cost rose 38.9% (Keel arithmetic).
- **Decision it serves:** IPS l.11 (only unpromised money in shares) and l.15 (half the shares kept for overruns and a stronger Taiwan dollar).
- **Report part:** 3e area (4) and 3b (what her flexibility can and cannot cover: about 25% if shares earn the 7.5% assumed, NUMBERS 8u; Table 3 row 7 watches the building-cost trend).
- **Source:** Carlson (2014); Zweig (2013); Benz (2015); Dahle (2025); store Q10e-002, -007, -022; NUMBERS 8q, 8u; BLS CPI-U in card Q61's data, Keel arithmetic.
- **Pedigree or aha:** half and half. "Stocks for the long run against inflation" is pedigree; the test on Taiwan's own building bursts (the cure works on size, fails on order) and the finding that the banned remedy would have covered about a fifth of the worst burst are new.

### Idea 5. Shares at zero is not a thought experiment: history has already run 3e's test, and the residency is the one thing the plan cannot spread

- (a) In Russia after 1917 and China after 1949 every shareholder lost everything ("total losses", Dimson, Marsh and Staunton), yet someone who owned the whole world market of 1900 lost only those countries' share of it, just under 5% for Russia and 0.4% for China, which is why Bernstein says a permanent loss on world shares needs "universal Armageddon".
- (b) 3e already sets VT to zero and finds the ten payments untouched and the range reduced to one figure, the bottom (NUMBERS 8u); the Russian and Chinese records show that this test is not hypothetical but the deep-risk outcome shareholders have actually suffered, and that one world fund (TNA l.23) makes that outcome far less likely for VT as a whole than for any one country's shares.
- (c) It breaks at the residency itself, the plan's one asset that sits in one place. Taiwan and China together are 6.1% of VT (store H9-103), about what Russia and China were of the 1900 world, but a war would not stay inside those weights, and devastation, the horseman Bernstein says little can be done about, would reach the building and the residency's reason to exist; all the plan can do, and does, is keep every dollar still owed in US Treasuries until its 1 January, so that a disaster can reach the place but not the money (IPS l.17; NUMBERS 8t).
- **Decision it serves:** VT as one world fund (TNA l.23; the team's refusal of a Taiwan fund, store H9-105); the operating reserve held in Treasuries from 2033 (IPS l.17).
- **Report part:** 3e (the shares-at-zero line and its limit, in words; no new figure in prose).
- **Source:** Dimson, Marsh and Staunton (2006), pp. 10, 21-22; Edesess (2013); store H9-103; NUMBERS 8t, 8u; GuruFocus review (2015).
- **Pedigree or aha:** DMS on Russia and China is pedigree to a well-read finance judge; using it as the historical run of the plan's own shares-at-zero test, and naming the residency as the one asset the plan cannot diversify, is the aha.

## Best three for the report

1. **Q197-020 with -019 (Idea 3): run Bernstein's four horsemen past the ten payments, add the fifth he leaves out for retirees (her income), and the two that still get through are IPS l.17's two reasons.** For 3e's two-reasons line. 2. **Q197-003 with -010 and -011 (Idea 1): Bernstein sorts money by how far away the bill is; the case's "not adjusted for inflation" makes the plan sort it by what the bill is paid in.** For 3e area (4). It turns a reassurance ("inflation cannot touch the payments") into an evaluation with a measured limit: the case gives inflation to the residency's other funders by name, and on the worst US record since 1913 the last payment bought about a third.
3. **Q197-002 with -006 and -008 (Idea 2): the Great Depression is shallow on Bernstein's clock and deep on a payment date.** For 3e (why "No fall in share prices can.") and one clause in 3c. A judge who knows the Depression as the worst crash learns that the man who defined deep risk files it as shallow, and sees why a date, not a crash, is what the plan was built against; the plan keeps its one dated sale, the move, before anything is stated.

Runner-up: Q197-024 (shares at zero has happened, in Russia and China), the historical anchor for 3e's shares-at-zero line.

## Gaps

- *Deep Risk* itself was not read whole: it is sold as a Kindle book and a paperback, with no free full text found. Its words here come from Carlson's quotations (2014) and from the publisher's page; its Table 3 (the horsemen's probabilities, severities and costs, captioned in Edesess's review) and its chapters on Weimar Germany and on devastation were not read.
- Parallel Search (free-tier rate limit) and Firecrawl (credits spent until 20 Nov) were unavailable; WebSearch and WebFetch were used, and the session's fetch budget ran out once.
- Not read at source: the 1934 Gold Reserve Act and the $35 gold price (the 41% figure is derived from *Perry*'s $16,931.25 alone); Reinhart and Rogoff's classification of 1933 as a US default; the 1979 late Treasury bill payments (store H5-042, still UNVERIFIED); Graham's "quotational loss" in Graham's own text (reported by Zweig).
- Not studied: Taiwan's rules on converting and moving large sums into Taiwan dollars (confiscation's mild cousin), since the case puts Taiwan's legal and regulatory requirements out of scope (l.168-169).
- The 2022 test of Bernstein's inflation cure: world shares (MSCI ACWI with dividends) fell 18.0% in dollars in calendar 2022, 22.9% after US inflation (Keel arithmetic on card Q61's data), but the Taiwan dollar's fall that year also cut the building's dollar cost (store Q10e-009), so 2022 is not a clean case and no row rests on it.
