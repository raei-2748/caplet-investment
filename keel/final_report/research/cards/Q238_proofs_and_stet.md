## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE. Both halves are true at source; the map holds for the plan's order and stops in four places.**
  - The stages and their costs: "To correct manuscript requires merely the stroke of a pen; while to correct type is
    laborious and expensive"; galleys have "flexibility", pages are "fixed accurately to the line"; "Corrections in
    plates are very difficult and always costly" (Appleton, 1904). Today: a change "during the prepress stage is a lot
    more cost effective than later on"; after printing it must be "repreflighted, replated, and reprinted" (Bookmobile,
    2019). A correction costs more than the setting it replaces: a 5% allowance "does not mean 5 percent of the number
    of lines" (Chicago, 1982); corrections can "triple or quadruple the cost of composition" (Peirce, c. 1902).
  - The allowance: "in excess of 15% of the original cost of composition shall be deducted from Author's royalties"
    (Authors Guild model); 10% "after type has been set in conformity with the copyedited manuscript" (Writers' Union of
    Canada, 2021); 15% (NZSA, 2025); "e.g. 10% of the cost of composition" (Society of Authors, 2018); 10% or 15% in
    1904. Printer's errors are always excluded.
  - What maps: each step buys a figure before the statement that rests on it (most of each payment January 2027; the
    rest, the extra Treasuries and the bottom January 2028; the top in January 2031, before the meetings), so every
    change to what she states is made at that figure's manuscript stage. And the reason late changes cost most is the
    same in both: others have built on the work (a page change can "overrun all pages to the end of the chapter" and
    break the index; co-sponsors count her top).
  - Where it stops: (a) a contract lets an author buy late changes; the plan sells none of Laura's own choosing (IPS
    l.17's two reasons are events, not choices). (b) It budgets the other way round: for others' slips (the 0.5% margin,
    the room under the top, the letter's C3), which in print are the printer's errors and cost the author nothing (row
    7). (c) Its money cost of a late change is tiny (0.04%-0.07%, card Q45), so cost cannot be the brake (claim 4). (d)
    The top is bought late on purpose: the decision is IPS l.15's, made in 2026, and only the purchase waits, so the
    lesson is "state after you own", not "decide early".
  - **Judge's note:** a publishing contract budgets for the author's own late changes and absorbs the printer's errors;
    Laura's plan does the reverse: it budgets for the funds' slips and allows none of her own.
- **Claim 2: KILL as a picture of holding (agrees with Q82 row 35, for new reasons read at source).**
  - Stet needs a prior mark: "To undelete or restore something that has earlier been marked for deletion or correction,
    place a row of dots ... and write 'stet'" (Chicago, 2017, 2.130); "Often added to cancel an instruction wrongly
    requested" (EUP card, after BS 5261-2); "Making the wrong mark: stet" (Publishing Training Centre, 2021).
  - Holding is the blank margin, not stet: "if nothing is marked in the margin, the typesetter will assume no change is
    needed" (PTC). A market fall is never marked in the plan (IPS l.17), so there is nothing to stet, and a stet would
    tell a judge the team keeps reconsidering.
  - The one true stet in the record is the copout (a proposed strike of the world fund, withdrawn against a bar written
    first): store only, since 3f is told plain.
  - **Judge's note:** stet is the undo of a mark. Laura's plan holds by leaving the margin blank: a fall in the market
    never gets marked.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE the craft fact, KILL the mapping.**
  - At source the author does not sign the final proof: "Page proofs are normally the last proofs seen by an author",
    after which the editor checks "that all material is there and in the correct order" (Chicago, 1982, 3.53); a
    foundry-reader re-reads (Appleton, 1904); Princeton reviews the printer's proof "in-house". In Taiwan, from the
    second proof only "細部的筆誤或嚴重事實錯誤" (small slips or serious factual errors) are fixed (editorstw, 2023).
  - Mapped to the move, "signing off the final proof" makes the range a draft until 2031 (Q82; V2.4 guard 3): killed.
  - What maps without that: the "000" placeholder. Page numbers still to come "are normally indicated by zeros (000)",
    and "each 000 is changed to a real number once page proofs are out" (Chicago, 1982). The rule for the top was fixed
    in 2026; the figure co-sponsors hear is filled in once the Treasuries exist; before that they hear no figure, which
    is neither a guess nor a draft (row 25, fallback for 3c).
  - **Judge's note:** before the move co-sponsors hear no top at all, as a book's cross-references carry "000" until its
    pages exist: a placeholder, not a guess.
- **Claim 4 (own): KEEP.** When hand lettering gave way to digital, "scripts were edited more tightly before getting to
  the lettering stage. That's usually not the case now", corrections became "extensive" and fee-free (Piekos, Blambot).
  Publishers kept their rule after metal type went: "It does not actually cost very much to change the text these
  days ... Even so" (Stroppy Author, 2010); "Press policy dictates" (Princeton). Laura's Treasuries and funds can be
  sold any trading day for a few hundredths of a percent (card Q45), so cost will never stop a late change; IPS l.17's
  written rule does.
  - **Judge's note:** cheap corrections multiplied late changes in comics. Laura's bonds are cheap to sell any day, so
    what keeps her plan still is a written rule, not a price.
- **Claim 5 (own): KEEP the craft fact, CHANGE the mapping (store).** "Always use pencil for anything that you are not
  sure about ... write the alteration in in ink or rub out the pencil" (PTC, UK); China's standard requires proof marks
  in coloured pen, "不能用灰色铅笔" (never grey pencil) (GB/T 14706-93). In proofreading ink is an instruction that will
  be carried out and pencil a question for someone else. It stops where the proofreader's pencil means "not sure", the
  law's deliberative sense the conceit avoids (Q114-036): the top is decided by IPS l.15 and pencil only because not
  yet owned. China's rule is the PRC's, not Taiwan's.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 7, the inversion (3e, one plain clause).** A publishing contract budgets for the author's own late changes and
   absorbs the printer's errors; Laura's plan budgets only for others' slips (the margin, the room under the top, the
   letter's C3) and sets her own changes at zero. It turns IPS l.17's "A fall in the market is not one of them" from a
   promise of nerve into a line in a budget.
2. **Rows 29-30, cheap change needs a rule (3e).** Comics lettering went digital, corrections became cheap and late
   changes multiplied; presses kept a written policy once metal type was gone. Laura's bonds and funds cost a few
   hundredths of a percent to sell (card Q45), so her plan is held still by IPS l.17's rule, not by price. It answers a
   judge who asks why a liquid portfolio would not be traded.
3. **Row 18, holding is the blank margin (3e and Table 3's prose).** "If nothing is marked in the margin, the typesetter
   will assume no change is needed." Said plainly: a check that finds none of the named events changes nothing. It is
   the clearest correction of the stet reading (claim 2) and keeps Table 3 from sounding like a trigger list.
4. **Row 31, changes go at the end of the paragraph (2.2 or 3b).** Type is reset from a change to the end of its
   paragraph, so printers put changes last; the order of claims puts the lines most likely to move (the shares) last,
   so a share fall resets nothing above it and nothing ever runs back into the ten.
5. **Row 25, the "000" placeholder (3c, fallback behind "inked before the 2031 meetings").** It maps the move without
   making the range provisional: the rule was fixed in 2026, the number is filled in once the Treasuries exist, and
   before then co-sponsors hear no figure at all. Use only if the current NP100 wording (Q141 row 27) fails its check
   and the footings (Q106) are not wanted.

Runners-up: row 13 (the trades' rule that meeting the brief costs the buyer nothing, behind C3), row 8 (the scale of
change set before the work starts), row 22 (proofreaders' own pencil-and-ink rule), row 27 (Klein's outlines).

  whether the tick is circled is UNVERIFIED.
- The Chicago Manual's 18th edition text is paywalled; its section numbers were read on the contents page and the text
  in the 13th (1982) and 17th (2017) editions on the Internet Archive.
- The Authors Guild model contract page loaded only in part through the fetch tool; the 15% sentence came from the
  search tool's excerpt of the same page.
- Taiwan: no Taiwanese standard for proof marks (a CNS number, if one exists) and no Taiwanese publishing contract's
  alterations clause (作者修改費) was found; the Taiwan rows rest on one editor's blog. The Chinese rows are the PRC's.
- Comics: no publisher's creator agreement charging creators for late art or lettering changes was found (Image, DC,
  Marvel and Scholastic terms were not read); Blambot says mainstream practice pays letterers no correction fees.
- No primary dataset measures the cost-of-change curve; Beach's $5 / $50 / $500 stays second-hand (Q47 A10).
- Not searched, for the next round: the French "bon à tirer" and author's corrections ("corrections d'auteur") in
  French contracts; the UK Minimum Terms Agreements' proof clauses; the current Society of Authors guide (after v.5.1,
