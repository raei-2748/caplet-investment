- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**

  2. "An iBonds Treasury fund pays its final net assets within days of its end date."
  3. "Bringing $50,000 into Taiwan for a foundation needs a declaration, not an approval" (外匯收支或交易申報辦法).
  4. "A US-dollar wire to a Taiwanese bank is normally credited within a day or two."
  5. "The money for each payment waits in cash for some weeks between maturity and January, and that waiting cash is what 'even if cash waiting earns nothing' refers to."
- **Problem class:** last-mile settlement of a scheduled cross-border payment: getting a known sum, on a known date, from a maturing security at a US broker into a named organisation's account abroad, across two bank calendars, two currencies and two sets of reporting rules. **Field that solved it first:** treasury operations and payments (Fedwire and Swift rules, and the G20/FSB cross-border payments targets and data), with US custody law for standing third-party payment instructions (the SEC's 2017 standing letter of authorisation conditions) and payments fraud control (FBI IC3).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, and add the sentence after it.**
  - 31 CFR 356.30: "If any principal or interest payment date is a Saturday, Sunday, or other day on which the Federal Reserve System is not open for business, we will make the payment (without additional interest) on the next business day" (already VERIFIED in Q16 row 12).
  - New: the same section says Treasury "discharge[s] our payment obligations when we credit payment to the account maintained at a Federal Reserve Bank for a depository institution". Treasury's promise ends at the custodian bank's Fed account. Everything after it (custodian, broker, wire, Taiwanese bank) is a separate chain of steps.
  - For Laura: her bonds mature 47 to 318 days before their payments and her funds 8-14 days before. A one-to-three-day holiday shift never reaches a payment.
  - A wire stuck after the Fed credit is never "a Treasury payment more than five business days late", so it never triggers Table 3's second exit (IPS l.17's "a Treasury default").
  - **Judge's note:** the Treasury's guarantee ends at a Fed account in New York, weeks before January. What can go wrong after that is paperwork, and paperwork can be checked.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, with a live 2026 case added to Q3c's five.**
  - IBTG (the December 2026 fund), notice of 15 Oct 2026: last trade 15 Dec 2026, final NAV "as calculated on December 15, 2026", trading suspended before the open on 16 Dec, proceeds "on or about December 18, 2026, subject to their brokerage firm's processes".
  - Between 16 and 18 Dec "we cannot assure you that there will be a trading market". The final NAV "will include any costs associated with the liquidation", so even the last payout is not a fixed sum.
  - BlackRock's press release of the same day lists every ending fund's last trading day, final NAV date and liquidation date in one table.
  - In its last weeks IBTG was almost all very short Treasuries: effective duration 0.08 years, 10 holdings.
  - So the payout comes "within days" (three in 2026), and it lands at the broker, not with Laura.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE (sharper and narrower).**
  - The declaration is triggered by converting foreign currency into NT$ at NT$500,000 or more (Art. 2), which is about US$15,300 at 32.768 (Keel arithmetic). So every $50,000 payment converted into NT$ needs a form.
  - Into a US-dollar account no NT$ changes hands, and the bank's own voucher 「視同申報書」 (counts as the declaration; Art. 14).
  - No central-bank approval is needed unless a year's conversions pass the organisation's yearly limit (US$10m since 1 Nov 2024; Q69 row 32) or "special cases" apply (Art. 6). Documents are checked only at US$500,000 or more per conversion (Art. 5).
  - The real find is bank practice:
    - CTBC credits a wire into a foreign-currency account directly.
    - Into an NT$ account, at NT$500,000 or more the payee 「須臨櫃辦理結匯」 (must convert at the counter and file the form) before the money is credited. Chunghwa Post works the same way: credited only after the filing, 「後始得入帳」.
  - These are facts for the watch list, never legal advice (the case puts Taiwan's legal questions out of scope).
  - **Judge's note:** sent to the wrong kind of account, a $50,000 wire arriving on time can sit uncredited until someone from the residency files a form at a counter.
- **Claim 4: KEEP for big payments; CHANGE what the plan assumes (allow a week).**
  - Swift gpi: "Nearly 60% ... credited to end beneficiaries within 30 minutes and almost 100% within 24 hours".
  - FSB 2026 report:
    - Wholesale (US$100,000 and up): 57% credited within one hour, 93.9% within one business day.
    - Retail: 32.9% within an hour, 59.8% within a day (down 7.5 points). A $50,000 wire is in the retail group.
    - Asia-Pacific is slower on the receiving leg (wholesale 85.5% within a day).
  - Swift: 75% of payments reach the receiving bank within ten minutes. The last mile "can account for around 80% of total processing time", slowed by "regulatory reporting requirements, operating hours, foreign exchange (FX) controls and manual processing".
  - Taiwanese banks' own guides say 2-5 working days for SWIFT wires (KGI). CTBC gives 2-7 working days for its own wires to the US.
  - **Judge's note:** the slow part of a cross-border payment is not the ocean but the receiving bank's desk. That is why the watch list sits in Taiwan, not on Wall Street.
- **Claim 5: CHANGE.**
  - The waits are 8-14 days (the five funds), 47 days (the three November bonds), 229 days (May-2038) and 318 days (Feb-2037) (NUMBERS), not "some weeks".
  - The standard "if cash waiting earns nothing" covers every coupon and distribution received from 2027 until its payment, not only the final wait.
  - A wire's days in transit add a little more waiting money that earns nothing. They add no new risk.
  - Money waiting in the short-term Treasury ETF (TNA note 1) must be sold at least one US business day before the wire (T+1).
- **Calendar find (aha):**
  - Since Taiwan's 2025 holidays law, 25 Dec is a day off. A holiday on a Saturday closes the Friday before.
  - Taiwan's FSC sets each year's bank holidays (Banking Act Art. 51). Its 2027 table closes banks Fri 24 Dec and Fri 31 Dec 2027.
  - On those rules, in December 2032 and December 2038 Taiwan's last banking day is Thu 30 Dec, while the Fed is open Fri 31 Dec. Taipei is 13 hours ahead in winter. A December credit in those years means sending from New York by about Wed 29 Dec (Keel arithmetic).
  - December 2032 is also the plan's biggest month: the late-2032 fund pays the first $50,000 and the facility contribution (NUMBERS 8w).
  - **Judge's note:** the one December where everything happens at once is one of the two where Taiwan's banks close a day before America's.
