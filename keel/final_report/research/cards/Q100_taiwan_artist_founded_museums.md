- **Problem class:** sustaining a founder-led cultural institution. The founder pays for the site, the building or the works; the question is who then pays the running costs each year, and what happens when that source stops.
- **Field that solved it first:** museum and nonprofit finance. In Taiwan that means museum-management research on private museums: 陳嬋娟 (2003), whose thesis interviews the heads of five private museums and cites 陳國寧's 1997 study of small private museums for the Council for Cultural Affairs, and 李政憲 (2007). Card Q9 holds the US reserve literature.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** The founder paid first in all 13 cases found. Co-sponsors came late, came small and paid for named parts or for a fixed number of years, and almost never for running costs.
  - **How co-sponsors came:**
    - At Juming: priced upkeep packages and a NT$500,000 space adoption in 2024, 25 years after opening.
    - At IT Park: one patron's three-year pledge (NT$400,000-500,000 a year) from 1999, eleven years in.
    - At Hong-Gah: NCAF project money from 2010.
    - At Li Mei-shu: a NT$300m building gift in 2025, 35 years after the family opened the gallery with its own money.
  - **Two founders drew no running money from anyone:**
    - Hong-Gah: 「都是由我個人來支持」, "all supported by me personally".
    - Yu-Hsiu: 「都是靠私人挹注」, "all private money".
  - **A rich backer could cut public money.** In 2001 officials assumed company-backed museums had money and weighed that when setting grants (陳嬋娟, 2003).
  - **What drew the largest gift was persistence, not size.** The NT$300m donor was moved that the Li brothers 「在資源有限的情況下，仍持續接力守護畫作」, "kept guarding the paintings, one after the other, with limited resources" (ARTouch, 2025).
  - **Say:** "the founder pays first; co-sponsors follow later, for named parts and fixed terms."
  - **Judge's note:** the largest co-sponsor gift found came 35 years after a family started with borrowed money, and the donor cited their persistence, not the size of their gift.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** The shape is wrong. These institutions never covered their running costs from what they earned. The gap was paid every year from a live income behind them, and the ones that stopped did so when that income stopped, not when a sum set aside ran out.
  - **The gap:**
    - Juming 2022: tickets 31.49% of income. Spending was NT$130.80m against NT$105.74m of income, a NT$25.06m gap (Keel arithmetic on its audited table).
    - Juming 2011: NT$20m a year 「必須向外募款」, "must be raised outside".
    - Its home page today: 「我們無法只以門票收入繼續營運」, "we cannot keep operating on ticket income alone".
    - Chimei, free in 2003: about NT$40m of costs against about NT$8m from souvenirs.
    - Yu-Hsiu: NT$20-25m a year, free entry, its shop and café 「入不敷出」 (costing more than they earn).
    - Hong-Gah: NT$14m a year from the founder, free entry, 「沒有收入」, "no income".
  - **Who paid the gap:**
    - A company's profit: Chimei, Shung Ye, Chang Foundation (鴻禧), Mountain (山), Cathay (國泰).
    - The founder's own earnings: Hong-Gah's 「我一年一定要去賺一千四佰萬來給這邊用」, "I must earn NT$14m a year to give here"; Yu-Hsiu; IT Park's photography studio.
    - Sales of the artist's works: Yuyu Yang, and the 楊三郎, 李石樵 and 李澤藩 museums.
  - **When it stopped, so did they:**
    - Cathay closed in 1985 (the founder's finances).
    - Mountain closed in 2002 (the group's crisis).
    - Chang lost its space in 2004 (the parent's finances).
    - IT Park went on "rest" in 2025 (digital photography ended the founder's trade).
    - A fifth, 迪化207, closed in 2022 when its founder retired.
  - **Survivors** cut their opening hours, sold works, appealed for help or found a late co-sponsor.
  - **Say:** "they ran on the founder's next year's income".
  - **Judge's note:** a reader expects founder museums to fail when the founder's money runs out. In Taiwan they stopped when the founder's income stopped, because the running money was never set aside to run out.
- **Claim 3: KEEP, sharpened.** None of the 13 cases owned even one year of running costs set aside before opening.
  - **Registered funds were small next to yearly costs:**
    - Hong-Gah: NT$5m (1991), against NT$14m a year.
    - Mountain: NT$5m (1992).
    - Chen Cheng-po: NT$10m (1999).
    - Juming: NT$2m on a 2012 record, against NT$55m of running costs a year in 2011.
  - **The one large fund is permanent and is not spent on running costs:**
    - Chimei Culture Foundation's fund: NT$685m in 2024.
    - Changing it needs the Tainan culture bureau's approval and a court registration.
    - In 2024 it paid the museum NT$35m 「作為營運所需」 ("for operating needs") out of income that was 77% investment income. That income had fallen 59% from 2023.
    - A 2003 interviewee whose fund was 「六億六千萬」 (NT$660m) said that running on its interest 「我們就完蛋了」, "we would be finished".
  - **Say:** "rare: none of the 13 Taiwanese cases found owned a year of running costs ahead". Never "no other founder", which the professor ruled untestable (SKELETON 3.0).
  - **Judge's note:** Taiwan's founders gave land, works and buildings, and none gave running years ahead. Laura's ten payments are running years, owned in Treasuries before 2033.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 41 (the pattern), for 3.0 and 3a.** It turns IPS l.9 into Taiwan's own record: museums ran on the founder's next year's income and stopped when it stopped, while Laura's ten payments are owned before 2033. This is a client-knowledge "aha" with no figure to defend.
2. **Row 30 (IT Park), for 3a, or 3.0 as the one example.** For 37 years an artist-founded space ran on its founder's photography business, and it ended when digital cameras ended that business. That is the clearest picture of a career as the income engine, the thing the plan removes.
3. **Rows 19-20 (Yu-Hsiu), for 3.0.** The founder said eight years of running costs would build another museum, and she asks her family to earn NT$25m of profit a year to carry it. This supports SKELETON 3.0's point that the running years are the hard part, and on Laura's plan they are owned.
4. **Rows 35 and 9-10 (small registered funds; Chimei's permanent fund), for 3d's half-clause and 3b.** None of the 13 owned a year of running costs ahead. The one large fund spends only its income, and its own side said running on interest would "finish" them. This adds the private founders to Q9c's yearly-voted public money. It also keeps Laura's reserve distinct from a permanent fund: hers is spent down on dates.
5. **Row 24 (Li Mei-shu's NT$300m, 35 years on), for 3d.** What drew Taiwan's largest co-sponsor gift was visible persistence with limited means. Paying first matters as proof that the founder will keep going, not because of the size of the gift. This fits "the founder pays first" and never "the largest share".
