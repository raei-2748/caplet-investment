- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Laura starts where pensions end": the state a mature defined-benefit pension works for decades to reach (hibernation, a cash-flow-matched endgame where bonds pay each benefit as it falls due) is where her plan begins on day one for the ten payments.
  2. The sharper form: "the stage pensions reach last (the payments locked first, the surplus kept for growth) is her first step, and her surplus (the floor and the shares) is what a pension calls its return-seeking assets."
  3. "A hibernating pension holds what Laura holds: government bonds matched to each payment."
  4. "The risks a hibernating pension still carries (longevity, inflation, credit, reinvestment) are Laura's too."
- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** running off a closed stream of fixed promises from a pool of assets, after the promises stop growing: defined-benefit pension finance (UK funding regime and actuarial profession; US liability-driven investing practice), with the corporate-finance case for matching made first by Black (1980) and Tepper (1981) in the US and Exley, Mehta and Smith (1997) in the UK, and first put into practice in full by one scheme, Boots, in 2000-2001.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, sharpened and with two limits. It is true in three ways a practitioner can check, and false in one.**
  - **By UK pension law's own clock.** A UK scheme reaches "significant maturity" when its liabilities' duration (the weighted mean time to payment, regulation 4(2)) falls to 10 years; it must be fully funded on a low dependency basis by the end of that scheme year (TPR code). Laura's ten payments have a weighted mean time of 10.06 years on 1 Jan 2027 on the 6 Nov Treasury curve, 10.02 at Treasuries + 0.5%, 10.22 at a flat 3.5% (Keel arithmetic on the engine's curve). So the ten cross the law's line in January 2027, or within about three months at 2023-like rates. The average UK scheme was at 13 years in 2025 (Aon), and a typical closed scheme was "around 15-20 years" from the line in 2020 (GAD).
  - **By the trigger pensions use.** "The next transition ... to hibernation ... is largely a question of funding" (Russell). Bodie (1990): "The incentive to immunize ... is strongest when the plan is fully funded." On day one the $300,000 is 99.5% of the ten's cost at the Treasury curve ($301,544, NUMBERS 1; Keel arithmetic), 104.5% at Treasuries + 0.5%. The textbook rule puts her into the matched state on day one.
  - **By how long pensions took.** UK schemes held 28% bonds and 61% shares in 2006, 71% and 15% in 2025 (Purple Book); 20% of schemes were in surplus on the PPF measure in 2006, 74% in 2025, and on a full buy-out measure the universe is still 95.8% funded (Mar 2025). The 100 largest US corporate plans ended every fiscal year from 2008 to 2023 in deficit (Milliman). For the average UK scheme, "between 2009 and 2017 ... reaching its long-term objective got no closer" (Aon).
  - **Limit 1, "day one".** True for the holdings and for the pension's yardstick. On Laura's own yardstick (paid even if cash waiting earns nothing) the $300,000 is 89.1% of the $336,656 needed (Keel arithmetic, NUMBERS 2); she arrives in January 2028 with the first $38,282 of the second deposit. Never "fully funded on day one" (FINAL l.13; store H7-009's warning).
  - **Limit 2, "where pensions end".** Most pensions end with an insurer: 52% of UK schemes aim to buy out as soon as affordable (Aon 2025/26), 75% ultimately target full insurance (LCP 2025), and in the US "plan termination is generally the ultimate endgame" (SOA 2021). Hibernation is the end state for the 40% that run on, and the waystation for the rest. Say "the state a pension reaches last" or "where a pension that runs itself off ends", never "every pension".
  - **Why she can start there.** Not because she is ahead of pension managers: her promise is small, fixed in dollars and dates, has no members, no inflation link and no options, ends in 2042 inside the Treasury curve, and the case's first deposit about equals its price. A pension's last benefit can fall "75+ years after the plan froze" (Russell), longer than the bond market reaches.
  - **Judge's note:** by UK pension law's own maturity test, Laura's ten payments are as mature on 1 January 2027 as a scheme the law requires to be finished de-risking. The end state pensions spend decades reaching is decided by funding, not age, and she is funded at Treasury prices on day one.
- **Claim 2 (sharper form): CHANGE. The first half holds; the floor half is wrong.**
  - **Payments locked, surplus free: KEEP.** The UK code's low dependency objective "does not apply to any surplus on a low dependency funding basis" (TPR). That is the IPS's own rule, l.11: "only money you haven't promised yet goes into shares". Run-on to generate surplus is the newest UK end state (WTW: 53% of run-off schemes name it as the main reason).
  - **"The floor is return-seeking": KILL.** The floor (IBTM, $81,889 in Jan 2028) is a Treasury fund for a second promise she will state in 2031: in pension terms a second matching block, bought before its promise is stated. Only VT is return-seeking. Calling the floor anything else clashes with IPS l.15 ("built on the ever-reliable Treasuries").
  - **The size fits the pension band.** At cost in Jan 2028, 93.4% of the $450,000 is Treasuries and 6.6% VT ($29,829; Keel arithmetic on NUMBERS 4). Hibernating US plans hold 80-90% liability-hedging bonds with up to 20% growth (Russell) or 0-15% growth (SSGA).
  - **Correct form (notes):** the stage a pension reaches last (every promised dollar matched, only the surplus at market risk) is her first step. Her shares are the part a pension calls return-seeking; her floor is a second matched block.
  - **Judge's note:** UK law now writes the IPS's rule into the pension code: matched up to the promise, free above it.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE. Laura's matched block is stricter than any hibernating pension's.**
  - UK schemes' bonds are 20.8% fixed gilts, 34.8% corporate and 44.4% index-linked (Purple Book 2025). Practitioners put investment-grade credit at "the core of a CDI portfolio" (Barnett Waddingham). "Cashflow-driven does not necessarily mean cashflow-matching" (EY).
  - US hibernation books are "a duration-targeted blend of Treasuries and credit" (SSGA), "reallocated ... every year" (Russell).
  - Laura's: US Treasuries only, nominal, no leverage, held to the end, never rebalanced.
  - The one pension that matched early and in full, Boots (2000-2001), held only AAA/Aaa long bonds, "held passively, with no trading", and did it while "neither closed nor very mature". Its trustees' aim: "ensure that the value of scheme assets is always enough to pay all pensions, regardless of movements in financial markets."
  - **Judge's note:** the closest pension precedent did in 2001, by choice, what the industry took twenty years to follow; its valuation even carried "a 5% cushion for re-investment risk".
- **Claim 4: CHANGE. Most of a hibernating pension's risks are absent; one is larger for Laura.**
  - Pensions keep demographic risk (Milliman), credit migration, expenses and PBGC premiums, assumption changes and lump-sum options (Russell), inflation-linked benefits (Boots: RPI "up to 5% a year") and the sponsor (store Q2a-027). Laura's ten have none of these.
  - Hers are the iBond funds' payout at net asset value (NUMBERS 3) and the reinvestment of coupons. The second is larger for her than for a hibernating pension. A UK scheme at significant maturity is "very broadly" one whose members have all or nearly all retired, and 78% of tranche-19 schemes already pay out more than they take in (Aon), so their coupons are spent as they arrive. Laura pays nothing until 2033, and $93,941 of interest waits by then (NUMBERS 2). The duration test counts her mature only because her stream is short.
  - That gap is what the extra Treasuries ($35,894 in Jan 2028) close.
  - **Judge's note:** in cash-flow terms Laura is a pension whose members have not yet retired; her bond book is a pensioner's. The extra Treasuries are the price of that one difference.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 22 (with 21 and 23), 3a.** By UK pension law's own maturity test, the ten are "significantly mature" in January 2027, while the average UK scheme sat at 13 years in 2025 and a typical closed one was 15-20 years away in 2020. It turns "she starts where pensions end" from an image into a checked fact a practitioner can verify, and it sits in 3a beside spine row 5 without repeating it (row 5 is about the funding test; this one is about maturity).
2. **Row 3 (with 4 and 36), 3a or 2.0.** What moves a pension into the matched state is funding, not age (Russell; Bodie 1990), and Laura is funded at Treasury prices on day one. Said with row 36's limit (89.1% of her own standard until Jan 2028), it survives the cold reader who struck spine row 1 for setting $302,000 against $300,000 without saying where the rest comes from.
3. **Rows 8-10, 3a or 2.3.** Boots 2001: the one pension that did it early, while open, and whose trustees' aim reads like the IPS ("always enough to pay all pensions, regardless of movements in financial markets"); it even carried "a 5% cushion for re-investment risk". This is the precedent a judge can look up.
4. **Rows 13, 17 and 18, 3a context (one clause).** How long it took the pensions: UK bonds 28% → 71% over 2006-2025, still 95.8% on buy-out; US largest plans in deficit at every year-end 2008-2023; "no closer" for 2009-2017. These figures make "end" concrete without a chart.
5. **Rows 25 and 37, 2.0 or 3b.** UK law writes the IPS's rule into the pension code ("does not apply to any surplus"), and the correct sharper form is "payments matched first, only money not yet promised takes market risk". VT at 6.6% of the money at cost sits inside the band hibernating plans hold; the floor is a second matched block, never "return-seeking".
