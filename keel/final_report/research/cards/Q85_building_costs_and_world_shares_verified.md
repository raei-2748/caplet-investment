- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** replication of a published count from primary series, and how much a finding moves with the analyst's choices (which years count as "other", which index variant, which day of the month). The second is the robustness check of empirical economics: rerun the count under every reasonable choice and report the range, not the friendliest point.

## Verdicts in one screen

**What Q66 counted (read from its scripts and data file).** World shares: MSCI World, US dollars, gross, calendar years; a "fall" is a year below -10% (1973, 1974, 1990, 2000, 2001, 2002, 2008, 2022). Building costs: one series per economy, yearly averages (Japan by April-March fiscal year), same calendar year: Taiwan DGBAS input index (from 1992), US Turner selling-price index, Hong Kong ArchSD tender index, UK PUBSEC tender index (1971-79 and 1990-2006 only), Singapore BCA tender index (from 2006), Japan MLIT deflator (three sub-indices spliced). Change years 1971-2025, not 1970-2000. The 35 are 1973 (4), 1974 (4), 1990 (2), 2000-02 (15), 2008 (5), 2022 (5). "140 of 172" counts only years world shares **rose**; the 22 years of small falls (0 to -10%) are in neither group.

1. **Claim 1: KEEP the finding, CHANGE the numbers and the words "other years".**
   - **The arithmetic holds:** Q66's file gives exactly 28 of 35 (80%) and 140 of 172 (81%). Every building series re-read today matched Q66's file value for value (HK 208 quarters; UK 27 years; Taiwan 238 values; Turner 20 year averages; Singapore 19 years); Japan was not re-read.
   - **The words do not:** "140 of 172" is "in years world shares rose". Against every other year it is 156 of 194 (80%).
   - **The data was incomplete:** UK PUBSEC 1980-89, read today in the same BERR table, adds UK 1990 (106 to 99, **-6.6%**, a fall in a world-fall year). With it: **28 of 36 (78%) against 149 of 181 (82%)**; against every other year, 165 of 204. Q66's "the seven that fell are all 2000-2002 in Asia" becomes eight, including the UK in 1990.
   - **The world years are only partly checkable:** 2001-2025 equal MSCI's own gross levels; 1998-2000 agree with MSCI's price index (the gross figures sit 1.1-2.0 points higher, as dividends would); 1970-1997 cannot be read at MSCI (its service answers "Calculation date cannot be earlier than 19970101"). So 10 of the 36 country-years (1973, 1974, 1990) rest on a Wikipedia table, UNVERIFIED at MSCI, though Cambridge Associates (2019) confirms MSCI World bear markets of -40.0% (Feb 1973 to Sep 1974) and -24.0% (Dec 1989 to Sep 1990). **On the years MSCI's own data covers (1998-2025): 18 of 25 (72%) against 76 of 99 (77%).**
   - **Every variant agrees on direction:** Dec-to-Dec for Taiwan and Q4-to-Q4 for Hong Kong 29 of 36 against 148 of 181; without Japan 23 of 28 against 115 of 141; input indices only 8 of 12 against 44 of 56; tender indices only 20 of 23 against 96 of 116. No difference anywhere (Fisher exact p from 0.35 to 1.00).
   - **But the count is a weak test, and size tells a little more.** Building costs rise in about four years in five whatever shares do, so a count of rises cannot show a link either way. On size, fall years were dearer: more than 5% in 19 of 35 against 59 of 172 (p = 0.035; with the UK added, 19 of 36 against 65 of 181, p = 0.063), mean +7.8% against +4.3%. That gap is the 1973-74 inflation (all 8 of those country-years rose more than 5%; UK +40.9% in 1973). Without them the fall years average +3.9%; since 2000 it is 10 of 25 against 21 of 89 (p = 0.13), medians +4.3% against +2.2%. Against their own trend, half the time either way (15 of 30 against 86 of 178, reproduced).
   - **Judge's note:** "costs rose in 80% of crash years" sounds like a link until you see they rose in 82% of the other years too; the honest test is size against trend, and there only the 1970s inflation shows.
2. **Claim 2: KEEP the episode and the pairing, CHANGE the numbers.**
   - **The pairing is apples to apples in time.** The shares run from purchase to month 60, the costs from month 36 (the meetings) to month 60 (the contribution), both ending the same month: the plan's own shape and NUMBERS 8q's convention ("VT purchase + 36 months to + 60 months").
   - **-6.6% is reproducible but the mildest choice.** From MSCI levels read today: ACWI price at the end of January 2000 (322.83) to the end of December 2000 (290.11), then ACWI gross (MSCI's gross series starts 29 Dec 2000) to the end of January 2005 (394.85): **-6.59%**. On Laura's calendar (bought at the start of January 2000, so the December 1999 close of 341.63; valued at the start of January 2005, so the December 2004 close) the same splice gives **-9.8%**; on the price index alone -16.7% (-13.8% on Q66's dates). January 2000 had already fallen 5.5% by its last day. MSCI World on the same dates: -11.0%.
   - **+17.9% is right on its dates** (DGBAS monthly index, January 2003 55.05 to January 2005 64.91; January 2005 and both Decembers read at DGBAS today). On Laura's calendar, January 2003 to December 2004: **+18.4%**; December 2002 to December 2004: +19.6%.
   - **Not apples to apples in currency.** The shares are in US dollars and the building in Taiwan dollars. In US dollars (FRED EXTAUS monthly, 34.57 to 31.85 NT$ per US$) the same building cost **28.0% more** from January 2003 to January 2005 (27.2% to December 2004): the size of NUMBERS 8q's 1-in-20 two-year joint rise (+28.0%). The example understates the dollar need.
   - **The cushion was smallest at the meetings, not at the contribution.** World shares bought in January 2000 were 41-42% down at the January 2003 meetings (40.6% on end-of-month dates, 42.1% on start-of-month dates) and then rose 56-57% to January 2005, while costs rose 18%. The slump came first, the burst came during the recovery, and the recovery was not enough. NUMBERS 8q's own replay (US shares, with the currency) lists a January 2000 purchase as one of only two January purchases in 31 whose cushion fell short (1988, 2000).
   - **Q66's January-start count does not survive a one-month shift.** On end-of-January purchases, costs beat 5% a year in 3 of the 5 losing windows against 4 of 20 others; on start-of-January purchases the losing windows become 1998, 1999, 2000, 2007, 2008 and the count is **1 of 5 against 5 of 19**. Only the single 2000 window holds on both.
   - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan dollars the 2003-05 burst was a 1-in-8 two-year move (48 of 405 windows since 1991 at or above +17.9%); in the US dollars her shares are counted in, it was a 1-in-20 move, the same size as the stress NUMBERS 8q tests.
3. **Claim 3: CHANGE "7 of 9".** The year after 1974 and 2008, costs **fell in 6 of 9** (Hong Kong -30.3% in 1975; all five in 2009: Hong Kong -15.9%, Singapore -15.5%, Taiwan -8.9%, US -8.4%, Japan -3.6%). The UK's 0.0% in 1975 is 36 to 36 on a whole-number index (one point is about 3%), Japan's fiscal 1975 was +0.8% and the US +4.7%. So "7 of 9" counts a flat whole number as a fall, and a 1% line makes it 8 of 9. Truest: "fell in 6 of 9; two more were flat or nearly so".
4. **Claim 4 (aha row 9): STANDS with "can", with fixed evidence.** History supports "a share slump followed by a cost burst can catch her" (2000-05 in ACWI and NT$; the same window fails in 8q's dollar replay). It does not support a tendency: costs rose as often in fall years as in others, and Q66's U-shape (dear buildings also come with the largest half kept: world shares bought in January 2003 rose 124-137% over five years while costs rose 21.5-24.0% from the meetings) still stands. "Smallest" is the weak word: in the one example her half kept was smallest at the meetings and had grown 56-57% by the contribution. Suggested evidence clause in note form, in the Best five below.

## Gaps

- **MSCI World 1970-1996 at MSCI.** MSCI's level service refuses dates before 1 Jan 1997 (error read today); MSCI's factsheets give only recent calendar years. A primary annual table (MSCI's own history file, or Dimson-Marsh-Staunton world returns) would firm up 1973, 1974 and 1990 (10 of 36 country-years).
- **Gross ACWI for 2000.** MSCI serves gross ACWI only from 29 Dec 2000, so the January 2000 window's total return is bounded (-9.8% with no 2000 dividends), not exact.
- **Taiwan January 2003 at DGBAS.** The excerpts read printed every month around it (Dec 2002, Jul-Dec 2003, Jan 2005) but not January 2003 itself; the repo copy matched DGBAS at all 238 values checked. The xls (ccispl.xls) arrived as unreadable binary through the fetch tool.
- **Japan (MLIT) not re-read** (e-Stat and its mirror refused); a single consistent sub-index back to FY1960 (MLIT's own Excel) would replace Q66's three-index splice.
- **US Turner 1964-2003 (Census quarterly) and 2000-2003 year averages** were not re-read; 2004-2023 were.
- **Singapore before 2005** (BCA quarterly from 1987) is behind CEIC's paywall; it would add Singapore 1990 and 2000-02.
- **Taiwan 2008's yearly +14.0% and Dec-to-Dec +1.2%** were not re-read this run beyond January-June 2007-2009 (which match).
