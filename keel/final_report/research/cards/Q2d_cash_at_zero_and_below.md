# Q2d. Cash at zero and below: how often waiting cash has earned nothing, why US cash stops near zero, and what lies under it

- **Date:** Sun 15 Nov 2026, Sydney (sources read Sun 15 Nov 2026).
- **Report parts:** 3a (the ten payments: why they are sized so that all are paid if waiting cash earns nothing), Figure 1's note (the standard said once), 3c (the range ends are also sized at zero interest), 3e (assumptions and limits: what lies below zero, and what it would cost). Most rows are for the store only.
- Notes for the Final Report store, not report prose.
- **Not repeated:** NUMBERS 2 and 3 (design H_plain, its $336,656 cost and $1,414 spare at zero, the iBond funds' 5.4-7.4 month cash waits and the $781-$1,043 a rung's cash would earn at forwards); card Q31 rows 3 and 26-27 (the escrow wording "without reinvestment"); card Q2b claims 1-3 (escrows, lotteries and settlements stress-tested at zero); card Q16 rows 1-12 (declining-yield risk; IBTF and IBTE earning bill rates in their last months); card Q8b row 10 (EIOPA's down-shock floors near -1.25%); card Q26 rows 15 and T22 (the money fund inside an iBond fund); card Q2a (Treasury's 2008 money fund programme, HP-1286); store row H7-011 (its dates are corrected here, not restated).
- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Cash earning nothing is history, not an invented stress." How many months did the 3-month bill pay under 0.10%, 0.25% and 0.50%; what was the longest run; what did $50,000 waiting six months earn?
  2. "Treasury money market funds paid about nothing after fees."
  3. "Cash cannot earn less than nothing in US dollars."
  4. "The standard is the escrow profession's own." Skipped as a claim: card Q31 row 3 already holds the MSRB's "full cash or gross refunding … without reinvestment", and cards Q2b, Q17 and Q20 hold the verification-agent practice. One new row (19) adds why escrow tests stop at zero.
- **Problem class:** stress-testing a cash-flow match against the reinvestment rate. How low can the rate on money waiting between a bond's payout and a payment date go, and for how long? **Field that solved it first:** municipal refunding escrows and defeasance, where verification agents test the escrow with no reinvestment and Treasury sets escrow cash (SLGS) at no less than zero by rule (2012). Then central-bank research on the effective lower bound: Fed staff 2010-11, the BIS 2016, and the FOMC's 2019-20 framework review.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, with corrected dates and a count.** Zero is half of the recent record.
  - **The Fed's target.** It was 0-0.25% from 16 Dec 2008 to 16 Dec 2015 (7.0 years) and from 16 Mar 2020 to 16 Mar 2022 (2.0 years; announced Sunday 15 Mar 2020). That is 9.0 of the 17.9 years from Dec 2008 to Nov 2026, or 50% (row 1). The store's "from Mar 2020" is open-ended; the range ended 16 Mar 2022.
  - **The 3-month bill (FRED TB3MS, 2008-2022).**
    - Under 0.25% in 108 months, half of the 216 months from Nov 2008 to Oct 2026. The longest run was 82 months (Mar 2009-Dec 2015), then 22 (Apr 2020-Jan 2022).
    - Under 0.10% in 69 months (longest 32, Mar 2013-Oct 2015).
    - Under 0.50% in 122 months (longest 97, Nov 2008-Nov 2016).
    - Averages: 0.081% for 2009-2015, 0.048% for Apr 2011-Oct 2015 and 0.075% for Apr 2020-Dec 2021 (rows 2-4).
  - **The 1930s and 1940s.** The bill was under 0.25% in 83 months from 1934 to 1947. Together with 2008-2022, that is at least 191 of the 1,114 months since Jan 1934, or 17% (row 8).
  - **$50,000 waiting six months in bills earned:**
    - $48 in Jan-Jun 2009;
    - $4.6 to $36.7 in every half-year from Jul 2009 to Dec 2015;
    - $9.6 to $25.8 from Jul 2020 to Dec 2021.
  - **Against the register.** On one rung's 5.4-7.4 month wait, the 2009-15 average gives $18-$25, against the $781-$1,043 that NUMBERS 3 says the same cash earns at today's forwards (rows 5-6). Keel arithmetic on FRED and register figures.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** Treasury money funds paid 0.01% net only because their managers gave up fees. A bill ETF that gave up nothing lost a little.
  - **The money funds.** SEC statistics show Government and Treasury funds at 0.08% gross and 0.01% net (Dec 2014, Jan 2015), and Treasury funds at 0.01% net in every month from Dec 2020 to Oct 2021, on 0.13% to 0.06% gross. Crane Data counted 646 of 811 funds at 0.00% or 0.01% on 28 May 2021 (rows 9-10, 12).
  - **The waivers.** ICI counts about $36bn of fee waivers in 2009-2015 and $8.4bn in 2021, when 97% of money funds were waiving (row 11).
  - **The bill ETF.** BIL, a 1-3 month bill ETF with a 0.136% fee, returned:
    - fiscal years: -0.04% (to Jun 2013), -0.09% (2014), -0.11% (2015);
    - five years to Dec 2016: -0.04% a year;
    - calendar 2010-15 (secondary): -0.04% to -0.13%;
    - calendar 2021 (secondary): -0.10% (rows 13-14).
  - **Against the register's test.** The fee test charges 0.15% a year on waiting cash that earns nothing. That is harsher than every bill-ETF year found. The engine pays all ten payments with $187-$1,034 left at net rates of -0.13% to -0.04% held for every year from 2028 to 2042 (row 15).
- **Claim 3: KILL as worded; CHANGE to "US cash has stopped a hair below zero; Treasury will not sell below zero; a fund holding it can still lose its fee".**
  - **Below-zero closes.** The 3-month bill closed below zero on 8 days: -0.01% to -0.02% in Sep-Oct 2015, and -0.04% and -0.05% on 25-26 Mar 2020. The 4-week bill closed at -0.01% in Dec 2008 (row 16).
  - **The auction floor.** Treasury never auctions a nominal bill, note or bond below zero: the rate "may be positive or zero". It floors its CMT curve at zero for that reason (rows 17-18).
  - **The escrow floor.** Escrow cash in SLGS is set at zero, never below, by a 2012 rule (row 19).
  - **The Fed's view.**
    - Fed staff in 2010 judged that currency "would seem almost certain" to prevent "significantly negative short-term rates", allowing "around -10 basis points" (row 20).
    - In Oct 2019, the FOMC judged negative rates "did not appear to be an attractive monetary policy tool in the United States", without ruling them out (row 22).
  - **Money funds since 2023.** A US stable-NAV money fund may cancel shares when its gross yield goes negative from negative rates (row 23).
  - **Abroad.** The ECB, SNB and BoJ ran negative policy rates for about 8 years each (-0.50% ECB, -0.75% SNB, -0.1% BoJ), and euro money funds' returns went "systematically negative" (rows 24-28).
  - **The cost on one rung.** $50,000 waiting 5.4-7.4 months costs:
    - $11-$15 at the US worst close (-0.05%);
    - $113-$154 at -0.50%;
    - $169-$231 at -0.75% (row 30).
  - **The cost on the whole plan.** Holding the net waiting rate flat from 2028 to 2042, the plan is:
    - short $0 at -0.15%;
    - short $934 at -0.25%;
    - short $3,242 at -0.50%;
    - short $5,509 at -0.75%;
    - no shortfall before 2036 in any of these (row 31).
  - **Clash recorded, plan unchanged.** IPS l.11 strains if net waiting rates stay below -0.15% for years.
- **Claim 4: SKIPPED** (held by card Q31 row 3 and cards Q2b, Q17, Q20). Row 19 adds the SLGS zero floor, which is why escrow verifiers test at zero and not below.
- **The number a judge could be given for "why would cash ever earn nothing?":** the Fed held its target at 0-0.25% for 9 of the 18 years since Dec 2008. In those years a $50,000 payment waiting half a year in bills earned $48 at most, and under $37 in every half-year from Jul 2009 to Dec 2015, against about $780-$1,040 at today's rates (Keel arithmetic on FRED TB3MS and NUMBERS 3).

## (b) Cards

### Card 1. Zero as history (rows 1-8)
- **What is it?** The Fed's target sat at 0-0.25% for 9.0 of the 17.9 years from Dec 2008 to Nov 2026. The 3-month bill paid under 0.25% in 108 months, with a run of 82 months from Mar 2009 to Dec 2015. It paid under 0.10% for 32 months running, and averaged 0.081% over 2009-2015. The 1930s and 1940s did the same for over a decade.
- **Did it work?** As an answer to "why would cash ever earn nothing?", yes. $50,000 waiting six months in any half-year from Jul 2009 to Dec 2015 earned $4.6 to $36.7. A rung's own 5.4-7.4 month wait at the 2009-15 average earns $18-$25, which rounds to nothing.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** Her waiting cash is money already received (a coupon, a fund payout) sitting in bills until a payment date. That is exactly what TB3MS prices.
- **How is it not?** The record is a policy rate and a bill rate before fees. A fund's fee makes it slightly worse (card 2). And the store's dates for the second zero era need an end: 16 Mar 2022.
- **What Caplet takes.** 3a, in one clause, or Figure 1's note: the standard asks waiting cash to earn what it earned for half the years since 2008. The number in row 1 (9 of 18 years) or row 2 (108 of 216 months) is the judge's answer.

### Card 2. Money funds at a penny, bill ETFs a penny below (rows 9-15)
- **What is it?** In both zero eras, Treasury money funds yielded 0.01% net on 0.06-0.13% gross. Their managers gave up about $36bn of fees in 2009-2015 and $8.4bn in 2021 to stay above zero. BIL, a bill ETF that did not waive its 0.136% fee, returned -0.04% to -0.13% a year.
- **Did it work?** For the funds' holders, yes: they got a penny. For the ETF's holders, cash shrank by about a dime per $100 a year.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** The register's fee test charges 0.15% on waiting cash that earns nothing. That is the bill-ETF experience made a little worse. The engine pays all ten payments at every net rate the ETF actually returned, with $187-$1,034 left.
- **How is it not?** Waivers were a sponsor's choice, and the plan does not count on one. One ETF is not the one WInS lists, which is still a student check.
- **What Caplet takes.** 3a or Figure 1's note: the fee test is harsher than any bill-ETF year found (row 14; confirm in the annual reports before printing a year). 3e: the assumption names a fee. Row 33's real fee is for the lead, not the report.

### Card 3. Why US cash stops near zero (rows 16-22)
- **What is it?** Several things make zero the practical US floor:
  - Treasury will not sell a nominal bill, note or bond below zero (31 CFR 356).
  - It floors its CMT curve at zero for that reason.
  - It sets escrow cash (SLGS) at zero, never below (31 CFR 344, 2012).
  - Fed staff judged that currency "would seem almost certain" to stop significantly negative short rates, allowing about -0.10%.
  - The FOMC judged negative rates "did not appear to be an attractive monetary policy tool" in 2019 and again in 2020.
- **Did it work?** Mostly. Secondary-market bills closed below zero on only eight days, at -0.01% to -0.05%.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** The escrow profession tests at zero because its own cash cannot go under zero. Laura's standard copies that zero.
- **How is it not?** Laura cannot buy SLGS. Her waiting cash sits in an ETF, which can net below zero by its fee. That is why NUMBERS adds the 0.15% test on top of zero.
- **What Caplet takes.** Never "cash cannot earn less than nothing" (gem2's guard holds). If the report touches it, 3e can say US cash has stopped a hair below zero, for days, and the plan is sized for a fund that loses 0.15% a year. Cite DTB3, not DGS3MO.

### Card 4. The door the SEC opened (rows 21, 23, 29)
- **What is it?** In 2011 the NY Fed expected US money funds to close rather than pay negative rates. Japan's did close in 2016. In 2023 the SEC let a stable-NAV money fund cancel shares in a negative-rate event, with board approval and plain-English disclosure in advance. The SEC calls this "economically equivalent to a floating NAV".
- **Did it work?** It has not been used: the US has had no negative-rate event since the rule.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** If US rates ever went below zero, waiting cash in a fund would shrink in dollars rather than vanish. The plan's waiting cash would lose a little, not all.
- **How is it not?** The rule covers money funds. A bill ETF has no stable price to defend; it just reports a negative return.
- **What Caplet takes.** Store only. It kills any wording that says dollar cash cannot shrink.

### Card 5. Below zero abroad (rows 24-28)
- **What is it?** Three central banks ran negative policy rates for about eight years each:
  - the ECB to -0.50% (Jun 2014-Jul 2022);
  - the SNB at -0.75% (Jan 2015-Sep 2022);
  - the BoJ at -0.1% (Feb 2016-Mar 2024).
  - Denmark went first, in 2012.
  - Two-year yields reached -0.98% (Denmark) and -1.29% (Switzerland).
  - Euro money funds turned "systematically negative" within months, while retail deposits stayed at zero.
- **Did it work?** As policy, it is debated. For holders of cash in funds, it was a steady small loss.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** Below-zero regimes are real in major currencies. They set the far tail for 3e.
- **How is it not?** Laura's money is in US dollars. The US declined negative rates twice, and the foreign regimes lasted about 8 years, not the 15 the plan's money waits.
- **What Caplet takes.** 3e only, at most one clause: the deepest regime abroad (-0.75% for about 8 years) would cost the plan little and late (card 6). No new rule; the plan is frozen.

### Card 6. What below zero would cost Laura (rows 30-31)
- **What is it?** Keel ran the register's engine with one below-zero net rate on every waiting dollar for every year from 2028 to 2042. It also priced one rung's wait at each rate.
- **Did it work?** Down to -0.15%, all ten payments are paid. At -0.25% the plan is $934 short in total; at -0.75%, $5,509 short. No payment before 2036 is short. One rung's wait costs $169-$231 at -0.75%.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** The test asks the real question: how much of the cushion a deeper floor eats, and when.
- **How is it not?** It holds one rate for fifteen years, where real regimes lasted about eight. A shortfall would fall on money held in shares, which is where IPS l.11 strains (Clash column, row 31).
- **What Caplet takes.** 3e: a limit stated in one sentence if a judge would ask. The plan pays every payment if waiting cash loses up to 0.15% a year, every year. Below that, the shortfall is a few thousand dollars, late, met from what Laura kept. The lead decides whether these engine figures enter NUMBERS; until then they are Keel arithmetic, never report figures.

## (c) What would surprise a judge

- Half the time since Dec 2008, the Fed's target has been 0-0.25%. In those years $50,000 waiting half a year in bills earned $48 at most, against about $780-$1,040 at today's forward rates.
- Treasury money funds paid a penny only because their managers gave up about $36bn of fees in 2009-2015. A bill ETF that kept its fee lost money four fiscal years running, and the plan's fee test is harsher than any year it had.
- The US government will not sell a nominal bill below zero, and since 2012 it has set escrow cash at zero by rule. That is where the escrow profession's zero comes from.
- Since 2023 a US money fund may cancel its holders' shares if rates go below zero. Japan's money funds simply closed in 2016.
- If Swiss-style -0.75% ruled US cash for all fifteen years, the plan would end $5,509 short across seven payments, none before 2036.

## (d) Gaps

- TB3MS for 1960-1980 and 2023 to Oct 2026 was not read month by month. The 216-month and 1,114-month bases rely on the Fed target never being below 3.50% after 2022, and on rates far above zero in 1960-1980.
- BIL's calendar-year returns for 2010-2015 and 2021 come from secondary pages. Read the annual reports (N-CSR) before any year is printed.
- Which bill ETF WInS lists, and its fee, is still the student check in NUMBERS 0. Never read WInS.
- The range ends (3c) were not run below zero; only the ten payments were.
- No time-varying path was run (for example, -0.75% for eight years, then forwards). The flat runs overstate the cost.
- Whether any bill auction cleared at exactly 0.000% was not checked. The rules allow it; no auction below zero is allowed.
- The iBond funds' own cash in their last months is in the engine's waiting rate. Whether their money fund or bill holdings could go negative was not checked separately (card Q26 rows 15, T22).
- The Nikkei body was behind a paywall, and the Powell quote is from news reports, not a Fed transcript.
