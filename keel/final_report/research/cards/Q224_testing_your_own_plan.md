- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** telling a test that could have embarrassed a plan from one that could not, and deciding when a passed test licenses stopping. Philosophy of science (Popper, 1934 and 1963) named the idea, statistics-and-philosophy (Mayo, 1996; Mayo and Spanos, 2006; Mayo, 2018) turned it into a question one can ask of any check ("would this have found the flaw if it were there?"), and the metascience of the 2010s (Nosek et al., 2018) turned it into a habit (write down before the run what would count against you).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** Neither Popper nor Feynman says that testing a plan "until it breaks" is thinking. What they say is narrower and better for the team. Popper: confirmations "should count only if they are the result of risky predictions", and "every genuine test of a theory is an attempt to falsify it". Feynman: "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself—and you are the easiest person to fool", and you "report everything that you think might make it invalid". The dividing line between thinking and doubt is not how hard you push but whether you said beforehand what result would make you drop the plan, gave it its best case, and then told the reader what the test could not see. The record has one clean case of the first (the fund bar of Wed 30 Sep, written "before any number was run"), and the first-plan check is a case of the second (it gave the first plan the full deposit and still found a miss). "Until it breaks" is the part to drop: a plan tested without a stated stopping point always breaks, and Popper's own rule is that the game "is, in principle, without end" (LScD, rule 1), which is a rule about science, not a way for a team to finish a plan by 1 Jan 2027.
  - **Where neither would fit a portfolio plan.** (i) Popper's logic is about universal laws; a plan is a decision, and its chance claims ("a miss in 1 in 31") are, in his words, "not strictly falsifiable" because "they can never be logically contradicted by any basic statements" (LScD ch. VIII); one bad market is no refutation, and Popper wants "a reproducible effect" before he calls a theory falsified (section 22). So a plan is tested through its model and through history, not through its one future. (ii) Feynman's cure is replication ("repeat in her laboratory the experiment of the other person"); Laura's plan runs once. (iii) A plan must stop: the 2027 purchase has a date.
  - **Judge's note:** a smart reader expects "we stress-tested it" to be the sign of rigour; the better sign is a sentence of the form "we wrote down what would make us drop it before we ran it", and the best is "here is what our test could not see".
- **Claim 2: CHANGE (do not call it a severe test; say what it was).** Mayo and Spanos (2006): a hypothesis "passes a severe test T with data x0 if, (S-1) x0 agrees with H, and (S-2) with very high probability, test T would have produced a result that accords less well with H than x0 does, if H were false". Mayo (2018): "bad evidence, no test (BENT)" when the method "had little or no capability of finding flaws with C even if they exist". Applied to 27 Sep (NUMBERS 8s): (a) **Against the first plan, the check was stringent in the right direction.** It gave the plan its best case on the one assumption the team later distrusted: the 2028 deposit (full $150,000) arrived whatever shares did, years drawn independently. A lenient test that still finds a flaw (a miss in 3.2% of 200,000 paths, 1 in 31.0) finds a floor for the danger, so a correlation between shares and the deposit could only add to it (Card Q209 row 17: after a 2027 fall in shares the same model's miss is 5.9% with the full deposit and 23.2%, about 1 in 4, with $75,000; after a rise 1.9% and 8.9%; Keel arithmetic, slices of existing runs). (b) **For the Treasury plan the check was not a test.** The 27 Sep model's "never" (0.0%) was written into its code (NUMBERS 8s), so it could not have come out otherwise: Mayo's BENT. "None" rests on NUMBERS 2 (the ten paid with the 2028 deposit's first dollars even if waiting cash earns nothing; all 200,000 paths; all 585 historical rate windows). (c) **Not leniency in every direction.** On US history (Keel's replay, not run on 27 Sep) the first plan missed in 25 of 1,758 six-year windows (1.4%), all from one episode (starts Oct 1927 to Oct 1929), which is below the model's 3.2%. So 3.2% is a floor for the model's assumptions, not a floor for every assumption.
  - **A more severe test** would have made the deposit depend on the share path (the deposit cut in the years shares fall), then asked whether the first plan still looked adequate at the team's stated bar. The team's own record does not show one; the nearest the repo has is Q209 row 17's slices. Whether to run one is NUMBERS' call (8s's own variant "left open").
  - **Strengthens or weakens the moment?** It strengthens the reason and weakens any claim that 3.2% (or 1 in 31) is the first plan's true danger. IPS l.9 ("the actual nature of a writer's income") is exactly the link the model left out, so the report's reason stands on the team's own words, and the figure is honest only as "the best case, with the deposit arriving" (decision D16 already says this). Do not write "severe", "stress test passed" or "the test proved". Say "we gave the plan its best case and it still missed once in 31".
- **Claim 3: CHANGE.** "Passes the usual pass mark, so no step further" is the inference every testing tradition warns against, for one reason: a pass mark is a number, and the number is only as good as the test that produced it. Mayo and Spanos: "no evidence against is not evidence for" (their "fallacy of acceptance"); Neyman, quoted there, called treating a non-rejection as confirmation "a little rash" and "dangerous" unless the test's power is studied. Popper: corroboration is a report that a serious attempt to refute failed, "at most a report" (Mayo and Spanos's paraphrase of him), never a licence to stop. Feynman (Appendix F): a shuttle ring eroded and management said there was "a safety factor of three", "a strange use of the engineer's term"; "if now the expected load comes on to the new bridge and a crack appears in a beam, this is a failure of the design". For Laura: the first plan's 96.8 in 100 was computed on an assumption (the full deposit) that is the very thing IPS l.9 distrusts, so the pass mark could not have stood even if the usual reading of "high" had accepted it. The step further was not a rejection of a pass mark; it moved the ten out of the test's reach (no share, no deposit, no market path in them). It had a price, which is what makes it a decision and not a fear: in the 27 Sep model's own terms about $19,000 less left over at the median (8%), and the first plan leaves more in 56.0% of markets (NUMBERS 8s, Keel arithmetic, Tue 17 Nov evening).
  - **Judge's note:** a finance judge reads "passed the 90% test" as the end of the argument; the point a smart reader may have backwards is that passing it said how the number was made, not how safe the promise was.
