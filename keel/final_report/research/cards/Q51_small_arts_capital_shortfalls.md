## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE. Campaigns rarely miss their goal; they move it, stretch it, or close the gap in other ways.**
  - **Reported success is high.** Capital Campaign Pro's self-selected surveys: 108% of goal on average in 2023 (lowest 75%, highest 170%), 116% in the 2026 report, 94-96% calling the campaign a success. Kresge: "substantially all grantees meet the matching requirement"; cancellations were $153,000-$1,250,000 a year in 2003-2005, about 0.2%-1.2% of the year's capital grants in 2004-2005 (Keel arithmetic).
  - **But who gets counted is chosen.** Funders admit only campaigns that have already raised a half to two-thirds (store Q19-022); Washington ranks highest those with "upwards of 50 percent".
  - **Goals move.** Set in Stone's AT&T Performing Arts Center raised its goal "and raised again", opening "with the expanded capital campaign unfinished".
  - **Time stretches.** In the 2008 crash, about three-quarters of Kresge's capital grantees asked for "more time to complete the campaign".
  - **The gap is closed by cutting, borrowing, or later.**
    - Scope cuts: 22%-43% of small Capital Works! projects (the report gives both figures, see claim 5).
    - Debt: "50 to 60 percent of all projects" in NFF's 1994 study.
    - Weak finances after opening: about a third of organisations had "serious long-term problems" (NFF 1994); about half of the $1-building organisations ended financially weak.
  - **Judge's note:** small arts campaigns almost never "fail" on paper. The shortfall moves into scope, debt and the years after the ribbon is cut, which is where Laura's flexibility sits.
- **Claim 2: KILL as a base rate.** Kresge chose its applicants, mostly large institutions. It paid only campaigns that finished, and it records cancellations, not how far short anyone fell. Its one small-agency figure is endowment, not buildings: 110 of 119 nonprofits met a 1:3 endowment challenge by the 2005 deadline (92.4%; Keel arithmetic). Use Kresge as evidence that funders pay only for finished campaigns (store Q11c-016), never as a completion rate.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE. Whether it is big depends on her unknown share of the building.**
  - **The cushion as a share of the project.** The cushion is 25.9% of her stated top ($29,823 of $115,000; NUMBERS 4, 8k). The case gives no facility cost (case l.164-166), so call her share of the project s. The cushion is then 25.9% × s of the project's cost (Keel arithmetic):
    - she pays the whole building: 25.9%;
    - she pays half: 13.0%;
    - she pays a quarter: 6.5%.
    - At the house model's 1-in-20 low ($16,508), it is 14.4% × s.
  - **Yardsticks for small projects, each a share of the project's cost:**
    - Still to raise after Washington's state grant, for 13 projects with goals under $5m: median 14%, range 0%-75% (Keel arithmetic on the awardees report).
    - One mid-size public funder: the Cultural Spaces Fund paid 18% on average in 2018-23 and 27% in 2013-18 (Keel arithmetic on its evaluations). Without it, 48% of recipients' projects "would not have materialized".
    - Owners' own money in small Capital Works! projects: 12%.
    - A late inflation top-up on a large UK museum: 14.5%.
    - Reopening idle Taiwan Ministry of Culture buildings: 5.8% of what they cost to build.
  - **Reading.** If she pays half the building, the cushion is about the median gap left after a lead public grant. It is above what small-project owners put in themselves, and twice a reopening cost. It replaces a missing co-sponsor the size of the Cultural Spaces Fund only if she pays about 70% or more of the building (18% ÷ 25.9%; Keel arithmetic).
  - A cushion, not a cure.
  - **Judge's note:** the flexibility looks small next to her $115,000 top, but against the typical gap left in small arts projects it is about right, if she pays half or more.
- **Claim 4: KEEP, with numbers; it points at what Laura bought first.**
  - **NFF 1994.** About a third of organisations "end up with serious long-term problems as a result of their facility projects".
  - **Woronkowicz (2016).** After completion the surplus ratio falls, and costs such as debt "place strain" (read as abstract only).
  - **The $1 buildings.** Budgets more than doubled for 65%, and about half ended financially weak.
  - **Taiwan.** 46 of 399 subsidised local cultural halls changed use or stopped by the end of 2017. All 22 that changed use did so in the years after their subsidies ended (Control Yuan, 2018).
  - **Judge's note:** Taiwan's halls failed when the yearly money stopped, not when the building was finished. Laura owns ten years of running money by January 2028, before the facility's cost is even known (IPS l.11-13; NUMBERS).
- **Claim 5: CONFLICTING; change card Q5d row 16.** Capital Works! says "78% of large and 57% of small recipients delivered their projects to the scale originally intended" in its summary, infographic and section summary. One body paragraph gives the reverse (78% small, 57% large), and another says 57% "had needed to reduce" scope. Three of the four statements say small projects cut scope more often (43%). Q5d row 16 used the body paragraph. The lead should mark it CONFLICTING. The safe wording is "between a fifth and two-fifths of small projects cut scope to match the money".

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 10 ("arts managers lack 'early money'").** For 3b and 3d. The 1994 field study names the sector's oldest failure: committing before the money is there. Laura's bottom is early money, owned from January 2028. A judge sees that the plan answers a known problem, not an invented one.
2. **Row 35 (Taiwan's 399 halls).** For 3.0 and 3a. All 22 changes of use came in the years after subsidies stopped. That is a Taiwanese base rate for the exact risk the ten payments remove. It is local, official and recent.
3. **Row 31 with row 42 (the Washington gap and Keel's yardstick).** For 3b. This is the closest size-matched yardstick: a median 14% of a small project is still unfunded after the lead public grant. Her cushion is 13% of the project if she pays half. That turns "is $29,800 enough?" into one honest sentence with s named.
4. **Row 27 (Canada: 48% would not have happened).** For 3b and Table 3. It sizes "a co-sponsor falling short" (IPS l.15): one funder of about a fifth of cost decides half of small projects. That is why the cushion is a cushion, and why the report never promises it to co-sponsors.
5. **Row 18 (theatres "locked up" their cash).** For 3b. Theatres moved their cushion into bricks and had none left for repairs. Laura's flexibility stays out of the building and is promised to no one until a use named in IPS l.15 arrives.

## Gaps

- **Kresge.** No distribution of how far short grantees fell, and no completion rate by project size or field. There are only cancellation totals for 2003-2005 (row 2) and store Q25-020's 0.62%. Its later evaluations of capital grantees (NFF's 2010-12 study, store Q11b-023) have no shortfall counts.
- **NFF State of the Sector.** The 2015 and 2025 surveys ask no question about capital-project shortfalls (rows 20-21 are the nearest). The 2015 arts-focus post was read; it adds cash-reserve figures only.
- **Australia.** Create NSW and Creative Australia: no evaluation with completion or shortfall data was found. The NSW Audit Office's 2022 report on grant integrity (seen only in listings, not read) covers integrity, not shortfalls.
- **Canada.** The Cultural Spaces Fund's 2017 evaluation was not found as a separate report; the 2019 grouped evaluation (2013-18) and the 2025 evaluation (2018-23) were used. Card Q5d dates the 2025 page as 2024; the fetched page carried 18 Jun 2025.
- **Woronkowicz.** Woronkowicz (2016) was read as an abstract only, and Woronkowicz and Nicholson-Crotty (2017) only in a review's table: the paper search quota was used up. The next round should read both in full for effect sizes.
- **Washington.** The outcome of each project after award (finished, scope, extra money) was not traced, nor why Orcas Center and Sequim City Band came back.
- **Massachusetts.** The Cultural Facilities Fund publishes no count of cancelled grants or unmet matches. A records request or the advisory committee minutes may hold one.
- **Taiwan.** There is no data on private residencies' or private arts buildings' fundraising shortfalls. All Taiwan figures here are public halls and public facilities. The Ministry of Culture's 2016- programme for museums and local halls may report completions by project.
- **Capital Works!.** Which scope figure is right (row 23) could be settled from the survey tables in the full report's annex (figures for small projects, n=92) or by asking Arts Council England.
- **UK Heritage Fund.** No count or size of the 2023-24 grant increases.
- **Capital Campaign Pro.** The raw distribution of percent of goal for small or arts campaigns is not public.
