- **Claims tested:**
  1. "World shares lose money over three years about one time in five." The plan's house model says 22%.
  2. "The longer you hold shares, the less likely a loss." This is time diversification, the instinct in IPS l.9: "6 years to wait would usually own shares".
  3. "NUMBERS 8p's 40% fall before the 2031 move is a once-a-generation case."
  4. "A world fund loses less often over several years than any one market." This is Trading Note 3's diversification.
  5. "Mapped onto 8p's $5,000 steps, world history never gave a stated top below $105,000."
- **Problem class:** shortfall probability over a fixed horizon: how often a risky asset ends below what it cost after N years, and how far below.
- **Field that solved it first:** financial economics. It runs from Samuelson's 1963 "fallacy of large numbers" (store Q39-016) and Siegel's US record, through Jorion (2003) and Dimson, Marsh and Staunton on survivor-free world histories, to Anarkulova, Cederburg and O'Doherty's block bootstrap across 39 developed markets. Dzemski, Farago, Hjalmarsson and Kiss (2025) then measured how little any of it can be known at long horizons.

## Verdicts in one screen

1. **Claim 1: KEEP, tightened. The model is the pessimist.**
   - Over three years, world shares with dividends lost dollars in:
     - 12.7% of month-end spans since Dec 2000 (World, 35 of 275);
     - 12.0% of the same spans for ACWI;
     - 16.7% of calendar spans since 1970 (Q6c).
   - After US inflation the shares are 15.6%, 16.4% and 18.5%.
   - So: "about one start in six to eight in dollars, one in five to six after inflation".
   - On its face-value inputs (7.5% compound, 17.88% volatility, independent years), the house model says 22.1%. That is more cautious than history about how often shares lose.
   - On depth the two sit close. The model's 1-in-20 three-year result is -21.9%; history's is -16.5% to -19.7%.
   - Judge's note: the plan's own model expects a three-year loss more often than world shares have delivered one, so the report's cautious figures are cautious on purpose, not by accident.
2. **Claim 2: CHANGE. True in dollars, not after inflation, and the bad case grows.**
   - On MSCI World calendar spans since 1970, the dollar loss share falls with the wait: 16.7%, 13.2%, 11.5% and 2.0% at 3, 4, 5 and 6 years.
   - After inflation it does not: 18.5%, 22.6%, 21.2% and 17.6%.
   - Jorion's thirty markets agree: loss chances fall "very slowly, if at all" while the 1-in-20 loss doubles from one year to five.
   - A longer wait also makes a deep fall along the way more likely. Since 2001, 12.7% of 3-year spans held a 40% peak-to-trough fall, against 29.7% of 6-year spans.
   - Judge's note: waiting cured dollar losses, not inflation losses and not crashes. Over six calendar years since 1970, world shares lost dollars once but lost buying power nine times.
3. **Claim 3: CHANGE. Two different events share one name.**
   - A 40% fall somewhere inside three years comes about once a generation.
   - Ending three years 40% below cost is much rarer:
     - 0 of 275 month-end World spans since 2001 (worst -38.0%, Feb 2006 to Feb 2009);
     - 1 of 54 calendar spans since 1970 (2000-02, Q6c);
     - 1.1% in US history and 0.94% in the house model (NUMBERS 8p).
   - That is about one start in fifty to a hundred, and about twice the 1-in-20 three-year loss.
   - Since 2001 it happened only on the days around 9 Mar 2009. A 3-year span ending on the trough lost about 43.5% (Keel arithmetic, approximate).
   - So 8p's test is a stress beyond anything a month-end has shown since 2001, and of the size a co-sponsor would believe.
   - Judge's note: "once a generation" is right for living through a 40% crash and wrong for ending three years 40% down. The report's test is the rarer one, and it still leaves the bottom and every payment unchanged.
4. **Claim 4: KEEP, with numbers.**
   - Jorion's global index lost less often than the median market at every horizon. With dividends, its ten-year loss chance was 12.0% against 19.9%.
   - In Anarkulova, Cederburg and O'Doherty's 2023 bootstrap, an international portfolio lost after inflation less often than a home market at every horizon from 1 to 30 years: 25.9% against 29.0% at five years.
   - The exception is the US investor, whose home market was the survivor. Over five years, international lost 27.5% of the time against 21.8% for the US.
   - ACWI and World moved almost together since 2001 (largest monthly gap 1.15 points). Adding emerging markets barely changed the 3-year loss rate.
5. **Claim 5: KEEP, as Keel arithmetic only, never as a promise.**
   - Each 3-year world return is mapped onto 8p's steps, with rates held at today's forwards.
   - The stated top would never have been below $105,000:
     - World since 2001: $105,000 in 2.2% of spans;
     - ACWI since 2001: 1.8%;
     - World calendar spans since 1970: 1.9%, only 2000-02.
   - US history in NUMBERS reaches $100,000 (1929), and 8p allows $100,000 after an 89.8% fall.
   - Judge's note: in every world history since 1970, a stated top made by this rule moved by at most two $5,000 steps below the central $115,000, while the bottom did not move at all.
- **Clash watch (no clash found):**
  - A judge could turn the six-year result (2.0% of calendar spans since 1970, 0 of 239 month-end spans since 2001) against IPS l.9 and say "so own shares".
  - The IPS already answers it: the turn after "You'd think ..." rests on timing with her income, not on how often shares lose.
  - "Usually" is not the case's "high degree of certainty". And 2000-05 did lose dollars over six years.
  - DMS's world index lost 58% after inflation in 1916-20.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 12 (3c, Table 2's note).** "Living through a 40% fall" and "ending three years 40% down" are two different events.
   - The first comes about once a generation; the second about one start in fifty to a hundred.
   - It says exactly how hard 8p's test is, in one sentence, and the plan still keeps its bottom and every payment.
2. **Row 6 (2.3, 3e).** Waiting cured dollar losses, not inflation losses.
   - Over six calendar years since 1970, world shares lost dollars once and lost buying power nine times.
   - It is the cleanest "aha" against time diversification, and it fits a plan whose promises are in dollars and whose facility money is not.
3. **Row 17 (3e).** The plan's own model is the pessimist.
   - Over three years it expects a loss 22% of the time; world shares delivered 12-17%.
   - A judge sees the assumptions lean cautious, with a number behind it.
4. **Row 8 (2.3).** A longer wait makes it more likely you live through a crash (13% of 3-year spans, 30% of 6-year) and less likely you end in one.
   - It joins IPS l.9's turn (the crash is the recession that cuts her income) to the reason only VT's value on two dates matters.
5. **Row 16 (3c), labelled Keel arithmetic.** In every world history since 1970, a top made by the range rule fell at most two $5,000 steps below $115,000, while the bottom never moved.
   - It shows IPS l.15's "increase or decrease the top ... but the bottom will remain constant" in numbers.

## Gaps

- **MSCI's public end-of-day service** refuses start dates before 1 Jan 1997 and returned nothing before 29 Dec 2000.
  - So monthly World data for 1970-2000 and ACWI data for 1988-2000 were not read at source.
  - World calendar returns for 1970-2000 rest on the Wikipedia table in `numbers/fall40.py` (UNVERIFIED). A licensed MSCI download or Bloomberg would close this.
- **Daily MSCI levels** were not available, so the trade-day result in row 10 is approximate.
- **Kenneth French's developed-market factors** are served only as zip files, which could not be downloaded here, so there is no second monthly world series before 2001.
- **DMS's annual world series** is not public, so 3-, 4- and 6-year loss frequencies since 1900 could not be computed. The summary editions give only worst periods.
- **Estrada (2013, Journal of Asset Management, author copy SRTvLTR.pdf)** has five- and ten-year loss probabilities by country (Table A1). The world row was not extracted.
- **Jorion's global total-return loss rows** at one and five years were not extracted; only the ten-year 12.0% was.
- **The panel order in Anarkulova, Cederburg and O'Doherty's Table B.II** is Keel's inference. Their 2022 and 2025 follow-ups were not searched for 3-6 year world figures.
- **VT's own history**, against FTSE Global All Cap, was not computed.
- **Two-year spans (2031-2033)** are left to NUMBERS 8f.
- **The 5th edition of Siegel** (-11.9% a year) was seen only in a secondary review (readingthemarkets blog, 2014): UNVERIFIED.
- **McQuarrie (2024, Financial Analysts Journal)**: only the abstract was seen, and it was not used.
