- **Problem class:** estimating a share market's expected return from an accounting identity (yield plus growth plus change in valuation), the supply-side or dividend-discount sum. **Field that solved it first:** dividend-discount valuation (Williams 1938, Gordon 1962; named as prior art, not read this run), used for decades by US utility regulators to set allowed returns (FERC's DCF, read in its 2019-2020 form) and given its five-block form by Grinold and Kroner (2002) at Barclays Global Investors.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** On today's own numbers the sum comes out clearly below 7.5%, at about 5%.
  - Inputs, each dated: dividend yield 1.61% (MSCI ACWI, 30 Oct 2026; VT 1.60%, 31 Oct); inflation 2.20% (10-year breakeven, FRED, 16 Nov 2026); real growth 1.89% (the IMF's October 2026 forecasts for 2031 weighted by ACWI's country weights, Keel arithmetic); net buybacks anywhere from -2.0% to +0.67% (rows 10-12).
  - Sum (Keel arithmetic): 3.7% to 6.4% with no change in valuation; 3.1% to 5.7% with J.P. Morgan's own valuation block of -0.65% a year. The middle anchors give 4.7% to 5.9%.
  - That is near Syzygy's 5.0% and below the fifteen-house median of about 6.5% (Q6b). The plan's base case therefore has a stated weakness, not an independent confirmation.
  - Judge's note: the forecast Caplet named in the TNA is a building-block figure. Rebuilt from today's yield, today's inflation and the IMF's growth, the same blocks give about 5%. The 2.5 points between them are J.P. Morgan's view on AI-driven sales, buybacks and a weaker dollar.
- **Claim 2: KEEP.** J.P. Morgan: "We build our equity return assumptions from revenues, margins, net dilution (buybacks less issuance), valuations and dividend yield".
  - Exhibit 6 blended to ACWI by MSCI's weights (Keel arithmetic): revenues +5.92, margins -0.77, gross dilution -1.53, buybacks +2.20, valuations -0.65, dividends +1.93, currency +0.38. Total 7.48 against the published 7.50. Each region sums exactly to its printed total (US 7.5, other developed 7.6, emerging 7.1).
  - Bridge from a plain sum of 5.90% (Keel arithmetic): +1.06 because company earnings grow faster than the economy (5.15% against 4.09% nominal), +0.47 more net buybacks, +0.32 a higher dividend block, +0.38 a weaker dollar, -0.65 valuations. Total 7.48.
  - One clause for 3e: built from company sales, margins, buybacks, dividends and valuations; it assumes sales outgrow the economy and valuations fall only a little (P/E from 20.82 to about 19.5 over ten years).
- **Claim 3: CHANGE (sharpen by horizon).**
  - Over ten years, valuation (0 to -2.6% a year if the P/E returns to 16) and net buybacks (a 2.7-point spread across published anchors) roughly tie. Growth spans about 1 point. Yield and inflation are known to about 0.3.
  - Over the plan's own three years, January 2028 to the 2031 move, valuation swamps everything. A P/E falling from 20.82 to 18 costs 4.7% a year; to 16 it costs 8.4%; a rise to 24 adds 4.9% (Keel arithmetic).
  - Grinold and Kroner: "the noisiest component of returns is clearly the P/E repricing component". Their 2002 forecast missed high on it (their own grading, 2011). Their 2011 forecast of 7.03% missed low on it: the S&P 500 returned about 14.2% a year to 2021 (Keel arithmetic, UNVERIFIED mirror).
- **Claim 4: CHANGE (mostly kill).**
  - The stated $115,000 needs VT about 3.4% a year to the move (NUMBERS 8p). Yield 1.61 plus inflation 2.20 already give 3.81% before any real growth: about 11.9% over three years against the 10.6% needed (Keel arithmetic).
  - So a lower ten-year forecast mainly lowers her flexibility. On 8p, Syzygy's 5.0% gives $18,828 against $21,179 at 7.5%, with the stated top unchanged at $115,000.
  - The step is lost mainly if prices fall against earnings in those three years. Depending on the buyback anchor, it survives until ACWI's P/E falls from 20.82 to about 20.6, 20.0, 19.3 or 19.0 by January 2031 (Keel arithmetic).
  - Only the gloomiest pair of blocks (3.1%) sits under the 3.4% hurdle. There is no NUMBERS run at these values (Gaps).
  - Never "the top stays $115,000 at every return" (8p wording rule).
  - Judge's note: a smart reader assumes a lower forecast lowers what Laura can promise; it mostly lowers what she keeps. The promise turns on three years of valuation, the block the method's own authors call the noisiest.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 16 (the bridge from 5.9% to 7.5%), for 3e area (1).** It gives the assumption's basis in one clause: sales outgrowing the economy, more buybacks, a weaker dollar, and only a small fall in valuations. That is the "basis" Wharton asks for, and it is a stated weakness rather than a hidden one.
2. **Rows 23-24 (the reverse sum), for 3c and 3e.** This is the aha: yield plus inflation (3.81%) already clears the 3.4% the stated $115,000 needs. So a lower forecast mostly lowers what Laura keeps, and the step is lost mainly through three years of valuation.
3. **Row 5, with row 4 (the method missed both ways, both on repricing), for 3e.** The method's own authors graded themselves too high in 2002 and too low in 2011. It shows why a forecast is a centre and why the plan states figures only once Treasuries pay them.
4. **Row 32 (Diamond's 2000 Social Security check), for 3e.** A strikingly dissimilar parallel from public pensions: the sum said a 35-45% real fall was needed, and one came, then a decade of strong returns.
5. **Row 38 (the drafted 3e content), for 3e and Table 2's note.** It covers one assumption, its basis and its cost if wrong ($18,800 against $21,200 of flexibility at 5%, stated top unchanged), all from NUMBERS 8p.

Runner-up: row 19 (J.P. Morgan's currency block and IPS l.15's stronger Taiwan dollar are the same move; never phrased as a hedge).

## Gaps

- **Grinold and Kroner (2002):** the only copy reached is truncated. Their forward inputs and forecast total were not read; the 2002 inputs come from the 2011 retrospective, and the premium "about 2.5%" from Song (2007).
