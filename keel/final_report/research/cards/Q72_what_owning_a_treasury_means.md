- **Problem class:** tiered holding of securities: who holds what for whom, who bears which failure, and the legal line between property held for you and a debt owed to you. **Field that solved it first:** the commercial law of indirect holding (Uniform Commercial Code Revised Article 8, 1994; Treasury's TRADES rule, effective 1 Jan 1997, built on it), broker-dealer customer protection (the Securities Investor Protection Act, 1970; SEC Rule 15c3-3), and investment company law (the 1940 Act's custody section 17(f); the Delaware statutory trust's ring-fenced series), with the Lehman (2008-2022) and MF Global (2011-2016) liquidations as the record.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, and "owned" is the legal word.**
  - What Laura holds is a "security entitlement" (UCC 8-501). The assets her broker holds for customers "are not property of the securities intermediary, and are not subject to claims of creditors of the securities intermediary" (8-503(a)). Hers is "a pro rata property interest" in all of that bond the broker holds (8-503(b)).
  - The broker "shall promptly obtain and thereafter maintain" enough of each bond (8-504(a)), "may not grant any security interests" in it without her agreement (8-504(b)), and "shall take action to obtain a payment ... made by the issuer" (8-505(a)).
  - SEC Rule 15c3-3(b)(1): a broker "shall promptly obtain and shall thereafter maintain the physical possession or control of all fully-paid securities". Securities in a cash account are "fully paid" (15c3-3(a)). The IPS borrows nothing, so every holding is in that class.
  - **The record.** Lehman Brothers Inc. (liquidation from Fri 19 Sep 2008): customers received $106 billion, "fully satisfying the 111,000 customer claims"; "Most customer claims were satisfied within weeks". The same firm's unsecured general creditors received 41.2841% (SIPC, 2022).
  - **Judge's note:** at the same failed broker, what customers owned came back in full and what the firm owed its lenders came back at about 41 cents in the dollar. "Owned, not owed" (SKELETON 3d) has a price tag.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE. SIPC's $500,000 caps SIPC's top-up, not what comes back.**
  - Customers first "share ratably in such customer property on the basis and to the extent of their respective net equities" (15 U.S.C. 78fff-2(c)(1)). SIPC then advances only "the amount by which the net equity of each customer exceeds his ratable share of customer property", "not to exceed $500,000 for each customer" (78fff-3(a)).
  - So near the end, when all the money is $589,655 in 1 Jan 2033 money (NUMBERS 8k), the $89,655 above $500,000 (Keel arithmetic) is at risk only if the broker's pool of customer property is itself short, by more than SIPC fills. A hole in the customer pool did open at MF Global (mostly in futures accounts), and even there every customer was paid 100%, after about two and a half years (rows 17-18).
  - SIPC's own record since 1970: "an estimated 773,000 investors", $143.8 billion recovered, $3.6 billion advanced, "no fewer than 99 percent" of eligible persons made whole.
  - **What remains is time.** Lehman's accounts moved "within weeks". MF Global's 330 retail securities accounts waited for a court order 39 days after the trustee's appointment (Keel arithmetic). A broker failure in December could hold a 1 January payment.
  - **For Table 3's custody row:** keep the row; reword its reason from "holdings above SIPC's limit" to "a broker's failure delays; SIPC's limit applies only to a hole in the customer pool".
- **Claim 3: KILL. The fund's Treasuries belong to the fund, held at a bank, and BlackRock owns none of them.**
  - **The fund is a ring-fenced series.** The iShares Trust Declaration, s.2.07: a series' assets "shall belong to that Series for all purposes, and to no other Series". Its liabilities are "enforceable against the assets of such Series only" and "not enforceable against the assets of the Trust generally or any other Series". This is the Delaware Statutory Trust Act's s.3804(a).
  - **Custody is at a bank by law.** The 1940 Act, s.17(f)(1): every fund "shall place and maintain its securities and similar investments in the custody of (A) a bank" or an exchange member, or itself under SEC rules. An iShares custodian "maintains, in separate accounts, cash, securities and other assets of the Trust and each Fund" (iShares Trust, 2022 supplement).
  - **A sponsor event is a vote, not a loss.**
    - An advisory contract must provide "for its automatic termination in the event of its assignment" (s.15(a)(4)).
    - It has happened to iShares itself: in 2009 "Barclays Global Fund Advisors, the adviser to the iShares Funds, is being acquired by BlackRock, Inc."; "Shareholder approval on three proposals is requested".
    - It has happened to a fund family whose parent failed: Lehman Brothers Holdings filed on 15 Sep 2008, and its Neuberger Berman funds' adviser "will continue to operate in the ordinary course of business" (supplement of 1 Oct 2008).
  - **The contrast that makes the point.** Lehman's Opta exchange-traded notes were "senior, unsecured debt securities of Lehman Brothers Holdings Inc." that "Do not represent ownership in the underlying assets" (Lehman, 2008). They were delisted on 1 Oct 2008, and holders recovered 44.5% as unsecured creditors (etf.com, 2018, secondary).
  - **Judge's note:** an exchange-traded note owes; an exchange-traded fund owns. In 2008 the difference cost note holders about 55 cents in the dollar. Laura's floor sits in the kind that owns.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE. The US promises to pay, but it pays the bank at the top of the chain, and the rest is holding, not promising.**
  - "The faith of the United States Government is pledged to pay, in legal tender, principal and interest" (31 U.S.C. 3123(a)).
  - Under TRADES, Treasury's obligation "is discharged" when a Reserve Bank credits the Participant's funds account (31 CFR 357.13(c)). For a redemption, "No action by the Participant is required" (357.13(d)).
  - "Participants have a direct claim against the United States for interest and principal even though, under state law, an Entitlement Holder would only have a claim against its Securities Intermediary"; "Persons at levels below the Participant level must present their claims to their Securities Intermediary" (Part 357, Appendix B).
  - So Laura's ink is the Treasury's promise held for her, not a promise made to her by name. Below the Participant every link is a holder with a legal duty to hold and pass on (8-503 to 8-505), and none promises a dollar of its own.
  - The IPS's "none of your 10 operating payments will depend on ... anybody else's money" (l.11) stays true: only a hole in a failed broker's customer pool would bring SIPC's own money in.
- **Claim 5: KEEP the definition as Caplet's words; name the chain once.**
  - **For the T-bond rows** (28.27% of the book at cost, NUMBERS 8g), "owned in Treasuries" is exact: a property interest in the bonds.
  - **For the five fund rungs and the floor** (iBond funds 61.28% at cost, IBTM 32.50%, NUMBERS 8g), Laura owns "a proportionate undivided beneficial interest" in a fund that owns Treasuries (Declaration s.3.08). The fund "does not seek to return any predetermined amount at maturity" (IBTM summary prospectus, 2026). So "will pay it on its date" rests on the fund's Treasuries, less its fee, which is how NUMBERS already counts each fund (CONCEIT section 8; card Q3c: five ended funds paid in cash within 0.07% of their last price).
  - **The definition is true.** What the evidence adds is one plain note for 3e or beside Table 1: "owned" means hers in law, held for her by a broker and, for five of Table 1's eleven holdings (and the floor bought in 2028), through a Treasury fund at a custodian bank. The only promise in any chain is the US Treasury's. Never "in her name": in street name the registered owner is a nominee such as Cede & Co. (investor.gov, 2023). Say "held for her".

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 16 (same broker: customers 100%, lenders 41%), for 3d and 3e.** It turns "owned, not owed" from a slogan into a measured fact a judge can repeat: Lehman's broker returned every dollar its customers owned and 41 cents of every dollar it owed.
2. **Row 32 (the ETN that owed and the ETF that owns), for 2.2 or 3e.** It shows a judge the exposure the plan avoided by its choice of wrapper: in 2008 holders of exchange-traded notes recovered 44.5%, while a fund's Treasuries are not its sponsor's to lose (rows 23, 29-31).
3. **Rows 4 and 5 (Treasury pays the bank at the top, automatically; below it, holders), for 3e and the definition in 2.0.** Together they give the exact, honest answer to "whose promise is the ink": the Treasury's, held for Laura by a chain with legal duties, and "No action by the Participant is required".
4. **Row 13 (SIPC caps the top-up, not what comes back), for Table 3's custody row and 3e.** It corrects a store framing that a bond reader would catch, and keeps the true residual: time, not money.
5. **Rows 22 and 28 (a proportionate undivided interest; a fund that promises no amount), for the definition in 2.0 and Table 1.** They make "owned in Treasuries" exact for the five fund rows of Table 1 and the floor without calling a fund's payout fixed.
