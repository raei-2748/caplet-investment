- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "CASE's Global Reporting Standards count outright gifts, written pledges over their payment period and irrevocable deferred gifts. They report revocable commitments apart and leave conditional pledges out. Asia has a counterpart."
  2. "Under US GAAP (ASC 958-605 after ASU 2018-08), Laura's 'nobody has to pay unless the total is promised by December 2032' makes her pledge conditional. A co-sponsor books nothing in 2031, and Taiwan and IFRS do the same."

- **Problem class:** recognising and counting a promise of future money: at what moment, and at what amount, a promise becomes a number in someone else's books. **Field that solved it first:** nonprofit contribution accounting (FASB's contribution standard, Statement 116, now ASC 958-605 as amended by ASU 2018-08), alongside the fund-raising profession's counting standards (CASE's reporting standards, 4th edition, now the Global Reporting Standards).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, with two changes.** CASE does not drop conditional pledges for good: it waits on an expectation. Asia's nearest counterparts are CASE's own Australia and New Zealand rules and Hong Kong's matching scheme. No Taiwanese counting rule was found.
  - **Two counts.** "New Funds Committed" holds new outright gifts, "new documented pledges", irrevocable planned gifts and qualified bequest intentions, "at face value". Since 2024 a long pledge counts "in their entirety". "Funds Received" counts payments when they arrive (CASE FAQ; CASE Insights on Campaigns guidance, 2025).
  - **Conditional pledges.** These stay out "until such time that it is anticipated the conditions will be met". CASE's example is a building pledge conditioned on matching money within a set time (CampaignSource guidance, pp. 9-10). This is already in Q11b and Q18. New here: CASE's test is an expectation, while FASB's says probability "is not a factor".
  - **Revocable commitments** are "collected and reported separately". Bequests count only for donors aged 65 or more. CASE: conditional and revocable pledges "remain in the control of the donor until the institution realizes the gift".
  - **Universities follow this.** Houston, Fresno State and Kent State count a conditional pledge on a "reasonable expectation". Stevens: "Conditional pledges are recorded in the fundraising database for recognition and campaign counting purposes only; they are not booked in the university's general ledger."
  - **Asia-Pacific.**
    - CASE's Australia and New Zealand survey (Feb 2026) counts only documented pledges, and no "oral pledges". A clause returning unspent money still lets "the full amount pledged" count, but other conditions may exclude the gift.
    - Hong Kong's government matching scheme counts bonds only when "ownership is completely transferred".
    - Taiwanese arts bodies book gifts when the money arrives (Taipei Culture Foundation; National Culture and Arts Foundation).
  - **Judge's note:** the same co-sponsor pledge can sit in a campaign total while it is zero in the ledger. A gift office counts what it expects. A finance office books only what is no longer conditional.
- **Claim 2: KEEP the rule, KILL the premise.** On SKELETON 3d (l.266) and round 8's own passages, Laura's figure is "first and unconditional" ("Mine comes first and carries no condition"). The December 2032 sentence is "said of the co-sponsors' pledges".
  - **If the sentence does not say whose**, FASB presumes any stipulation "not clearly unconditional" is conditional (958-605-25-5E). Her figure would then book as nothing in 2031. So the sentence must name the co-sponsors' pledges.
  - **What a co-sponsor books in 2031.** A co-sponsor that pledges in 2031 on that condition books nothing then. A giver records "unconditional promises to give cash ... as payables and contribution expenses", and a conditional one only when the condition is met (720-25-25-1). Giver and receiver book "at the same time" (958-605-25-2). The residency's notes would list such pledges in a group like "raising matching gifts by a specified date" (958-310-50-4). The co-sponsor books a payable and an expense on the day the total is promised.
  - **Taiwan.**
    - The Ministry of Health and Welfare's foundation standard says the same in Chinese (應付捐贈款: 「有條件捐贈承諾則應俟條件滿足後，方認列為負債」). It adds that a pledge the giver may revoke is never a liability.
    - The Ministry of Culture's standard sends culture foundations to Taiwan's business standards (EAS, with IFRS allowed). Those have no pledge rule.
    - One foundation's own manual keeps the "remote chance" test that FASB deleted in 2018.
  - **IFRS family.**
    - IPSAS 23 never books a pledge: pledges are "unenforceable undertakings", at most a contingent asset.
    - The UK SORP books a donation "usually ... on receipt".
  - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan, a co-sponsor's board approval is the date its promise becomes its debt. The National Culture and Arts Foundation books grants "approved by its board and announced" as payables: NT$239,291,599 at the end of 2025.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE.** No rule read here records a range as a range. Each records one amount: a stated figure, a stated minimum, or nothing.
  - **Practitioners.** A consultant on the Advancement Services forum: "You cannot record a bonafide pledge - unless the donor stipulates a minimum amount". Offices record "$0 pledges", because there is "nothing here you can count on - only the money as it comes in the door".
  - **University policies.** EWU counts an uncertain bequest at "the reasonable minimum estimated value", or $5,000. Fresno counts a revocable commitment of unknown amount "at $1".
  - **Software.** Raiser's Edge keeps "Ask", "Expected" and "Funded" as separate numbers. Blackbaud CRM and Salesforce weight a prospect by likelihood ($40,000 at 50% is $20,000).
  - **So** a gift office handed two numbers books the lower one. That is what the funder test found: framing A was read at $95,000, and framing B (one figure) at $115,000.
  - **Judge's note:** a range does not reach a funder's books as a range. It reaches them as its bottom.
- **Claim 4: KEEP one figure. CHANGE how the bottom is said and who carries the condition.**
  - **One figure books everywhere.** $115,000, written with commitment words, dated, and with no condition on her part, is what every book above can carry: a documented pledge at face value (CASE), a receivable (FASB), and 應收捐贈款 backed by "可供辨明之文件" (MOHW).
  - **The bottom.** It helps only as a date on part of that figure ("held since January 2028"). Read as a lower end ("from $95,000"), it is the number the office books.
  - **The middle path.** If Laura wants the money back should the facility fail (card Q77), a clause returning unspent money keeps the figure countable in full (CASE ANZ). Under FASB a right of return with no barrier leaves a gift unconditional (BC31, Q90 row 31). A condition on the building itself would make her figure book as nothing.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 15 (with rows 12 and 14), 3d.** A gift office cannot book a range. It books a stated minimum or nothing. This gives the funder test's result a reason a judge can check: one figure, $115,000, is the only form that reaches a funder's books as itself.
2. **Row 24, 3d.** Round 8's December 2032 sentence must name the co-sponsors' pledges. Under FASB's presumption, an unscoped condition would make Laura's own figure book as nothing in 2031.
3. **Rows 3-4, 3d and 3c.** CASE counts a conditional pledge on an expectation, while FASB says probability "is not a factor". So the same co-sponsor pledge can sit in a campaign total and at zero in the ledger. This is an aha for a judge who thinks "pledged" means "booked".
4. **Row 31, 3d and Table 3.** Taiwan's national arts funder books its own grants as debts once its board approves and announces them (NT$239,291,599 at the end of 2025). The co-sponsors' board dates are the thing to watch, and this shows client knowledge specific to Taiwan.
5. **Row 41, 3d.** A clause returning unspent money keeps Laura's figure countable in full and unconditional. A condition on the building would not. This settles the tension between card Q77 and SKELETON 3d.

## Gaps

- **CASE Global Reporting Standards.** The full text (2nd edition, 2024 update) is member-only. This card reads CASE's public FAQ, its survey guidance and university policies that quote it. Chapter 3.4.1 on conditional pledges was not read in full.
- **No Taiwanese campaign-counting rule.** Searches for a university or foundation rule in Chinese (認捐 計入, 募款總額 認列) found only accounting standards and pledge forms (Q83, Q128).
- **No CASE rule for Asia beyond Australia and New Zealand.** Nothing was found for Hong Kong, Singapore or Taiwan beyond the UGC's matching scheme.
- **The Ministry of Culture's standard** defers to EAS, and no EAS bulletin on donations or pledges was found. How Taiwanese arts foundations' auditors treat a written pledge is therefore known only from one set of statements (Taipei Culture Foundation) and one manual (鄒濟勳).
- **Ames & Mason (2015).** Only the abstract and two summaries were read. The range widths and pilot shares are UNVERIFIED.
- **INPAG** (the international nonprofit accounting guidance, 2025) was not read. Its pledge rule may be the IFRS-family counterpart a Taiwanese auditor would cite next.
- **FASB ASC 958-310-50-4** was read in a codification copy, not on FASB's own site (the codification is behind a login).
- **No source said how a funder records a founder's stated range** (as against a donor's own pledge) in those words. Row 15 joins sources that each speak of one amount.
