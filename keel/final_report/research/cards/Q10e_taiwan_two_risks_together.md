# Q10e. Taiwan's two risks together: building costs and the Taiwan dollar against a US-dollar contribution

- **Date:** Sun 15 Nov 2026, Sydney (sources read Sun 15 Nov 2026; the US date was Sat 14 Nov).
- **Report parts:** 3b (her flexibility and its three uses), 3e (assumption area 4, inflation and the Taiwan dollar; limits; what to watch as 2031 approaches), Table 3 row 7 ("Her flexibility's three uses"), a possible exhibit (the joint index, 1991-2026). Most rows are for the store only.

- **Not repeated:** card Q10 rows 18-25 (the Taiwan dollar's levels and ranges, the September 2026 index, the 5-, 10- and 25-year building-cost rates, 6-year currency windows); card Q10b rows 1-4 and 10 (currency-only two-year windows on daily data since 1983, the worst currency moves, the weak side); card Q10c rows 2-5, 9 and 16 (costs rise in bursts that stay; 2021-22 was materials-led; public works reprice above 2.5%, with the Legislative Yuan's payment totals); card Q5e verdict 1 (building costs against TAIEX, the S&P 500, Turner and BEA in the same year and the next); NUMBERS 8d (what the dollars buy), 8j (the currency test, house model and history), 8k (what returns to Laura). - **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Taiwan building costs and the Taiwan dollar have moved against a US-dollar budget in the same years."
  2. "A 10% joint stress is the right size for the plan's test."
  3. "In the years the two moved together, shares were falling too, so Laura's half kept was small exactly when needed."
  4. "Taiwanese building contracts pass cost rises to the owner."
- **Problem class:** a foreign-currency capital budget funded in a home currency: a sum fixed in US dollars in 2031 and spent on Taiwan building work from about December 2032, exposed to the price of building in Taiwan and to the price of the Taiwan dollar at once. **Field that solved it first:** corporate treasury budgeting for overseas capital projects (the "budget rate") and construction cost-escalation analysis (AACE International's escalation and currency practice; owners' contingency guidelines), with Taiwan's own public-procurement price-adjustment rules for who carries a cost rise during the build.

**Method (Keel arithmetic on primary data, all read at source today).** The US-dollar cost of a fixed Taiwan building budget = DGBAS's construction cost index (營造工程物價指數, total index, 2021 = 100, monthly from Jan 1991 to Sep 2026) divided by NT$ per US$ (FRED EXTAUS, the Fed's G.5 monthly average, Oct 1983 to Oct 2026). A window's "joint move" is the change in that ratio: the percentage more US dollars the same NT$ building needs at the end of the window. This is NUMBERS 8j's convention ("10% more US dollars for the same NT$ budget"). Every 18- and 24-month window, all start months. World shares: MSCI ACWI in US dollars, gross total return, month-ends from Dec 2000 (price index from Jan 1997 spliced on for the longer test). DGBAS's index was used, not a Ministry of the Interior series, because it is the index Taiwan's public contracts adjust by (the PCC's model contract names DGBAS by default; rows 26-27), it is monthly since 1991, and it is the register's (8d). Scripts and outputs are in the scratch area (`.../scratchpad/w3s/joint*.py`, `.../scratchpad/w3/joint*_out.txt`). Dollar sizes against the register are Keel arithmetic on register figures: the central top $115,000 and the bottom $95,000 (NUMBERS 8k, 8j); her flexibility $29,823 (half kept $21,347 plus excess $8,476, central, NUMBERS 8k); house 1-in-20 lows $16,193 (with excess) and $9,881 (half kept alone, NUMBERS 8j). On $115,000, her central flexibility covers a joint move of 25.9%, the half kept alone 18.6%.

## Verdicts in one screen

**Headline.** The single worst joint move since 1991: **+38.9% in 24 months, Jul 2006 to Jul 2008** (building costs +29.5%, the Taiwan dollar 7.2% stronger; index 71.10 to 92.10, NT$32.617 to NT$30.414). On the central top that is **$44,756 more** for the same Taiwan building, $14,933 beyond her central flexibility ($29,823). The worst 18-month move is the same episode: +37.6%, Jan 2007 to Jul 2008. (Keel arithmetic on register figures.)

- **Claim 1: KEEP, with numbers, and CHANGE the episode named.**
  - **They do move together, more than chance.** Over 24-month windows since 1991 the building-cost change and the currency change correlate +0.27 (+0.30 since 2000; +0.16 in the last ten years). Their co-movement supplies 21% of the joint variance (cost 38%, currency 40%).
  - **What that costs in the tail.** The 1-in-20 two-year joint move is +28.0%; if the two were independent it would be +24.8%. Moves above 20% came in 14.6% of windows, against 9.3% under independence: about 1.6 times as often.
  - **Distribution, 24 months (405 windows since 1991):** median +3.0%, 1 in 10 above +24.5%, 1 in 20 above +28.0%, 1 in 100 above +34.3%. Above 10% in 30.6% of windows, above 15% in 21.0%, above 20% in 14.6%. Since 2000 (297 windows): median +6.4%, 1 in 20 above +28.1%; above 10% / 15% / 20% in 36.7% / 24.9% / 16.8%.
  - **Distribution, 18 months (411 windows since 1991):** median +2.0%, 1 in 20 above +24.6%; above 10% / 15% / 20% in 25.5% / 16.1% / 10.7%. Since 2000: 1 in 20 above +24.1%; 29.4% / 17.8% / 10.9%.
  - **The 2020-22 episode, exactly.** The joint index bottomed in Aug 2019 (index 89.20; NT$31.389) and peaked in Mar 2022 (index 106.88; NT$28.431): +32.3% in 31 months. The worst 24-month window inside it is Aug 2019 to Aug 2021, +29.0% (costs +14.5%, currency +12.7%). It is the only episode since 1991 in which both halves were large at once. The Taiwan dollar's monthly average reached 27.672 in Jan 2022, its strongest since Apr 1997; daily, 27.56 on 1 Jun 2021 and 27.52 on 4 Jan 2022 (Fed H.10). The building index rose fastest in Jul 2021 (+13.71% on a year).
  - **But 2021-22 was not the worst.** The worst joint moves were cost-led: 2006-08 (+38.9%), 1991-93 (+31.0%, the 1992 sand-and-gravel crisis plus a strong Taiwan dollar), 2002-04 (+29.4%). Currency-led episodes were smaller: 2009-11 (+24.6%; currency +16.3%) and 2016-18 (+19.5%).
  - **The two halves come apart afterwards.** After Mar 2022 the currency half reversed within seven months (Oct 2022: NT$31.959; joint index -10.4%) while building costs stayed (107.69). By Sep 2026 the joint index is 0.8% above its Mar 2022 peak. After Jun 2008 both halves reversed (-24.4% by Mar 2009), and the 2008 level was not regained until Jul 2020.
  - **Better wording (note form):** the two Taiwan risks have moved the same way more often than chance since 1991; the worst two years for a US-dollar building budget were 2006-08, led by building costs; 2020-22 is the one time both moved hard together.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** 10% is a currency test, not a joint test.
  - **Where 10% sits.** Joint moves of 10% or less make up 69.4% of 24-month windows since 1991 and 74.5% of 18-month windows (63.3% and 70.6% since 2000). As a joint stress 10% is about the 70th percentile: roughly 3 windows in 10 were worse.
  - **As a currency-only test it is about right.** The currency half alone has a 1-in-20 24-month move of +11.0% on monthly averages since 1991 (Q10b's daily figure since 2001: +11.9%). That matches 8j and Table 3 row 7, which test the currency only.
  - **Even net of expected cost growth.** Taking out 3% a year of building-cost trend (the register's central rate), the 1-in-20 24-month joint move is still +20.6%, and 10% is passed in 18.5% of windows.
  - **Sized against the register (Keel arithmetic on register figures):** 10% = $11,500 on $115,000 (8j). The 1-in-20 two-year joint move (+28.0%) = $32,191 on $115,000 ($26,593 on $95,000), $2,368 beyond her central flexibility. The 1-in-20 18-month move (+24.6%) = $28,276, inside it by $1,547. The 2019-21 window (+29.0%) = $33,380, $3,557 beyond. The worst (+38.9%) = $44,756, $14,933 beyond.
  - **How often history passed her pots on $115,000 (24 months since 1991):** her central flexibility (25.9%) in 8.6% of windows; the central half kept alone (18.6%) in 16.5%; the house 1-in-20 low flexibility (14.1%) in 23.0%; the house 1-in-20 low half kept (8.6%) in 35.6%. Over 18 months: 3.9% / 12.7% / 17.0% / 29.9%.
  - **On Laura's own calendar** (January meetings to January payment, 34 non-overlapping starts 1991-2024): above 10% in 11, above 15% in 8, above 20% in 4 (1991, 2003, 2006, 2020), above 25.9% in 2 (1991 +31.0%, 2003 +28.0%); median +2.2%.
  - **Convention:** every figure is "% more US dollars for the same NT$ budget", 8j's convention. The stricter reading 8j shows beside it (NT$ per US$ down 10%, 11.1% more) applies to the currency alone and has no joint equivalent.
  - **The note a judge could use:** the 10% row tests one of the three uses; tested together, the two Taiwan risks have a 1-in-20 two-year move of about 28%, about her whole flexibility.
- **Claim 3: KILL. CHANGE to "they came in share booms; the squeeze is a Taiwan-dollar rebound after a crash".**
  - **Shares rose with the joint moves.** Over 24-month windows since 2001, the joint move and world shares (MSCI ACWI, USD, gross) correlate +0.42 (+0.45 on the price-spliced series since 1997; +0.44 over 18 months). The currency half drives it (+0.47); building costs +0.22.
  - **In the windows that mattered, shares were up.** Of the 50 24-month windows since 2001 with a joint move above 20%, world shares fell in 2 (4%); the median share return in those 50 was +40.1%. The worst episodes' share returns over the same months: 2006-08 +11.0%, 2002-04 +55.8%, 2019-21 +51.3%, 2009-11 +79.2%.
  - **When shares fell, the joint move was small.** In the 31 24-month windows since 2001 with world shares down more than 10%, the median joint move was +1.9% (largest +20.5%).
  - **The only overlap:** windows from autumn 2006 to autumn 2008 (joint +26.9% with shares -8.4%; +20.5% with -29.2%), and the 18-month windows starting Jan to Mar 2007 (+37.6% with -3.1%; +22.9% with -18.2%).
  - **The squeeze that does happen is later.** After a crash the Taiwan dollar rebounded while the five-year share return was still low: Mar 2009 to Mar 2011, joint +24.6% (currency +16.3%) while world shares over the five years to Mar 2011 were up only 18.6%.
  - **A rough replay** (Keel arithmetic on register figures: the half kept scaled by world shares' five-year return to the window's end against 7.5% a year; the excess held at $8,476; 250 two-year windows ending Dec 2005 to Sep 2026): the half kept alone falls short in 37 windows (14.8%; median gap $4,700, largest $17,839); with the excess, in 9 (3.6%; largest $9,363, the window starting Jul 2006). The nine start Mar to Sep 2006 (the cost burst) or Mar to Aug 2009 (the rebound). Over 18 months: 16 (6.4%) and 3 (1.2%; largest $7,862). The proxy's half kept (median $23,062, 1-in-20 low $14,246) sits close to 8j's joint replay ($23,601; $13,874).
  - **Correction to NUMBERS 8j and 8f (record only):** 8j's "+0.09, close to none" for the currency move against shares averages two opposite periods. On US stocks, 24-month windows ending by Dec 2000 give -0.39 (183 windows) and windows since 2001 give +0.32; on world shares since 2001, +0.42. The whole span since 1983 gives +0.11, which matches 8j. Since 2001 a stronger Taiwan dollar has come with rising world shares.
  - **The note a judge could use:** the half kept tends to be large in the years Taiwan gets dearer in dollars, because the same weak-dollar, strong-Asia years lift world shares; the exception is a Taiwan-dollar rebound in the two years after a crash.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE.** Public-money building contracts pass index rises above 2.5% to the owner by default. Private fixed-price contracts do not, though courts have shifted part of an abnormal surge.
  - **Public works (beyond Q10c row 16).** The PCC's model construction contract (工程採購契約範本, art. 5(1)6) adjusts by DGBAS's index by default, in three levels: named items above 10%, sub-groups above 5%, the total index above 2.5%. Only the excess is adjusted, monthly after each valuation, both up and down. Fees, design, management, insurance and profit are not adjusted; imported items outside the index are not adjusted unless stated.
  - **The PCC's letters.** Works of a year or more must carry an index clause (2001). All works should, and "本工程無物價指數調整規定" (no price adjustment) clauses should not be used (2006). The thresholds went to 0% in Apr 2008 and back to 2.5% / 5% / 10% in Apr 2009. A bidder's declaration waiving adjustment was allowed from 2009 and stopped on 30 Dec 2021 (quoted in a 2025 judgment, row 29).
  - **Contracts without a clause.** The PCC's letter of 31 Aug 2020 lets the parties amend a contract by agreement with reference to the Civil Code's change-of-circumstances rule (art. 227-2), for work not yet done.
  - **In court (judgments read in part):** Tainan District Court 2025: the index rose 13.18% from tender to completion on a contract that excluded adjustment; the court split the adjustment 50/50 (NT$12.30m of NT$24.61m). Taichung District Court 2026: a 19.96% rise over 2020-22; NT$24.56m of NT$26.13m awarded. Chiayi District Court 2022: a private subcontract with a "no adjustment" clause; dismissed because 2017-2020 rises of about 2.4% a year were ordinary.
  - **Which regime a residency would meet (code facts, no legal advice).** The Government Procurement Act (art. 4) applies to a private body's procurement when government subsidy is half or more of it and at least the announced threshold. Arts procurement is exempt (art. 4(2)), but the Ministry of Culture defines arts procurement as services and goods (勞務、財物), not works. So building works for an arts residency with half or more public money would fall under the Act and its model contract (Keel inference). Otherwise the contract decides.
  - **Why it matters for the joint window:** the currency half stops when the dollars are converted, but under a PCC-style clause the building-cost half keeps running through the build. Adding 12 months of cost after a 24-month joint window gives a 1-in-20 move of +31.8% (largest +39.9%, from Jun 2003).
- **Clash with the FINAL IPS or the TNA: none.** IPS l.15 names "building costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar or a co-sponsor falling short"; this card sizes two of the three together and does not change the plan. **Risks to record (no change):** Table 3 row 7 tests the currency (10%) and the building-cost trend (1.5%-5.0%) apart, while the joint 1-in-20 two-year move (about 28%) is about her whole central flexibility (25.9%); NUMBERS 8j l.1018 and 8f l.724 call the currency move unlinked to shares (+0.09), which hides a +0.42 link with world shares since 2001.

## (b) Cards

### Card 1. One series for two risks (rows 1-6, 11-13)
- **What is it?** The US-dollar cost of a fixed Taiwan building budget, monthly since 1991: DGBAS's construction cost index divided by NT$ per US$. Its two-year change is what Laura's US-dollar contribution must absorb between the 2031 meetings and the December 2032 payment if the facility's NT$ needs move with Taiwan's building prices and its currency.
- **Did it work?** It shows what the two tests apart cannot.
  - The two halves move the same way more often than chance (+0.27 over 24 months).
  - The 1-in-20 two-year move is +28.0%, against +24.8% if they were independent.
  - The worst was +38.9% (Jul 2006 to Jul 2008).
  - The tail is stable across start years: 1 in 20 at +28.0% since 1991, +28.1% since 2000, +27.1% since 2016.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** Her contribution is fixed in US dollars at the meetings and spent in NT$ on a building. IPS l.15 names "building costs that overrun" and "a stronger Taiwan dollar" as two of the three uses of her flexibility; this series is those two together.
- **How is it not?**
  - The national index is not her building's cost (Q10c claim 3: site and age matter more in one job).
  - Monthly averages miss daily extremes.
  - About 16-17 independent two-year spans exist since 1991.
  - Co-sponsors may budget expected cost growth in 2031; net of 3% a year the 1-in-20 move is still +20.6%.
- **What Caplet takes.**
  - 3e area 4: one sentence that the two Taiwan risks were tested together on history as well as apart, with the 1-in-20 two-year figure (about 28%, about her whole flexibility). The figure needs a NUMBERS entry first: the lead decides.
  - An exhibit option: the joint index, 1991-2026, with the 2006-08 and 2019-22 rises marked.

### Card 2. 2006-08, not 2021-22, was the worst; 2021-22 was the only time both moved hard (rows 2, 7-10, 13-14)
- **What is it?**
  - **2006-08 (cost-led):** building costs +29.5% in two years, while the Taiwan dollar strengthened 7.2%.
  - **2019-22 (both at once):** costs +14.5% and the currency +12.7% over Aug 2019 to Aug 2021; the joint index rose 32.3% from Aug 2019 to Mar 2022. The Taiwan dollar's Jan 2022 monthly average (27.672) was its strongest since Apr 1997.
- **Did it last?**
  - **2008:** both halves reversed (-24.4% by Mar 2009), and the 2008 level was not regained until Jul 2020.
  - **2022:** the currency half reversed by Oct 2022 (NT$31.959) while costs held. Sep 2026 sits 0.8% above the Mar 2022 peak.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** A burst that peaks around December 2032 hits her contribution at full size; the building part of it tends to stay in the price level (Q10c row 3).
- **How is it not?** Each episode had its own cause: a world metal spike (2008), a Taiwan gravel crackdown (1992), COVID and materials, then wages (2021-22, Q10c row 9). None repeats on schedule.
- **What Caplet takes.**
  - The store's hole is closed with the correct episode: "2021-22 overlap" becomes "2006-08 was the worst; 2020-22 the one time both moved hard".

### Card 5. Who pays a cost rise during the build (rows 26-32)
- **What is it?**
  - **Public-money contracts:** Taiwan's public works use the PCC's model contract. It adjusts the price monthly by DGBAS's index above 2.5% (sub-groups 5%, named items 10%), both ways, and the PCC tells agencies not to write "no adjustment" clauses.
  - **Contracts without a clause:** the 2020 letter and the courts (art. 227-2) let part of an abnormal surge reach the owner; Tainan split one 50/50 in 2025.
  - **Private contracts:** they follow their own terms.
  - **A subsidised residency:** the Procurement Act reaches a private body's procurement when government money is half or more of it. The arts exemption covers services and goods, not works.
- **Did it work?** For contractors, yes: payments under the clauses rose from NT$147.7m (2020) to NT$1,513m (2022) (Q10c row 16). For owners it means the budget moves during the build.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** Her dollars are converted around December 2032. The currency half of the joint risk stops there; the building half keeps running through the build if the contract has an index clause. A 24-month joint window plus a 12-month build gives a 1-in-20 move of +31.8%.
- **How is it not?**
  - Laura gives to a facility; she is not the contracting owner.
  - Whether the residency's works fall under the Act is a legal question (out of scope by the case).
  - A private fixed-price contract can move ordinary rises to the builder.
- **What Caplet takes.**
  - 3e, at most a clause: building costs during the build reach the owner in Taiwan's public-money contracts, so the building risk does not end when the dollars are converted.
  - Code facts only, no legal advice; for the store.

### Card 6. The field: one cash flow, two indices, a padded budget rate (rows 33-36)
- **What is it?**
  - **Cost engineers (AACE)** model escalation and currency on the same time-phased cash flow with the same probabilistic methods. They note both are "driven by economic conditions" and that capex prices are "sticky".
  - **Corporate treasuries** set a budget rate. Unhedged, a spot budget rate is met about half the time, so firms pad it to their "maximum amount of downside tolerance".
  - **Owners** estimate escalation at P50 and P90.
- **Did it work?** It is standard practice, and it is why the joint series, not two separate rows, is the field's answer to this problem class.
- **How is Laura's case like it?** Her US-dollar figure is a budget at spot; her flexibility is the padding; the joint 1-in-20 move is her P95.
- **How is it not?** Firms hedge the currency with forwards; Laura's plan holds only Treasuries and VT, and derivatives are not permitted. Her padding is market-valued (the half kept), not a fixed sum.
- **What Caplet takes.** 3e's framing for assumption area 4 (note form): the contribution is a budget set at today's exchange rate and today's building prices; the room for both moving against it is her flexibility, which has been enough in most two-year spans since 1991 and not in the worst.

## (c) What would surprise a judge

- The worst two years for a US-dollar building budget in Taiwan since 1991 were 2006-08 (+38.9%), and building costs, not the Taiwan dollar, did most of it.
- In the years Taiwan got dearer in dollars, world shares were usually booming: of 50 two-year windows since 2001 with a joint move above 20%, shares fell in 2. The danger is the Taiwan dollar's rebound after a crash (2009-11), not the crash itself.
- The register's "+0.09, unlinked" is two opposite periods averaged: -0.39 before 2001 and +0.42 with world shares since.
- 10% is the right size for the currency alone and about the 70th percentile for the two together. The joint 1-in-20 two-year move (about 28%) is about her whole flexibility ($29,800).
- In Taiwan's public-money building contracts the owner, not the builder, carries index rises above 2.5% during the build, and the arts exemption from procurement law does not cover building works.

## (d) Gaps

- **No building index before 1991.** DGBAS's linked table (ccispl.xls) starts in Jan 1991, so the 1986-88 revaluation (+39.7% currency-only in two years, Q10b row 4) has no joint figure. An older DGBAS or Ministry of the Interior series may exist in printed statistical yearbooks. No Ministry of the Interior index was found or used in this run.
- **The building sub-index was not run.** The building-construction sub-index (建築工程類) as a long series was not read; Q10c row 8 shows it rose more gently than the total since 2021, so a joint series on it would probably give smaller moves. It is probably in DGBAS's database (nstatdb) or the CCI platform the repo's `A2_taiwan_cci.py` queries.
- **The digest's CBC series was not used.** The digest asked for the central bank's monthly NT$; FRED's G.5 monthly averages were used instead (Q10 row 24: the two differ slightly).
- **ACWI stood in for VT.** VT's own history from 2008 and FTSE Global All Cap levels were not read.
- **Unchecked legal details.** The current value of the Procurement Act's announced threshold (NT$1m in the 2020 Ministry of Culture-PCC handbook) was not checked. The PCC's 30 Dec 2021 letter ending bidder waivers was read only as quoted in the Tainan judgment.
- **Private arts-building contracts.** No survey of the clauses Taiwan's private arts buildings actually use was found. Trade bodies (the general contractors' association) or the Ministry of Culture's facility-grant rules may hold it.
- **No peer-reviewed paper.** None on joint currency-and-escalation risk in overseas capital budgets was read (the Consensus allowance is used up until 1 Dec). AACE RP 68R-11's full text is member-only.
- **City indices were not built.** Joint series on Taipei's and Kaohsiung's own indices (the model contract's two alternatives) were not made; Q10c row 15 shows city indices can sit five points apart.
- **The replay is a proxy.** Row 24 is a rough replay. The numbers owner could add a joint row to `numbers/joint_stress.py` that moves the building index and the currency on the same months as VT.
