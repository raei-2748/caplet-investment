- **Problem class:** residual risk in a design that passed its checks: changes made after the check, cases outside the check's reference set, and inputs not yet in hand. **Field that solved it first:** not structural engineering, whose Citicorp watch was improvised after the flaw was found. ## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** Watching catches what it is aimed at. At Citicorp no watch found the flaw. LeMessurier found it by re-running his own design for a Harvard lecture, "in the spirit of intellectual play", after a chance phone call (row 1). The named watchers and signals came after, and they watched a known flaw for one hurricane season (row 9). What a watch after design can catch is narrower and still important: (a) **changes made after the check**, the one event in the Citicorp story nobody disputes took place (bolts for welds, checked only against the loads of the design they replaced, rows 3-4; whether it mattered is disputed, rows 18-19); and (b) **inputs not yet in hand** (for Laura, the 2028 deposit). Cases nobody imagined are not caught by watching. They are kept away by where the promised money sits: Laura's payments rest on what Treasuries pay, tested with cash waiting earning nothing (row 31). **Changed claim:** "A plan certain at design still needs a named reader and a dated signal for each thing its check left out by name, changes made after the design and inputs not yet held, and each row must say what it may change and what it never changes."
- **Claim 2: CHANGE; the famous half is contested.**
  - *The quartering-wind case.* The code then asked only for perpendicular winds, "the only calculation required by New York City's building code" (Morgenstern). But the code had narrowed only in late 1968: before that it asked for wind "from any direction" (Kremer). LeMessurier's own managing principal says LeMessurier studied quartering winds and "concluded that the quartering wind did not govern" (McNamara, via Kremer). Modern analysis finds corner winds "less demanding than face winds, both globally ... and locally". Corner-wind overturning moments come out about 20% and 50% lower, and member demands 20-30% lower (Park et al. 2019). So the case "missed" may not have governed (row 18). Duthinh (2020) traces the 1978 alarm to a hand method that added two faces' loads "as if these wind loads were static and perfectly correlated, which they are not" (row 19). These modern results have caveats: public tunnel data for an isolated building, no neighbours, no crown.
  - *The bolted joints.* The change was real and was checked. The fabricator designed the bolts "Employing the loads at each joint calculated by LeMessurier's firm", and they "were entirely adequate for the designated loads" (Kremer). The New York team also classed the braces "not as columns but as trusses, which are exempt from the safety factor" (Morgenstern). So the check passed a change against the old case, under a name that removed a margin (rows 4-5).
  - *Who caught it.* A re-check run for another reason, after a call from a student who "knew nothing about quartering wind loads or the steel frame being bolted" (DeCarolis, row 15). Diane Hartley's sharper question, the quartering-wind one, stopped with a junior engineer (row 14). It was not a watch.
- **Claim 3: KEEP.** Within hours there were generators for the damper, beepers, two vice-presidents managing the repair, and the damper maker's technician on call round the clock. Strain gauges were wired to Robertson's office "like a patient in intensive care", and "two independent weather forecasters" sent forecasts "four times a day". An evacuation plan was ready: police and the mayor's Office of Emergency Management would act on a wind alert, with 1,200-2,000 Red Cross workers. DeFord was "the bank's point man". Each signal had a reader, a frequency and an owner of the action. The watch also had an end: "The weather watch ended on September 13th" (rows 9-10).
- **Claim 4: KEEP, with three points for 3e's notes (no new row or rule, CANON A34).**
  - Table 3's look-closer rows are the plan's list of what its model leaves out. D2 says "the model contains no fund events", and row 4 watches exactly those (row 32).
  - The "Never" cells cap what a false alarm can cost, so the watch can be sensitive. If Citicorp's alarm was a false one, it cost $4.3m-$8m against a $1m first estimate (row 20).
  - Every row ends on a date, so the list shortens as 2033 nears (row 10).
- **Judge's note (aha):** the Citicorp story most engineering courses teach may be wrong in its central fact. Modern analysis finds that the corner winds the code ignored did not govern. What was certainly real was a change made after the design and checked against the old case. A change after design is exactly the event Laura's Table 3 watches all year, in fund notices, and her model says outright that it contains no fund events.
- **Never in the report:** Citicorp as a parallel in 3e or anywhere (row 34); "a student's question saved the building" (row 15); "the check missed a fatal case" as settled (row 18); LeMessurier as a model of disclosure without the secrecy record (row 24); any new trigger or reason to change (CANON A34).

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 32 (3e, Table 3):** the watch is aimed where the model is silent: D2's "the model contains no fund events" and Table 3 row 4. One clause answers the judge's "what if your model is wrong?" with the plan's own list.
2. **Row 4 with row 28 (Table 3 row 4's notes):** the one certain Citicorp failure was a change checked against the old case. The plan re-tests every swap against its hardest standard, cash waiting earning nothing. This is management of change in process safety's own terms, and a fund for a bond is not a "replacement in kind" (Trading Note 2).
3. **Row 20 with row 19 (Table 3's "Never" cells):** a re-check can raise a false alarm, so a watch needs a cap on what an alarm can cost. "Never: a sale of shares" and "Never: a forced sale" are that cap. This is the aha for a judge who knows the revisionist Citicorp literature.
4. **Row 5 (3c, 3e wording):** a name removed a safety factor ("trusses, which are exempt from the safety factor"). Calling a fund share a bond would drop the 0.5% margin, which is the reason behind D2's way of saying a fund share.
5. **Rows 9-10 (Table 3's Who column and each row's end):** each signal had a named reader, a frequency, an owner of the action, and an end date ("The weather watch ended on September 13th"). Table 3 already has all four, so 3e's notes can say the table shortens as 2033 nears.
