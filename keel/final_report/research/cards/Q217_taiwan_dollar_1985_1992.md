## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP the job; state the limit.**
  - IPS l.15 names "a stronger Taiwan dollar" as one of three uses of her flexibility, and nothing at source contradicts that.
  - The largest rise on record would have outrun it. From Jan 1986 to Jan 1988 a fixed NT$ bill came to need 38.3% more US dollars from the currency alone (Fed monthly averages). On her $115,000 that is $44,045, against 8q's central $28,682: $15,363 short. The half kept alone ($21,179) would be $22,866 short. All Keel arithmetic, never plan figures.
  - Who covers it, per Table 3 ("Never: the range"): the ten payments and the stated range do not move. A move beyond her flexibility falls on the building's NT$ budget, which shrinks or finds other money.
  - **Judge's note:** an international-finance judge who knows the Plaza years will ask whether the currency history starts after the biggest move. If the report names it first, with a figure and who covers it, it reads as a limit stated, not a hole found.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE. It is the history since 1991, not the history.**
  - The 30 windows are right on their own definition. Their start months run Jan 1991 to Oct 2024 because Taiwan's national building-cost index begins in 1991 (DGBAS).
  - The same Fed series holds a larger episode: Aug 1985 to Jun 1992, monthly averages 40.50 to 24.77 NT$ per US$, 63.5% more US dollars (Keel arithmetic; the CBC's own monthly averages give 63.3%).
  - Its largest two-year move, Jan 1986 to Jan 1988, +38.3% from the currency alone, is above 29.0% before any building cost is added. It is 2.3 times the largest currency-only move since 1991 (Feb 2009 to Feb 2011, +16.9%).
  - On Taipei City's building-cost index, which runs from 1981 (not 8q's national series), the joint rise reached +48.1% (Jan 1986 to Jan 1988) and +50.4% at its highest (May 1987 to May 1989). Keel arithmetic, labelled as a city index.
  - Every use of "30 of 406" or "9.3% to 29.0%" must say "since 1991". Table 3's note already does.
  - **Judge's note:** "since 1991" without a reason reads like a chosen start. The reason is the data: the national index starts then. Say so in the note.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE the wording in the register (a flag for the numbers owner; this card edits nothing else).**
  - 8j's +29.4% (Nov 1986 to Nov 1988; Keel re-derives 29.35%) is the largest on 8j's own windows, whose moves start in Oct 1986 or later. It is the tail of the 1985-92 rise.
  - The series' own largest two-year move is +38.3% on monthly averages (Jan 1986 to Jan 1988), or +39.7% on daily rates (Q10b, Dec 1985 to Dec 1987).
  - 8j's "the 1986-1988 appreciation" should read "the later part of the 1985-1992 appreciation, from Nov 1986".
  - The mirror itself is complete: 123 of 123 months from Oct 1983 to Dec 1993 match FRED EXTAUS (Keel check, 0 mismatches).
- **Claim 4: KEEP as a trigger, never as a size.**
  - Measured from the Aug 1985 level, a 10% watch would have fired in Oct 1986 (36.65, +10.5%), 14 months in.
  - Two years after the start (Aug 1987) the move was 33.7%, and three years after (Aug 1988) 41.2% (Keel arithmetic).
  - In the largest episode on record, the watch fires early and most of the move comes after it. That matches Table 3's note, where "history puts the cost near what her flexibility covers, not near $11,500" since 1991. In 1986-88 the cost went well past her flexibility.
- **Claim 5: CHANGE the cause; recurrence partly.**
  - The currency lever was the US Treasury, under Section 3004 of the Omnibus Trade and Competitiveness Act of 1988. Taiwan was found to have "manipulated" in Oct 1988, was named again in Apr 1989, cleared in fall 1989, and named again in May and Dec 1992.
  - Section 301 cases in 1986 were about market access (customs valuation; tobacco, beer, wine). Never "Section 301 forced the rise". The pressure began after the Plaza statement of Sept 1985 (MAC) and sharpened from Aug 1986 (Emery, Fed IFDP 313).
  - What recurs today:
    - A surplus about a fifth of output: 19.6% of GDP in 2025 (Q10b), and Q1-Q3 2026 at US$188.92bn, about two-thirds above the same months of 2025 (Keel arithmetic from the CBC releases).
    - Taiwan on Treasury's monitoring list in Jun 2025, Jan 2026 and Jul 2026, with new language on smoothing "to resist depreciation pressure in the same manner as ... appreciation pressure".
  - What does not recur:
    - The rate floats; the daily limit ended in Apr 1989 and capital controls were relaxed from 1987.
    - The surplus now leaves through private investors: in 2026 the financial account rose US$178.70bn while the CBC's reserve assets fell US$0.77bn.
    - The NT$ weakened in Nov 2026 (32.947 on 18 Nov; 33.04 intraday on 20 Nov).
  - Not a forecast either way. VT is never a Taiwan-dollar hedge.
