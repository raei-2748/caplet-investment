- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**

  2. "In Taiwan the words that separate a promise from an estimate are fixed in practice and in law."

- **Problem class:** carrying a stated certainty across a language line without it growing or shrinking. **Field that solved it first:** risk communication for international assessments. The IPCC's translation studies (Harris, Corner, Xu & Du, 2013; Budescu et al., 2014, card Q4c) found that numbers cross languages and words do not. Statutory drafting had solved the threshold half long before: Taiwan's Criminal Code fixed by statute that 以上 and 以下 include the number, so a threshold means the same to every reader. Taiwan's weather service solved the public half by saying probabilities as "60 of 100 similar cases".

## Verdicts in one screen

1. **Claim 1: KEEP, sharpened.** The shift is large, and it runs one way: toward 50%, for every word except the plain word for "certain".
   - Harris et al. (2013), Chinese medians:
     - "virtually certain" (几乎确定): 86.5% (students) and 80% (public), against the IPCC's 99-100%;
     - "very likely" (很可能): 70% in both samples, against 90-100%;
     - "likely" (可能): 50% in both, against 66-100%.
     - None of the Chinese participants read all seven terms inside the IPCC's ranges.
   - The IPCC's own Chinese writes "unlikely" as 不可能, the everyday word for "impossible". In Study 1, 51% of Chinese participants read it as zero. AR6 (2021) still prints it.
   - Sui et al. (2025), mainland lexicon:
     - 可能: mean 52%;
     - 很可能: 80%;
     - 一定 (certainly): mean 98%, median 100%, and an interquartile range of 0, so everyone heard the same number.
   - Taiwan's own translation of the AR6 summary has no word list, and it renders "very likely" two ways.
   - What survives translation: dollar figures, and the plain word for certain. What does not: "likely", "very likely", "almost/virtually certain", "unlikely", and "about" when it is rendered 大概 (a "probably" word in Taiwanese Mandarin research).
   - **Judge's note:** in the IPCC's official Chinese, "unlikely" is printed as 不可能, "impossible". Half of the Chinese readers in one study read it as zero. A hedge word can flip in translation; a number cannot.
2. **Claim 2: CHANGE.** Only a handful of the words are fixed.
   - **Fixed by law or official rule:**
     - 以上, 以下 and 以內 include the number (Criminal Code art. 10; the Executive Yuan says other laws follow this by custom). 未滿 and 未達 exclude it.
     - 保證 is suretyship, one party answering for another's debt (Civil Code art. 739). In fund law, a fund may use 保證 only if a rated institution guarantees it. A fund protected only by its own holdings may not use 「保證、安全、無風險」 (guaranteed, safe, risk-free), and its cover must say 「因保護並非保證」 (because protection is not a guarantee).
     - 承諾 in the Civil Code is the acceptance of an offer (arts. 154, 158), not a one-sided pledge.
     - 核定 is the approval without which a matter has no legal effect.
   - **Not fixed by any statute found:** 預計, 預估, 約, 至少. In grant rules, 預估 is a figure the funder re-prices when the actual cost comes in.
   - In practice, Taiwan's arts money draws the line between promise and estimate with 核定 (approved) against 申請 (asked):
     - NCAF paid approved regular grants in full in 94% of cases (2025);
     - it gave funded applicants 49.3% of what they asked.
3. **Claim 3: KILL for the top; CHANGE for the bottom.**
   - **The top.** Every Ministry of Culture grant rule read uses 上限 or 最高 for a funder's cap (NT$1m to NT$5m). Caps are rarely reached: the 2026 art-village grants were NT$0.7m-1.8m against a NT$3m cap (card Q10d row 9), and NCAF met about half of what funded applicants asked. So 上限 is heard as "at most, and usually less". That is the reverse of NUMBERS 4, where the contribution equals the stated top in every path.
   - **"At least" (以上) on either end.** 以上 includes the number:
     - on the top, it becomes the "at least $115,000" frame that card Q52 struck in English;
     - on the bottom, 至少 or 以上 is true but puts the co-sponsors' count on the bottom. That runs against the funder test, where co-sponsors count the top and 3d says the bottom once, as the part held since January 2028.
   - **What survives:** the two dollar figures, named as US dollars, and the plain certainty word with the stated range as its subject after the move (NUMBERS 4).
   - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan's arts world, 上限 is the figure a funder will not exceed, and grantees get about half of what they ask. Laura's top is the one figure in the room that is paid as stated.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 2 (3c, 3e):** the IPCC's official Chinese prints "unlikely" as "impossible", and half of the readers in one study heard zero. It is the cleanest aha for why 3c gives a dollar range with no likelihood word.
2. **Row 9 (3c):** "almost certain" is heard at 80-86.5% in Chinese and plain 一定 at 100% with no spread. It backs NUMBERS 4's plain "certain", with the stated range as its subject, as the one confidence word that survives translation.
3. **Row 32 (3d):** 上限 is the funder's cap in Taiwan and is usually not reached. It kills the idea of stating Laura's top as a cap, which co-sponsors would hear as "less".
4. **Row 26 (3d, 3c):** Taiwan's fund law reserves "guaranteed, safe, risk-free" for funds with a rated guarantor. It is the Taiwanese reason to keep the certainty word on the Treasuries and the stated range, never on the fund.
5. **Row 34 with row 33 (3d):** Taiwanese arts applicants get about half of what they ask, and even approved grants are paid in full about 19 times in 20. Laura's top is the figure that is paid as stated, client knowledge a Taiwanese co-sponsor feels at once.

## Gaps

- **No Taiwanese numeric norms for probability words.** No study of Taiwanese readers (traditional characters) was found. Harris et al. (2013) and Sui et al. (2025) used mainland samples, and 陳韻竹 (2010) gives ranks without numbers. Only the thesis abstract was read; the full thesis was not.
- **No Mandarin listener test of the words this card discusses** (至少, 以上, 最高, 上限, 一定, 確定, 保證) when used for a dollar range. Rows 23 and 32 rest on sourced meanings plus Keel's reading.
- **至少 has no legal definition in anything read.** The Executive Yuan's list covers 以上, 以下, 以內, 未滿 and 未達, as read. Whether any statute or ruling defines 至少 was not checked.
- **Not read:**
  - 確定 in civil procedure (判決確定, a judgment that has become final);
  - Taiwan's statistics terms for confidence interval and level (信賴區間, 信賴水準) in the NAER term database;
  - Hong Kong Observatory's probability-of-rain words.
- **No Taiwanese pledge form with a range or a minimum.** None was found; card Q83 holds the forms read.
- **The Executive Yuan's own 2025 manual** (ey.gov.tw File/9AAF90C72F253EA) was cut off before the 以上 entry, so the NAER-hosted copy was used. The Ministry of Justice letter 法律字第10303514570號 (19 Dec 2014) was read only as quoted on a secondary site.
- **Sources read in part:**
  - Harris, Kau & Liefgreen (2024): passages through a paper index, not the full PDF;
  - Lau & Ranyard (1999), Xu, Ye & Li (2009) and Hashim (2024): abstracts only;
  - NCAF's 2023 如數撥款 figure (92.4%): a search excerpt of the 2026 budget only.
- **One source read with no row:** Rakotondravony, Ding & Harrison (2022, arXiv 2207.09608), in excerpt. A Mandarin participant called 貌似可信 (a dictionary rendering of "plausible") unnatural.
- **Unknown:** whether Taiwanese co-sponsors (foundations, companies) would read Laura's English directly or through a translator. No source was found.
