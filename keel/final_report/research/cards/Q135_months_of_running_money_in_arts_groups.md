- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**

- **Problem class:** liquidity adequacy of a nonprofit's operating budget, measured as months of spending a group could pay from money it can reach. **Field that solved it first:** public budgeting's "rainy day" (budget stabilisation) funds for states and cities, which Calabrese (2018) names as the model; nonprofit finance took it up through financial-vulnerability work (Tuckman and Chang, 1991, as cited by Irvin and Furneaux, 2022) and the Urban Institute's Nonprofit Operating Reserves Initiative definition (2008).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** It held for US arts groups before the pandemic and is wrong about "small".
  - **Before 2020, more than half.** NFF's arts respondents were 60% at three months or less of cash in 2013 and 61% in 2014 (16% under one month, 21% at six months or more). SMU DataArts counted 56% under three months of working capital in 2019.
  - **Since the pandemic, about four in ten.** SMU's share under three months was 56%, 45%, 26%, 30%, 37% and 41% for 2019-2024; its own page says "42% in 2024".
  - **Not "small".** Small groups (under $250K) held the most months: SMU's median was 4.21 months in 2019 and 7.31 in 2024, against 1.84 and 3.09 for groups over $1M.
  - **On the stricter measure, about none.** SeaChange's FY2014 Form 990 data put the median US arts group at 0.1 months of operating reserve, with four in ten at zero or below. Calabrese (2013) found the median nonprofit at about one month and "nearly 40 percent" at none.
  - **Say:** "About four in ten US arts groups held under three months of working capital in 2024, and more than half did in 2019."
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** There is no single guidance. The norms run from about one to six months, and some funders set a ceiling as well as a floor.
  - "No accepted 'benchmark' exists internationally for reserves levels for arts organisations" (Positive Solutions & GHK, 2012, for Hong Kong's government).
  - **United States.** 3-6 months is the "common rule of thumb" (ARTS blog, 2014); NCCS suggests 0.25 of a year (Calabrese, 2013). Calabrese (2018) finds even "up to 6 months ... is inadequate" to keep all spending on trend through a bad stretch.
  - **United Kingdom.** The charity "rule of thumb" is 26 weeks (Cause4), and Artquest uses 6 months.
  - **Australia.** The major companies must keep reserves of "at least 20 per cent of annual turnover", about 2.4 months, as a funding condition (AMPAG, 2012).
  - **Hong Kong.** The nine major groups may keep "not more than 25% of the total expenditure", about 3 months; anything over is returned or netted off next year's grant.
  - **Taiwan.** An accountant at NCAF's 2025 Taiwan Top workshop advised 10-15% of the total budget, about 1.2-1.8 months.
  - **The ceiling.** BBB's Standard 10 fails a charity whose available unrestricted net assets exceed three years of expenses.
  - (Month figures for Australia, Hong Kong and Taiwan are Keel arithmetic.)
- **Claim 3: CHANGE the wording; the point survives.** The 120 months are months of her own $50,000 a year, not of the residency's budget, which the case does not give.
  - **Owned, then paid down.** Most of each payment is owned from January 2027 and the rest from January 2028 (NUMBERS 8h). On 1 Jan 2033 ten payments are owned, and one fewer each January after.
  - **What it is worth.** NUMBERS 8h puts the reserve at $445,655 on 1 Jan 2033, 111.7% of the $399,089 the ten need at today's forward rates, with $500,000 still owed at face.
  - **In whole-budget months.** The 120 months equal 120 × s months of the whole budget, where s is her share of running costs (Keel arithmetic):
    - above the median arts group's months of cash (3.4) at s above about 3%;
    - above the 90th percentile of arts operating reserves (27.4 months, SeaChange 2014) at s above about 23%;
    - above the 90th percentile of cash and investments (52.2 months) at s above about 44%.
  - **The true and stronger form.** No arts group in these data holds ten years of dated, owned operating money for anything, and funders' own rules would mark a charity that did as over-reserved.
  - **Say:** "Ten years of the founder's own operating payments are owned five years before the first is due; in 2024 only about one US arts group in six held more than a year of its whole budget (SMU, 17%)."
- **Claim 4: KILL.** Reserves are penalised at both ends.
  - Too little: the Charity Commission warns of "a refusal to fund on the basis that the charity's finances are unstable" (as quoted by Irvin and Furneaux, 2022).
  - Too much: BBB's three-year cap, and its view that donors "expect the money they contribute will be used for current needs". Hong Kong's clawback above 25%. Funders cut support above about five times the annual budget (Calabrese, 2011, as cited by Irvin and Furneaux, 2022).
- **Claim 5: CHANGE.** Taiwan's groups are thinner than America's.
  - NCAF's 2009 study: the surveyed groups' 2005 deficit was NT$333,925,540; groups "cannot keep much cash on hand" and "use one show to feed the next" (團隊無法常備大量的現金，團隊依然以戲養戲).
  - Small groups took 52-60% of their income from public money in 2004-2008. NCAF's 2020 study: "團隊虧損是常態" (deficits are the norm).
- **Judge's note (aha 1):** a residency that held ten years of its running money in its own bank would fail America's charity watchdog test (three years) and Hong Kong's clawback rule (three months). Laura's plan holds the ten years for her and pays one January at a time. So the residency is never over-reserved, and never short.
- **Judge's note (aha 2):** "appropriate" is not one number. The field's own norms run from 10% of a year in Taiwan to six months in Britain, and the median US arts group held about 0.1 months of operating reserve.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 22 with rows 23 and 16 (3d, 3a): funders cap reserves.** BBB fails a charity holding more than three years. Hong Kong claws back anything above 25% of a year. Funders pulled support above five budgets. This is the aha: ten years in the residency's own bank would look like hoarding, so the plan holds the ten Treasuries for Laura and pays one January at a time.
2. **Row 8 with row 31 (3a): the yardstick, honestly.** The median US arts group held about 0.1 months of operating reserve (FY2014), and the 90th percentile 27.4. Laura's 120 months of her own share pass that 90th percentile once her share of running costs is above about 23% (Keel arithmetic). The report states the share, or says only "ten years of the founder's own payments".
3. **Rows 17-19 (3a): the claim corrected.** The share under three months was 56% in 2019 and 41% in 2024; 17% held more than a year. Small groups hold more months than large ones. It answers the case's "appropriate" with the field's own numbers.
4. **Rows 35, 37 and 38 (3d): Taiwan's own record.** Deficits are "the norm". Groups "use one show to feed the next". Local advice is a 10-15% buffer. A Taiwanese co-sponsor will notice a founder whose share is owned for ten years.
5. **Row 14 with row 40 (3a, 3d): reserves do little in a bad patch.** Six months "is inadequate" to keep spending on trend. Residencies' own endowments fell 29% in 2008-09 just as applications doubled. Laura's payments are dated Treasuries, so the founder's share does not dip.

## Gaps

- **NFF 2018.** The percentages for months of cash, overall and for the arts, are only in chart images in the survey PDF and on nff.org/packard. The 50% and 19% rest on Irvin and Furneaux's quotation.
- **NFF 2022.** "39% have 3 months or less" was seen only in an accountant's secondary PDF. NFF's own 2022 page was not read in full for the arts.
- **NFF's 2010 report on the 2009 survey.** Not opened; Calabrese's quotation is the only source.
- **SeaChange's operating-reserve formula.** Not extracted from the report's method notes. Whether it matches NORI's ratio is unknown.
- **Calabrese (2011)**, the "five times budget" funder finding, is cited, not read. The full published 2013 tables by subsector (arts) were not reached.
- **Alliance of Artists Communities, "From Surviving to Thriving" (2013, 157 organisations).** Members-only, not read. The Alliance's own months of runway (16.3 months, FY2024 990) is on philanthropy.org only, a secondary aggregator, and is not used.
- **Arts Council England's NPO annual data (official statistics, 2018-2025).** The workbooks are public (artscouncil.org.uk) but were not analysed. A median free-reserves series from them would replace Cause4's single year.
- **The Australian National Opera Review (2015) and its Reserves Incentive Scheme.** Seen, but the URL and the scheme's terms were not recorded at source.
- **Taiwan.** No balance-sheet series (months of cash) for Taiwanese residencies or arts foundations was found. A 2013 thesis on 優人神鼓's 2013 creative pause is secondary and not used. Japan and Korea's arts council reserve rules were not searched.
- **Calabrese (2013) on arts.** The paper says arts "generally show declines". A separate arts median was not found.
