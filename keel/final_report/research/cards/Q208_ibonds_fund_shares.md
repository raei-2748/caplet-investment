## The settlement in one screen

- **What Laura owns.** Shares of a fund. The fund owns the Treasuries (card Q72). The fund's own words: it "does not seek to return any predetermined amount at maturity or in periodic distributions", and "By December 15, 2032, the Fund is expected to consist almost entirely of cash", after which, "After appropriate provision is made for any Fund liabilities, its net assets will be distributed" (IBTM summary prospectus, 27 Feb 2026). The sponsor's own Treasury sentence makes the same split the report must make: Treasuries "have historically involved little risk of loss of principal if held to maturity, but the market value ... is not guaranteed".
- **The one sentence for the report (2.0, beside Table 1):** "Each iBonds share is part-ownership of a fund that holds Treasuries maturing in one year and, about 15 December of that year, pays its holders everything it then has; the fund promises no set amount, so the plan counts only what its Treasuries will pay, after the fund's fee and as if no waiting cash earned interest."

- **The complete list (3e), what can make a fund pay Laura less than its Treasuries, after the 0.07% fee:**

## The ideas, each in three plain sentences

### 1. Ginnie Mae puts the government's promise on the security; an iBonds fund keeps it on the bonds inside

- (a) A Ginnie Mae mortgage security passes its holders the payments from a pool of home loans, "less amounts required to cover servicing costs and Ginnie Mae guaranty fees", and the US government promises, on the face of each security, that the holder will be paid on time. An iBonds Treasury fund also passes its holders what it receives, less a 0.07% fee, but it says the opposite of a guarantee: it "does not seek to return any predetermined amount at maturity".
- (b) So in Laura's plan the US government's promise sits one level down, on the Treasuries inside the fund and not on her fund shares, which is why the plan counts what those Treasuries will pay (after the fee, with waiting cash earning nothing, less the 0.5% margin) and lets her state only that. It is also why the report's word "certain" must take as its subject the Treasuries or the range she states, never the fund's payout.
- (c) Where it breaks: Ginnie Mae needs its guarantee because what passes through is uncertain (borrowers fall behind and repay early), while what passes through IBTM is already fixed in amount and date. The only gaps are the fund's own leaks (costs, its board's choices, a broker in the chain), so the report can list them in one sentence instead of buying a guarantee: the margin and Table 3 row 4 are the plan's own wrapper.
- **Decision it serves:** holding five payments and both ends of the range in iBonds funds counted on their Treasuries (IPS l.13, l.15). **Part:** 2.0, the definition of ink; 3c's "certain".
- **Source:** Ginnie Mae MBS Guide ch. 1 (2026); IBTM summary prospectus (27 Feb 2026).
- **Pedigree or aha:** aha. Every finance judge knows Ginnie Mae; few will have set its guaranty beside a term fund to show exactly where the promise stops.

### 2. The escrow verifier re-checks every swap; the late-2032 fund swaps every month, and most of its 2032 notes have not been sold yet

- (a) A defeasance escrow may swap one Treasury for another only after a verification agent proves again that the new set pays the debt "sufficient, without further reinvestment" (store Q54-020). IBTM swaps its Treasuries every month to follow its index, 70% of its average portfolio in its last reported year, and no one checks those swaps against Laura's figure.
- (b) The Treasuries that will pay her contribution are not all the ones she buys into in January 2028. The fund holds 15 notes today, exactly the four 10-year notes of 2022 and eleven 7-year notes of 2025 that mature in 2032; on Treasury's present auction pattern 34 more notes maturing in 2032 will be sold from 2027 to 2030 (5-, 3- and 2-year notes), and each joins the index once it settles (Keel count). So ink inside a fund is a maturity year, not a list of bonds, and the plan does the verifier's job once, at purchase, on holdings with higher coupons than the fund's real ones (4.25% against 3.50%, the cautious choice; NUMBERS 3), with the 0.5% margin as room.
- (c) Where it breaks: every swap happens at market prices, so at market rates it changes nothing; it moves only the zero-interest count, by about 0.06% for a full swap with two years left and 0.39% with five at today's yields (Keel arithmetic), and every new note still matures by 15 December 2032. That is why FACTS l.50's "does not change after that" should become "is measured once, in January 2028", and why the register should add a turnover test (its nearest, a fund of all-new par bonds, leaves the top not owned in 0.31% of house paths, by at most $1,965).
- **Decision it serves:** the range rule's "measured once, at purchase, never re-measured" (NUMBERS 3) and the floor bought in January 2028 (IPS l.15). **Part:** 3c (what the bottom rests on); 3e (the list's second item).
- **Source:** IBTM summary prospectus (2026) and 2025 annual financial statements; TreasuryDirect auction pages; store Q54-020.
- **Pedigree or aha:** aha. Escrow substitution is pedigree; that most of the notes behind Laura's 2033 contribution have not been auctioned yet is a fact no other team will have counted.

### 3. Lending: the one moment her Treasuries leave the fund, and where the leak really is

- (a) In 2008 AIG lent securities and put the cash collateral into longer, riskier assets, and its losses came from that collateral, not from borrowers failing to return securities (store Q65-031). An iBonds Treasury fund may lend up to a third of its assets, must take cash of at least 102% of a loan's value at the start, and BlackRock "allows for full replacement of the securities loaned" if a borrower defaults and the collateral falls short.
- (b) So the leak that remains is the collateral's own money fund: BlackRock's indemnity "does not guarantee, assume or otherwise insure the investment performance or return of any cash collateral vehicle". For these funds that vehicle has been a Treasury money fund (IBTQ's holdings list "BLK CSH FND TREASURY SL AGENCY"); IBTM had nothing on loan at its last annual report (31 Oct 2025), and lending income, of which the fund keeps 82%, can only add.
- (c) Where it breaks: AIG chose where its collateral went, while here the agreement sets the vehicle, and it names a prime fund too (BlackRock Cash Funds: Institutional, which "may impose a discretionary liquidity fee of up to 2%"). So lending belongs in the report's list as a cost beyond the fee, the 0.5% margin's job, not as a new kind of risk.
- **Decision it serves:** the 0.5% margin and what it covers (NUMBERS 0, 3; WORDS). **Part:** 3e, inside the list's first item; store otherwise.
- **Source:** iShares 2025 annual financial statements; iShares SAI (2026); BlackRock 10-K (2025); IBTQ holdings (2 Nov 2026).
- **Pedigree or aha:** pedigree for AIG; the indemnity's edge (borrowers covered, collateral not) is niche.

### 4. Survivors cannot show an early end; the census and the fund sizes can

- (a) A margin tested only on funds that ran to their end cannot show what an early end would cost, which is the judge's point; the answer is the record of every comparable fund, about 82 ended defined-maturity ETFs, of which none merged, none ran past its date and two small emerging-market debt funds ($25.8m and $33.9m) ended months early (card Q151).
- (b) Laura's other four payment funds are today about 3 to 15 times those closed funds' size (IBTO $520m, IBTP $379m, IBTQ $301m, IBTR $112.5m; IBTM $641m), and one of the five ended Treasury funds, IBTA, spent its last year with bills at about 0.05%, so the record already contains the cash-rate risk at its worst.
- (c) Where it breaks: size today says nothing certain about size in 2032 (holders leave near the end, card Q3c row 19), and a board's decision has no odds, so the report should say what the 0.5% is sized for (costs) and what it is not (an early end's lost interest, Table 3 row 4), and never that five funds prove the margin enough for everything.
- **Decision it serves:** the 0.5% margin's size and Table 3 row 4's watch. **Part:** 3e; Table 3's note.
- **Source:** iShares product pages (6-17 Nov 2026); cards Q151, Q3c.
- **Pedigree or aha:** pedigree (the reference class is decision science's outside view); the sizes are the new facts.
