- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Institutions that fund fixed future payments routinely show, in one exhibit, each year's asset cash against each year's payment."
  2. "Figure 1's right half (each payment split into 'Its own Treasury' and 'Extra Treasuries') follows a professional form."
  3. "Figure 1 is clearer with both worlds in it (today's market rates and cash earning nothing)."
  4. "The lay-reader form is the payment as a fixed line with the sources stacked under it."
  5. "Professionals lead with one headline number or verdict for the whole match."
- **Problem class:** proving that dated assets pay dated fixed payments, in one exhibit, to a reader who must trust it fast (an asset-liability cash-flow schedule). **Field that solved it first:** municipal defeasance escrow verification (the sufficiency schedule; card Q28), then state lotteries funding annuity prizes with zero-coupon Treasuries (Missouri "Since March 1990"), then public pension accounting's depletion-date test (GASB 67, 2012). UK annuity insurers and pension schemes solve the same problem but publish it as a sentence or one number.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KILL as stated.** Of the 28 documents read this run (22 at source, 6 only as search extracts), only the GASB 67 projections (four plans) and card Q28's escrow verification reports pair a payment with the money that pays it in the same row. Everyone else splits the two sides or reduces them to words:
  - **State lotteries** that buy Treasury zero-coupon bonds for each annuity payment publish the bonds and the payments in two separate notes, with different groupings and different value bases. Florida 2025 (read in full): STRIPS in eight maturity buckets at fair value ($205,131 thousand) against prizes by fiscal year at face ($280,615 thousand; net present value $215,588 thousand). A reader cannot line a bond up with a prize. Missouri proves the match only by two equal totals in two notes: "maturity value of securities held ... $34,904,000" and "Total installment payments due 34,904,000".
  - **UK annuity insurers** say it in words ("We seek to ultimately invest in assets which match our liability cash flows", Rothesay 2025). The regulator receives one percentage per test per portfolio (PRA MA.03.01). No year-by-year chart appears in the parts of the five insurer documents read.
  - **UK and Dutch pension funds** chart liability cash flows alone (Hoogovens 2025), or contributions against benefits with a net line (Surrey and Northamptonshire actuaries' papers, 2023). They give matching as one ratio: BTPS's "cashflow coverage ratio" of 70%, Campina's 100% hedge, ABP's 49.1% at end-2024.
  - **Judge's note:** a judge may assume every pension fund prints a year-by-year match. Public reports almost never do, and a lottery holding a bond for every prize still prints them in two tables that do not line up. A row-by-row pairing of ten payments is the rarer, stronger form.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, and borrow the professional labels.** GASB 67's Table 2 (New Jersey PERS, 2026) splits each year's projected payment into a "Funded" portion and an "Unfunded" portion, with the arithmetic printed in the head: "(d) = (c) if (b) >= (c)", "(e) = (c) - (d)". That is Figure 1's right half in shape, under card Q28's escrow basis ("sufficient, without reinvestment"). The professional versions do two things Figure 1 does not yet do:
  - they print the arithmetic in the column heads, so the reader sees the two parts add to the payment;
  - they show one basis per schedule.
- **Claim 3: KILL.** Every professional schedule read shows one basis:
  - the escrow verifier shows only "without reinvestment" (card Q28);
  - each GASB projection shows one assumption set;
  - PRA Test 1 has one basis.
  Figure 1's "At today's market rates" column prints $50,000 ten times. The 11 Nov fresh reader "read it twice to check I was not missing a difference" (`research/panel/2026-11-11_fresh_reader_exhibits.md`). Move that world to the note or the title: at today's market rates each own Treasury pays the whole $50,000 and the extra Treasuries are never touched, $74,123 left by 1 Jan 2042, $45,456 in 1 Jan 2033 money (NUMBERS 8h). If both worlds must stay in the figure, print the today's-rates world as GASB crossover tables do, $0 needed from the extra Treasuries in every row (TriMet's "Unfunded" column), never a repeated $50,000.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE.** Stacked sources under a payment line is a fund manager's chart (card Q28 rows 7-8). The reporting forms read here put the numbers in a table, or use bars with a net line. For ten payments of the same size the numbers stay printed. If a dollar picture is ever drawn, Few's bullet graph is the form that keeps the $50,000 on a common scale from $0: one short perpendicular target line per row, with the own-Treasury bar reaching toward it. But Figure 1's black tick already marks a date (1 January), and one figure should not use a tick for two meanings.
- **Claim 5: KEEP.** Professionals compress the whole test into one line:
  - Oklahoma OPERS, page 1: "Year in which Fiduciary Net Position is Projected to be Depleted N/A";
  - BTPS: "cashflow coverage ratio ... is currently at 70%";
  - PRA: the highest accumulated shortfall "should not exceed 3% of the present value of liabilities";
  - Missouri: two equal face totals.
  Laura's headline, all from NUMBERS 8h:
  - if cash waiting earns nothing, her own Treasuries pay $444,080 of the $500,000, about 89% (Keel arithmetic);
  - the extra Treasuries pay the other $55,920;
  - no payment is short in any year.
  - **Judge's note:** Figure 1's test is stricter than the UK annuity insurers' own regulator. PRA Test 1 lets surpluses roll forward "at the risk-free rate" and allows a shortfall of up to 3% of the liabilities' value. Figure 1 lets waiting cash earn nothing and allows no shortfall.
