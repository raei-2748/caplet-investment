- **Problem class:** income risk of a self-employed earner in a winner-take-all, release-driven trade: how big the falls at the top are, when they come, and how much of the swing is the cycle and how much the earner's own calendar. **Field that met it first:** cultural economics' author-earnings surveys (the UK ALCS series from 2006, the Authors Guild's US surveys, Macquarie's Australian national surveys), and tax law, which met creators' lumpy income with averaging rules (UK ITTOIA 2005 ss221-222A; Australia's special-professionals averaging). ## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE the SKELETON phrase "the link is strong for top earners"; IPS l.9 stands as partly true (CANON B4).** At the top of the author trade, the shock hit speaking and the biggest advances, not book income.
  - **2008-10.** On the Forbes top-ten lists for the year to June 2008 and the year to June 2010, the eight authors other than J.K. Rowling who appear on both went from $228m to $219m (-3.9%). The whole list without Rowling went from $263m to $259m (-1.5%) (Keel arithmetic on the lists as printed by the BBC and the Guardian). In February 2009 a London agent said "For the big names, for the big brands, we may even see increases" (The Bookseller). The whole US arts field's receipts fell 11.6% in 2009 (Q1c-005).
  - **2020.** In the UK, authors at the 80th percentile earned £50,000, "a real growth rate of 2% since 2019/2020". The 20th percentile lost 16%. The primary-occupation median lost 11.7% (Q1c). Authors with agents kept a £10,000 median writing income in both years. In Australia the share of authors with total income above A$200,000 was 5.3% in FY2020/21, against 2.5% in FY2013/14.
  - **The one place the top fell more.** US established full-time authors' top-decile book income fell 17% in real terms between the 2018 survey and 2022 (two cross-sections five years apart), while the median rose 20% (Keel arithmetic, AG 2023 slide 82).
  - **Where the top clearly fell in 2020.** "Major deals" ($500,000 and up) were down 17%, the second fall in a row, and at a five-year low (Publishers Lunch, read only in a copy). Speakers making $500,000 or more fell from 7% to 4% (SpeakerFlow).
  - **Judge's note:** in the 2020 shock the top fifth of UK authors earned slightly more in real terms while the bottom fifth lost 16%. At the top, the shock hit the stage, not the page.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, with sizes.**
  - 17% of UK writers earned nothing from writing in 2013, though 98% of them had work published in every year 2010-13 (Gibson et al., 2015). 18% of full-time US authors earned $0 from books in 2017 (Authors Guild, 2019).
  - Over 2008-13, 15.6% of UK authors at least doubled their writing income and 23.0% at least halved it: about 2 in 5 moved by a factor of two in five years, in both directions (Keel arithmetic on Table 6.1).
  - 96% of a fiction bestseller's sales (94% for nonfiction) came in its first year (Yucesoy et al., 2018).
  - One Forbes author fell 97% between two windows (Rowling, $300m to $10m) while another rose 40% (Patterson).
  - **Judge's note:** two governments wrote lumpiness into tax law. A British author whose profit in one year is under 75% of the next year's can average the two years. That relief is written for creators (and, over five years, for farmers). The law treats a swing of a quarter as an ordinary year for an author.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE: it depends on the stream.**
  - **Book sales** did not fall at the top in 2020. Deals in early 2021 were "up 19 percent over 2020" (Friedman, reporting Publishers Lunch).
  - **2009 receipts.** For the whole arts field, receipts were still about 5% below 2008 after 2010's +7.0% (Keel arithmetic on Q1c-005: 0.884 × 1.070 = 0.946).
  - **Speaking took two to three years after 2020.** In-person events ran at 57-68% of 2019 attendance into mid-2022. Cancellations were 20-30% of gigs in 2021. One bureau called 2022 its "most profitable year".
  - **The typical author's long slide does not come back,** but it is a trend, not a recession: Canada's average writing income fell 27% in 2014-17, Taiwan's book publishers' revenue fell 31% from 2016 to 2023, and the UK and US medians are in Q1.
- **Claim 4: KILL as a recession effect; KEEP as a calendar risk. The plan already covers it.**
  - **Recession falls are smaller than the thresholds.** At the top, the falls measured in a shock year are about -2% to -17% for book income and deals and about -40% for the share of speakers at $500,000 or more. Single famous authors moved -24% to -40% between two windows. All sit below the 49.0% at which "most" fails and far below the 74.5% the escrow standard survives (NUMBERS 5).
  - **What could clear 74.5% is a near-zero year,** which the surveys find in about 1 in 6 active writers' years.
  - **The ten payments need little of the deposit.** At today's yields they need only its first 1.6%, the $2,388 top-up; the escrow standard needs its first 25.5%, the $38,282 (Keel arithmetic on NUMBERS 5).
  - **A cut deposit is not a reason to change the plan.** A smaller deposit is not one of the IPS's two reasons, and the plan's order absorbs it.
  - **Caution: a fall in income is not the same fall in the deposit.** The case says her living costs are met outside the portfolio, but gives neither those costs nor how much of the $150,000 is already earned.
  - **Judge's note:** the real danger to the 2028 deposit is a quiet year between books, not a falling share market. The plan needs only the first 1.6% of the deposit to keep the ten payments whole.
- **Claim 5: KILL as anything the report says about Laura.**
  - "It is sufficient for an author to have written a single book that appeared on the NYTBL for a single week to be labeled a 'bestselling author', a label that sticks for life" (Yucesoy et al., 2018). Most list books sell 10,000-100,000 copies in their first year.
  - The case gives her sources of income (case p.2), never its size. The decile tables show what "successful" can mean, never where she sits.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 9 (2.3 and 3e, SKELETON row 4).** In 2020 the top fifth of UK authors rose 2% in real terms while the bottom fifth lost 16%. This replaces "the link is strong for top earners" with a tested, author-specific finding, and keeps IPS l.9 as partly true.
2. **Row 5 (3e area (2), Table 3 row 1).** 17% of UK writers earned nothing in 2013 although 98% of them had work out every year 2010-13. It explains in one fact why the deposit is confirmed in writing and nothing is promised before it lands.
3. **Row 35 (3e, Table 3 row 1).** Recession falls at the top (about -2% to -17% for book income and deals, about -40% for the share of top speakers) sit far below the 74.5% the escrow standard survives, and the payments need only the first 1.6% of the deposit. The risk is a quiet year, not the share market.
4. **Row 30 (2.3 or 3e).** UK tax law lets creators average two years when one is under 75% of the other: an outside institution, not a model, saying an author's income swings by a quarter as a matter of course.
5. **Rows 14-15 (3e).** Through 2008-10 the Forbes top ten's combined income barely moved without Rowling (-1.5%), while single authors moved -97% to +40%. The swing at the top is each author's own calendar, not the cycle.
