- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**

  2. "Laura first sees next year's Taiwan arts money in late August each year, when the cabinet sends its budget to the legislature."
  3. "Taiwan's public arts funders pay for residency buildings (capital works), so a residency can expect public money for its building."

- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** an appropriation calendar (when a public funder's money is first proposed, when it is voted, what may be spent while the vote is late) and capital grants for cultural buildings routed through local government. Public budgeting law solved the first: Taiwan's Budget Act (預算法) fixes the proposal date (Art. 46), the vote date (Art. 51) and the rule for a late vote (Art. 54), and the Local Government Act (地方制度法) Art. 40 does the same for cities and counties. The second is Taiwan's own long programme of matched local capital: the 地方文化館 (local culture halls) programme from 2002, built on earlier reuse programmes from 1990.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** The law says 30 November: "總預算案應於會計年度開始一個月前由立法院議決，並於會計年度開始十五日前由總統公布之" (the legislature shall pass the general budget one month before the fiscal year begins, and the President shall promulgate it 15 days before; Art. 51). In practice the deadline was met in **0 of 11 years**, FY2016-FY2026:
  - Passed in December twice (18 Dec 2015; 19 Dec 2023).
  - Passed in January 8 times (19 Jan 2017; 30 Jan 2018; 10 Jan 2019; 20 Jan 2020; 29 Jan 2021; 28 Jan 2022; 19 Jan 2023; 21 Jan 2025).
  - Passed in August once (14 Aug 2026, 257 days after 30 Nov).
  - The median lateness is 51 days, about 20 January. In 9 of 11 years the year began with no passed budget (Keel arithmetic on DGBAS releases).
  - **Judge's note:** in no year from 2016 to 2026 did Taiwan's legislature pass the budget by its legal deadline. The date that does not move is the cabinet's draft in late August.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, with the dates.** Art. 46 sends the draft "於會計年度開始四個月前" (four months before the year begins, so by 31 August).
  - The cabinet (Executive Yuan) approved the draft between 13 and 26 August in all seven years found: 18 Aug 2016, 17 Aug 2017, 15 Aug 2019, 13 Aug 2020, 26 Aug 2021, 21 Aug 2025 and 20 Aug 2026.
  - The Ministry of Culture published its own headline figure the same day (21 Aug 2025; 20 Aug 2026 through CNA).
  - The Ministry's line-item budget books followed about two weeks later (posted 3 Sep 2025).
  - The same week, cities learn their next-year general grants from the Ministry by letter (Taichung: letter of 29 Aug 2025).
  - So "late August" is the first public sight, about four months before the year begins. It is a proposal, not a promise. Certainty comes at passage (claim 1), and only after any freeze is lifted (row 13).
- **Claim 3: CHANGE.** Capital money exists, in four forms, and most of it flows to cities and counties, not to a founder.
  - (i) The art-village grant still has a capital line today: "空間發展計畫：包含添購或升級藝術村之創作設備暨設施（含電腦軟體）、展覽設施等資本門項目" (space development: buying or upgrading creative equipment and facilities, including software, and exhibition facilities, all capital items). It sits inside the same NT$3m cap as running costs.
  - (ii) The local culture hall and forward-looking infrastructure (前瞻) lines funded building works, but their applicants are cities and counties.
    - The largest item found near a residency is NT$50m (of NT$64.9m) in 2019 for Kaohsiung's city-run Pier-2.
    - Items with a residency in their name are NT$0.2m-1.8m.
  - (iii) The placemaking rules (地方創生) give private bodies at most NT$2m a year. Renovation is allowed only with a public-use pledge of 2-4 years.
  - (iv) Taipei's own culture bureau pays no capital at all: "不補助購買資本性財產所發生之費用（設備費）" (no grant for the cost of buying capital assets, i.e. equipment).
  - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan, public money for buildings is real but goes to cities. A private residency's public capital line is equipment-sized (at most NT$3m, about $91,550, shared with running costs). That is why Laura's own capital contribution is the money a public funder cannot replace.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE.** Art. 54 keeps running money going. Other spending continues "依已獲授權之原訂計畫或上年度執行數" (at the authorised plan or last year's actual level). But "新興資本支出及新增計畫，須俟本年度預算完成審議程序後始得動支" (new capital spending and new programmes may be spent only after this year's budget is passed). Cities and counties have the same rule (Local Government Act Art. 40). A late vote delays exactly the money a new residency building would need. It does not stop existing grants.
- **Claim 5: KILL.** The Bureau of Cultural Heritage's own budget fell from NT$3,580.687m (2025) to NT$1,181.145m (2026), down 67% (Keel arithmetic). Much of that money moved rather than ended:
  - from 2026 the Ministry's general grants to local governments carry NT$2,386.843m;
  - that includes the third year of a continuing heritage plan (NT$1,187.988m) and the first year of "再造歷史現場2.0" (Reconstructing Historical Sites 2.0, NT$471.5m).
  - The rules changed on 29 Aug 2025: general grants now cover "具經常性、普及性、基本建設或維運性質" items (recurring, universal, basic-construction or maintenance items).
  - **Judge's note:** reading one agency's budget book can show a two-thirds "cut" that is mostly a move. Laura's watch has to read the Ministry's general-grant line too.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 10 (0 of 11 years on time) with row 4 (late August, every year).** Table 3 row 7 and 3e. It gives Laura a dated, honest public signal: first sight in late August, certainty usually in January. "Certain by law" is not "certain in fact".
2. **Row 12 (Art. 54: new capital waits).** 3d. When Taiwan's budget is late, a new residency's public building money is the first thing held back. Laura's Treasuries are owned and wait for no vote. The contrast does a job no adjective can.
3. **Rows 27, 30-31 with 39-42 (capital goes to cities; a private residency's public capital is equipment-sized).** 3d and 3b. Public money for buildings is real but routed through cities: NT$50m for a city-run district, at most NT$3m (shared) or NT$2m a year for a private body. Laura's contribution is the capital public funders do not give a founder directly.
4. **Row 25 (public 2033 money is visible about four months before she pays).** 3b and Table 3. Public co-sponsors' 2033 money shows in late August 2032, before the floor fund pays. Private foundations' filings show only in Feb-Mar 2033 (Q30). Her flexibility's third use gets an early public warning.
5. **Row 19 (a cut that is a move).** 3e. Reading Taiwan's arts money needs the general-grant line: one bureau's two-thirds fall in 2026 was mostly a transfer. It is a "smart reader had it backwards" moment for the monitoring paragraph.
