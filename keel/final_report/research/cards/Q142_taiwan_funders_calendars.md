- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "The Executive Yuan must send the budget to the Legislative Yuan by a set date, the Legislative Yuan should pass it before the fiscal year, and recent budgets have often passed late (2026's 257 days after its deadline)."
  2. "Local governments and their culture bureaus budget on the same calendar."

- **Problem class:** a lead-time problem: one party's promise has to arrive before each counterparty's fixed budget calendar closes, or it waits a full year. **Field that solved it first:** public budgeting. Governments that budget one year at a time solved multi-year promises by approving multi-year plans before the annual budget is drafted. Taiwan's own version is the cabinet's approval of mid- to long-term plans by 15 April of the year before, followed by the National Development Council's (NDC's) pre-review (rows 7-9).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP, with dates and two corrections.**
  - **The legal dates.** Taiwan's fiscal year is the calendar year (Budget Act Art. 12). The Constitution sets the sending date at three months before the year (Art. 59), and the Budget Act at four, so 31 August. The legislature "不得為增加支出之提議" (may not propose more spending; Constitution Art. 70, read at source, closing Q49's gap).
  - **The record.** Adding FY2010-15 to Q49's FY2016-26 gives 0 of 17 budgets passed by 30 November. Only 3 of 17 passed before 1 January: FY2012, FY2016 and FY2024. All three had a January national election ahead; FY2020 is the one such year that slipped (Keel arithmetic).
  - **The 257 days.** VERIFIED as Keel arithmetic: from 30 Nov 2025 to 14 Aug 2026 is 257 days, or 226 days into the year the budget was for. The press counts differently: the Speaker's "拖了266天" gives no base, and "351天" counts from the day the budget was sent.
  - **Passed is not yet in force.** Promulgation came 22-36 days after passage (FY2012, FY2013, FY2026). In FY2026, NT$299.2bn could not be spent while the budget waited. NT$101.7bn of that was new programmes, and NT$63.7bn of those benefited local governments.
  - **FY2027 now.** It was referred to committees on 27 Oct 2026, and the session was extended to 31 Jan 2027.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** The legal dates are the same (Q49 row 14), but practice splits by year.
  - In ordinary years, cities vote late. Kaohsiung voted its city budget in January or February of the budget year itself four years running (FY2023-26). FY2026 passed on 6 Feb 2026, 68 days after the legal date.
  - In a local-election year, a city either moves early or leaves the vote to the new council. Taichung opened its council two months early and passed FY2027 on 13 Oct 2026. Kaohsiung left FY2023 to the new council, which voted it in February 2023.
  - Cities' drafts reach councils in late July or early August: Taipei on 21 Jul 2026, Kaohsiung by 4 Aug 2026.
- **Claim 3: KEEP.** The windows are in Q10d and are not repeated here. New calendar facts:
  - NCAF is 99.93% government-endowed, so its own budget follows the government's calendar (Foundations Act Art. 55), not a private foundation's.
  - Its board settles next year's financial plan in April (15 Apr 2024 for FY2025) and meets every three months. Its budget book is out by late August or early September each year (2024, 2025, 2026).
  - Its grant decisions come before the budget that pays them, as Q10d showed.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE, then KEEP the company half with evidence.**
  - **Foundations.** The filing deadline differs by kind. A private foundation files within one month after the year begins (Art. 25), a government-endowed one before the year (Art. 55). Both are already in the store and not repeated.
  - **Companies.** The fourth-quarter claim now rests on evidence, not assumption. Seven boards (six listed companies and one listed group's subsidiary) approved the next year's budget or business plan between 7 Nov and 30 Dec 2025. One, Chang Wah Electromaterials (長華電材), approved a separate "2026 年度捐贈預算案" (2026 donation budget) on 18 Dec 2025. One counter-case, Taiwan Asia Semiconductor (台亞), approved its 2025 budget on 3 Jan 2025.
  - **How far it goes.** This is a search sample, not a census, and Q50 found that most firms keep no fixed sponsorship budget. So say "many listed companies set the next year's budget in November-December", never "all".
- **Claim 5: KEEP as Keel arithmetic on the calendars.** For 2032 money, the public deadlines all fall in April-July 2031:
  - the NDC gate on 15 Apr 2031, for large building plans only;
  - ministries' estimates from 1 May to 6 Jun 2031;
  - the cabinet's ceiling meeting about 25 Jul 2031;
  - cities' drafts to councils from late July to 30 Sep 2031;
  - NCAF's financial plan in April 2031 and its budget book by about August 2031.

  Companies' boards (November-December 2031) and private foundations (January 2032) come later. A figure stated in January-March 2031 therefore reaches every kind of co-sponsor's 2032 budget. A figure stated after about June 2031 reaches public money only in 2033.
- **Judge's note (3b, NUMBERS 8r):** A careful reader expects the 2031 move date to be a question of money. NUMBERS 8r shows the stated top is $115,000 whether the move is on 1 January, 1 April or 1 July 2031. What changes is the calendar. A 1 July figure would arrive after every public 2032 draft had closed, and a 1 April figure after the NDC's 15 April gate. Taiwan's budget calendars, not the markets, are why 1 January is the right day.
- **Judge's note (3e area 5):** A government co-sponsor is usually taken to be the steady one. Taiwan's central budget has been late 17 years in a row. In 3 of 17 years it passed before its year began, and each of those had a national election weeks away. Companies, by contrast, were the most punctual in this sample.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 36 (3b, 3d):** NUMBERS 8r shows the move date does not change the $115,000 top. Taiwan's calendars do the work: January 2031 meets every public 2032 deadline, while July meets none. That is an aha a judge can check in one table.
2. **Row 35 (3d, Table 3):** the 2032 windows in one line. It answers the question in 3d that co-sponsors actually ask: "when can we use your figure?"
3. **Rows 11-12 (3e area 5, Table 3):** 0 of 17 central budgets were on time. The only three that passed before their year each had a national election weeks away. Read with the plan's own dates, this is the case for watching dates and changing nothing (A34).
4. **Rows 31-32 (3d):** companies set the next year's budget in November-December, and one set its giving budget in the same December meeting. This is evidence, not assumption, for the company half of claim 4.
5. **Row 30 (3e area 5):** Taiwan's national arts grant-maker runs mostly on the income of a fund about 57% in shares, and its next-year budget is set in mid-year. A weak 2031 market can lower both Laura's top and NCAF's 2032 budget, so 3e names a link the reader might not expect.

## Gaps

- Kaohsiung's internal deadline for city agencies' 2027 estimates. A date of 17 Apr 2026 was seen earlier this run, but neither 主計處 report confirmed it on re-reading. Cities' internal estimate deadlines generally are not established.
- The exact day FY2011 passed. The gazette shows the item-by-item debate of 7-12 Jan 2011 but not the vote day.
- The Ministry of Culture's committee review date for FY2027 was seen in a legislature weekly schedule, but the file could not be matched to its URL, so it is left out.
- Budget Act Art. 79 (when a supplementary budget is allowed) was not read.
- Whether the Ministry of Culture's residency and venue grants sit in the general budget or in a special fund was not checked. It matters for row 15.
- Culture-bureau grant windows for Kaohsiung, Tainan and Taichung were not searched this run (Taipei's are in Q49).
- The listed-company sample is seven boards found by search. No census exists of the month in which companies set budgets. Only one company showed a separate donation budget.
- The month in which corporate foundations' boards approve next-year plans was not found. The law (Art. 25) sets only the filing deadline after the year begins.
- The month in which NCAF's board approves the next-year budget itself was not found. Only the April financial plan and the late-August book were found.
- Parallel's free tier ran out near the end of the run, and Firecrawl was out of credits. The last checks used WebFetch.
