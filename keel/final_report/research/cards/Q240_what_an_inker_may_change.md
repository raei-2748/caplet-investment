- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** delegated execution of someone else's finished intent (an agent who converts a plan into a binding form: what discretion the agent has, who set it, and who may change the content). Comics production settled it in practice in the 1960s-70s through editors' assignments, credit lines ("pencils/inks" against "breakdowns/finishes" or "embellisher") and pencillers' rules for their inkers; law and finance call it the scope of an agent's authority, and portfolio practice calls it a rules-based rebalancing with no discretion at the trade.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE. True to the craft as a norm with named exceptions; the map holds in shape and stops exactly where an inker's latitude begins.**
  - The craft. An inker's own field is the line's weight, the blacks and the finish: "Mainly, I was concerned about spotting blacks" (Sinnott, 1995 interview). A former Marvel editor lists what pencillers object to: line weight, rendering, "the addition or subtraction of black", "something that was removed or added to the panel", and above all "FACES. Little subtle changes to a face would drive a penciller crazy" (Wright 2020). Kirby's own rule to Mike Royer, who had touched up Big Barda's face: "Never touch the faces!"; Royer's working rule ever after: "You should never know another artist had touched it" (Royer at San Diego 2017, reported by Bleeding Cool).
  - The exceptions. Some inkers do fix the drawing: Klaus Janson says a penciller "may need some help with their anatomy or their wrinkles ... And I try to push it in that direction" (PBS, *Comic Culture*). Finishers over breakdowns add the lighting, the blacks and the backgrounds the pencils left out (Wright 2020; Sinnott 2007: "He's completing it. He's adding to it. He's an embellisher."). Stan Lee gave Sinnott leave to "take any liberties with Jack's work"; Sinnott withdrew them himself: "Even when Jack's eyes weren't on the same plane, this was Kirby. So I reverted back."
  - The map. The move cannot touch the faces of Laura's page: the ten $50,000 payments and the $95,000 bottom were inked in January 2027 and January 2028, and "After the move nothing changes either end" (NUMBERS 4); the bottom and the half kept are identical path by path whatever the move date (NUMBERS 8r). Its only "line weight" is how the top is stated: the moved half ($18,349) buys about $20,138 of the late-2032 fund, the two lots pay $122,503, and the top is $117,142 unrounded, stated $115,000 (NUMBERS 4, 8y).
  - Where it stops. An inker's latitude is taste exercised at the board; the move has none. Its size (half), fund (the late-2032 fund), date (before the meetings, IPS l.15; 1 January, NUMBERS 8r) and rounding were written years before; the one live choice is execution (a limit order within 0.5% of net asset value, Table 3 row 5). So the true twin is not "the inker's latitude" but Royer's fidelity: the move inks what half the shares buy that day and adds nothing. And never call the IPS "the pencils" or "tight pencils" (V2.4 guard 3): in this mapping the pencil is the shares' value before the move.
  - **Judge's note:** in comics the one thing an inker never touches is the face; on Laura's page the faces are the ten payments and the bottom, inked years before, and the 2031 move only states the last line, rounded to the safe side.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE (kill "a broken promise"; keep the structure underneath it).**
  - The fact is real but contested. Vince Colletta did leave out pencilled figures and backgrounds on Kirby's *Thor* (Kirby Kinetics 2009; Steibel, *The Comics Journal* 2014, on Thor #166; Brevoort 2021: "a favorite of editors, especially when a story was in trouble schedule-wise"). But a reader of IDW's *Thor* Artist's Edition found most omissions were background bits beside word balloons and "exactly one panel" where whole figures went without a readability reason, calling the reputation "highly exaggerated" (Kleefeld 2022); some removals were Stan Lee's own editorial directions (Hannum in the *Jack Kirby Collector* #54, as reported by Mendryk 2010), and an editor confirms removals "might actually be something the editor requested" (Wright 2020). So "the inker broke the promise" is CONFLICTING evidence, and the pencils were never a promise to anyone.
  - What is true underneath. Comics traded detail for the date: Colletta "certainly didn't enhance Jack's work, but he got the work done, and that's what Stan wanted ... I don't think he ever missed a deadline in his life" (Sinnott 1995). And the last stage before a fixed date absorbs every earlier slip: inkers handed a job "in 1/4 of the time" take shortcuts, and are blamed "when the penciller was the reason for the lateness" (Wright 2020).
  - The map. That structure is the first plan's exactly: all ten payments were to be bought on one date, 1 Jan 2033, so that one purchase had to absorb every share shortfall of 2027-2032. It missed in 6,453 of 200,000 paths (about 1 in 31); 97.5% of the misses had shares lower after 2027-2030; and "A shortfall that day cannot be made up later: no money comes in after January 2028" (NUMBERS 8s). Laura's payments are fixed in both date and amount, so unlike a comic neither can give; the plan puts the promised money in the first purchase (January 2027, completed January 2028), and the last one, the 2031 move, absorbs slips only in the top, which is stated after it.
  - Where it stops. Colletta's cuts were a person's choices; the first plan's misses were market outcomes. And his name carries a fifty-year feud that would take over a sentence. A plain clause, never a turn and never his name in the report.
  - **Judge's note:** comics trade detail for the date; Laura's payments allow neither, so the money is owned before the date.
- **Claim 3: KILL.**
  - False as a craft generality. Traditionally the ink went on the penciller's board and the pencils were erased (Evanier 2015), which is why Kirby's pencils survive mostly as his own photocopies (Bleeding Cool 2016). But inkers have long inked on copies: a Marvel editor was set to ink "a Xerox of tight pencils ... on a lightbox" (Wright 2020), and today pencillers scan and inkers print bluelines on their own board, "leaving the original pencils unaffected" (Book Riot 2014).
  - False as a map. "The original" pushes "owned" toward "her own bonds", which the law rules out: a security entitlement "is not a claim to a specific identifiable thing" (UCC 8-503, Official Comment 2; CONCEIT V2.1; card Q157 row 32). What makes ink count in the craft is that it is what gets printed, whatever sheet it is on, just as what makes her Treasuries ink is that they will pay, not which bonds they are. That residue is a store note, not a turn.
- **Claim 4 (own): KEEP, as a fact behind a working word, never a new turn.**
  - The craft. Tight pencils "could easily not be inked and used as they are"; looser ones leave the inker "a lot more decisions regarding line weights and rendering style"; a finisher gets pencils with "no lighting, no indication of black, sketchy backgrounds"; and the fights were over how much had been decided before the inker began ("a penciller claimed to be doing full pencils and the inker felt they were more like breakdowns", settled by the art director; Wright 2020). Who inked whom was the editor's call (Wright 2020; Evanier 2015: "That decision was made by editors and publishers, not by artists"). Over Kirby's finished pencils the inker he chose added nothing: "Jack made no subsequent changes to the ink work" (Evanier, at the 2017 panel).
  - The map. Every question about the 2031 move was answered before 2031: the IPS fixed it (l.15) and named the only two reasons to change anything (l.17: a missed deposit or a Treasury default; "A fall in the market is not one of them"); NUMBERS fixed the date (8r) and the rule for the top (4). At the move nothing is left to decide but the order's limit.
  - **Judge's note:** comic inkers argued about how much had been decided before they picked up the brush; Laura's plan writes it down, so at the 2031 move there is nothing left to decide.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 20, the last stage absorbs every earlier slip** (3f, as a plain fact behind "the first plan's payments were in pencil", or 3.0). It explains the first plan's one date, 1 Jan 2033, in a sentence a judge already knows from any project with a fixed end, and the reversal (the promised money in the first stage, only the top in the last) is the whole plan's order. No comics word needed.
2. **Row 2 with row 3, "Never touch the faces"** (3c, behind "inked before the 2031 meetings"). The truest answer to claim 1: two independent accounts (a penciller's rule, an editor's list) put the inker's limit at the face; on Laura's page the faces are the ten payments and the bottom, which the move cannot touch (NUMBERS 4, 8r).
3. **Row 19, comics trade detail for the date; Laura's payments allow neither** (3.0, "certainty has dates"). A short contrast that shows why the payments are bought six years early.
4. **Row 14, only the one who answers for the whole book may change it** (3e, one clause behind the two reasons). It maps who may change what the page shows (IPS l.17) and gives the IPS the editor's role, never the pencils'.
5. **Row 4, leave granted is not leave to use** (3b or 3c). Sinnott's "that was Jack's style, I shouldn't be doing it" is the craft's own reason the move never "improves" the top or the half kept (NUMBERS 8o; card Q154).
