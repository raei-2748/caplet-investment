## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KILL "like other books"; CHANGE to "later, harder and all in manga".**
  - **Later.** Graphic novels grew in 2008 (+5% in dollars to $395m, ICv2), the year the S&P fell 36.55%. They fell in 2009 (-6%) and 2010 (-8%), and BookScan's graphic novel units fell every year from 2009 to 2012. ICv2 dated full recovery to 2013 (rows 2, 3, 33).
  - **Harder.** On BookScan, graphic novel units fell 9.3% in 2009 and 13.9% in 2010, against 3.3% and 4.5% for all print books. That is about three times as much (row 3).
  - **All in manga.** On BookScan's top 750 in 2009, manga units fell 21.5% while everything else rose 18.8% (Keel arithmetic). The publishers that are neither manga nor DC or Marvel rose 27.2%, and Scholastic's Graphix imprint rose 24.8% (rows 4, 5). New releases fell 9% in 2009: manga releases fell 19%, the rest 2% (row 6).
  - **Judge's note:** in the 2009 recession the part of the graphic novel shelf a graphic memoir sits on grew by about a fifth. The fall was a manga bubble bursting.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** Her genre has had two slumps since 2007. Both were mostly a manga bust. The non-manga shelf grew in the first and held flat in the second.
  - **2009-12** came a year after a share crash (rows 3, 33).
  - **2023-24** came in a share boom. In 2023 manga fell 26.2% on BookScan's long tail, while the Western (non-manga) long tail fell only 0.6% (row 9). BookScan's -22.4% and -11.5% (Q12-018) measure an adult-fiction subcategory, and manga was 42% of adult graphic novel units in 2024. Kids' graphic novels rose 1% in 2024 (row 10).
  - **The whole market** fell 7% in 2023 and reached a record $2.2bn in 2025 (rows 11, 12).
  - **Better wording:** "Her genre's sales have their own weather: they fell a year after the 2008 crash and again in the 2023 boom, both times mostly in manga." Her income and share prices do not always fall in the same years. Co-sponsors' budgets "can" fall in the same downturn as shares; never "together".
  - **Judge's note:** "her genre fell by a third in a bull market" is true of BookScan's graphic novel line, and almost all of it was manga and two YA hits. The non-manga long tail lost 0.6%.
- **Claim 3: KILL.** In both slumps the first thing to go was new deals. The lists grew for a while: they carry deals signed one to three years earlier.
  - **2008.** Houghton Mifflin Harcourt "turned off the spigot" on acquisitions while keeping "a very robust pipeline" (Q1-031). Tokyopop cut its releases by more than half and reopened its licences in June 2008, both at once (row 15).
  - **2009-10.** BookScan's listed graphic novel titles rose 67% from 2007 to 2010, while sales per title fell 53% (row 7).
  - **2023-24.** Listed titles rose 6.8% in 2023, and Simon & Schuster published 367 graphic novels that year. Macmillan and Abrams opened graphic novel imprints in 2024. Meanwhile children's deals fell 17% in Q2 2024, to the lowest second quarter since 2016 (rows 8, 26, 27).
  - **The closures were manga lines** (CMX, Del Rey Manga, Tokyopop), one misplaced experiment (Minx) and firm-specific collapses (IDW, Oni) (rows 17-19, 25).
  - **Judge's note:** publishers turn off the tap on new contracts first. The shelf keeps filling for a year or two with books bought before.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 9 (2.3; the opening idea).** In her genre's worst recent year, manga fell 26% while non-manga graphic novels held flat (-0.6%). This keeps the pitch's "a giant stock she can't sell" honest: the stock is the shelf she works on, not the BookScan headline.
2. **Row 4 (2.3, 3e area (2)).** In 2009, manga fell 21.5% while the rest of the shelf rose 18.8%. It shows the judge that "in a recession" was tested on her genre, not assumed, and why the IPS rests its case on advances and fees, not on book sales.
3. **Row 3 with row 33 (3e area (2)).** Graphic novels fell a year after the crash, about three times as fast as all books, and bottomed in 2012. This is the cleanest evidence for IPS l.9's "later" in her own genre.
4. **Rows 26-27 (3e area (2); Table 3 row 1).** In the 2023-24 slump, new children's deals fell 17% in a quarter while publishers opened graphic novel imprints. The money that dries up first is the next contract, which is why the deposit is confirmed in writing by 1 Dec 2027.
5. **Row 31 with row 16 (3e).** Her genre's sharpest cash shocks came from a retailer and a distributor failing, Borders in 2011 and Diamond in 2025, in rising share markets. Her career carries risks of its own that no share index shows. That is the single-stock reading of the pitch.
