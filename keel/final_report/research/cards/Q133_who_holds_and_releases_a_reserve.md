- **Problem class:** custody and release of money set aside for named future payments: who holds it, what event lets it out, who may refuse, and who certifies each year that what is held still covers what is owed. **Field that solved it first:** trust law (a holder bound to a purpose and to account for it), refined by equipment leasing (release against a named event and its invoice) and by nuclear-plant regulation (notice to a regulator before any withdrawal; a funding report every two years).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP the facts, CHANGE the lesson.** The aircraft rule is exactly as claimed, but it protects the holder against the payer. It does not make the money reliable for the user.
  - **Collected monthly, by use.** Air Lease collects reserves "monthly based on reports of usage by the lessee or ... as fixed monthly rates", for named "Qualifying Events" (airframe checks, engine overhauls, life-limited parts, landing gear, the APU) (10-K for 2018).
  - **Released only on invoice.** Payment is "upon the lessee's presentation of invoices evidencing the completion of such Qualifying Event", capped at reserves paid less earlier reimbursements. Unclaimed reserves "are recognized as rental revenues at the end of the lease" (10-K for 2025). A filed engine lease adds "provided no Event of Default has occurred and is continuing", an invoice "WITHIN SIX MONTHS AFTER COMMENCEMENT ... AND BEFORE THE EXPIRY DATE", any cost "in excess of the reserves" is the lessee's, and balances left "will be retained by Lessor" (Volaris, 2013).
  - **Whose money it is.** IATA: reserves "constitute security held by the Lessor". A practitioner guide: "reserves become the property of the lessor immediately upon payment". AerCap asks for them "Based on the credit quality of the lessee", in 28% of its owned aircraft leases at end-2025 (32% a year earlier).
  - **Scale and direction.** Air Lease held $1,477,046 thousand of reserves at end-2025 ($1,180,741 thousand in 2024). In 2025 it received $489,668 thousand of deposits and reserves and paid out $90,027 thousand, about 18% (Keel arithmetic; 6% in 2024, 4% in 2023). Its "Maintenance rentals and other receipts", which include kept reserves, were $69.2m of revenue in 2025.
  - **Where it breaks.** At release: "an event is not considered as a qualifying event if an incorrect work scope has been performed" (IATA); reserve clauses are among "the two provisions most likely to trigger multi-million dollar disputes" (Maheshwari & Co., 2026). At insolvency: Avianca "failed to pay rent or reserves for the first two months after the filing" (Bankr. S.D.N.Y., 2005); if the lessor fails, a lessee with lessor-held reserves is "an unsecured creditor".
  - **Laura is the reverse on all three.** Her release rule is a maturity date, so nobody approves it and nobody can refuse it (IPS l.13: "each maturing just before it's needed"). Each fund pays out 8 to 14 days before its 1 January, each bond 47 to 318 days before (NUMBERS 8h). And where aircraft reserves sit in one account per event with no transfers, her ten share one pooled backstop that covers $2,662 to $7,820 of any payment if cash waiting earns nothing, $55,920 in all, with $1,177 left (NUMBERS 8h).
  - **Judge's note:** a lessor takes reserves from the airlines whose credit it is less sure of, and lets them out only against an invoice it approves. Laura's reserve needs nobody's trust, her own included, and lets itself out on a date.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, and the direction is the finding.** In every agreement read, the report is what unlocks the next payment.
  - **Mott:** "Multiyear grants generally require the submission and approval of interim reports before subsequent payments can be made." "The commitment letter contains projected payment dates." The acceptance letter's signature is checked as "a properly authorized officer".
  - **Mellon (sample award letter):** payment "in accordance with the attached payment schedule after the Foundation receives the countersigned version"; "narrative and financial reports according to the attached report schedule". It "reserves the right to modify the grant, discontinue funding, or terminate the grant at any time" on three grounds.
  - **Ford (a 2017 grant run by IIE):** reports "annually during the grant period, on each anniversary", "signed by an appropriate officer", with a financial accounting "signed and certified correct by the responsible financial official"; Ford may stop if it "is not satisfied with your organization's performance".
  - **Ford, Nov 2026:** the newest multi-year commitment from the funder whose BUILD programme served as a model: "A Floor of 50% Multiyear General Support", "ranging from three to five years". The same reorganisation set up a "Sunsetting and Relationship Management" unit to "wind down grants". Most of Ford's flexible grants were still "one- or two-year commitments" (Inside Philanthropy).
  - **For Laura:** nothing needs unlocking, because each payment is already bought. So the yearly report runs the other way: the residency confirms each $50,000 arrived, and Laura's side shows the next rung is held.
  - **Judge's note:** in the grant world a report buys next year's money. In Laura's plan next year's money is already bought, so the report only records what happened.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE for pledges, KILL for donor-advised funds.**
  - **Pledges are written, dated and payee-named, but five years is the norm.** Michigan: "up to a maximum of five years". Cal Poly Pomona: "schedule of payments (not to exceed 5 years)" and "acknowledgement by all parties of unconditional nature". ETSU: five years, longer "case-by-case, but will generally not exceed ten years". The CSU counting rule: "For pledges with payment schedules exceeding five years, report only the first 5 years."
  - **Pledges are chased by schedule.** Reminders at 30, 60 and 90 days past due; a multi-year pledge needs "some payment in a 24-month period" to stay open; write-offs of $10,000 or more need the CFO and of $50,000 or more the audit committee (UMB Foundation).
  - **Only an owner can pledge.** "A pledge can be made only by the entity exercising legal control over the assets to be given" (CASE, quoted by SFSU). The IPS's own line: "money you already own" (l.15).
  - **A DAF's "schedule" is a formula, not dollars.** Fidelity Charitable's Endowed Giving Program: minimum term five years; each year pays the prior year-end balance divided by the years left; beneficiary nominations "are not binding and are subject to review and approval by the Trustees in their sole discretion"; each grant is reviewed before it is paid.
  - **Judge's note:** the norm for written giving schedules is five years, and one public counting rule counts only five. Laura's ten fixed $50,000 payments are twice that, and none of them waits on a reminder letter.
- **Claim 4: KEEP.** The plan answers who holds (Laura: the case says she "will set aside a portion of the portfolio", l.94-95), what releases (each maturity), how much ($445,655 on 1 Jan 2033 against $399,089 owed at forwards, 111.7%, NUMBERS 8h) and who can refuse (nobody). It leaves to people: the instruction that sends each $50,000 from her account to the residency's, the residency's written receipt, and what happens to the promise if she dies (card Q55 rows 36 and 44). In Taiwan the residency's own yearly public list of gifts would show each payment arrived (store Q113-011). That is a line of 3e's monitoring, not a rule and not a change to the plan.

## Best five for the report

1. **Rows 2, 5 and 16 (3a, a judge's aha):** a lessor takes reserves from the airlines whose credit it is less sure of (AerCap: "Based on the credit quality of the lessee") and lets them out only on an invoice it approves. Laura's reserve is the reverse: it needs nobody's trust, her own career included (IPS l.9), and lets itself out on a maturity date.
2. **Rows 21-23 (3a or 3d's half-clause):** in November 2026 the funder whose BUILD programme served as a model for multi-year support set its new floor at grants of "three to five years", while setting up a unit to "wind down grants". Laura's ten owned years are twice the top of Ford's new range.
3. **Rows 18-20 and 24 (3a or 3e):** in every grant agreement read, the yearly report is what unlocks the next payment. In Laura's plan nothing is locked, so the report only records: the residency confirms each $50,000 arrived. One clause can say that.
4. **Rows 41-42 (3a, the one real founder-to-residency case):** Artpace's founder paid its running costs for 12 years, then left an annuity "to sustain Artpace for up to 20 years", passed through a trust and her foundation. Laura's ten have the same shape with one holder, herself, and an end date known from 2027.
5. **Row 45 (3e or Table 3's note):** Table 3 watches the Treasury paying Laura, but nothing yet watches Laura paying the residency. A single line (the January instruction, the residency's receipt, Taiwan's yearly public list of gifts) closes the digest's hole without a new rule.
