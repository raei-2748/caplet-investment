## Verdicts in one screen

**Claim 1. KEEP, with the range, and CHANGE "a discount" into two cuts.** Nine statements of US museums and arts centres read today book between 73 and 98 cents for each pledged dollar (Keel arithmetic on each statement's own figures):

| Statement (year) | Allowance for doubt | Discount for time | Booked per $1 pledged |
|---|---|---|---|
| MoMA (FY2025) | 19.8% | 6.8% | 73.4 cents |
| Broward Performing Arts Foundation (FY2021) | 1.3% | 18.5% | 80.3 cents |
| Ruth Eckerd Hall, annual campaign (2017) | 5.4% | 10.1% | 84.6 cents |
| Metropolitan Museum of Art (FY2011) | 2.1% | 8.9% (at 5.25%) | 89.0 cents |
| LACMA, building years (FY2023) | 4.0% | 5.9% ("approximately 0% to 5%") | 90.0 cents |

| MCASD, building campaign (FY2025) | none | 1.8% (4.49% to 4.79%) | 98.2 cents |

The cut has two parts: a discount for time and an allowance for doubt. The time cut is often the larger (LACMA, the Met, DMA, Broward; University of Akron Foundation 12.9% against 3.9%). Several statements set the time cut at a **Treasury rate** ("discounted using the U.S. Treasury note rate in effect the year the pledge is received", Akron; "the five-year U.S. Treasury note rate", Colorado State's foundation; "the Daily Treasury Yield Curve as of the date of the signed pledge"; "the applicable risk free rate", ASU). Promises that depend on a donor's death count least: one Indiana university foundation books its deferred-gift pledges at 39 cents.
*Judge's note:* a charity's own books first value every pledge as if it were a Treasury, then cut it because it is not one. Laura's figure is a Treasury.

**Claim 2. KEEP, with measured cases, and say "can", not "do".** ASU Foundation's allowance went from 3.6% of pledges (June 2008) to 8.8% (2009), 30.7% (2010) and 32.1% (2011). It was still 22.2% in 2018 and 13.2% in 2024: doubt booked in a crisis stayed for a decade. Duke expected not to collect $46.5 million in 2009, against $14 million in 2008 (3.3 times). Billion-dollar campaigns' collections fell 32% in a year as donors "postpone or rethink gifts". The cleanest downturn measure is old: the Baptist 75 Million Campaign (1919-24) counted $92,630,923 pledged and received $58,591,713 (63 cents on the dollar) after "cotton dropped from 40 cents a pound to 10 cents". Its agencies had already "begun planning and spending according to the amount pledged". Its own pledge card let a donor cancel on "financial reverses". Counter-case: donors with money in hand paid early in April 2009 (Sanford, $400 million; the Weills, $170 million in cash). There is no national fulfilment statistic ("No national statistics are kept", Chronicle of Philanthropy, 2010). Measured rates exist only for small pledges: 23% to 36% followed through in a Danish door-to-door field experiment, and 53% in a laboratory.
*Judge's note:* an "unprecedented" US church campaign counted pledges and collected 63 cents on the dollar when cotton fell, and its agencies had already spent on the pledged figure. That is the failure Laura's rule, "state only what she owns", is built to avoid.

**Claim 3. KEEP.**
- **A co-sponsor's conditional pledge.** A co-sponsor that pledges only if the facility's total is reached by one close date makes a conditional promise under ASU 2018-08. The barrier is an "identified event"; FASB's own example of a condition is "a like amount of new gifts ... raised from others within 30 days". The right of release is that it pays nothing if the total is missed. FASB calls a challenge (matching) grant "a common form of a conditional promise to give". So the residency books nothing for such a pledge until the total is substantially met; it only discloses it. The pledge then becomes unconditional and takes on an allowance and a time discount like any other.
- **A building pledge paid by milestones** is conditional too (FASB's museum-wing example).
- **Laura's 2031 statement** sits wherever its words put it:
  - worded as what she "expects", it is an intention (FASB's will example), and nothing is booked;
  - written as an unconditional promise of the bottom, it is booked from the day it is given, at present value less any allowance the residency judges;
  - an "up to" top is presumed conditional if ambiguous (Q18 row 3).
- Never call it a legal pledge: FASB says a promise to give "generally includes a legal obligation but does not require it" (BC39).

*Judge's note:* on a residency's books, every co-sponsor pledge written as the plan asks counts zero until the total is reached. Laura's figure, written as an unconditional promise, is the one promise booked from the day it is stated.

**Claim 4. CHANGE.**
- **Why the claim fails as worded.** "The only number that needs no allowance" is not literally true:
  - a conditional pledge is not booked at all, so it carries no allowance either;
  - cash already received needs none;
  - some charities book no allowance on anyone (MCASD; Tulsa Performing Arts Center Trust);
  - Laura's written figure would still take a time discount on the residency's books if it is paid more than a year after it is stated (pledges due within a year are booked at "net realizable value").
- **What holds (Keel's reasoning on the sources):** hers is the only promise of future money in the campaign that is already owned, and unconditional from the day it is stated. The allowance is an accountant's judgment built from "prior collection history", "an assessment of economic conditions" and "the donor's ability to pay". For Laura, that judgment is replaced by a holdings statement anyone can check.
- **What can still move it.** By 2031 one of the IPS's two reasons is spent: whether the 2028 deposit came was settled before the floor was bought in January 2028. What remains is a Treasury default, and the funds' costs beyond their 0.07% fee passing the 0.5% cut, which takes about $120 off the top for each 0.1% above it (NUMBERS 0 and 4; SKELETON 3c).
- **Suggested wording for notes:** "the one promise in the campaign that is already owned", never "the only number that needs no allowance".

*Judge's note:* an allowance is a guess about whether someone will pay. Laura's figure replaces the guess with a statement of holdings.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 23 (75 Million Campaign, 63 cents on the dollar), part 3d or 3e.** A real campaign that counted pledges, spent on them and collected 63% when a price collapsed is the plainest picture of what Laura's "state only what she owns" avoids. Its pledge card even let donors cancel on "financial reverses" (row 24), the reverse of IPS l.17.
2. **Row 10 (pledges discounted at Treasury rates), part 3d.** A one-clause aha: a charity's books value every promise as if it were a Treasury and then cut it. Laura's figure is a Treasury, so the comparison makes the case without any adjective.
3. **Row 29 (a co-sponsor's conditional pledge counts zero until the total is reached), part 3d.** It explains why her figure goes first and unconditional. On the residency's books, hers is the one promise counted from the day it is stated, which is the reason the ask is built in that order.
4. **Row 8 (73 to 98 cents across nine arts statements), part 3d.** It gives a number to "co-sponsors count the bottom". Their finance offices already cut every promise; her bottom is the one figure they need not cut for doubt.
5. **Row 16 (ASU's allowance 3.6% to 30.7% in 2008-10), part 3e.** It is measured evidence that co-sponsors' promises can lose value in the same downturn in which shares fall, which is when the half kept is smallest. Said as "can", never "fall together".

## Gaps

- **A measured fulfilment rate for capital pledges to arts buildings in 2008-10.** None found; the Chronicle says "No national statistics are kept". The next round could read CASE's Voluntary Support of Education tables, or the Giving USA 2010-11 special reports, for pledge payments by year.
- **Taiwan.** How a Taiwanese foundation (財團法人) or a public body books a pledge (認捐, 應收捐贈): on promise, or on receipt? The Ministry of Finance page found (law-out.mof.gov.tw, GL007468, 台財稅第861895866號, 6 May 1997) turned out to be a gift-tax ruling, not an accounting rule: property given to set up a foundation leaves the donor's gift total only after the foundation's registration is complete. Taxes and Taiwan legal questions are out of the case's scope, so it is not used. Taichung's model foundation accounting system (基金會會計制度範例) was found but not read, because the fetch budget ran out. This matters for row 29: the residency may not follow US standards.
- **More arts centres with campaigns.** Not reached: Lincoln Center (David Geffen Hall), Carnegie Hall (its site refused the fetch) and the Kennedy Center. The Kennedy Center's 2026 reports of collapsing donations may include cancelled pledges, a non-market cause of failure; not read.
- **The FASB Codification text itself** (ASC 958-310-35 on allowances, and 958-310-30 on present value) sits behind a login. The measurement rules above rest on ASU 2018-08 and on each statement's own policy notes.
- **Identities not confirmed from the excerpts:** the Indiana foundation behind report 000001098A (row 13), and a Colorado foundation whose allowance went from nil (2008) to $158,679 (2009) (leg.colorado.gov, func_2009; not used).
- **Why ASU's allowance stayed high** (row 17): one large pledge or many small ones. The full notes were not read.
- **Tools:** WebFetch's shared budget, Firecrawl's credits, Parallel's free tier and Consensus's monthly allowance all ran out during the run. Rows marked "excerpt" were read through Firecrawl's government search, which returns the document's own text; nothing rests on a third-party summary.
