  1. "A defeasance escrow could hold an iShares iBonds Treasury fund, so the ten are escrow-grade whether held as T-bonds or as iBond funds."

- **Problem class:** making a fixed schedule of payments count as already paid by owning default-free securities that mature in time (defeasance, a form of cash-flow dedication), with sufficiency shown on a no-reinvestment basis by an independent accountant. **Field that solved it first:** US municipal finance (advance refunding escrows, rated on the escrow alone; Fitch's "AAApre" notation in 2005, Moody's "#" sign), then commercial-mortgage securitisation (CMBS loan defeasance from the early 1990s), and now corporate and project debt (Fitch's 2026 scope; S&P's 2024 Iowa action).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KILL. No agency's escrow may hold a bond fund or ETF.**
  - **Fitch (2026)** requires "an explicit prohibition against investing in mutual funds and unit investment trusts" for substitute and reinvestment securities. Its eligible list is only "noncallable and/or nonprepayable" direct or US-guaranteed obligations, with some agency debt.
  - **S&P (Temporary Investments ¶16)** says an eligible investment "must have a fixed principal amount due at its maturity and cannot include any embedded options". An ETF share pays its net asset value and has no principal amount due (Keel reading). Collateral that must be sold to pay is valued under S&P's market value criteria (escrow criteria ¶23).
  - **Moody's (2020)** admits only one kind of fund: "SEC 2a-7 money market funds, provided that they are rated Aaa-mf". A bond ETF is not one (Keel reading).
  - **Law outside munis says the same.** CMBS defeasance must use "government securities", meaning securities "issued or guaranteed ... by the United States" (15 U.S.C. 80a-2(a)(16); 26 CFR 1.860G-2(a)(8)). A fund's share is issued by the fund (Keel reading).
  - **For Laura (Table 1, NUMBERS l.65).**
    - These pass every agency's asset list: the five T-bonds (4.750% Feb 2037, 4.500% May 2038, 4.375% Nov 2039, 4.250% Nov 2040, 3.125% Nov 2041), for the 2038-2042 payments.
    - These would fail it: the five iBond funds for 2033-2037 (IBTM, IBTO, IBTP, IBTQ, IBTR), the floor fund IBTM bought in 2028, $4,521 of the backstop, and the Treasury-bill ETF that holds waiting money. NUMBERS 2 already says so.
    - The funds were forced for 2033-2036, because WInS listed no T-bond maturing Feb 2031 to Feb 2036. For 2037 they were a choice (SKELETON 2.2, CANON D13).
    - The plan answers with arithmetic, not an asset list. It runs the 0% test on each fund's own holdings at its fee (NUMBERS 8a), holds the backstop of extra Treasuries, and watches for fund events (Table 3 row 4).
    - Never call an iBond fund's payout fixed. The exclusion test is the property TNA note 2 already names: "their terminal payout is a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par".
  - **Judge's note:** the line that keeps a bond fund out of every rated escrow, "a fixed principal amount due at its maturity", is the line Caplet drew in its own Trading Note before reading any rating criteria.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, with the wording fixed on Fri 13 Nov.**
  - **The three agencies.**
    - S&P: "potential reinvestment earnings are not considered" (¶23).
    - Fitch: CPA reports "without regard to reinvestment earnings". In its words, "all verification agents determine escrow sufficiency without regard to any future reinvestment earnings".
    - Moody's: "without further reinvestment".
  - **CMBS uses the same test.** Fitch (2001): "sufficient to timely meet all scheduled mortgage payments without reinvestment, including the balloon payment".
  - **What counts.** Every agency counts each security's scheduled coupons and principal and excludes only the reinvestment of that cash. That is why "if interest earns nothing" was replaced by "if cash waiting earns nothing" (WORDS l.24).
  - **Laura's test is stricter in one way.** It also charges the bill fund's 0.15% fee on every waiting dollar (NUMBERS 2).
  - **It is weaker in three ways.** No CPA certifies it, nothing is irrevocable, and the IPS keeps two reasons to change. So the report says "the same arithmetic, not the same legal standing" (WORDS l.24, card Q31).
- **Claim 3: CHANGE.** The escrow agreement's list decides what counts; the CPA checks the arithmetic on whatever the list allows.
  - **Agency debt.** Wisconsin's 2015 escrow defines its securities as "the direct noncallable obligations of the United States of America and the Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation". It held $16,476,396 of SLGS at 0.010%, $292,270,954 of open-market securities and $922.56 of cash. Fitch admits senior Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac debt, FHLB, Farm Credit and REFCORP.
  - **Money funds and deposits.** Moody's 2020 typical lists add Aaa-mf money funds and P-1 bank deposits.
  - **Small escrows.** Kentucky's school commission allows bank CDs and collateralised repos.
  - **Never a fund.** No list found admits a bond fund or ETF.
  - **Suggested NUMBERS wording:** "an escrow holds only securities that promise a fixed sum on a fixed date (direct Treasuries, some agency debt), short top-rated money funds and cash; never a bond fund".
- **Claim 4: KILL, both halves.** Escrows are rated as the US itself, and every cut so far changed a label, not a dollar.
  - **S&P, August 2011.** It cut "thousands of pre-refunded bonds with escrows of Treasuries to AA-plus from AAA" and more than 11,000 issues tied to the US.
  - **Fitch, August 2023.** It cut pre-refunded bonds "wholly dependent on 'AA+'-rated United States government and agency obligations" (about $3.5 billion, Bank of America's estimate).
  - **Moody's, May 2025.** Escrow-backed debt went to Aa1 from Aaa, with "no changes to the structural elements or cash flows".
  - **Covenants ask for "direct obligations", not "AAA".** No sale was forced (Bond Buyer, 2011). On 1 Aug and 8 Aug 2011 pre-refunded yields sat below the AAA municipal scale (2021 maturity 2.67% then 2.38%, against 2.86% then 2.68%; Cumberland).
  - **Judge's note:** a company's 'BBB-' bonds became 'AA+' the day Treasuries were owned against each payment (S&P, Iowa, 2024). When the US was cut, those escrows fell one notch, and not one payment changed.
- **Claim 5: KILL.** In real escrows, 0% is often the law's requirement, not a stress.
  - **The rule.** For a refunding escrow, a yield "materially higher" than the bonds' yield means more than "one-thousandth of 1 percentage point" (26 CFR 1.148-2(d)(2)(ii)).
  - **How escrows meet it.** Issuers "blend down" the yield by putting money in zero-interest SLGS. In an IRS sample, 44 of 51 escrows (86%) were funded wholly with SLGS, "either all interest bearing or a combination of zero interest" and interest-bearing. The IRS letter says some escrows "require that their escrow funds earn no interest for a specified period".
  - **What this adds to Q20 row 21.** That row has the Austin 0% clause; this gives the reason for it and how common it is.
  - **Judge's note:** a professional escrow is often required by tax law to let its waiting cash earn nothing. Laura's "even if cash waiting earns nothing" copies a world where it happens on purpose.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 27 (zero-interest SLGS by law), for 3a.** It answers the bond reader's "why 0%?" with a fact: real escrows are often required to let waiting cash earn nothing, so Laura's standard copies practice and is not a scare.
2. **Row 38 (BBB- to AA+ on defeasance), for 3.0 and 3d.** It is the cleanest picture of the governing thought: once Treasuries are owned against each payment, the promiser no longer matters. A funder sees why Laura's career risk does not reach the payments.
3. **Rows 6 and 8 (downgrades cut the label, not the cash), for 3e and Table 3 T11.** They give the rater's own words, "no changes to the structural elements or cash flows", behind "a downgrade is not a default".
4. **Row 3 with row 45 (fixed principal against net asset value), for 2.2.** A rater's eligibility line is the same line Caplet's TNA drew. It explains the T-bond/fund split and is coherence a judge can check across all three deliverables.
5. **Row 2 (reinvestment not counted; sold collateral at market value), for 3c.** It maps both halves of the plan onto one rater's paragraph: the rungs at 0%, and the floor fund valued on what its holdings pay and cut 0.5% because it pays a net asset value.
