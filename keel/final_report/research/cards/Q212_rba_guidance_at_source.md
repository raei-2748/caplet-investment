## Verdicts in one screen

1. **Claim 1: KEEP the parallel, CHANGE the wording.** Three things are wrong with the current wording.
   - **"Its 2022 review" is ambiguous.** The RBA published three reviews in 2022: the yield target in June, the bond purchases in September, forward guidance on 15 November.
   - **Only the November review says "promise".** Its words, section 3, PDF p.14: "The fact that many people interpreted the forward guidance as 'a promise' that there would be no rate raises until 2024 led to considerable reputational damage to the Bank. When the cash rate was increased in May 2022, many people saw the Bank as having broken 'its promise'" (row 1).
   - **The June review says the opposite about guidance.** It calls forecasts and time-based guidance "by their very nature conditional". It keeps the word "commitment" for the yield target (row 3).
   - The independent Review (March 2023, p.52) heard it from the public: "many in the public understood this to be a commitment ... a 'broken promise'".
   - **So write it as** "the Reserve Bank of Australia's own review of its forward guidance (November 2022)". "Promise" is always what "many people" heard, never what the Bank did. The Governor at the time: "I haven't pledged anything" (row 4).
2. **Claim 2: KEEP, and it is stronger at source than in Q94.**
   - **The speaker's text was right and it still lost.** Official Hansard, 28 Nov 2022: "we never said that we wouldn't raise rates until 2024 ... it was a conditional statement ... I accept that people interpreted that as that we wouldn't raise rates until 2024, but that's not what we said". And: "My language was always caveated ... The community heard 2024; they didn't hear the conditionality" (rows 5-6).
   - **Two later softenings did not move the anchor.** In July 2021 the date became the Bank's "central scenario". On 16 Nov 2021 the Governor named the upside case: "there would be a case to lift the cash rate before 2024" (row 9).
   - **The independent Review then ruled out the Bank's own cure.** The Bank's fix was clearer caveats. The Review: "messages intended for the public cannot rely on detailed caveats given that they may not be well understood" (p.62, row 11).
3. **Claim 3: KEEP, with the layer it leaves out. Before the move Laura stands exactly where the RBA stood. After it she stands where no central bank can.**
   - **Before the move, her top is like the Bank's date.** It hangs on something she does not control: VT's price on the move day, as the Bank's date hung on inflation. A top named 12 months early would exceed what the move buys in 10.6% of house paths (NUMBERS 4).
   - **So the RBA's own rule applies to her first layer: publish the rule and the risks, not the figure.** The rule: "leave it to the market to work out expected timing by combining forecasts ... with the Board's decision-making framework". The Bank "does not intend to publish its own forecasts of the expected policy path" (row 14).
   - **For Laura that means three things before 2031.**
     - The odds (about 91 in 100 for at least $100,000, about 57 for $115,000) belong to the judge in 3c, dated "from today".
     - The residency's planners hear only the held bottom, "at least $95,000" (D12).
     - No co-sponsor hears a top.
   - **After the move she does what the RBA could not.** No asset pays an inflation path, but a Treasury pays a dollar figure on a date. Her stated figure is what Treasuries held for her will pay (row 15).
4. **Claim 4: the Swiss National Bank fits best; the Fed second; the Bank of Canada to the store.**
   - **The SNB's words were as hard as words get.** It said it would enforce CHF 1.20 per euro "with the utmost determination and is prepared to buy foreign currency in unlimited quantities". There was no end date and no exit condition in the release (row 17).
   - **It reaffirmed the commitment four weeks before ending it.** Twenty-eight days earlier: "Without the minimum exchange rate, price stability in Switzerland would be seriously compromised". Then on 15 Jan 2015 it was "no longer justified", with "immediate effect" (rows 18-19). The exit had to be a surprise: "even the slightest hint ... would have triggered massive speculation" (row 20).
   - **The pair closes the argument from both sides.** Soft words were heard as hard (RBA). The hardest words broke anyway (SNB). What holds a stated figure is the money already set aside, not the wording.
   - **Where the SNB breaks for Laura.** Its promise was about a market price. It needed buying every day at an open-ended cost, "potentially to a level several times Swiss GDP". Laura's promise is about a payout. It needs only that she not sell, at a cost already paid (row 22).
   - **The Fed fits the form of her bottom, not its reason.** Its "at least through" dates were all kept: liftoff came on 16 Dec 2015, after "mid-2013", "late 2014" and "mid-2015". But a slow economy kept them, not money held (row 24).
   - **The report allows one parallel per part.** So 3c keeps the RBA alone. The SNB goes to the store as the RBA's partner, for a judge's question or 3e's notes.
5. **Claim 5: the sentence and the banned words.**
   - **Candidate sentence (content for the writers).** "In February 2021 the Reserve Bank of Australia said it did not expect the conditions for raising interest rates to be met 'until 2024 at the earliest'; when it raised them in May 2022, its own review found that 'many people interpreted the forward guidance as "a promise"' and saw the Bank as having broken it." The turn to Laura can follow in her terms: she states her range only once Treasuries held for her will pay both ends, so nothing in it is left to forecast.
   - **Never write:**
     - "the RBA promised", "broke its promise" or "pledge" in the report's own voice;
     - "its 2022 review" without saying which one;
     - "expected" for forward rates or for Laura's range (say "implied by today's prices", Q205);
     - "95%" or "confidence interval";
     - "apologised" unless the Hansard's words are quoted;
     - any mention of the team's home beside it (Q94 row 40).

**Judge's notes (aha candidates):**
- **A. The Governor refused the word "pledge"; Laura signs one.** In Feb 2021 the Governor said "Can I just take an issue with the word pledge? I haven't pledged anything ... I'm not pledging, but I'm giving you my best guess". The public heard a pledge anyway. Laura can sign a pledge letter because Treasuries held for her will pay it. That is 3c's two layers in one exchange: a best guess with "a high degree of confidence" before the move, a pledge after it (row 4).
- **B. The Bank's text was right and it still lost.** "That's not what we said" was true, and the Review still found its public credibility "significantly affected". Being right on the page does not protect a figure. Only having the money does (rows 5, 12).
- **C. The RBA's own June 2022 review drew 3c's line itself.** Forecasts are "by their very nature conditional". A commitment tied to a dated bond was "understood ... as a strong commitment ... until the time that bond matured". The RBA's dated commitment broke because it promised a bond's price along the way. Laura's promises only what a dated Treasury fund pays at the end (rows 3, 13).
- **D. Determination is not money.** The SNB's "utmost determination" and "unlimited quantities" lasted 28 days after their last restatement (rows 17-19).
