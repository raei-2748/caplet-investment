**M1, "a copout" (about 220 words in 3f).**
- The detail that carries it: the bar existed five days before the objection, and gold with property came within a
  hair of it (0.8991 against 0.90) and still failed, because the bar said every run. Never write that a test was run to
  settle the argument: the test came first (card Q21, X100).
- The team's own lines: "We decided, in the end, to keep the one world fund (VT)" (Experience tab). The 5 Oct journal
  entry: "Outcome and reasons: [fill in]". The word "copout" is the objection's own.
- Order: objection, bar, how close gold came, then three of RANKING_4 section 3's reasons as what the team understands
  now (rows 3 and 19). One sentence of evidence at most (row 11 or Q46 rows 2-5).
- Avoid: naming who objected ("a teammate"); "devil's advocate" (row 18); TN3's "vain illusion" clause; any return of EWT,
  GLD or VT since the trade; "optimal"; VT as a hedge for the Taiwan dollar.

**M2, the first plan failed its own test (about 130 words).**
- The detail that carries it: one simulated market in thirty, two in five if one deposit slipped (27 Sep model:
  3.2%, 13.7%, 40.8%), and the weight argued over worth about $2,000.
- The team's own line: "first model of our original plan ... We changed strategy." (Experience tab).
- Reconcile with IPS l.9, which gives a writer's income as the reason for the same switch. Both are dated that weekend.
  Tell this moment as the test that made the instinct fail in numbers; the career idea stays in 2.3.
- Literature: row 9's title says both turns at once (a bold forecast from the inside view, then the charge of
  timidity); row 8 says why the median is not the test.
- Avoid: counting paths; "we were wrong" kickers; time-diversification jargon without its plain gloss.

**M3, buying it (about 130 words; one clause in 2.5).**
- The detail that carries it: the 00:30 open (one clause), a cancel with no confirmation step, and two bond prices a
  full point of yield off the curve (111 and 92 basis points, screen readings of 29-30 Sep).
- The team's own lines: the journal, "our time is freaking 12:30 AM that the market would open" (quote or paraphrase
  as Caplet's voice wants); the Experience tab, "An artificially low yield would mean immediately overpaying ... an
  artificially high yield would probably signal extreme underlying illiquidity" (that paragraph also holds "optimal":
  never quote that part).
- Tell what happened, not who did it: "a click landed on the wrong row as the page scrolled"; "a teammate fell asleep";
  "only the team leader places or cancels orders". Row 20 is the frame; row 21 if one source.
- The fills are luck (rows 5 and 7). Never claim timing as skill (NUMBERS 8b: "luck of timing, not skill").
- Avoid: the Nov-2040 note's price analysis (a Trading Note); the IBTR choice (2.2); more than one clause on 00:30.

**Craft for all three.**
- Concreteness (row 23): one physical, checkable detail per moment, never a summary adjective ("rigorous", "careful").
- Hindsight (row 3): verbs of finding, dated ("on 27 Sep the model showed"), not of realising ("we came to see"); a

- Reflection without cliché: no "journey", no "this taught us that", no one-line endings, no slogan flips; each moment
  ends on the part of the plan it explains (the shares, the order of claims, the implementation table) and on what is
  still unknown.
