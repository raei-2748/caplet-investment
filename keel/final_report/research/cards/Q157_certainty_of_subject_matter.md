- **Problem class:** when does a buyer who has paid own something, rather than hold a promise of it? This is certainty of subject matter: a court must be able to say which property is yours before it can be yours. **Field that solved it first:** the English law of sale of goods ("the very nature of things", Blackburn's 1845 treatise, quoted in Goldcorp) and of trusts (Knight v Knight, 1840). Commercial statute then moved the line: UCC 2-105(4) for goods in a bulk, Revised Article 8 (1994) for securities held through brokers, and the UK's Sale of Goods Act s.20A (1995). Insolvencies were the tests: Goldcorp (1988-1994), Lehman (2008) and MF Global (2011).

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE. The first half holds. "Identified" is wrong for Laura. "The same line as pencil and ink" holds for only half of ink's definition.**
  - **A promise to deliver from someone's general stock is a debt (KEEP).**
    - Goldcorp's customers bought gold "on a non-allocated basis". The brochure promised a "certificate of ownership" and metal "stored in a vault on your behalf". Peat Marwick audited the stocks monthly "to ensure there are sufficient stocks to meet all commitments".
    - The Privy Council held: "common sense dictates that the buyer cannot acquire title until it is known to what goods the title relates", and "It makes no difference what the parties intended if what they intend is impossible" (rows 3-8).
    - The bank's charge exceeded all the company's assets, so the more than 1,000 customers "will receive nothing at all".
    - London Wine's buyers held certificates calling them "sole and beneficial owner". They owned nothing, even though the stocks were enough for every customer (row 11).
    - The bullion market still runs on this line. An unallocated account holder "is an unsecured creditor"; more than 90% of wholesale metal clears that way, for "Convenience" (rows 39-40).
  - **"Identified" is wrong for Laura (KILL that word).**
    - Her Treasuries are not identified bonds. The Code's official comment says brokers "generally do not segregate securities in such fashion that one could identify particular securities as the ones held for customers".
    - It also says "A security entitlement is not a claim to a specific identifiable thing" (UCC 8-503 comments 1-2; rows 30-32).
    - What makes them property is a pool held apart for customers, which the holder may not use for itself and must keep full. That is Goldcorp's own winning group, the Walker & Hall claimants, whose bullion was "stored en masse, but ... still kept separate from the vendor's own stock" (rows 9-10).
    - It is also Lehman's losing affiliates in reverse: securities LBIE was free to lend and use gave "only ... personal rights" (row 25). And it is Knight v Knight's 1840 test (row 2).
    - The UCC turned exactly what Goldcorp's customers had not bought, "a shifting proportion of a shifting bulk", into the definition of property: "a pro rata property interest" (rows 6, 31-32).
  - **"The same line as pencil and ink" (CHANGE).** The law's line is owned against owed, which matches ink's word "owned".
    - The definition has a second half, "in Treasuries that will pay it on its date", and the property cases do not draw that line.
    - VT is owned property in law and pencil in the conceit. Co-sponsors' pledges, the 2028 deposit and her next advance are promises: pencil in both (row 42).
    - Safe form for the notes: "the law has drawn ink's word 'owned' for centuries. A promise to deliver from someone's stock is a debt. A share of a pool held apart for you is yours."
  - **Judge's note:** Goldcorp's customers held printed certificates naming them the "registered holder", backed by a monthly audit, and owned nothing. Laura holds no certificate at all, only a book entry at her broker, and owns hers. The paper was pencil; the entry is ink (row 44).
- **Claim 2: KEEP. "Held for her" is the statute's own phrase. "In her name" names a different and stronger legal status that she does not have.**
  - The Code's words: the broker's holdings of each security "are held by the securities intermediary for the entitlement holders" (8-503(a)).
  - "In her name" means on the issuer's register. "The legal owner of the shares is the person in whose name the shares are registered: that is the position in English, Australian, and US law" (Re Harvard Securities; row 24).
  - SIPA gives securities "registered in the name of the customer" their own class, "customer name securities". The trustee "shall deliver" them to the customer, and they are kept out of the shared pool of customer property (row 36).
  - Laura's T-bonds and fund shares sit at her broker, which holds them through the Fed and DTC. They are customer property, shared pro rata if the pool were short. To say "in her name" would claim the stronger status for her.
  - Store Q77-003 should be reworded.
- **Claim 3: KEEP "owned", with three limits that keep it true.**
  - The drafters' own words: a security entitlement is "a property interest, and not a contract claim". The term "was coined to describe this new form of ownership" (Uniform Law Commission, 2024; row 34).
  - **The limits:**
    - It is a share of a pool, not her own bonds (8-503(b)).
    - She enforces it only against her own broker (8-503(c); "no look through" in England, row 41).
    - In a broker failure, SIPA shares all customer property "in proportion to the dollar value of their total positions, rather than dividing the property on an issue by issue basis" (row 37). The shortfall lands on SIPC's top-up first, and history says delay, not loss (store Q72-012, Q72-013, NUMBERS 8t).
  - The two exceptions in 8-511 reach only securities a customer agreed to pledge, such as a margin loan. "No individual investor has ever suffered a loss" through them (row 35). The IPS borrows nothing.
  - **Never say:** "her own bonds", "registered owner", "legal owner" or "identified".
- **Claim 4: CHANGE, slightly. The click is a binding contract, and "there's no rubbing it out" is true. Ownership arrives a business day later.**
  - A security entitlement arises when the broker "indicates by book entry that a financial asset has been credited" to her account (8-501(b)(1)).
  - "Settlement" is "the official transfer of securities to the buyer's account" (Investor.gov). That has been T+1 for ETFs and bonds since 28 May 2024.
  - Briggs J: "the earliest date for the passing of title from the street is the settlement date rather than the trade date" (rows 27-29).
  - The 2.5 turn stays as written, about a click that cannot be undone. Never "owned the moment she clicked".
- **Claim 5: CHANGE. What protected customers was a rule that the pool is held for them and kept full, not the physical act of separation.**
  - Walker & Hall's bullion was pooled, not separated bar by bar, and it still counted as owned (row 9).
  - At Lehman's UK arm, money that had been segregated was shared with clients whose money never was. The trust "arises at time of receipt", and sharing ran on what should have been segregated (UK Supreme Court, 2012, 3-2; row 38).
  - SIPA likewise pools all customer property (row 37).
  - So Table 3's custody row watches the broker's duty (8-504, Rule 15c3-3: store Q72), not whether bonds sit in a separate vault.
- **One guard for 3d (row 2).** By Knight v Knight's test, money its holder may "apply ... to his own use" is not held on trust for anyone else. Laura may use her Treasuries as she likes (store Q3b-033), so they are hers, not the residency's or the co-sponsors'.
  - Never "held for the residency", "set aside for co-sponsors" or "secured" (WORDS).
  - Ink is what she owns and has stated, which is why IPS l.15 says "money you already own".

## Five questions for the strongest rows

1. **Goldcorp's two groups in one judgment (rows 3-10).**
   - *What is it?* A 1994 Privy Council advice on a failed New Zealand bullion dealer. More than 1,000 non-allocated buyers lost to the bank. The Walker & Hall buyers had their trial-judge win restored, because their bullion was pooled but kept apart, equal to what was owed, and held "as custodian".
   - *Did it work?* For Walker & Hall, yes, but their recovery "could not exceed the lowest balance of metal held by the company" after their rights arose. They "received the same certificates" as the losing buyers: the paper was the same, and only the stock held apart differed. The bank did not appeal their liability point, and the Board did not decide it afresh.
   - *How is Laura like it?* Her Treasuries are a pooled holding, held apart from her broker's own and held for customers: the Walker & Hall position, written into the UCC.
   - *How is she not?* Goldcorp's gold was a chattel sold by a dealer. Hers are securities under a statute with a duty to keep the pool full, a regulator and SIPC. No court had to imply anything.
   - *What Caplet takes:* the line is "held apart for you" against "owed to you from their stock", not "identified" against "unidentified".
2. **The certificate was pencil (rows 3, 11, 44).**
   - *What is it?* Goldcorp's "registered holder" certificates and London Wine's "sole and beneficial owner" certificates: printed ink that gave no ownership.
   - *Did it work?* No. Both groups were held to have contracts only.
   - *How is Laura like it?* She, too, is shown her holdings in a document, an account statement.
   - *How is she not?* Her statement records a credit that is itself the legal act (8-501(b)(1)). Theirs recorded a promise.
   - *What Caplet takes:* a strikingly dissimilar, true parallel for 2.0 that teaches "owned, not signed" without naming signing.
3. **The Code's comment to 8-503 (rows 30-32).**
   - *What is it?* The official comment on why the Code does not say "customers' securities".
   - *Did it work?* It has been the law in all 50 states for over a quarter-century (row 35). In Lehman's US broker, 111,000 customer claims were met in full (store Q72-016).
   - *How is Laura like it?* Her ten Treasuries and floor are exactly such entitlements.
   - *How is she not?* Nothing differs; the limit is the pro rata share in a failure.
   - *What Caplet takes:* "owned" without "identified".
4. **SIPA's customer name securities (row 36).**
   - *What is it?* The statute's separate class for securities registered in a customer's own name.
   - *Did it work?* They are delivered to the customer, outside the shared pool.
   - *How is Laura like it?* She is not like it. Her holdings are in street name.
   - *What Caplet takes:* the legal reason "in her name" overclaims, and the reason "held for her" is the accurate phrase.
5. **Pearson v Lehman Brothers Finance (rows 25-27).**
   - *What is it?* Briggs J (2010) on whether Lehman affiliates owned securities that LBIE held in its own house accounts.
   - *Did it work?* Before the RASCALS processes: no, "only ... personal rights", because LBIE was free to lend and use the securities.
   - *How is Laura like it?* Her broker may not use her fully paid securities (store Q72-011).
   - *How is she not?* An affiliate dealing with its own group is not a retail cash account.
   - *What Caplet takes:* a pool its holder may use for itself holds nothing for you. That is the same test as Knight's in 1840.

## Best five for the report

1. **Rows 9-10 with rows 3-5 (2.0, and 3e's custody sentence).** One 1994 judgment holds both sides of the line. The non-allocated buyers, with certificates and a monthly audit, owned nothing. The Walker & Hall buyers, whose bullion was pooled but held apart and held for them, owned theirs, up to what was left in the pool. That is Laura's position, written into US law, and it shows why "identified" is the wrong word and "held for her" the right one.
2. **Row 44 (2.0, the conceit).** The certificate was pencil and the entry is ink. It is a strikingly dissimilar parallel with a startling turn: the people holding paper owned nothing, and the person holding only a screen entry owns hers. It sharpens "owned" against "signed" without saying "signed". It waits for a cold test.
3. **Rows 30-32 with row 6 (2.0 or 3e).** Goldcorp refused to treat "a shifting proportion of a shifting bulk" as property. Twenty years later the UCC defined a broker's customer's property as exactly that, "a pro rata property interest", "not a claim to a specific identifiable thing". A judge who assumes "owned" means "her own bonds" has it backwards.
4. **Row 36 with row 23 (wording, everywhere).** In law, "in her name" means registered to her, and SIPA treats such securities as a separate, stronger class. Laura's are not. "Held for her" is the statute's phrase (8-503(a)). This settles WORDS' rule with a source, and store Q77-003 should change.
5. **Row 2 with row 25 (3d's guard).** A holder free to use the property for itself holds nothing for anyone else: Knight, 1840; Lehman's house accounts, 2010. Laura's Treasuries are hers and not the co-sponsors', so 3d never says "set aside for the residency". IPS l.15's "money you already own" is the exact legal claim.
