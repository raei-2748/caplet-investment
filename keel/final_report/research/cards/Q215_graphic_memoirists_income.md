## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** "Later and smaller" fits the new money: advances, events, school budgets and licences. It does not fit the big pieces, which keep their own calendar.
  - **New money, later and smaller.** In 2020 Yang cancelled an eight-stop tour (row 5). Craft's Beijing, Dubai and ALA trips went (row 22). Comic-Con was cancelled for the first time in 50 years (row 26). Scholastic's US book fairs fell 57% in the year to May 2021 (row 21). In 2009, 54% of school librarians expected cuts (row 27).
  - **Big pieces, on their own clock.** Persepolis took off after 9/11, in the 2001-02 share slump (row 11). The Persepolis film was pre-sold in 2006 and released in 2007-08 (row 12). New Kid won the Newbery in January 2020 and more than doubled its sales (row 22). *Maus* jumped 8.5 times in a week in 2022, when shares fell 18.04% (row 16). Fun Home's musical, the MacArthurs, Smile, Guts and the Disney+ series all came in booms (rows 4, 8-10, 18; row 30).
  - **Judge's note:** in 2022, a year shares fell 18%, *Maus* had its biggest week in years. A school board did that, not the market.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, strongly.** One book contract was worth "a decade of my comic strip" (Bechdel, row 7). A first volume of 16,000 copies became two million (Satrapi, row 11). Yang had ten years of losses before 2006 (row 1). Guts sold a third less in each of its second and third years (row 19). The worst S&P year in the Damodaran file since 2000 is -36.55%.
- **Claim 3: KILL "countercyclical"; CHANGE to "its own weather".**
  - **What grew.** Kids' graphic novels grew 13% in 2020, 15% in the book channel (row 24), and children's digital library loans rose 79% (row 25). In 2009 Graphix rose 24.8% (Q108 row 5).
  - **What fell.** The money schools pay fell in both shocks: book fairs -57% in FY2021 (row 21), school library budgets in 2009-12 (row 27; Q12 rows 19-21). One creator's backlist fell 7.4% in 2020 while her shelf grew (row 20).
  - No stream rose because shares fell (row 32).
- **Claim 4: KEEP.** In 2020 events stopped outright: tours, festivals and cons (rows 5, 22, 26; Q1 row 33). Before 2009-10, budgets were expected to fall (row 27). **Add:** they also fall without a recession. In October 2026 an agency for 80+ children's authors reported far fewer 2026-27 school visits, because of enrolment, insurance and book-ban politics (row 28).
  - **Judge's note:** New Kid won the Newbery in January 2020. Its sales more than doubled that year, and every trip its author had planned was cancelled.
- **Claim 5: CHANGE (narrowly).** Keep "later and smaller", background risk and the Census -7.8%. Add that the career is lumpy and that its big pieces keep their own calendar. The reference class shows most of a graphic memoirist's risk is her own: one book, a prize, a licence, a ban. Background risk theory says an independent risk still argues for holding fewer shares (Q206 rows 1-2). So 2.3 needs no claim that her book income falls with shares. **Exact sentence, row 35.**
  - **Judge's note:** all six paid for the book that made them with other work: a teacher's salary, a bubble-gum company, another author's series, office jobs, children's covers, a one-person press. Creators spread their careers before they spread their portfolios (row 29).
- **The disanalogy, kept in view.** Famous names are a survivor sample, and none of them is Laura. The case gives her a bestselling memoir, classroom readers and a deposit built from advances, speaking and licensing. The class is used only to show how such money arrives, never to guess hers.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 33 (2.3, 3e area 2).** The case funds the deposit from advances, speaking and licensing, which are exactly the streams that come later and smaller. So IPS l.9's list is the right one, and 2.3 can say why.
2. **Row 29 (2.3).** All six creators paid for their breakthrough with other work. It shows a career treated as a risky holding, from creators' own conduct. A judge will not have seen it.
3. **Row 22 (2.3, 3e).** In one creator's 2020, book money more than doubled while every trip was cancelled. It sharpens "later and smaller" to the event stream, beside Laura's own 2020 publication (Q1 row 46).
4. **Row 16 (2.3).** *Maus* rose 8.5 times in a week in a falling market, because of a school board. This is the lumpy, own-clock risk in one dated number.
5. **Row 21 (3e area 2).** For classroom authors, the school channel fell 57% in the shock year. It is the plainest dated example of money schools pay arriving later and smaller.

## Gaps

- None of the six publishes yearly income, so payment dates are inferred from event dates (books, prizes, opening nights). Fun Home's, American Born Chinese's, Smile's and New Kid's advances are not public.
- Speaking fees for these names are hidden behind bureaus. No survey counts author school visits in 2009-10. The 2009 evidence is budgets (row 27; Q1 row 34; Q12 rows 19-21).
- Per-title BookScan figures for 2008-10 for these authors sit behind ICv2's paid Pro site. Telgemeier's and Craft's figures here come only from PW's public lists of books above 100,000.
- Satrapi's share of the film's revenue, and Persepolis's sales by year, were not found.
- The Atlantic's 2022 piece on bans and schools' bulk buying was blocked to the fetch tool. Only a one-line extract was read, and no row rests on it.
- Comichron's own pages for 2008-09 were not re-read. Q108 has ICv2's figures for those years.
