## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE. The log binds by signature and date, so it is the conceit's disanalogy, not a parallel.**
  - **Today's US Navy rule is ballpoint, not "ink" in the craft sense, and there is no fair copy.** OPNAVINST 3100.7C (24 Feb 2014, the current instruction; Naval History and Heritage Command says preparation "is governed by the current edition"): "Make all manual entries in the ship's deck log with a black ball point pen or typewritten", or electronically, or by voice recording. "Rewriting of OPNAV 3100/99 should not be required": the sheet written on watch is the original.
  - **Corrections.** "Draw a single line through the original entry so that the entry remains legible ... Only the individual required to sign the record for the watch will make corrections ... and that individual will initial in the left-hand margin." A late entry goes "on the next available line" with an asterisk at both places, never squeezed in at its hour. "No change shall be made in a log after it has been signed by the commanding officer, without the permission or direction of the commanding officer." OPNAVINST 3120.32D 4.13.1: "No erasures shall be made". So the claim's core holds, said precisely.
  - **Rough and smooth logs are history.** The US National Archives: a rough log is "a preliminary draft of a logbook"; the smooth log is "the final version ... and considered the official log"; "Changes can be made to rough logs but a smooth log is considered final and erasures are not permitted." No source found says the rough log was kept in pencil. In the age of sail the step was slate or chalk board to ink book: the mate showed "the information on the log slate to the master for any corrections or additions, after which he transcribed the data into the Logbook" (Mystic Seaport, Stein 1992).
  - **Merchant law, UK and US.** UK: "an entry shall not be amended or cancelled"; a correction is "a further entry referring to the entry and amending or cancelling it", signed; "Each entry shall include the date when it is made" (Merchant Shipping (Official Log Books) Regulations 1981, regs 8 and 4, as made). US: each entry "shall be made as soon as possible after the occurrence" and signed by the master and another seaman (46 U.S.C. 11302).
  - **Where it maps.** Rough to smooth is a draft becoming final (a decision), and every log binds by a signature: the "signed" reading the definition keeps out (Q42; Q114 claim 3). Laura's pencil is decided but not yet owned. One narrow writers' rule survives: the late entry. Each inking keeps its own date; the January 2028 top-up is never written as if bought in January 2027 (row 3).
  - **Judge's note:** the US Navy no longer makes a neat copy of its log. The sheet written on watch is the legal record because it was written at the time and signed, which is why a log is the wrong picture for Laura's ink: she signs nothing, she owns something.
- **Claim 2: KILL as stated; CHANGE to what the sources say.**
  - **The surveyor's binding record is pencil, by rule.** US Army FM 6-2: "Always use a sharpened pencil ... (3H or harder). Entries will **never** be made in ink." "Erasures are not permitted in the field notebook." An open surveying text: "A pencil is used for field notes to minimize smearing, not to allow erasing mistakes. A Surveyor's field notes are one of the few documents recorded in pencil allowed as evidence in court." Pencil is chosen because ink smears on wet field paper: for lasting, not for being provisional.
  - **The federal survey does have a pencil-to-ink step, but ink is not what binds.** The BLM's *Manual of Surveying Instructions* (2009) has three stages: field data, draft field returns, and the final official record, whose field notes are on "archival paper with permanent black ink" (9-14, under "Inking the Field Notes"). Yet "The survey is not official until it has been approved and accepted by the authorized official and officially filed" (9-12). What binds is filing, an authority's act.
  - **The one place ownership hardens the record.** The BLM's general rules: the lines "as surveyed ... are unchangeable after the passing of title by the United States". Land law again: it joins Q79-004 (Taiwan's register over the deed) as store, never a new turn.
  - **Judge's note:** in surveying the legal record must be pencil ("Entries will never be made in ink", US Army). The medium binds nothing anywhere; a rule does. That is why the conceit defines ink by owning, not by the medium.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE. Two of the three fields fail; hydrography and chart correction, found in this run, are the fields whose line matches Laura's.**
  - **Lab notebooks: KILL as a turn.** Permanent ink is the rule ("Entries should be made in permanent ink, not pencil", La Trobe University; "Write all entries in indelible ink", Idaho National Laboratory's university procedure). But plans are inked too: "New ideas and plans for experiments should be recorded. This is because the date of conception ... is important in establishing first to invent" (La Trobe). Lab ink means "dated and unaltered", not "done" or "owned". Set beside Laura, it would ink the 2031 move before it is made.
  - **Theatre: KILL as a turn.** Blocking and cues go in pencil "as things can (and do) change right up until the final performance" (ADC Theatre). At one US regional theatre, "After opening night, no further changes are made and the stage manager finalizes the prompt book"; the show is "frozen" after opening (Merrimack Repertory Theatre). A theatre plays to its first audience from pencil and fixes the book afterwards. Laura fixes first and shows co-sponsors only ink: the order is inverted.
  - **Hydrography: KEEP.** NOAA's *Hydrographic Manual*: the smooth sheet "is ultimately archived as the official permanent record of the survey and is the principal source and authority for charted hydrographic data". On it, each line waits in pencil for its own condition: "If an adjoining survey has not been completed or verified, pencil notes are used ... until such time as they can be inked"; dashed shorelines "left in pencil until the survey has been verified in its entirety. When verification has been completed, such shorelines are shown in ink."
  - **Chart correction: KEEP.** The Admiralty: "Only updates given in Section II of *Weekly Editions of Admiralty Notices to Mariners* should be used to correct any chart in ink. Updates ... from authorities other than the UKHO may be noted in pencil"; "permanent Notices in waterproof violet ink, Temporary and Preliminary Notices in pencil". A Preliminary Notice covers work that "will shortly be taking place (e.g. harbour developments)", and "A permanent chart updating NM will be promulgated ... when the work is complete" (*The Mariner's Handbook*, NP100, 2.69 and 4.29). The UKHO's own weekly notices: "Information from these notices is not included on charts before issue; charts should be updated in pencil on receipt."
  - **Judge's note:** on every Admiralty chart, a harbour wall that has been approved and is being built stays in pencil until it is finished. Pencil there means "not yet there", not "not yet decided". The law's pencil means the opposite (probate reads pencil as "deliberative", Q114), and that is the reading the conceit had no answer to.
- **Claim 4: KEEP for four of the five steps; no exact fact for her flexibility.**
  - **A figure stated only once owned (2.0).** EXACT twice. The Admiralty inks a chart only from one source and lets any other be noted in pencil (row 26): Laura's ink comes only from a Treasury she owns that pays on its date, and every other source (a forecast, a share price, the 2028 deposit before it arrives) is pencil. And the UKHO tells mariners to write a correction's number in the chart's corner "only ... once you have applied the correction and not before" (row 30): the line other readers rely on is written after the work is done.
  - **Every end rounded down (3b).** EXACT. IHO S-4, B-412 (edition 4.10.0, March 2026): "Rounding of depths ... must always be on the safe (shoaler) side (that is: soundings must be rounded down ...)"; "a recorded depth ... of 4,38m rounds down to 4,3m". Laura's unrounded top of $117,142 is stated as $115,000 and the bottom's $97,605 as $95,000 (NUMBERS, Tue 17 Nov evening re-size). The figure printed for someone else to rely on goes to the side that cannot hurt them.
  - **The two inkings (3c).** EXACT on structure. NOAA's smooth sheet inks each pencil line when its own condition is met (row 15); the Admiralty pencils approved works and inks them "when the work is complete" (row 27). Most of each payment is inked in January 2027; the rest and the bottom in January 2028, once the deposit arrives; the top before the 2031 meetings, once the move is made. The condition differs (verification or completion there, owning here); the shape is the same.
  - **Her flexibility, never inked.** No exact fact. The nearest, temporary notices pencilled all their life and rubbed out when cancelled (row 28), fail: a temporary notice is pencil because it will lapse. Her flexibility is pencil because no Treasury pays it and it is promised to no one, and it stays hers. NOAA's place names, pencilled until the Chief Geographer approves them (row 18), fail on "never": names are inked once approved.
  - **Erase every pencil and the page still reads (3e).** The chart does it (temporary and preliminary notices rubbed out, the chart still current for every permanent notice, row 28), but Adams (1872, Q114 row 1) and the comics fact already carry it. Nothing new.
  - **Judge's note:** rounding every stated end down is not timidity. Every nautical chart made to the international standard rounds every depth down, because a ship steers by the printed number; and the same standard warns that "The cartographer must not err too much on the side of caution" (B-417), which is why the rule rounds once and holds back only 0.5%.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 27 (3c, 3.0): on Admiralty charts, approved works in progress stay in pencil until they are finished.** Pencil means "not yet there", not "not yet decided". This answers the one disanalogy the law left open (Q114: probate reads pencil as undecided) and it is the true fact behind "inked before the 2031 meetings".
2. **Row 22 (3b): every printed depth is rounded down, by international rule (IHO S-4 B-412).** It is the exact craft fact for "every end rounded down" ($117,142 stated as $115,000), and it gives a judge the aha that rounding down is how anyone who prints a figure for others to steer by behaves.
3. **Row 26 (2.0): only one authority's notices may ink an Admiralty chart; anything else may be noted in pencil.** It is the definition (ink only from a Treasury she owns, every other source pencil) found intact in a strikingly dissimilar field.
4. **Row 15 (3c): NOAA's official survey sheet keeps a line in pencil "until such time as [it] can be inked", once the work it depends on is complete.** It is the true fact behind "two inkings, two dates": one record, each line inked on its own date.
5. **Row 31 (3d): the chart the office prints carries permanent notices only; temporary and preliminary ones are the user's own pencil.** The printed page shows only ink outside comics too, so 3d's turn rests on two fields, not one.

## Gaps

- **Royal Navy:** the current rule for the RN's deck log (the "Ship's Log", S.322, or Queen's/King's Regulations) was not found; the UK merchant regulation stands in for it. The 1981 Official Log Books Regulations were read as made; later amendments and current status were not checked.
- **Pencil rough logs:** no source found that US or British rough logs were kept in pencil. The age-of-sail sources say slate or chalk board, and one says paper (row 5, CONFLICTING).
- **Admiralty wording today:** *The Mariner's Handbook* (NP100) was read only as c. 2009 extracts in a training institute's reproduction, and *How to Keep Your ADMIRALTY Products Up-to-Date* (NP294) was not read. The UKHO's own Weekly Edition 35/2024 and leisure guide confirm the pencil rule for temporary and preliminary notices and the corner note; the next round should read the current NP100 or NP294.
- **Music engraving** (the composer's pencil and the engraved plate; Beethoven's pencil pocket sketchbooks) was not reached. Nor was a NIST or state-board rule (for example, a county's rule that recorded plats be in permanent black ink).
- **Read only in search extracts of the documents' own text** (not opened in full): OPNAVINST 3120.32D, 46 U.S.C. 11302-11303, NARA's Introduction to Navy Deck Logs, Turnbull, Rhodes (Met Office 1995), USFWS, AOLS, EN0591, the NOAA AFSC memo, La Trobe, INL, IU, UPRM, ADC Theatre, CCSU and The Complete Stage Manager.
