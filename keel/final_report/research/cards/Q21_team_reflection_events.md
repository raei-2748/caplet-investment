## (b) Cards: the five strongest prior-art ideas

### Card 1. Wharton's own test: "influenced its final recommendation", and nothing from the Trading Notes (rows 1-4)
- **What is it?** This year's instructions ask 3f to show how research, collaboration, disagreements, decision-making
  and learning influenced the final recommendation; the criterion adds uncertainty; the Guide forbids redesign after
  results and asks for reasoning, assumptions and trade-offs.
- **Did it work?** It is the rule the report is scored on; no evidence of how judges apply it beyond the criteria text.
- **How is the team's case like it?** Most strong events end in a decision the IPS still carries: payments first
  (X076, X057), dated holdings (X013, X044), the floor's instrument (X003), VT kept (X100), the completion rule (X026,
  X028, X087), the owned range (X045).
- **How is it not?** Six of the 27 rows repeat, partly repeat or clash with the TNA (X013 clashes with TN1; X044,
  X100, X003, X059 and X097), and the ladder-cost events (X026, X028, X087, X032) restate the IPS's completion rule.
- **What Caplet takes:** each event names the decision it changed or held; anything already argued in TN1-TN3 is
  either left out or told only as the argument between people, never the analysis.

### Card 2. Description is not reflection: the level ladders (rows 13-16)
- **What is it?** Moon, Kember and Hatton and Smith each grade writing from plain description to critical reflection;
  at the bottom, ideas are "linked by the sequence of the account / story rather than by meaning"; at the top, the
  writer's own frame of reference changes.
- **Did it work?** Kember's four assessors reached "very good agreement"; Bell's coders reached 0.802, and found 65%
  of business students' journal content non-reflective.
- **How is the team's case like it?** Two events reach the top level, because the team's own assumption changed: the
  model that sank its own plan (X076) and the test of its own central claim (X001).
- **How is it not?** These are coursework rubrics, not Wharton's; a 1-1.5 page report has room for few events.
- **What Caplet takes:** order 3f by the argument (what the team believed, what broke it, what it now holds), not by
  date; the timeline belongs in the evidence, not the structure.

### Card 3. The after-action review: what, why, and half the words on what next (rows 18-20)
- **What is it?** The Army's review asks what happened, why, and what to sustain or improve; Garvin gives the last
  question about half the time; Gibbs warns that learners jump from description to conclusions without analysis.
- **Did it work?** It is standard practice in the US Army and copied by firms and agencies (USAID's guide); the time
  split is Garvin's recommendation, not a measured result.
- **How is the team's case like it?** Caplet's 2025-26 reflection (row 12) already did event plus rule; the journal's
  own template asks for "date, decision, options, reason, who argued what, what we'd do differently" (journal l.25).
- **How is it not?** The journal leaves "[who argued what]" (l.16) and "Outcome and reasons: [fill in]" (l.23) blank:
  the record cannot yet supply the "who" and "why" for most events.
- **What Caplet takes:** for each event, one line of what happened, one of why, and the rest on what it changed in the
  recommendation and what the team would do differently (without redesign).

### Card 4. The disorienting dilemma, and transformative against confirmatory learning (row 17)
- **What is it?** REFLECT scores whether the writing describes a conflict or "disorienting dilemma" and whether the
  learning changed the writer (transformative) or only confirmed what they thought (confirmatory).
- **Did it work?** Developed and tested on medical students' reflective writing; the paper itself was not read here.
- **How is the team's case like it?** The team's own plan sinking on its first model (X076), a former judge's verdict
  (X082) and the 2020 counter-example to the IPS's central claim (X001) are each a dilemma.
- **How is it not?** Its emotions criterion fits weakly; an investment report is not a personal journal.
- **What Caplet takes:** open the strongest event on the moment the team's view broke, and say plainly where the
  learning confirmed the plan (the payments) and where it changed the team's view (how far her career moves with
  shares).

### Card 5. Few things, remembered; reasoning over results (rows 5-7, 10)
- **What is it?** Wharton's own reports of finale judges: reasoning behind every decision distinguishes managers; "Cut
  slides, cut words, cut minutes"; teams that "said five things" were remembered; "less is more".
- **Did it work?** These are the judges and teams of winning years, quoted by Wharton; not a controlled comparison.
- **How is the team's case like it?** Caplet's plan is simple by design (one world fund, one ladder, two reasons to

- **What Caplet takes:** three or four events, each told fully with who, when, what changed and why, rather than a
