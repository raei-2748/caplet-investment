## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim (a): CHANGE. The substance is right; the quoted words are from the wrong work.**
  - **Whose words.** "An environment that is sufficiently regular to be predictable" and "an opportunity to learn these regularities through prolonged practice" are the two bullets of Kahneman's 2011 book, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, chapter 22 ("Expert Intuition: When Can We Trust It?"), introduced as "the two basic conditions for acquiring a skill" (row 1). A search of the 2009 paper's full text finds neither phrase.
  - **The 2009 paper's own words.**
    - p. 520: "Two conditions must be satisfied for skilled intuition to develop: an environment of sufficiently high validity and adequate opportunity to practice the skill."
    - p. 520: "Skilled intuitions will only develop in an environment of sufficient regularity, which provides valid cues to the situation."
    - p. 515 (abstract): judging an intuition "requires an assessment of the predictability of the environment in which the judgment is made and of the individual's opportunity to learn the regularities of that environment."
    - p. 524: the speed of feedback ("prolonged practice and feedback that is both rapid and unequivocal"; already Q227-001).
  - **A page fix for the store.** The stock-price passage and the fireground-commander sentence (Q227 rows 2 and 4, given as p. 517) are on p. 520 of the published article (row 3).
  - **Scope.** The paper is about skilled *intuition*. 3f's "judgement" is broader, which is fine in the team's words, but References should not make the paper say more.
  - **Judge's note:** the most quoted lines on when to trust an expert come from Kahneman's 2011 book. The 2009 paper with Klein says the same thing in its own words and adds the piece that matters most here: feedback must be "rapid and unequivocal".
- **Claim (b): KEEP, now read at source.**
  - **The source.** Shanteau, J. (1992), "Competence in experts: The role of task characteristics", *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 53(2), 252-266.
  - **The tables.** Table 1 (p. 258) puts "Stock brokers" on the poor-performance side and weather forecasters at the head of the good side. Nurses, physicians and auditors appear on both sides. Table 2 (p. 259) lists ten task features. Predictability and feedback are two of them; the others are static stimuli, decisions about things, agreed stimuli, errors allowed, repetition, objective analysis, decomposability and decision aids.
  - **Paraphrase errors in Kahneman and Klein.** Their summary (2009, p. 522) leaves weather forecasters out of the good list and calls "Student admissions" "college admissions officers", so quote Shanteau's table, not their summary (row 11).
  - **The useful new idea.** Shanteau's third hypothesis: poor-side tasks improve "if they can be made more like left-side tasks" (row 15). Laura's 2031 move does exactly that. It turns a forecast about shares into a reading of what Treasuries will pay, before she names a figure.
  - **Judge's note:** Shanteau's point is that competence belongs to the task, not the person: "experts cannot be described generically". The copout argument was never "are we good at investing?" but "which task is this?"
- **Claim (c): KEEP, with numbers, and with the horizon fixed.**
  - **The horizon.** The top Laura states rests on the half of VT moved on 1 January 2031. That is three years after the January 2028 purchase (IPS l.15; NUMBERS 8r). Only the half promised to no one runs five years (row 28).
  - **How big an edge is, and how hard to see.**
    - Fama and French: before costs the spread of true skill is about 1.25% a year, and fewer than 2.3% of funds have more than 2.5% a year. After costs, true alpha is "negative for most if not all active funds" (row 17).
    - Jiang and Zheng, on Fama and French's figures: showing a one-standard-deviation skill at the usual test takes "more than 100 years of return history" (row 19). Keel arithmetic on their inputs gives an expected t-statistic of 0.31 over 36 months and 0.40 over 60, against the 1.96 needed (row 20).
    - Kothari and Warner's standard test window is Laura's: 36 months. An edge of 3% a year, which "seems quite high and difficult to obtain", was detected only 59% of the time (row 21).
  - **Persistence.** In S&P's latest persistence scorecard, none of the 505 top-quartile US equity funds of 2021 stayed top quartile through 2025. Over three years, 33.01% of 2023's top quartile did, against 6.25% by chance (Keel arithmetic). S&P notes the 29% for large-cap funds was "higher than the 0% reported in 2024" (rows 24-25). A three-year read flips between editions.
  - **The mean itself.** Merton (1980): getting an accurate estimate takes "a very long history of returns"; its accuracy "depends only upon the total length of the observation period", and "sixteen years of data is not a long time series" (row 27).
  - **Judge's note:** a three-year window is too short to judge a picker, in either direction. The same S&P scorecard read over three years gave 0% one year and 29% the next. Finance's standard power study uses exactly that window and misses even a rare 3% edge about four times in ten.
- **Claim (d): STRENGTHENS the lesson, once two words change.**
  - **Why the break is real.** The ten Treasuries' payments do arrive slowly (2033-2042). Kahneman's harbour pilot works in a regular world but learns slowly "because of the long delay between actions and their noticeable outcomes" (row 5). So by the lesson's own words nobody could have learned to choose Treasuries well either (row 29).
  - **Why the choice still holds.** It needed no learned skill and no forecast. Under NUMBERS section 2's standard, all ten are paid "even if no coupon, fund payout or waiting dollar earns anything from 2027", after the waiting fund's fee, with every iBond fund flow 0.5% smaller and $1,177 to spare (row 30). Whether the match pays could be checked the day it was bought.
  - **Both halves sit where a rule beats a person.** Kahneman and Klein (2009, p. 524) say algorithms beat people at both ends: very low validity (choosing shares) and very high validity, "where ceiling effects are encountered and occasional lapses of attention can cause humans to fail" (the printed schedule of ten Treasuries). Laura's plan sits at both ends (row 32). The one-trader rule (2.5) is the guard against lapses of attention.
  - **The forecasts that were left.** Two small ones remained in the Treasury half: an iBond fund's payout is a net asset value (TNA l.15), and one bond's proceeds wait 10.5 months (TNA l.5). The plan bought them out rather than judging them: the extra Treasuries are sized as if waiting money earns nothing, and fund flows carry a 0.5% margin (row 34).
  - **Two words of goldmine 43 change (row 35).**
    - "The answer comes back quickly" becomes "the answer can be checked".
    - "The team's one real act of picking was the ten Treasuries" becomes "matched to printed dates": the ten were never picked in Kahneman and Klein's sense.
  - **Judge's note:** a reader expects "index fund for shares, careful selection for bonds". The plan has no place for anyone's flair on either side, and that is by design. Each half sits at one of the two ends where the psychologists say a rule beats a person. What the team did judge was Laura: what to promise first (row 36).

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 32 with row 30 (rules beat people at both ends; the Treasuries checked on the day).** For 3f, the lesson's strongest form. Kahneman and Klein's own page puts the world fund at one end and a printed bond schedule at the other, and NUMBERS shows the ten pay with nothing earned. A judge who expects "index the shares, pick the bonds carefully" learns the plan left no room for anyone's flair on either side, by design.
2. **Rows 25 and 24 (S&P: three years flips from 0% to 29%; four years gives none).** For 3f, claim (c), at most one clause. A three-year window, the one Laura's top has, gave opposite verdicts on picking in consecutive editions. That is why the copout argument could not be settled by 2031 in either direction.
3. **Rows 1 and 2 (whose words).** References and 3f integrity. The famous two bullets are Kahneman's 2011 book. The 2009 paper's own words are on p. 520, with the feedback condition on p. 524. Rows 3 and 11 fix two smaller citation slips before anything is printed.
4. **Row 21 (Kothari and Warner's 36 months).** For 3f, claim (c), store or one clause. Finance's standard power study tests exactly Laura's window and misses even a rare 3% edge about four times in ten.
5. **Row 15 (Shanteau: make a poor-side task a good-side one).** For 3c or 3f. The 1 January 2031 move is this hypothesis in action: Laura states a figure only once it is read from Treasuries rather than forecast from shares.
