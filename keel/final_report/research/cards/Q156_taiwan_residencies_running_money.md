- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:**
  - This is operating-subsidy dependence and going-concern risk in small cultural nonprofits, plus public contracting of cultural venues (公辦民營).
  - Nonprofit finance names the risks: thin net assets, revenue from one source, thin margins (Tuckman and Chang, 1991). Named here, not read this run.
  - Public procurement law sets the year-by-year service contract. Taiwan's Government Procurement Act lets an agency let a service contract with an "expansion" option, which is how Hsinchu let its village in half-years.

## Verdicts in one screen

**Claim 1. CHANGE.**
- Taiwan's public money for running a residency is voted one year at a time:
  - The Ministry's village grant (Q9c-001).
  - NCAF's operating grant, which can be cut pro rata after the legislature's review (Q55-009). It was cut 23% for one 25-year-old space in 2026 (row 18).
  - Hsinchu's village contract. It was let in half-years in 2019-2020 and yearly since. The 2020 and 2026 contracts were awarded after their year had begun (rows 1-2).
  - Kinmen's residency, whose contract is signed only after the council passes the budget (row 17).
- "Funders give one year at a time" goes too far. Fangliao's F3 had a 2.5-year contract (row 12), Board 435 gives studios two years plus one (row 24), and private sponsors have pledged five years (Q11e-007).
- Say instead: "In Taiwan, public money for running a residency is voted one year at a time, even when the same operator keeps it for a decade. Laura's ten payments are owned before the first year begins."
- Judge's note: the same foundation has run Hsinchu's village since 2016, yet the 2026 contract was signed on 6 January, six days into the year it pays for. A run of renewals is not a promise.

**Claim 2. CHANGE (size it).** $50,000 is NT$1,638,400 at 32.768. Against what was found (Keel arithmetic, row 36):

| Benchmark | NT$ a year | $50,000 against it |
|---|---|---|
| F3's yearly contract | about NT$1.10m | about 1.5 times |
| Hualien's building upkeep alone | NT$0.82m-1.54m | 1.1-2.0 times |
| The Ministry's yearly grant to Soulangh | NT$1.8m-2.1m | 78-91% |
| Hsinchu's operating contract | NT$4.0m-5.4m | 30-41% |
| Paul Chiang's operator, at its 2025 pace | about NT$5.6m | about 29% |

- So $50,000 a year is the core running money of a small residency in a building it pays no rent on, with one or two staff and a few artists. A Hsinchu-scale village costs about three times that.
- Judge's note: the money that reaches resident artists is the smallest line. Hsinchu's two long residents get NT$310,000 between them, 5.7% of the 2026 contract. Most running money pays people and the building (rows 6-7).

**Claim 3. NOT FOUND as stated.**
- No residency's own reserve, sized against a year's costs, was found.
- What Taiwanese bodies do hold is a legal minimum fund. It is NT$30m for a national culture foundation (Q11d-015) and NT$3m-10m locally (row 29).
- That fund is locked by law (row 30). It is meant to be kept while its income is spent (Taichung Art. 20, row 31).
- At the big banks' posted 1.70% one-year rate (row 27), NT$30m earns NT$510,000 a year: under a third of one of Laura's payments (row 28).
- Judge's note: to earn one of Laura's payments each year at Taiwan's deposit rate, a foundation would need about NT$96m (about $2.9m) locked. Her ten fixed payments are worth that fund's income, delivered as dated payments she already owns.

**Claim 4. CHANGE.**
- Every closure or shrinkage found came from the site:
  - 空場 lost its lease in 2018 (row 21).
  - Chiayi's council cut the warehouse rent line (Q9c-024).
  - Taichung's and Chiayi's villages stopped for city replanning, and F3 was handed down to its township (Q9c-026).
  - Hualien's park had "poor results for many years" under outsourcing (row 10).
- Grants were cut (row 18), but the spaces went on.
- Judge's note: Taiwan's residencies die of losing the building, not of losing a grant. That is why the plan keeps the ten operating payments separate from the facility promise (row 8 shows a city doing the same).

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 28 (3d, 3a).** "To earn one of Laura's payments each year at Taiwan's 1.70% deposit rate takes about NT$96m locked." It turns $50,000 into a Taiwanese co-sponsor's own terms and shows why ten fixed years are rare.
2. **Row 2 (3a, 3d).** The operator has run Hsinchu's village since 2016, yet the 2026 contract was signed six days into the year. This is the plainest local proof that renewal is not a promise.
3. **Row 36 (3a).** It sizes the claim "$50,000 covers a small residency's core costs": yes for a small village in a donated building; about a third of a Hsinchu-scale village.
4. **Row 16 (3d).** Each of Laura's payments is about what the Ministry gives Tainan's international village in a year, except hers is fixed for ten years. It is one sentence a co-sponsor will remember.
5. **Row 31 (3d).** Taiwan's own rule for culture foundations lists deposits and government bonds first and caps shares at 5%. Laura's reserve follows an instinct Taiwanese funders already write into law.
