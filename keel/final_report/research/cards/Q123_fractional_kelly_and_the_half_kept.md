## Verdicts in one screen

- **A. KEEP the numbers.** At a fraction c of Kelly, long-run growth is c(2-c) of the most and the risk is c times, so half Kelly keeps 3/4 of the growth (Thorp 2006: "half Kelly has 3/4 the growth rate but much less chance of a big loss"). Half Kelly doubles before it halves 8 times in 9 (full Kelly 2 in 3), and its chance of ever falling to half is 1 in 8 against 1 in 2. MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba's Table 3 gives 0.75 of the growth at half and 0.00 at twice Kelly (rows 1-3). "Half the volatility" is right as a spread; the variance is a quarter.
- **B. CHANGE.** Half is a rule of thumb, not a finding. Baker and McHale (2013): "Bettors often use a 'half-Kelly' strategy". Benter (1994) bet a fraction "e.g. 1/2 or 1/3". MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba judge Keynes at about 80% of Kelly and Buffett at about full Kelly (row 10). The right shrink depends on how big the error in the estimate is against the edge. Half is right only when the two are about equal, and sometimes the right shrink is to zero (rows 11-13).
- **C. KILL.**
  - Kelly is a rule for many reinvested bets. In Kelly's own words, it is "the logarithm which is additive in repeated bets". A game stopped after N bets "would depend on the relative values (to the gambler) of being broke or possessing a fortune" (row 4). Laura makes one promise once.
  - The IPS's half is a split of a known value, made before the meetings. The 2033 contribution equals the stated top in every path (NUMBERS 8o). So no estimate of VT's edge enters the promise: VT growth from 4% to 10% gives a $115,000 stated top in every column (row 14).
  - On J.P. Morgan's figures the plan stakes about 0.19 of Kelly before the move and about 0.10 after (Keel arithmetic, row 21). For estimation error alone to explain 0.19, the error would have to be about twice the edge (row 22). So Kelly would call the plan too timid, not cautious. The size comes from the payments (IPS l.11), not from doubt about the 7.5%.
  - The one parallel that survives: a Kelly bettor's half is a stated taste, matching power utility with risk aversion 2 (row 25), and the IPS's half is Caplet's choice in the IPS's own order of cause (card Q5b, NUMBERS 8o), never the best possible split (NUMBERS 8o's banned wording).
- **D. KEEP as a principle; the plan goes a step further.**
  - Granger's result: with costs worse on one side, the best forecast is "the conditional mean plus a constant bias term". With straight-line costs it is a quantile (row 31). The lean grows as uncertainty grows (row 32).
  - Real institutions do this on purpose: the Fed's 4-to-1 (row 33), US states and cities (rows 35, 37).
  - But shaded forecasts are caught out in recessions. More than 70% of states overestimated fiscal 2009 revenue (row 36).
  - The plan states only figures it owns, so there is no estimate left to shade, and a downturn cannot catch it out (row 39).
- **Judge's notes:**
  - A smart reader would guess the half is cautious Kelly. On J.P. Morgan's own figures, Kelly would put more than all the facility money in shares. The plan's shares are about a fifth of that, and they are sized by what the payments leave, not by doubt about the forecast.
  - Bettors shrink the bet because their odds may be wrong. Laura's plan keeps the odds out of the promise altogether: any forecast from 4% to 10% gives the same $115,000 top, and the forecast's error lands only in money promised to no one.
  - Budget offices shade their forecasts down on purpose and are still caught out in recessions. A figure that is owned cannot be.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 21 (with 20 and 22), for 3b.** On J.P. Morgan's figures the plan stakes about a fifth of Kelly before the move. Kelly would call it timid, and doubt about the forecast cannot explain it. The size comes from the payments (IPS l.11). This turns "is half cautious enough?" around for the judge.
2. **Row 14, for 3b and 3c.** Any forecast from 4% to 10% gives the same $115,000 top, and the forecast's error lands only in money promised to no one. This is the plan's answer to the problem fractional Kelly exists to solve.
3. **Row 36, for 3c.** Budget offices shade low on purpose, yet more than 70% of states overestimated fiscal 2009 revenue. A shaded forecast fails in the downturn, and an owned figure does not.
4. **Row 19, for 3b.** Benter's "effective wealth is less than their actual current wealth" is IPS l.11 in a famous bettor's words, from a field a judge will not expect.
5. **Row 4, for 3c.** In Kelly's own words, a game stopped after N bets "would depend on the relative values ... of being broke or possessing a fortune". That sends a one-time promise to the cost of each mistake, not to a growth rule.
