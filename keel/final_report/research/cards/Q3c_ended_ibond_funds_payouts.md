# Q3c. What the five ended Treasury iBonds funds actually paid, and whether the 0.5% held back is the right size

- **Date:** Sun 15 Nov 2026, Sydney (sources read Sun 15 Nov 2026).
- **Report parts:** 3c (the 0.5% held back on each end of the range: what it is measured against), 3e (assumptions and limits; Table 3's fund-notices row), Section 2 (one clause on what an iBonds fund pays, beside the holdings table), 3a (the reserve's funds pay in December). Most rows are for the store only.
- Notes for the Final Report store, not report prose.
- **Not repeated:** card Q16 rows 3-10 (the index rules and its "Cash does not earn any reinvestment income" line; the board's extension power; the declining-yield risk; IBTD, IBTE and IBTF's end dates; the October notices; IBTF's 1.15% since launch to 30 Jun 2025; IBTF's last holdings and monthly payouts; IBTE's last trade); card Q3a rows 20-35 (the prospectus's four lists of costs, lending, yield and timing; the five-fund count; launch-yield comparisons; "trade at market price"); card Q36 rows 1-23 (every board power: extension, early end, merger, in-kind payout, Invesco's early ends, the ETF closure studies); NUMBERS sections 0 and 3 (the range rule and its friction table: 0.75% gives 5.0%/6.0% of paths not owned).
- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "Every ended iShares iBonds Term Treasury ETF paid its holders, at the end, within 0.5% of what its holdings' cash flows promised a year before the end." Tested on IBTA, IBTB, IBTD, IBTE and IBTF.
  2. "The last months' cash yield is the only material source of payout variation; fees and liquidation costs are small."
  3. "Buying an iBonds Treasury ETF on the exchange costs little above NAV."
  4. "No iBonds Treasury fund has ever ended early, been merged or had its date extended."
- **Problem class:** realised tracking of a defined-maturity fund against the bond ladder it stands in for: what the holder is paid at the end, set against what the holdings promised, with every cost between them measured. **Field that solved it first:** target-maturity fund analysis by the sponsors (iShares' iBonds case studies since 2010; Invesco BulletShares maturity reviews), with bond-ETF tracking-error work in finance journals (Drenovak, Urošević and Jelic on European sovereign bond ETFs) and the March 2020 stress studies (ICI, IOSCO, BIS, NBER). Treasury funds differ from the rest of the field: no credit losses, so what is left is the fee, the cash rate and the exchange price.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** True in spirit, but no fund publishes "what its holdings promised a year before", so the claim cannot be proved at 0.5% as worded. The measurable facts say more and are stronger:
  - **Against its own index, over its whole life, none trailed by more than its 0.07% fee.** IBTD 0.42% a year against the index's 0.48% (to 31 Dec 2022); IBTE 0.62% against 0.67% (to 31 Dec 2023); IBTF 1.29% against 1.32% (to 30 Sep 2025); IBTM, still running, 0.39% against 0.43% (fact sheet, Oct 2026). Costs beyond the fee were below zero in all four (rows 11-12).
  - **The worst calendar year:** IBTF 2022, -6.43% against the index's -6.33%: 0.10 points, so 0.03 beyond the fee (row 12).
  - **Last market price against the cash paid:** within 0.066% for all five (IBTD -0.066%, IBTE +0.066%; IBTA -0.007%, IBTB +0.005%, IBTF +0.037%; Keel arithmetic on iShares' closed-funds page; row 4).
  - **Against the yield shown at launch** (iShares' own table, net of the fee): IBTA -0.08 points a year, IBTB +0.10, IBTD +0.44, IBTE +0.40, IBTF +0.24 (row 2). Against the yield a year before the end (rough Keel arithmetic): about -0.1 to +0.4 points, set by what cash earned in the last months (row 15).
  - **Judge note (note form):** each ended Treasury fund paid what its bonds paid, less its fee, within about 0.07% of its last market price; where it differed from the yield shown earlier, the cause was the cash rate in its final months, not cost.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, with sizes.** The cash yield is the only material source; every other item measured is a few hundredths of a percent.
  - **Cash yield:** -0.08 points a year over IBTA's life (cash paid about 0.05% in 2021) to +0.44 over IBTD's (cash paid over 5% in 2023) (rows 2, 16).
  - **Fee:** 0.07% on every Treasury fact sheet read, 2022-2026, and for all five ended funds (row 18). The engine already deducts it.
  - **Money-fund fees in the cash phase:** waived by BlackRock through termination; IBTD's waiver in its last fiscal year was $411,302, about 0.02% of its assets (Keel arithmetic; row 17).
  - **Liquidation costs:** inside the final NAV, which matched the cash paid to the cent in all five (row 6). Not separately visible; bounded by the tracking figures above.
  - **Extraordinary expenses:** none seen, but the Treasury funds' expense lines were not read (gap).
  - **For Laura:** the range rule counts cash at zero, so the cash yield can only lift what is paid above the stated ends; the frictions are a tenth of its size.
- **Claim 3: KEEP for ordinary days; CHANGE for stressed days.**
  - **Ordinary days:** IBTM's premium was -0.06% to +0.09% on the six dates read (Jul 2025 to Nov 2026), its 30-day median bid/ask spread 0.05%, its 30-day average volume 312,736 shares (row 20). IBTF, six weeks before its end: premium 0.01%, spread 0.04% (row 21).
  - **Stressed days:** "Even government bond ETFs ... had discounts in excess of 100 basis points on March 11 and March 12" 2020 (ICI; row 22). After the Fed stepped in on 23 Mar, "steep discounts ... transformed into large premiums" (BIS; row 23). A buyer on a premium day pays it; the size of Treasury-fund premiums in late March 2020 was not found.
  - **Judge note:** read the published premium on the trade day (a watch item, never a rule).
- **Claim 4: CHANGE.** No extension or merger was found (Q36 holds the search and the powers). But one fund ended early: IBTA's prospectus said it "will terminate on or about December 15, 2021"; it last traded on 30 Nov 2021 and paid on 3 Dec 2021, 15 days before the stated date. The supplement was filed on 28 Sep 2021, 63 days before the last trade, and says the board approved the planned liquidation "On December 3, 2019, in connection with the establishment of the Fund" (rows 7-8).
  - **For Laura:** an early end brings cash sooner, before the 1 January payment, never after it. It does change the watch: IBTA's notice came in September, not October.
  - **The record to cite:** five ended, all paid in full and in cash, four in the week after their mid-December date and one 15 days early.
- **The 0.5% haircut: supported, and larger than needed for every fund-level friction measured.** The largest adverse frictions found: 0.066% between last price and cash paid (IBTD); 0.09% premium on a purchase day (IBTM, 6 Nov 2026); 0.03 points beyond the fee in the worst calendar year (IBTF 2022). Stacked as if all hit one purchase held from January 2028 to December 2032 (Keel arithmetic on these figures): 0.09 + 0.025 (half the spread) + 5 x 0.03 = about 0.27%, about half the haircut. It is not sized for a stressed purchase day, when government bond ETFs closed more than 1% from their NAV.

**Row 2. Realised return against the launch yield, five Treasury funds**
- **What is it?** iShares' own table for each ended Treasury fund: the yield of its bonds at launch, less the 0.07% fee, against the return a holder from launch to the end received.
- **Did it work?** Four of five paid more than the launch yield (+0.10 to +0.44 points a year); IBTA paid 0.08 less.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** IBTM is the same product in the same series; the floor is held to its end, as these were.
- **How it is not:** these funds were bought at launch at 1% yields and lived through rising rates, which lifted what their cash earned; Laura's funds will be bought at about 4-5% and could see falling rates.
- **What Caplet takes:** in 3e, a clause of evidence: the five ended Treasury funds paid within about half a point a year of the yield shown at purchase, and where they differed it was what their cash earned. Use the sponsor's table, not the 5-year Treasury comparison.

**Row 4. Last market price against the cash paid**
- **What is it?** For each fund, the closing price on its last trading day set against the cash per share it then paid.
- **Did it work?** All five within 0.066%, in both directions; the smallest fund (IBTA) was off by 0.007%.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** Laura holds to the end and takes the cash; this shows that waiting costs nothing measurable against selling on the last day.
- **How it is not:** it measures only the last day; costs earlier in the fund's life are rows 11-12.
- **What Caplet takes:** the largest end-of-life friction found, 0.066%, is the decisive number for the 0.5% held back.

**Row 7. IBTA ended 15 days early**
- **What is it?** The Dec 2021 fund was to "terminate on or about December 15, 2021"; it last traded on 30 Nov and paid on 3 Dec.
- **Did it work?** Holders were paid in full, in cash, 29 days before 1 January 2022; the last-day gap was 0.007%.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** each iBonds fund in the ladder pays in the December before a payment; an end like IBTA's would still pay before 1 January.
- **How it is not:** IBTA's early date seems to have been fixed at launch ("approved ... On December 3, 2019, in connection with the establishment of the Fund"), not a board's later choice; IBTM's own plan of liquidation was not read here.
- **What Caplet takes:** say "five have ended, each paid in full and in cash before 1 January", never "all on schedule"; Table 3's watch starts in September.

**Rows 11 and 12. Fund against its own index**
- **What is it?** The fund's NAV return set against its ICE index, over its life and year by year.
- **Did it work?** Over each life read, the gap was 0.03-0.06 points a year, below the 0.07% fee; the worst year was 0.03 beyond the fee.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** NUMBERS' engine deducts the 0.07% fee from the holdings; these figures say that is enough.
- **How it is not:** the index is not the promise: it reinvests coupons monthly and keeps its last-six-month cash at zero.
- **What Caplet takes:** in 3c's notes, "costs beyond the fee were below zero over every fund life read" is the plain support for the 0.5% being enough.

**Rows 13 and 14. The index assumes what Caplet assumes**
- **What is it?** From 30 June of its final year the index keeps matured money as cash earning nothing; the fund invests it in cash that does earn, so in its last year the fund beat the index (IBTF 4.13% vs 4.08%).
- **Did it work?** Yes for IBTF, the one fund whose last-year index figure was found.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** the range rule counts each end "if cash waiting earns nothing": the same convention.
- **How it is not:** the index's zero runs six months; Caplet's runs from the trade day to the end.
- **What Caplet takes:** in 3e, one clause: the assumption is the one the fund's own index uses; reality has paid more.

**Row 15. Final year against the yield a year before**
- **What is it?** A rough reading, by Keel arithmetic, of what the last year paid against the yield shown when it began.
- **Did it work?** About -0.1 (IBTE) to +0.4 (IBTD) points; IBTF +0.15 against the 1-year Treasury.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** this is the claim's own test: what the holdings promised a year before the end.
- **How it is not:** the figures mix two sources and carry about 0.3 points of error; IBTA and IBTB have none.
- **What Caplet takes:** a store note only; the report should rest on rows 2, 4 and 11, which are primary.

**Row 16. IBTA in a zero-rate year**
- **What is it?** The one fund that fell short of its launch yield: cash paid about 0.05% in 2021.
- **Did it work?** It paid 0.86% a year against a 0.94% net launch yield: 0.08 short.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** the worst cash case the series has met; in Laura's rule cash already counts at zero.
- **How it is not:** IBTA lived 1.8 years from a 1% yield; Laura's IBTM is held about five years from about 4-5%.
- **What Caplet takes:** the cash risk, at its worst, cost 0.08 points a year and stays inside the stated ends.

**Row 17. The money-fund layer is waived**
- **What is it?** BlackRock waives its fee by the amount of the money-fund fees the fund pays in its cash phase, through termination.
- **Did it work?** Waivers were paid: $411,302 for IBTD in its last fiscal year, about 0.02% of its assets.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** IBTM will hold money funds in its last months; the same clause applies through its end (IBTA's wording; IBTM's not re-read).
- **How it is not:** a waiver is a contract that "may be terminated prior to the Fund's termination only upon the written agreement of the Trust and BFA".
- **What Caplet takes:** the prospectus's "fees of money market funds" warning is offset in practice; no change to any figure.

**Row 20. IBTM's exchange price today**
- **What is it?** Premium to NAV, spread and volume on the fund Laura's floor buys.
- **Did it work?** Premium -0.06% to +0.09% on six dates; spread 0.05%.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** the January 2028 purchase and the move before 2031 are exchange buys.
- **How it is not:** six snapshots; a given day can differ; WInS prices are not evidence and were not used.
- **What Caplet takes:** on an ordinary day the purchase cost above NAV is under 0.15%; the 0.5% covers it with room.

**Rows 22 and 23. March 2020**
- **What is it?** On 11-12 Mar 2020 government bond ETFs closed more than 1% below NAV; after 23 Mar discounts turned into premiums.
- **Did it work?** The gaps closed within weeks; the funds kept trading.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** Laura buys, so a premium day is her risk.
- **How it is not:** discounts help a buyer, and the Treasury-fund premiums of late March were not measured.
- **What Caplet takes:** the one place the 0.5% could be too small; the answer is a watch item (read the published premium before each fund purchase), never a rule change.

**Row 27. Treasury funds against the other sleeves**
- **What is it?** The same realised-against-launch-yield table for corporate, high-yield, muni and TIPS iBonds.
- **Did it work?** High yield fell 1-3 points a year short; corporate up to 0.39 short; Treasury from 0.08 short to 0.44 over.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** Laura's funds are the Treasury sleeve only.
- **How it is not:** different start years and rate paths.
- **What Caplet takes:** a judge who knows "target-date bond funds miss their yield" is thinking of credit funds; the Treasury record is different, and the reason is plain: no defaults.

**Row 28. BulletShares trail by more than the fee**
- **What is it?** Invesco's corporate target-maturity funds against their own indices.
- **Did it work?** BSCO trailed by 0.22 points a year against a 0.10% fee.
- **How Laura's case is like it:** the same idea of a fund that ends on a date.
- **How it is not:** corporate bonds cost more to trade and sample; Treasury iBonds trail by less than their fee.
- **What Caplet takes:** a store note: the 0.5% is sized for the Treasury fund it is applied to, not for target-maturity funds in general.

## (c) What would surprise a judge

- The index the floor fund tracks already assumes, in its last six months, exactly what Caplet's range rule assumes: cash waiting earns nothing. In its last reported year the real fund beat that index (IBTF 4.13% against 4.08%).
- Five Treasury iBonds funds have ended; the widest gap between the last market price and the cash paid was 0.066%. A high-yield sibling's was 0.23%.
- The Dec 2021 fund paid out on 3 December, 12 days before the date in its name: early, apparently set at launch, and announced 63 days ahead.
- The one fund that fell short of its launch yield (by 0.08 points a year) did so because cash paid 0.05% in 2021, not because of costs; the three funds that ended in 2023-2025 beat their launch yields by 0.24 to 0.44 points.
- On 11 and 12 March 2020 even government bond ETFs closed more than 1% from their NAV: on a bad day, the price paid can cost more than the whole 0.5%.
