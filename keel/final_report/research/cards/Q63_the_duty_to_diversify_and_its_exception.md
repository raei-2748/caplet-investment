- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "A plan with about 89.55% in one issuer breaks the legal duty to diversify."
  2. "Regulators treat Treasuries as having no credit risk."
  3. "When a fiduciary buys fixed payments, the law asks for the safest source, not a spread of sources."
  4. "Courts have punished bond-only portfolios, so a Treasury ladder is legally exposed."
  5. "The law already treats a person's career as part of what has to be diversified."
- **Problem class:** concentration on purpose against a general duty to diversify. A fixed dollar liability can be matched by only one issuer that every regulator treats as having no credit risk.
- **Field that solved it first:** trust law framed the duty and its exception (Restatement of Trusts §228, then UPIA 1994). The Investment Company Act of 1940 first wrote a Treasury carve-out into a numeric issuer test. Tax, money-fund, insurance and bank law then copied the same shape.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE (as stated, it is false).**
  - **The duty is real, and each statement of it carries its own exception:**
    - UPIA s.3: "unless ... because of special circumstances, the purposes of the trust are better served without diversifying".
    - ERISA 404(a)(1)(C): the exception applies "under the circumstances".
    - UK Trustee Act 2000 s.4(3)(b): "in so far as is appropriate to the circumstances of the trust".
  - **Every numeric issuer cap found in US financial law lifts for US government debt:**

    | Rule | Cap on one issuer | Treatment of US government debt |
    |---|---|---|
    | 1940 Act s.5(b)(1) | 5% per issuer, on 75% of assets | Government securities exempt |
    | Tax code s.851(b)(3) | 25% | "other than Government securities" |
    | Tax code s.817(h)(3) | n/a | Treasuries "treated as adequately diversified" |
    | Rule 2a-7 | 5% | Exempt |
    | NAIC model act | 3% | The US is not subject to it |
    | Basel LEX30.31 | 25% of Tier 1 capital | Sovereigns exempt |

  - **iShares files IBTM itself as a "Diversified Fund"** (rows 30-34, 39, 42, 43).
  - Keel arithmetic on NUMBERS 8g: every asset in the WInS book sits in classes those tests set aside (row 32).
  - **The true line:** concentrated by issuer on purpose, as every one of these laws allows. It is not a loophole.
  - **Judge's note:** a judge expects 89.55% in one issuer to fail a diversification test. The legal tests pass it, and the fund holding the floor (IBTM) is labelled "diversified" in its own SAI.
- **Claim 2: KEEP.**
  - **Banks:** 12 CFR 217.32 gives the US government a zero percent risk weight, "Notwithstanding any other requirement". Under that rule the 0% does not depend on any rating. Basel CRE20.7 gives 0% to sovereigns rated AAA to AA-, against 250% for listed equity.
  - **Insurers:** the NAIC adds Treasuries to its list "automatically", as NAIC 1.A "by a policy-based convention", and "Exempt from RBC".
  - **Money funds:** Rule 2a-7 asks for no credit review of a government security.
  - **The counterweight:**
    - Basel reviewed sovereign treatment (2015-17) and kept it: no "consensus to make any changes".
    - The founding US trust case (Amory, 1830) warned that government credit can be "impaired".
  - **What follows for the report:**
    - Say "risk-free" plainly (Books 3, 5, 19). The IPS already names its one exception (l.17).
    - Note once that "no credit risk" is not "no price risk". In GIW (row 16), Treasuries sold early lost $56,312.50.
- **Claim 3: KEEP.**
  - **IB 95-1 (29 CFR 2509.95-1) asks for the safest source:**
    - Fiduciaries must seek "the safest annuity available".
    - "Reliance solely on ratings ... would not be sufficient".
    - "increased cost or other considerations could never justify" an unsafe annuity.
    - A fiduciary "may not purchase a riskier annuity solely because there are insufficient assets". The sponsor may have to add contributions instead.
  - **The 1974 Conference Report** allows a plan "invested wholly in insurance or annuity contracts".
  - **DOL reviewed the bulletin in 2024 and kept it.** Pension risk transfer premiums were $52 billion in 2022.
  - **Judge's note:** the IPS's 2028 rule (if yields fall, the second deposit completes the ladder, l.13) is this regulator's rule: add money, never add risk.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE.** The cases punished dates, missing reasons and missing reviews, never the issuer.
  - **GIW (11th Cir. 1990):** an ERISA plan fund was about 85% in long and zero-coupon Treasuries.
    - It lost a diversification case because the dates ignored its cash needs.
    - The court measured the loss against "staggered maturity dates": $537,000.
  - **Baker Boyer (Wash. App. 1986):** a trust held almost all tax-exempt bonds with no "considered conscious balancing of risk and advantages". The surcharge was raised from $22,950 to $41,020.
  - **Nestle (EWCA 1992):** gilts held for a tax reason were not a breach. The trust was criticised for failing to review its holdings, but no loss was proved.
  - **What the plan has instead:**
    - Its dates are the payment dates (IPS l.13).
    - Its reason is written down (l.9-11).
    - Table 3 is the review.
  - **Judge's note:** the leading case that punished an all-Treasury pension fund punished its dates, and the court's yardstick for a sound fund was a ladder.
- **Claim 5: KEEP, as an analogy and never as compliance.**
  - Washington's trust statute lists "Other assets of the beneficiary or beneficiaries including earning capacity". The Baker Boyer dissent cites Restatement (Second) §227 cmt o(8) for the same factor.
  - Restatement (Third) §90 cmt f allows departures for "peculiar risks facing its beneficiary families".
  - The old §228 cmt e aims the duty at holdings "dependent upon the success of one enterprise". Laura's career is exactly that.
  - Laura is not a trust, and Caplet is not a trustee (SKELETON 2.3, H7-081).
  - **Judge's note:** trust law's unit of diversification includes the beneficiary's earning capacity. That is IPS l.9's "your career is a large holding of shares" in a statute's words.

## Best five for the report

1. **Rows 16-17 (GIW), for 2.3.** The opposite case turns into the strongest support. The court that punished an all-Treasury plan fund punished its dates and measured the loss against a ladder.
2. **Rows 31 and 43 (IBTM is "diversified"; every cap lifts), for 2.3 and a Table 1 note.** These answer, before it is asked, the judge's likeliest objection to 89.55% in one issuer.
3. **Row 26 (IB 95-1: add money, never risk), for 3a or 2.0.** The IPS's 2028 completion rule is a US regulator's rule for buying fixed payments. That makes it prior art, not an invention.
4. **Row 15 (earning capacity as a trust-investing factor), for 2.3.** The law's own unit of diversification includes the career, which puts aha 2's "diversify Laura" in a statute's words.
5. **Row 34 (Treasuries "treated as adequately diversified"), for 2.3, one clause.** It is the shortest true legal sentence behind "diversifying the payments would make them less safe". Quote it only as how the law sees Treasuries.

## Gaps

- **DOL guidance on dedicated or immunized bond portfolios:** none found. The closest is IB 95-1 (rows 25-28). The 1979 investment-duties regulation (29 CFR 2550.404a-1; 44 Fed. Reg. 37,222, cited by Haskell, "the portfolio as a whole") was not read.
- **Courts on a date-matched, all-Treasury portfolio:** no case found approving one against a diversification claim. Commercial Trust Co. v. Barnard (N.J. 1958), cited in Baker Boyer for not diversifying where all tax-exempt holdings suited the beneficiaries, was seen only as a citation.
- **The Restatement volumes:** not read. This covers Third §§90, 227 and 228 with their comments, and Second §228 cmt c and §227 cmt o. Each was read only as quoted in Haskell, Sterk, the Washington AG opinion or Baker Boyer.
- **The Athene appeals:** Lockheed (4th Cir. No. 25-2061, argued 16 Sep 2026) and Bristol-Myers (2d Cir. No. 26-1021) were seen only in law-firm summaries. No appellate decision was found.
- **US equity risk weights:** the weight for listed equity under 12 CFR 217 was not read; only Basel's 250% was.
- **NAIC risk-based capital:** the life RBC formula's own factor for Treasuries was not read, only "Exempt from RBC" in the Blanks WG proposal and the P&P Manual.
- **Basel's 2017 discussion paper:** the full text (positive risk weights, concentration add-ons) was not read, only the landing page.
- **Nestle at first instance:** Hoffmann J's judgment ([2000] WTLR 795) on judging a portfolio as a whole was seen only in a barrister's summary. The fund figures (£53,963 to £269,203) were seen only in a copy of the law-report headnote.
- **Taiwan:** Taiwan's trust-investment law (where the residency is) was not searched. The case puts Taiwan legal questions out of scope.
