- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "In Taiwan, a rise in building costs after a contract is signed falls on the owner through a price-adjustment clause or a changed-circumstances ruling."
  2. "Laura's flexibility is for overruns; when her building contract is signed decides which cost bursts can reach her" (context for 3e and Table 3 row 7; no new rule).
  3. "Taiwan's government relief in past cost bursts would not reach a co-sponsored private residency. Even government owners paid a burst first from the project's own money, so a privately funded building's own spare money does that job" (this card's own claim).
  4. "NUMBERS 8d's September +7.29% is the latest building-cost reading."
- **Problem class:** who carries a rise in input prices between signing a fixed-price building contract and finishing the work.
  - **Field that solved it first:** public procurement and construction contract law. Index-linked escalation clauses with thresholds are standard in Taiwan's public works model contract (PCC letters from 2001). Taiwan's Civil Code art. 227-2 follows Germany's doctrine of a lost basis of the contract (§313 BGB), as 吳從周 (2018) shows. Official statistics practice adds DGBAS's rules for writing an index into a contract.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Data (NUMBERS 8d): CHANGE the latest reading to October 2026, read at source.**
  - **The index.** The October 2026 construction cost index is 120.50 (2021 = 100): +7.18% on a year and +0.20% on the month. September stays 120.26 (+7.29%, unrevised) (DGBAS ccispl.xls; stat.gov.tw page "11510統計表", updated 16 Nov 2026).
  - **Longer rates (Keel arithmetic).** Five years to October: 3.19% a year (to September: 3.27%). Ten years: 3.96% a year. October 2000 to October 2026: 3.20% a year.
  - **What stays.** The central 3.0% and the 1.5%-5.0% band stand. MacroMicro's +7.18% (Q10c row 6) is now confirmed at source. October can still be revised ("最近3個月資料均可能修正"); November is due about 8 Dec 2026.
  - **Judge's note:** October's +7.18% hides a slowdown. The index rose 5.24% from December 2025 to May 2026, then 1.46% from May to October, a pace of about 3.5% a year (Keel arithmetic).
- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** It holds for public contracts and is wrong as a general statement.
  - **Public contracts with the model clause.** The owner carries only the excess above a threshold (total index 2.5%) on direct works cost: fees, design, management, insurance, profit and tax are outside.
    - On October's 7.18%, that is at most 4.68 points, 65% of the rise. On the NLMA's 80% valuation base it is 52% (Keel arithmetic).
    - An index clause can also miss a material that bursts on its own. Taipower's cable works paid NT$20.96m under its clause, yet the builder still bore NT$60.27m more. The Supreme Court split that in half (106台上2716, 2018).
  - **Without a clause.** Courts may split an abnormal burst, often in half, but years later. Chiayi City Hall: a NT$791.8m fixed price from 2001, NT$73.73m claimed, sent back by the Supreme Court in 2008 (97台上1624) and finally split "由兩造共同承擔".
    - Ordinary rises stay with the builder: Kinmen 2025 found about 1% a month foreseeable; Chiayi 2022 is in Q10e.
    - The Supreme Court is divided on whether a "no adjustment" clause shuts out art. 227-2. 100台上1392 held that it does; the Tainan branch of the High Court (96建上9) held such a clause void.
  - **Taiwan's relief principles of 2004, 2008 and 2009** are administrative rules (行政規則) for agencies. They cannot change a private contract (鄧勝軒, 2024), and each one excludes "法人或團體接受機關補助辦理工程採購".
  - **Private contracts follow their own terms.** The Interior Ministry's model renovation contract sets a fixed total price with no adjustment clause.
  - **Judge's note:** a fixed price does not end an owner's cost risk in Taiwan. Courts have moved half of an abnormal burst back to owners, even against a "no adjustment" clause.
- **Claim 2: KEEP, reframed.** Both the signing date and the contract's form decide which bursts reach the owner. No rule is added.
  - **Before signing,** a burst reaches the owner in full, through the price bid or through failed tenders. The Taichung Green Museumbrary failed to attract a single bid three times in 2018 before its award on 13 Jun 2019.
  - **After signing:**
    - With a clause, the excess above the threshold comes back to the owner monthly, both up and down.
    - Without one, the builder carries ordinary rises, and a court may split an abnormal burst.
    - Delay moves the risk too. Under the NLMA's points a builder who finishes late is priced at the lower of two indices. A delay the owner causes keeps the builder on the original schedule (and 106台上4 shows how the owner's design changes became a price claim).
  - **What the signing date cannot do:** it moves the risk between the parties. It never removes it from the project.
- **Claim 3: KEEP (new).** In 2008 the government paid for a burst in a fixed order:
  - first the job's tender savings,
  - then the plan's approved budget,
  - then other budget lines,
  - then a contract change that cut the work ("調整工程內容"),
  - and last a supplementary or special budget.
  - The 2004 rule's "應同意" applied only where "機關原預算相關經費足敷支應". All three relief rules left out subsidised legal persons and groups.
  - **What the parallel means.** A co-sponsored private residency sits outside every past relief rule. Its own spare money plays the part of the government's savings and budget, and Laura's flexibility is that money.
  - **Where the parallel breaks.** A government can tax, borrow and pass a supplementary budget; Laura has no outside money (case) and only two reasons to change the plan (IPS).
  - **Judge's note:** Taiwan's own burst-relief rules wrote subsidised private builders out by name. Money promised to no one is how such a building meets a burst.
- **Claim 4: CHANGE** (see Data): October +7.18%, read at source, replaces September as the latest reading.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 9 (3b, 3e area 4).** Taiwan's own burst-relief rules of 2004, 2008 and 2009 excluded subsidised legal persons and groups by name. A co-sponsored residency meets a burst with its own spare money. This is the judge's "aha" behind keeping half promised to no one.
2. **Row 11 (3b, Table 3 row 7).** In 2008 even the government paid a burst from the project's own savings and budget first, and cut the work before seeking a supplementary budget. Laura's flexibility is that first money, and the row says plainly where the parallel breaks.
3. **Rows 23-25 (3e area 4).** A fixed price does not end the owner's cost risk. Courts have split abnormal bursts in half, years later. "No adjustment" clauses may not hold, and an index clause can miss a material.
4. **Rows 1-3 (NUMBERS 8d, Table 3 row 7).** October +7.18% is read at source, five years run at 3.19% a year, and the pace slowed after May. The band stands, and the register's latest figure can move to October.
5. **Rows 35-37 (3b).** A real arts building in Taiwan met the 2021-22 burst. Its clause payment was about 2.4% of the contract, while its budget rose about 15% for prices, delay and upgrades, all paid by its owner.
