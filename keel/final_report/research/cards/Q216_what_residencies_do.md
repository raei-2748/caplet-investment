- **Problem class and the field that solved it first:** programme evaluation in the arts, that is, separating what a programme does from whom it chooses (selection against treatment). Labour and education economists solved it first with lotteries and near-miss comparisons. The arts papers below borrow those tools: one lottery (Creatives Rebuild New York, 2026), one near-miss comparison (Rijksakademie, 2022), panel studies (Rengers 2002; Mitchell and Kuld 2026), before-and-after windows (Venice, 2022). Evaluators' own studies (the Alliance of Artists Communities, IFACCA, France's CNAP, Taiwan's NCAF) come first in the rows.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **C1: CHANGE.** Kill "measurably changes careers"; keep a narrower claim about what a residency gives.
  - Careers: no causal study of a residency's own effect on careers was found. The only near-miss test of a famous residency (Rijksakademie, 8,557 applicants over 11 years) finds those accepted ranked 26,375 on ArtFacts in 2016 against 29,904 for those interviewed and turned away. That gap is modest, and the authors assume performance is independent of the programme, so it also holds the jury's eye (rows 11-12).
  - What the field says of itself: the US residencies' own alliance, in 2010, said success "is not easily measured" (row 1). Of arts councils in 18 countries, 38% had evaluated their residencies and "none were able to share their reports" (row 2). France's national survey calls the effect on careers "equivocal" (row 9). The best-known numbers (84% more chances to exhibit or publish) are artists' own reports with no comparison group (row 3).
  - The work, measured through a residency's ingredients:
    - Time and money: a lottery gave 2,400 New York artists $1,000 a month for 18 months. They did 3.884 more hours a week of art (control mean 19.36) and 2.382 fewer hours of other work (row 14).
    - Peers: among 370 British and Irish authors, each personally connected writer living nearby goes with about 15% more lasting works, and a "creative circle" with roughly double. This is a fixed-effects panel, not a lottery (row 17).
  - Changed wording, for the store: "A residency gives artists time, money and peers. Lotteries show that reliable money buys artists about four more hours a week for their art, and living near connected peers goes with more lasting work. No study yet shows that a residency itself changes careers. The US residencies' own alliance wrote that success 'is not easily measured.'"
  - **Judge's note:** a smart reader assumes the famous residencies prove the case. The only near-miss test of one finds the chosen modestly ahead of the nearly chosen, and the jury beat a lottery by gains the authors call "small". That is the team's own reason for VT in Trading Note 3, "the vain illusion that we can pick winners", found in a jury room (row 12).
- **C2: KILL.** Alumni prizes count whom a residency chose, not what it did.
  - Support follows reputation: Flemish grants went to artists with past grants and shows (row 20).
  - Selection juries choose artists who are already ahead (rows 11-12).
  - The counts grow with time: Yaddo's National Book Awards were 61 in Slate's 2011 telling and are 73 on Yaddo's page today (row 21).
- **C3: KILL.** Nothing about residents can be shown in 2031: the residency opens in 2033 (case p.3), and career effects are measured over 3 to 10 years (Caldera's follow-up runs 3 years, Venice 5, Rijksakademie up to 11). In 2031 Laura can show co-sponsors her money, not results (row 34).
- **C4: KILL.** The Ministry of Culture's own evaluation of its funded villages, run by NCAF (10 villages visited by 23 Oct 2025), scores operations, space use, budget execution and the quality and quantity of shows and exchanges. It never scores residents' careers (row 29). Taiwan's own studies of residents are qualitative or self-reported (row 31).
- **C5: CHANGE.** In 2025 awarded money was withdrawn in mid-grant:
  - Civitella Ranieri's $30,000 federal grant for five American fellows was withdrawn three months in, with the money already spent;
  - a Richmond studio lost "$230,000 in grant funds" in 110 days, "36% of our budget";
  - Iowa's International Writing Program lost three State Department grants worth more than $950,000, about half its yearly operating budget, after 58 years (rows 24-25).
  - Wording: a grant awarded is a promise to pay, not money held.
  - **Judge's note:** US residencies' federal money was paid "on a reimbursement basis", so they spent first and were paid later. Laura's plan runs the other way: the Treasuries are held for her before she promises the figure. This is aha 8 in ARGUMENT_TREE, owned versus signed (card Q200), shown from the residency field's own 2025.
- **What the report may say in 3d:** no outside evidence in Laura's words. The purpose comes from the case only: a "collaborative creative residency in Taiwan", "a small, community-oriented space where artists, writers, designers, entrepreneurs, and educators can temporarily live, work, teach, and collaborate". 3d's one outside parallel is already Spelman/Lehman (ARGUMENT_TREE), so any outside sentence goes in the store, 3e or the team's lead-in, never in Laura's words. If the team wants one sentence, the exact wording is row 35.
- **Must never claim:**
  - "residencies are proven to" (or "measurably") boost careers;
  - MacDowell's or Yaddo's prizes as effects;
  - "the first residency in Taiwan" (Taipei Artist Village opened in 2001);
  - a reason for "why Taiwan" that the case does not give;
  - the International Writing Program's Taiwan founding as anything of Laura's;
  - one figure for the number of artists AIR Taipei has hosted (its reports give about 600 and more than 500);
  - any outcome for Laura's residents.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 24 with row 25 (awarded is not paid; reimbursement):** 3d's reserves, behind Spelman/Lehman, or the store for aha 8. The residency field's own 2025 shows that a grant awarded is a promise and Treasuries held are money. Laura's order is the reverse of reimbursement.
2. **Row 12 (the jury beat a lottery by "small" gains):** the store, as pedigree for the share half. The best art jury in the Netherlands could not pick winners among strong finalists much better than chance, the same reason Trading Note 3 gives for VT. Never in Laura's words.
3. **Rows 1, 2 and 8 (the field says it does not measure outcomes):** these back the must-never list for 3d and 3e's one limit. They are the reason the report claims nothing about residents and states only the money.
4. **Row 14 with row 35 (four more hours a week, by lottery):** the one outside sentence if the team wants one. It is the only randomised evidence on what a residency gives, and it says nothing about careers.
5. **Row 29 (Taiwan scores villages on operations and money):** 3d's background. Taiwanese funders already judge a residency on how it is run and paid for, which is the question Laura's figure answers.
