**Problem class and the field that solved it.** Telling a change of a governing document from a decision taken under
it. Three fields have worked rules for this. Institutional investment governance (CFA Institute's IPS guidance and
Standards III(C), V(B), V(C); US pension law under ERISA and the UPIA; UK pension regulations; university and public
fund IPSs) answers it by who approves and by materiality. US administrative law (the APA's interpretive-rule exemption,
*American Mining Congress*, 1993) answers it by asking whether a later text needs new authority or only spells out an
existing duty. Performance reporting (GIPS) and charity law (New York's contemporaneous record) answer how such decisions
are written down.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: CHANGE.** Practice draws the line, but not as a clear list.
  - The line is drawn by governance (who must approve) and by materiality (how much the decision moves). The UPIA's
    own comment says the old line between discretionary and ministerial acts was "frequently arbitrary" and quotes the
    Restatement: "There is not a clear-cut line".
  - On the policy side in every text read: objectives and risk tolerance; constraints (liquidity, horizon); the
    strategic allocation or "the balance between different kinds of investments" (UK reg 2); benchmarks; and the
    process or triggers for refreshing the policy (CFA 2010 s.2b).
  - On the implementation side: choice of instruments, sizing inside the policy's limits, timing, procedures and
    valuation methods (UC Regents: "Changes to procedures and related documents do not require Regents approval").
  - The range rule is a procedure: implementation. The top-up's first $2,388 is l.13's completion: implementation.
    The other $35,894 is implementation only when it is named as the operating reserve's size, which Wharton asks
    Section 3 to "Recommend", derived from IPS l.11; sourced to l.13 it fails l.13's own condition (NUMBERS 2).
  - Strongest test against Keel: materiality. The 2028 deposit's share going to the payments moves from 1.6% ($2,388)
    to 25.5% ($38,282) (Keel arithmetic on NUMBERS 2), and the floor payout from $136,328 with no margin to $103,178
    (NUMBERS 2). Under CFA III(C) that size calls for a review against the IPS, written down; it does not by itself
    make a policy change, because no objective, constraint, asset class, order of uses or trigger moves.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** A tie by words is necessary, not sufficient. A strict judge also asks whether the frozen
  clause's condition is met, whether the call is larger in effect than the words allow, and whether it is labelled for
  what it is (*American Mining Congress*: "crisper and more detailed lines" are not an amendment; a rule that
  "effectively amends" is). Classification (detail in (c)):
  - (i) no-interest standard and top-up size: $2,388 implementation of l.13 (LOW risk); $35,894 implementation as
    the reserve's recommended size under l.11, l.17 and TNA l.5 (MEDIUM risk with that label; HIGH if sourced to l.13);
  - (ii) range rule: implementation, a valuation procedure for l.15; the cap and "nothing stated before the move" are
    interpretation (LOW to MEDIUM);
  - (iii) "most" as 73.3% of what is left: implementation read off the frozen WInS book (LOW);
  - (iv) the two definitions: interpretation, recognition tests for l.17's words (default LOW; 31 March 2028
    MEDIUM-LOW because Keel's own Tue 10 Nov row called a cut-off "a new rule added after submission");
  - (v) the $75,001 minimum: interpretation, a guard that keeps "most" true (MEDIUM: it cannot hold when the
    completion passes $74,999, between a 1-point and a 2-point fall before January 2027, NUMBERS 5).
  - No call is a change of policy. Two cannot be tied to l.13 at all and must not be: the $35,894 and the standard.
- **Claim 3: CHANGE.** KEEP "in the IPS's own words". CHANGE "never call any of them new" to: call each call by its
  kind and give its reason in substance. Two calls answer Wharton's own Section 3 requirements ("Recommend an
  appropriate operating reserve"; "Provide a credible dollar range"), so they are recommendations; three are readings
  of the IPS's words. Never "new", "changed" or "after the freeze"; never present a measure made afterwards as one the
  WInS book was built to (GIPS 3.A.6, no retroactive change of a definition). The plainest form is the UK
  implementation statement's pattern, used as one clause per call, not as a new table: the IPS's words, what is done,
  one reason.

## (b) Cards

**B1. The line is drawn by who approves and by how much a decision moves (rows 1-4, 9, 21, 22, 25, 26).**
- *What is it?* In institutional practice the IPS holds objectives, risk tolerance, constraints, the kinds of assets,
  their balance and the triggers for review. Instruments, sizes inside limits, timing and procedures are carried out
  by staff and changed without re-approval. Whether a staff decision still needs the board is settled by materiality
  (CFA III(C): review "prior to material changes"; UK reg 2: "any significant change in investment policy"; CalPERS:
  "modifications deemed material").
- *Did it work?* It is the standing structure of the CFA guidance, UK pension law, the UC Regents and CalPERS. The
  UPIA comment shows its limit: "There is not a clear-cut line".
- *How is Laura's case like it?* The IPS is the policy; Caplet is the approving body; Keel's calls of 9-11 Nov are
  staff work under it. None of the five calls moves an objective (l.11), an asset class (Treasuries and VT), the order
  of uses (l.13, l.15) or a trigger (l.17).
- *How is it not?* No one can re-approve: the IPS is frozen by Wharton. And by size the top-up is material: 25.5% of
  the deposit against 1.6% (Keel arithmetic on NUMBERS 2), floor payout $103,178 against $136,328 with no margin
  (NUMBERS 2). Practice answers a material staff decision with a written check against the policy, which the report
  can give; it cannot give a re-approval.
- *What Caplet takes.* 3a, one clause: the reserve's size and its tie to l.11 (not to l.13). No sentence claims a
  bright line. Store: the materiality numbers.

**B2. Recommendation, not interpretation: the reserve's size answers a question Wharton reserved for the report
(rows 14, 16, 30, 31, 32).**
- *What is it?* A policy leaves some powers open; filling one is an exercise of that power, not a reading of the
  policy's words (*Picciotto*, as read in AMC). IB 2016-01 separates "general instructions" from "directions as to the
  purchase or sale of a specific investment at a specific time".
- *Did it work?* Courts struck down an agency's later conditions when it presented them as interpretation; they upheld
  them when presented as what they were and made through the right process.
- *How is Laura's case like it?* The IPS sets the reserve (l.17) but not its size. Wharton's Section 3 must "Recommend
  an appropriate operating reserve". So the right process for the $35,894 is that recommendation, made in the Final
  Report, with its reason in substance: at the IPS's own sizing a reinvestment shortfall, the risk TNA l.5 names, could
  be met only from the shares (short somewhere in 55.6% of 585 historical starts; 1 in 20 short by $34,555; NUMBERS 2),
  which l.11 rules out.
- *How is it not?* WN l.15 told judges the top-up comes "If yields fall". A judge holding that note sees an
  unconditional purchase. The answer is the split: $2,388 is that conditional top-up, its condition met; $35,894 is the
  reserve. INTENT failure 7 and WORDS already say this (Thu 12 Nov evening); this card adds the governance reason.
- *What Caplet takes.* 3a: the reserve's size as Wharton's "recommend", tied to l.11 and TNA l.5; never "the IPS's own
  clause" for the whole sum. Figure 1's note may carry the two parts ($2,388 and $35,894, NUMBERS 2). Lead check:
  WORDS row "the top-up" has the two sources; the label "recommended" is the addition.

**B3. Interpretation or amendment: the AMC test applied to the three readings (rows 15, 29, 33, 34).**
- *What is it?* A later text is interpretive when the earlier text already gives an adequate basis for the action and
  the later text only draws "crisper and more detailed lines"; it is legislative (a change) when it "effectively
  amends" the earlier one.
- *Did it work?* It is the D.C. Circuit's four-question test (1993) for the APA's interpretive-rule exemption, and it
  kept an agency's precise x-ray threshold as an interpretation of the word "diagnosed".
- *How is Laura's case like it?* "Most" read as at least just over half, and l.17's two words read as five business
  days and 31 March 2028, are crisper lines for words the IPS uses, like the x-ray threshold for "diagnosed".
- *How is it not?* Two places where a reading changes an outcome: (1) the 31 March date, if a deposit arriving after
  it would count as missed rather than late (Keel's own Tue 10 Nov row warned of this); (2) "Most" in a deep fall,
  where no reading can keep the word true (row 33), so the IPS's own order (payments first) must settle it.
- *What Caplet takes.* Table 3's note in 3e: each definition as how the word is recognised, with what it changes;
  for the date, that money arriving later is used in the same order when it arrives (lead's decision, (c)). "Most":
  store only; never print $75,001 (INTENT failure 7). The deep-fall break: store and risk record.

**B4. Ex ante and never backwards (rows 5, 19, 27).**
- *What is it?* Prudence is judged on facts known when the decision is made, "not by hindsight" (UPIA s.8); a changed
  definition "must not be applied retroactively" (GIPS 3.A.6); metrics are not chosen to "highlight or disguise" risks
  (CFA 2010 s.4b).
- *Did it work?* These are standing rules in US trust law and the global performance standard.
- *How is Laura's case like it?* The sizing is decided before the money moves (January 2028) and in response to a
  named risk, not to an outcome. Wharton's Guide draws the same line ("may not redesign your strategy after observing
  the results").
- *How is it not?* The WInS book was built before the test existed, and its notes say "reinvested coupons fund the
  rest" (WN l.18). So the test cannot be shown as a criterion the book met.
- *What Caplet takes.* 2.5: "measured afterwards" (already in SKELETON), never as a target the book was built to.
  Store: the GIPS and UPIA grounds, if a judge asks.

**B5. The plainest record: the implementation statement (rows 13, 23, 24, 28, 35).**
- *What is it?* UK trustees publish each year how and how far they followed their statement, any review, any change
  with its reason, and the date of the last review. Investments outside the statement are listed with reasons and a
  remedy. New York asks for the substance of a decision, recorded at the time, never a conclusory line. CFA V(C) keeps
  the reasons and the model inputs, and counts a review that kept the position.
- *Did it work?* It is a legal duty for UK occupational pension trustees (S11) and for New York institutions managing
  endowment funds (S16).
- *How is Laura's case like it?* Section 2 is Wharton's version of an implementation statement ("Evaluate how
  effectively your team implemented its strategy"). Each call has IPS words, an action and a reason ready (table in (c)).
- *How is it not?* A pension statement can say "we changed the policy, and here is why"; Caplet's report cannot,
  because Wharton forbids a revised IPS. So the pattern is used only for decisions within the IPS, and the one
  divergence (no top-up in the WInS book) is named with its reason, once, in Section 2.
- *What Caplet takes.* A drafting pattern for 27 Nov, one clause per call in 2.5 and 3a: the IPS's words, what is
  done, one reason in substance. No new table (density). Store: the UK and New York texts.
