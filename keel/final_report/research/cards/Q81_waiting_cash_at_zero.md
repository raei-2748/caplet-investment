- **Problem class:** choosing a stress scenario and saying how it sits against the record: is the bad case drawn from history, or harsher than history? **Field that solved it first:** bank supervision. The Fed's stress tests (DFAST) write a "severely adverse" path for the 3-month bill and say how it compares with past recessions; insurers' asset adequacy tests (NY Regulation 126, card Q20) do the same for reinvestment rates. Before them, the escrow verifiers' "no reinvestment" test (card Q31) set the zero.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KEEP the substance, CHANGE the dates and the measure.** The store's dates are the Fed's target, not what cash earned. Counted on the 3-month bill itself (FRED TB3MS, monthly, Jan 1934 to Jun 2026, 1,110 months):
  - **below 0.07% (the iBond funds' own fee, NUMBERS 0):** 83 months since 1934 (7.5%), 53 of the 222 months since Jan 2008 (23.9%); longest spell 19 months (Jan 2014-Jul 2015) (row 1-2).
  - **below 0.15% (the waiting ETF's fee in the register's test, NUMBERS 0):** 141 months since 1934 (12.7%), 90 since Jan 2008 (40.5%); longest spell 58 months (Feb 2011-Nov 2015) (rows 1, 2, 4).
  - **below 0.25%:** 191 months since 1934 (17.2%), 108 since Jan 2008 (48.6%); longest 82 months (Mar 2009-Dec 2015) (row 1).
  - Bills sat under 0.25% from Nov 2008 to Dec 2015 (one month's blip, Feb 2009) and from Apr 2020 to Jan 2022, and left zero in Jan-Feb 2022, before the Fed moved (row 6). Say "the 3-month bill paid under 0.15% in 90 of the 222 months from January 2008", not the Fed's dates.
  - **Three eras, not two.** The 1930s had 30 months under 0.07% (1938-41) and 48 months running under 0.15% (Nov 1937-Oct 1941). The lowest calendar years on record are 2014 (0.033%), 1940 (0.036%) and 2021 (0.044%) (rows 5, 8).
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.** "Twice in fifteen years" is literally right for the last fifteen years (Jul 2011-Jun 2026: 73 months under 0.15%, in the 2011-15 and 2020-21 eras; row 7). But "tested against something that really happened" undersells the test and is wrong about its length:
  - the test holds zero, and a 0.15% fee on top, for every month from 2027 to 2042 (NUMBERS 0, 2);
  - the longest real spell under 0.15% was 58 months; no 16-year stretch on record had more than 90 of its 192 months under 0.15% (rows 9-10);
  - the worst 16 years on record (Jan 1934-Dec 1949) still compounded at +0.21% a year after the same 0.15% fee; the worst 12 months after the fee were -0.126% (Aug 2014-Jul 2015), above the test's -0.15% (rows 11-12);
  - replaying every 16-year path of real bill rates since 1934, less 0.15%, through the register's engine pays all ten in all 919 start months, with at least $2,454 left (start Jan 1934), against the test's $0 (row 13, Keel arithmetic).
  - **Better wording (for the lead):** "the test takes the lowest rate cash has really earned, charges a fund's fee on top, and holds it for all sixteen years, which no sixteen years since 1934 have done."
- **Claim 3: KILL "not a stress test"; CHANGE to "both".** The level is history; it is also the Fed's own severe stress. The Fed's 2026 severely adverse scenario takes the 3-month bill "to 0.1 percent by the second quarter of 2026" and keeps it "there for the remainder of the scenario", 12 quarters to Q1 2029, on the same series as TB3MS (row 15). The plan's test is lower (0% and then a fee) and five times longer (16 years against 3).
- **Claim 4: KEEP, with primary numbers.** Read in the funds' own SEC filings:
  - BIL (1-3 month bills, fee 0.1345-0.1368%, no waiver) returned -0.04% in 2012, -0.07% in 2013 and -0.11% in 2015 at net asset value, and -0.07% a year for 2011-2015, while its index earned +0.03% to +0.08% (rows 17-18).
  - BILS (3-12 month bills) returned -0.08% in 2021; no quarter of 2021 earned more than 0.01% (row 21).
  - SHV's fee is 0.15%, the register's own figure, and its sponsor waived fees in the zero years (row 23).
  - The worst fund year at net asset value found at source is -0.11% (BIL, 2015); the engine pays all ten with $374 left if every waiting dollar earned that every year (row 14).
  - **Correction to card Q2d row 14 (secondary pages):** BIL's 2013 and 2015 at net asset value were -0.07% and -0.11%, not -0.09% and -0.13%.
- **Judge's note:** a judge may read "if cash waiting earns nothing" as a gloomy extreme. In fact the US Treasury itself borrowed at exactly 0.000% in 54 bill auctions from 2008 to 2021, three of them for three months (row 16). The test is harsher than that: it adds a fund's fee, and holds the result for sixteen years, which nothing on record has done.

## How the months were counted

- **Data.** FRED series TB3MS (3-month Treasury bill secondary market rate, discount basis, monthly average of business days, Board of Governors H.15), read in full from FRED's table page (copy last updated 1 Jul 2026, data Jan 1934 to Jun 2026, 1,110 months). Saved as `keel/final_report/research/cards/data/Q81_bill_rate_months.csv` (date, rate, a 0/1 flag for each threshold, the rate less 0.07 and less 0.15). Jul-Sep 2026 are not in that copy: the repo's daily DGS3MO (`archive/rab/data/fred/DGS3MO.csv`, to 25 Sep 2026) never went below 3.82% then, and NUMBERS 0's 6 Nov curve has the 1-year at 4.33%, so Jul-Oct 2026 add four months above every threshold (1,114 months to Oct 2026; the shares change by at most 0.4 points).
- **Rule.** A month counts if its average is strictly below the threshold (0.07, 0.15 or 0.25; FRED gives two decimals). A spell is a run of consecutive counted months. "At or below" gives 90 / 156 / 194 months since 1934 (59 / 94 / 108 since 2008), with the longest spell under-or-at 0.07% 31 months (Apr 2013-Oct 2015).
- **Why these thresholds.** 0.07% is the iBond funds' fee: when bills pay less, cash inside a fund in its last months nets below zero. 0.15% is the fee the register charges every waiting dollar (NUMBERS 0, row "Where waiting money sits"). 0.25% is the top of the Fed's zero range.
- **Daily cross-check.** The repo's DGS1MO and DGS3MO (constant-maturity yields, floored at zero by Treasury, so they cannot show the few negative days card Q2d row 16 found in DTB3), business days from 2 Jan 2008 to 25 Sep 2026.
- **Windows and replay (Keel arithmetic).** 16-year windows are 192 consecutive months; growth compounds each month at (rate - fee)/12. The replay maps a historical month onto Jan 2027 and walks forward month by month, rolling every waiting dollar (coupons, fund payouts, carried surplus, fund cash in its last months) at that month's bill rate less 0.15%, through the register's own ledger (`numbers/completion.py`, design H_plain, rungs and backstop as bought, which reproduces $1,414 left at 0% and $0 with the fee). Scripts in the scratchpad, not in the repo.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 16 (54 auctions at 0.000%, three of them 3-month bills)** for 3a, beside "even if cash waiting earns nothing": the judge learns that zero is a price the US government has actually paid, not a modeller's invention.
2. **Row 13 (the replay)** for 3a or Figure 1's note, once the lead puts it in NUMBERS: every 16-year path of real bill rates since 1934, less the fund's fee, pays all ten with at least $2,454 left; the test is harsher than all of history.
3. **Row 15 (the Fed's 2026 stress at 0.1% for 12 quarters)** for 3a or 3e: the plan's test sits below the Fed's own severe case for the largest banks, and runs five times as long.
4. **Rows 2 and 4 (90 of 222 months since 2008 under 0.15%; longest spell 58 months)** for 3a: the count a judge can check, with the honest limit beside it.
5. **Row 17 (BIL's own filings: -0.04%, -0.07%, -0.11%)** for 3e: what a real bill fund lost in the zero years, all inside the test's -0.15%; corrects card Q2d row 14.

## Gaps

- BIL's calendar 2011, 2014 and 2021 returns at net asset value were not read at source (only five-year figures, year-to-date figures and the 2021 worst quarter). The 2016-2022 BIL summary prospectuses on EDGAR hold them.
- SHV's year-by-year returns 2009-2015 and 2021, its waiver amounts and dates, and its monthly distribution history were not found at source (the prospectus bar chart has no text values; the annual reports for fiscal years to Feb 2012-2016 would hold them).
- No 30-day SEC yields for BIL or SHV in 2011-2015 or 2021 were found; a negative SEC yield would be a sharper fact than the after-tax inference in row 19.
- Government money fund 7-day yields were not read beyond card Q2d's months.
- The FRED copy read ends Jun 2026; Jul-Oct 2026 were filled from the repo's daily file and NUMBERS 0, not from TB3MS.
- DTB3 daily since 1954 was not read (the WebFetch budget ran out; Firecrawl and Parallel limits were hit); the daily rows use the repo's constant-maturity files, which are floored at zero.
- The replay (row 13) changes only the waiting rate; it does not re-price the January 2027 and January 2028 purchases in a zero era.
- The 1930s context (tax exemption of bills, banks' demand for them) was read only through Cecchetti's abstract.
