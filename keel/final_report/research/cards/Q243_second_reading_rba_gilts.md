## Verdicts in one screen

- **(a) KEEP, cite the right release.** The words are the last paragraph of the RBA's *Statement by Philip Lowe, Governor: Monetary Policy Decision*, Media Release **2021-01**, dated **2 February 2021**: "The Board will not increase the cash rate until actual inflation is sustainably within the 2 to 3 per cent target range. For this to occur, wages growth will have to be materially higher than it is currently. This will require significant gains in employment and a return to a tight labour market. The Board does not expect these conditions to be met until 2024 at the earliest." (row 1). "The conditions for raising rates" is a fair paraphrase of "these conditions" (row 3). February 2021 is when the date first appeared; before it the wording was "at least three years" (review, printed p.7, row 4). Two traps: release 2021-02 is a Payments System Board update of 19 Feb 2021 (row 2), and the review's own later paraphrase "2024 or later" (p.13) is not the 2021 wording (row 6). The rise came on 3 May 2022 (Media Release 2022-12, 25 basis points to 35 basis points; row 7).
- **(b) KEEP, page confirmed.** *Review of the RBA's Approach to Forward Guidance* (released 15 November 2022, Media Release 2022-37), section 3, under the heading "Communication regarding the conditionality of the forward guidance could have been clearer", **printed page 14**, first paragraph: "The fact that many people interpreted the forward guidance as 'a promise' that there would be no rate raises until 2024 led to considerable reputational damage to the Bank. When the cash rate was increased in May 2022, many people saw the Bank as having broken 'its promise'." Q212 inferred p.14 from the contents page; this reading confirms it from the page footers (the footer "14 RESERVE BANK OF AUSTRALIA" closes that page). The single quotes around 'a promise' are the Bank's own and stay inside the report's double quotes (row 12). One correction to Q212 row 4: the "pledge" exchange is endnote 17 on printed **p.18**, not p.17 (row 14).
- **(c) KEEP the words, CHANGE how they are used.** The words are exact, in the second summary point of *Written evidence from the Pension Protection Fund (PPF)*, reference **LDI0031**, to the Work and Pensions Committee's inquiry *Defined benefit pensions with Liability Driven Investments*; the document is signed off "November 2022" and the Committee published it on **1 December 2022** (rows 15-16). But the PPF put 'insolvent' in quote marks for a reason: a scheme enters the PPF when its *employer* becomes insolvent, not the scheme (the PPF's own "About" paragraph; its chief executive to the Committee, 14 Dec 2022: "the most important driver of that is insolvency in the employer, rather than necessarily the scheme funding"; row 17). So "no pension scheme became insolvent" is a weak fact on its own, and a UK pensions judge knows it. The stronger sentence is the one before it in the same bullet: "The recent crisis was one of short-term liquidity, not fundamentally the solvency of DB schemes using LDI." "Saw no claim" must carry its date: by July 2025 the PPF's own modelling put the episode's effect on its claims as "small", not nil (row 22).
  - **Corrected words (content, not prose):** "In November 2022 the pensions' insurer told Parliament that the crisis 'was one of short-term liquidity, not fundamentally the solvency' of the pension schemes, and that it had seen no claims from it." Never "no claim ever", never "no scheme lost anything" (a minority ended worse funded: the PPF, 27 Apr 2023, row 20).
- **(d) CHANGE two parts, keep two.** The Bank of England's own words, in its *Financial Stability Report: December 2022* (published 13 Dec 2022), section 5:
  - "borrowed against their gilts" names one of two routes. The Bank: "LDI funds generate leverage either through repo borrowing or via derivatives" (5.1), and the calls came "on their secured borrowing or ... on interest rate derivatives positions" (5.2) (rows 23-24). The Bank's single word is "leveraged" ("Leveraged LDI funds came under particular pressure"). Its plain gloss, in the Deputy Governor's letter of 5 Oct 2022, p.4: "use leverage (i.e. to borrow)" (row 31). CHANGE to "had borrowed against their gilts or used derivatives".
  - "forced to sell" KEEP: the FPC's own summary, "a vicious spiral of collateral calls and forced gilt sales" (row 25); every seller in the Bank's count held "an open gilt repo or interest rate derivative position" (Chart 5.2, row 27).
  - "pooled LDI funds above all" KEEP for pressure, not volume: "a particular problem for many pooled LDI funds, given operational lags and the large number of smaller investors" (FSR 5.2); "pooled LDI funds became forced sellers of gilts" (Breeden, 7 Nov 2022); but segregated mandates, "85-90% of the market", "were also contributing to selling pressure" (rows 26, 28, 30).
  - "within days" CHANGE: what happened within days was the price move (30-year gilt yields up 140 basis points over four days from 21 September, FSR 5.2); the pooled funds' investors "were typically given a week, in some cases two, to rebalance" (Breeden, row 29). No Bank document gives a day count for the banks' calls. The mechanism is two clocks, not one.
  - "September-October 2022" KEEP: sales counted 23 September to 14 October; the Bank's purchases 28 September to 14 October, "13 business days" (rows 27, 36).
  - **Corrected words (content, not prose):** "In the 2022 gilt crisis the funds forced to sell were those that had borrowed against their gilts or used derivatives, pooled funds above all: long gilt yields jumped more than a percentage point in a few days, and the banks on the other side asked for more collateral faster than the funds' pension-scheme investors could send cash." Laura's turn after it is unchanged (Q214: "Laura borrows nothing, so no fall in prices can make her sell; her one planned sale, the 2031 move, waits for a price near the funds' value").
- **Judge's note (pensions):** the PPF's quote marks around 'insolvent' are a signal a UK pensions judge will read: schemes do not go insolvent, employers do. Quoting "short-term liquidity, not fundamentally the solvency" shows the team knows the difference.
- **Judge's note (general):** the crisis was a race between two clocks. Prices moved in days; pension schemes' cash took "a week, in some cases two". Laura has no second clock, because nothing she holds can ask her for cash.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 17 (3e).** The PPF's quote marks around 'insolvent' are there because schemes enter the PPF when the employer fails. Leading with "short-term liquidity, not fundamentally the solvency" is the sentence a UK pensions judge cannot fault, and it is closer to Laura's case (the 2022 problem was cash on a day).
2. **Row 29 (3e).** Two clocks: yields moved in days, and pooled funds' investors had "a week, in some cases two". It is the mechanism under the parallel, in the Bank's words, and it says why Laura cannot be caught: no one can ask her for cash.
3. **Row 8 (3c).** Claim (b) is word for word, with its printed page (p.14) read off the review's own footers. The writers can cite it with a page and lose nothing.
4. **Row 23 with row 24 (3e).** "Borrowed against their gilts" names only repo; the Bank says repo "or via derivatives", with calls on both. A bond judge would notice the gap. The change costs four words.
5. **Row 22 (3e).** By July 2025 the PPF's own word was "small", not nil. Date "no claims" to November 2022, and the parallel keeps its strength without claiming a total nobody has.

## Gaps

- **The PPF 2024/25 annual report's page** for the "small" sentence was not fixed: the passage was read in excerpts, which placed it in the actuarial section near p.146. Read it in full before citing a page.
- **Two documents were read only in excerpt:** the PPF's 6 Dec 2023 letter and the Pensions Regulator's FOI page on discovery of the LDI crisis. Neither is needed for the report's sentence.
- **No Bank of England document gives a day count for the banks' calls** on LDI funds. The day counts found are TPR's (five days for buffers, three to four working days to settle; Q214, Q207) and Breeden's "a week, in some cases two" for investors. A source on the usual margin-call settlement time (for example ISDA's or a dealer's terms) was not looked for.
- **The RBA statements of March, April and June 2021** were not read; the review's table skips them, so the last month that said "2024 at the earliest" is not fixed (May 2021 did; July 2021 did not).
- **No later PPF document** was found that names the 2022 episode as a cause of any particular claim; "small" (2025) is the latest word found.
- **Not re-read:** the government's independent Review of the RBA (2023, pp.52 and 62; Q212 read it on 22 Nov), the 1 Nov 2022 minutes in full, and Hauser's speech in full (excerpts only).
- **What the store already held** (greps in the header): the February 2021 words through the review's table, "a promise" (three rows), "No schemes became" (one row), "repo borrowing or via derivatives" (Q207-004), "proved incorrect" (Q212-008) and Breeden's "poorly managed leverage" through the Committee's report.
