# Q48. What a Taiwanese funder asks a founder to prove about her own share of a building or residency, when, in what form, and what happens if it falls short

- **Date:** Mon 16 Nov 2026, Sydney (every source read Mon 16 Nov 2026).
- **Report parts:** 3d (words for co-sponsors: what her owned figure means to a Taiwanese funder), 3b (the facility contribution: how a co-sponsor's grant scales with what she actually spends), 3e (assumptions and limits: her money is US dollars in Treasuries, while Taiwan's rules count Taiwan-dollar spending). Most rows are for the store only.
- Notes for the Final Report store, not report prose.
- **Not repeated:** card Q11e rows 15-21 and claim 4 (GL001184's 50% cap, 「配合款額度高者」 priority, overruns 「不得要求追加補助數額」, the 30/40/30 instalments, heritage's 5% floor, the range as NT$3.11m-3.77m); card Q11d (GL001572's tiers and caps, GL001057's private 50% cap and four-year site right, NCAF paying after the project, the Foundations Act's 10% single-grantee cap and NT$30m minimum); card Q10d (the art-village rule FL049953 pt 8 documents); card Q9c; card Q11a (seed money and assurance); card Q22 row 12 (DGBAS pt 4(1)). Store checked: 0 hits for 存款證明, 0 for 專戶, 1 for 配合款 (Q11e-016).
- **Claims tested (each to keep, kill or change):**
  1. "A Taiwanese funder will want a bank certificate (存款證明) or other proof that the founder's share is in hand when she applies."
  2. "The founder's share must be paid in first as a lump sum, or held in a dedicated account (專戶), before the state pays."
  3. "A statement of Laura's Treasury holdings meets what Taiwanese funders accept as proof of the founder's share."
  4. "If the founder's share falls short, the grant is clawed back (追繳)."
- **Problem class:** proving committed matching money in a co-funded capital project: who must show what, when, so that one funder's money is not spent on a project the others never pay for. **Field that solved it first:** public grant administration (cost-sharing rules that pay a fixed ratio of verified spending), here in Taiwan's own budget rules (DGBAS, the central-local subsidy rule) and the Ministry of Culture's and cities' subsidy rules; foundation registration law and concession (BOT/ROT) tender practice for the two places where a balance is checked.

## Verdicts in one screen

- **Claim 1: KILL for grants; CHANGE for two other routes.**
  - **None of the fourteen arts, heritage and space grant rules read at source asks for a bank certificate, or a 專戶, for the owner's share.**
    - What those with an application list ask for (rows 1-7): a plan, a budget split by funder, proof of the right to use the building, and a signed undertaking (切結書).
    - The rules: MoC GL001184 pt 5, GL001057, GL001572, GL000106, GL001504, the 2026 regional-revival rule (地方創生), NCAF 2027, Taipei (two repair rules and the general arts grant), Tainan, Changhua, Hsinchu, Taichung (rows 1-9).
    - **Exceptions.** One 2016 heritage-bureau rule asks for 「相對自籌經費，並有證明文件」 without saying what proof (row 9). One Taichung rule asks for a copy of the group's bankbook without saying why (row 8).
  - **Self-funding is scored or ranked, not proved.**
    - The 2026 regional-revival rule gives 15% of the score to the self-funding ratio (row 6).
    - Tainan, Changhua and Hsinchu put owners who fund 70% or more first (row 35).
  - **Where a balance IS checked:**
    - Founding a foundation: 「應附存款證明」, cash only, Taiwan dollars, a time deposit (rows 38-41).
    - Bidding for a concession: audited accounts, an own-capital ratio of at least 30%, lenders' letters and bonds (rows 42-43).
  - **Judge's note:** a judge may picture a Taiwanese co-sponsor asking to see Laura's bank statement. Taiwan's grant rules never ask for one. They take her word, then check her receipts afterwards.
- **Claim 2: CHANGE.**
  - **No grant rule read requires the founder's share as a lump sum up front or in a 專戶.** 專戶 appears only for foundation capital (rows 39-40).
  - **The share is proved as it is spent.** Each instalment needs original receipts and a table splitting the cost between the owner and each funder (rows 16-19). The last payment is 「依補助比率及工程結算金額，核實撥付」 (paid at the ratio of the final, checked works bill).
  - **Several rules release the state's money only after the owner has spent.**
    - Changhua pays nothing until the works are 50% done, with receipts (row 20).
    - The MoC's space rule pays grants up to NT$1m once, after completion (row 21).
    - The central-local rule says 「不得先行支用補助款」 (the grant may not be spent ahead of the matching share) (row 22).
  - **Taipei goes the other way.** Its repair grants pay 70% within ten days of approval; its 2016 rule paid 30% (row 23).
  - **So** "the founder pays first" (Q11e: the founder's site came first) also holds for timing in several rules, but not all of them.
  - **A public co-funder proves its share with a council vote, not a balance.**
    - The proof is 「納入預算或追加預算證明」 (proof the money is in the budget), or a council letter agreeing to advance it (row 24).
- **Claim 3: CHANGE. It is more than any application asks for, and a different kind of thing from what is counted later.**
  - **At application,** nothing is asked, so a Treasury statement is more than enough.
  - **At each instalment and at close-out,** the rules count money the applicant body has actually spent in Taiwan, on receipts. Owning US Treasuries is not spending.
  - **For a foundation,** the registry asks for a Taiwan-dollar deposit certificate. New Taipei's sample charter says 「不得以外幣方式存放」 (may not be held in foreign currency) (row 39).
  - **After founding,** the Foundations Act lets the foundation buy 「公債、國庫券」 (government bonds, treasury bills). Whether US Treasuries count is not stated (row 41).
  - **Keel reading** (no ruling on Laura; Taiwan law is out of scope): her owned figure answers a question the rules leave open, "will the owner's share be there when the bills come?" To count under them, the money must reach the residency's own body and be spent in Taiwan dollars (row 44).
- **Claim 4: CHANGE.**
  - **Spending less shrinks the grant in proportion.**
    - Taipei's public-art rule: 「實際支出之配合款不得低於本局核定之補助款；如…低於…應按比例繳回」 (the matching money actually spent may not be below the grant; if it is, return it in proportion) (row 25).
    - MoC GL001057, Changhua, Taichung and Hsinchu work the same way (rows 26-27).
  - **Cancellation (撤銷), clawback (追繳) and one- to five-year bans** are for false papers, misuse, hidden other funders, unapproved changes or work not done (rows 29-31).
  - **Judge's note:** in Taiwan a co-sponsor's grant is a ratio of her spending, not a fixed sum. If her share comes in low, theirs falls with it. Her owned bottom figure is what lets a co-sponsor size its grant without that risk (row 45).

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 25 (3d, 3b).** Taipei's rule says the matching money actually spent may not be below the grant, or the grant goes back in proportion. Row 45 draws the consequence. A Taiwanese co-sponsor's grant is a ratio of her spending, so the bottom she owns is the number they can safely match. This is the clearest "why the range is owned" a funder judge will see.
2. **Row 11 with rows 12-13 (3b, the conceit).** Taiwan's own grant law writes the state's figure in pencil:
   - 「暫匡數」 (a provisional sum), settled on receipts;
   - 「先行匡列」 (earmarked ahead), waiting on a council vote;
   - cut if the legislature cuts.
   Laura's figure is the one in ink. These are strikingly dissimilar parallels: a ministry's rulebook and a private founder's bond ladder.
3. **Rows 16, 20-22 (3d).** In Taiwan the founder's share is proved by receipts at each instalment. Changhua, small MoC grants and the central-local rule pay the state's money only alongside or after the owner's spending. The judge sees why owned money matters in that system: the owner must spend before the state does.
4. **Rows 39-41 (3e).** The one Taiwanese proof form found is a Taiwan-dollar time deposit, 「不得以外幣方式存放」. Her US-dollar Treasuries meet the spirit (government bonds are what Taiwan's law lets foundations hold) but not the form. Converting is a step in December 2032, and the Taiwan-dollar risk on it is what the half kept covers.
5. **Row 24 (3d).** A public co-funder proves its share by a council vote (「納入預算證明」), a figure that a later vote can cut. Laura proves hers by ownership. This gives co-sponsor language a fair comparison, not a boast.

## Gaps

- **Foundations Act Art 19 「公債」:** whether foreign (US) government bonds qualify is not stated in the Act. No ministry interpretation was found.
- **The heritage bureau's current rule:** not checked for the 2016 clause 「相對自籌經費，並有證明文件」 (Q11e read the 2026 text in part).
- **NCAF's grant contract template and its payment clauses** were not read.

- **No rule was found on proof for a foreign founder's money sent from abroad.** Foreign-exchange and tax steps are out of scope.
