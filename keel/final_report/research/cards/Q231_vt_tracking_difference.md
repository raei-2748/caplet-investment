## Verdicts in one screen

**Claim 1, "VT has trailed its index by about its fee": CHANGE.**
- **Long periods hold.** To 30 Sep 2026, VT trailed its spliced index by these amounts a year:

  | Period | VT at NAV | Index | Gap a year |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Ten years | 12.32% | 12.40% | 0.08 |
  | Five years | 11.34% | 11.39% | 0.05 |
  | Since 24 Jun 2008 | 8.78% | 8.84% | 0.06 |

  Source: Vanguard fact sheet F3141.
- **Fair value removed.** Vanguard's own split of the gap to 31 Oct 2026 removes the part due to fair-value pricing. What is left is 0.05 a year over 3, 5 and 10 years, and 0.07 over the fund's life (Keel arithmetic on Vanguard's columns).
- **The fee was higher than 0.06% for most of the fund's life:**
  - 0.30% in 2010
  - 0.22% in 2012
  - 0.19% in 2013
  - 0.18% in 2014
  - 0.14% cut to 0.11% in 2017
  - 0.08% in 2021
  - 0.07% from 2022
  - 0.06% from 1 Feb 2025

  So over long periods VT trailed by about today's fee, and by less than the fees it actually charged.
- **Single years do not hold.** Calendar years ran from +0.64 (2012) to -0.71 (2013).
- **The life figure is also less firm than it looks.**
  - The Feb 2014 prospectus has VT ahead of its index by 0.17 a year from launch.
  - The Feb 2017 prospectus has it behind by 0.06 a year from launch.
  - The yearly gaps of 2014-2016 add up to only about -0.5 (Keel arithmetic).
  - So the early benchmark history appears to have been restated, for a reason not found. This is CONFLICTING.
- *Judge's note:* the fund charged 0.18-0.30% in its first six years, yet its ten-year lag is under a tenth of a point. Most of any one year's lag is a clock difference that reverses the next year: the fund prices foreign shares at the New York close, the index at each local close.

**Claim 2, "a cost Laura can see in advance": CHANGE.**
- Only the fee is known in advance: 0.06% in the 27 Feb 2026 prospectus (management 0.05% plus other expenses 0.01%).
- The rest shows up only afterwards, and it changes sign:
  - fair-value timing, -0.20 in the year to Oct 2026;
  - securities lending, about +0.01 a year;
  - tax reclaims;
  - a standing accrual for foreign capital-gains tax, about 0.065% of assets;
  - sampling.
- VT's tracking error is 0.92% a year, about fifteen times its average gap (Keel arithmetic).
- Plain version: the fee is the cost she can see; the tracking difference is the cost she can count only afterwards. Over ten years or more it has come out close to the fee.

**Claim 3, "a fund can beat its index after fees": KEEP, but the usual reason is the yardstick, not lending.**
- iShares MSCI ACWI charges 0.32% and still beat MSCI ACWI Net:
  - by 0.11 a year over ten years (12.48% against 12.37%);
  - by 0.13 a year since March 2008 (8.49% against 8.36%).
- MSCI's Net index deducts the "maximum" withholding rate for investors "who do not benefit from double taxation treaties", including 30% on US dividends, which a US fund never pays.
- VT's index already deducts the treaty rates a US fund does pay, so VT's lag is the honest one.
- VT's securities lending is about 0.01% a year, a fifth of its expenses (Keel arithmetic on the April 2026 semi-annual report).
- VT itself beat its index in 4 of 16 calendar years, and over the year to October 2026 once fair value is removed (+0.05).
- *Judge's note:* the fund that "beat its index" costs five times as much as VT. Compare funds' own returns on like indexes, never each fund against its own yardstick.

**Claim 4, "premiums matter only on trade days": KEEP, sharpened.**
- Over VT's life, its market-price return equals its NAV return: 8.78% and 8.78%.
- Single calendar years differ by up to 0.31 points, because year-end premiums differ (Keel arithmetic). A holder's premiums wash out.
- The cost is the premium paid on the buying day minus the premium received on the selling day. Vanguard: "The biggest risk arises when an investor purchases an ETF when it's trading at a substantial premium and then sells it at a substantial discount."
- Across US ETFs, Petajisto (2017) finds prices within a band of about 200 basis points around NAV, about 100 once stale prices are allowed for, wider for international funds.
- On the plan's two VT trade days, the price to compare is the fund's fair-valued NAV. On 20 Nov 2026 ACWI's fair-value adjustment alone was 0.67% (Keel arithmetic).

**Claim 5, "the TNA's 0.06% is the cost of owning the world through VT": KEEP as the fee; CHANGE as the whole cost.**
- The fee is right: 0.06% since 1 Feb 2025. The holdings figure is right too: 10,185 stocks at 30 Sep 2026, against the TNA's "about 10,000".
- Against a gross world index, the full yearly drag is the fee plus foreign dividend tax of about 0.1% of assets (Keel arithmetic on the semi-annual report).
- That tax is charged before VT's index is counted too. It is a cost of owning foreign shares, not of VT.
- *Judge's note:* in 1963-74 an American who bought foreign shares from a foreigner paid a 15% Interest Equalization Tax up front, which is 250 years of VT's fee (Keel arithmetic). In 1998-2001, holders of international funds lost 0.5-1.1% a year to traders exploiting stale prices. Today's 0.06% is the end of a long fall, and the fix for the stale-price leak is exactly what makes VT's single years look noisy.

## Best five for the report

1. **Row 3, Vanguard's own split of the gap (parts 2.5, Table 1's note).** Of the last year's -0.15, -0.20 was fair-value timing. Without it, VT's 3-, 5- and 10-year lag is 0.05 a year. This turns "it trailed its index" into "it delivered the index for about its fee", with Vanguard's own columns as the evidence.
2. **Rows 14-15, ACWI "beats" its index after a 0.32% fee (part 2.1, Table 1 rationale).** The lead comes from a yardstick that deducts 30% tax from US dividends a US fund never pays. A judge who reads benchmark gaps as skill has it backwards: compare funds, not each fund against its own index.
3. **Row 38, the 15% Interest Equalization Tax of 1963-74 (part 2.1, one clause at most).** In 1963-74 an American paid 15% up front to buy foreign shares from a foreigner, which is 250 years of VT's fee. It is a dated, statute-backed way to say how new it is to own the world cheaply.
4. **Rows 31-33, the stale-price leak and its cure (parts 2.5, 3e).** International fund holders lost 0.5-1.1% a year in 1998-2001 until fair-value pricing became standard. That cure is why VT's single years look noisy, so the noise is a sign of protection, not cost.
5. **Rows 34 and 37, premiums cost only on trade days (part 3e, Table 3).** VT's life return at market price equals its NAV return. The cost to watch is the premium paid in January 2028 minus the premium received in January 2031, measured against the fund's fair-valued NAV on the day.
