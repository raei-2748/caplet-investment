course useful, but not particularly meaningful in making our entire Final Report genuinely impressive and

## What the work is for

Team Caplet (six Sydney high-school students) writes the Final Report for the Wharton Global High School Investment
Competition 2026-27. The client is Laura Gao, a writer and graphic memoirist planning an artists' residency in Taiwan.
$300,000 at the start of 2027 and $150,000 at the start of 2028 (from her book advances, speaking and licensing); ten
fixed $50,000 payments each January 2033-2042 to run the residency, "with a high degree of certainty"; an operating
reserve from 2033; a facility contribution towards the building, with flexibility; in 2031 a dollar range and a
confidence level for co-sponsors (other funders). Caplet's plan (frozen, `keel/final_report/FINAL_IPS_as_submitted.txt`,
read it whole first, it is a one-page letter): her career is a large holding of shares she cannot sell, so the
portfolio leans the other way; ten Treasuries bought in January 2027, one maturing before each payment; the 2028
deposit completes them if needed, then most of it buys a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032 (the bottom of her range)
and the rest a world share fund, VT (the top); half the shares are promised to no one (her flexibility for overruns, a
stronger Taiwan dollar, a co-sponsor falling short); the other half moves into Treasuries before the 2031 meetings, so
whatever figure she names is money she already owns; only two reasons to change the plan, a missed deposit or a
Treasury default. Caplet's closing line: "You take risks on the page. Fair enough; that's your job. With these
promises, ours is to take none." The report has a conceit, pencil and ink: ink is a dollar figure owned in Treasuries
that will pay it on its date, pencil a figure nothing she owns will pay yet; Laura states a figure only once it is
inked (`keel/final_report/CONCEIT.md`).

## What Caplet wants from research (keel/EXPECTATIONS.md Part 2; read 2.1-2.10 in full)

- Wide reading "which no other team could ever do", from "the most unexpected places in the nooks and crannies of the
  web and the world"; niche over obvious; not only investing ("Every single part of our Final Report MUST be
  underpinned by a breathtaking level of research and analysis").
- Never name-dropping: every idea applied, explaining, testing or changing a decision in Laura's plan.
- Critique every idea: how Laura's case is like it, how it is not, did it work where it was tried. "The disanalogy is
  where the insight comes from."
- Synthesis and creation: "we will see further, because we will stand on the shoulders of giants".
- Find the connection nobody else would make: "Something to which a judge would think, 'aha'." An analogy must explain
  something about Laura's plan that was harder to see without it; at best it predicts something true we can check.
- The big picture, never minutiae. Caplet's own writing shows the bar: an essay that opens on Octavian's staged
  surrender in 27 BCE and argues through Rousseau, Plato, Polybius, Golding, Hobbes and Macbeth to "Consent, in the
  end, is the only currency that even those who despise it are forced to counterfeit."

## The test every idea must pass (this is what separates an idea from decoration)

A parallel earns its place only if what it maps is a RELATION that does work in the plan, not a surface likeness
(Gentner's structure-mapping: a good analogy carries a system of causes, not shared attributes). Ask:
1. Which decision in Laura's plan does it explain, justify, test or change? Name the decision.
2. What does the reader see after it that they could not see before? Say it in one plain sentence.
3. Where does it break? (Its disanalogy, and whether the break teaches something.)
4. Is it true, and read at source? (Primary text, page, act and scene, chapter, paper.)
5. Would a well-read finance judge already know it? If yes, it is pedigree, not an aha.

Refuse anything that is only colour, only a name, or only clever.

## What to write

One card, `keel/final_report/research/cards/<id>_<short_name>.md`:
1. Title line `# <id>. <the question in plain words>`, then the usual bullets (Date, Report parts, "Notes for the
   Final Report store, not report prose", Not repeated, Problem class).
2. `## The ideas, each in three plain sentences`: your three to six strongest ideas, each as (a) the parallel in one
   sentence a clever 16-year-old follows on one reading, (b) what it shows about Laura's plan, (c) where it breaks and
   what the break teaches. Then the decision it serves, the report part, the source, and "Pedigree or aha" with a
   reason. Write these sentences so they make sense on one reading: people and things doing things, in the order
   they happen, every "because" a real cause.
3. `## Rows for the store`: the ten-column harvest table exactly as ROUND2_WORKER_BRIEF gives it, 15-35 rows, never
   padded.
4. `## Best three for the report`: the three most likely to make a Wharton judge say "aha", with one line each on why.
