## The conceit in one screen (read `keel/final_report/CONCEIT.md` lines 11-210 for the whole of version 2)

- **Ink** is a dollar figure that Treasuries held for Laura will pay on its date. **Pencil** is a figure no Treasury
  held for her will pay yet: her future income, the 2028 deposit before it arrives, the world share fund's value
  before half of it moves, the interest cash earns while it waits, every forecast and model result.
- **The rule:** Laura states a figure only once it is inked. It is the IPS's own rule made visible ("whatever figure
  you choose is money you already own", IPS l.15), and it comes from her trade: in comics only the inked line is
  printed, and the pencils are rubbed out.
- **When each part is inked:** most of each of the ten $50,000 payments in January 2027; the rest of them, the extra
  Treasuries (the operating reserve's size above the ten) and the range's bottom ($95,000) in January 2028; the top
  ($115,000) in January 2031, when half the shares move into the late-2032 Treasury fund (IBTM), before the 2031
  co-sponsor meetings. Her flexibility (the other half of the shares) is never inked: co-sponsors never see it.
- **Turns already on file (do not re-find them):** pencils rubbed out after inking; non-photo blue; trim, bleed and
  safe area; dot gain; registration marks; knockout and overprint (retired); the key plate (retired); "let the ink
  dry" (retired); "ink only goes one way" (3b, Richmond 2006); "two inkings, two dates" (3c); "erase every pencil
  line and the page still reads" (3e); "in a market, a click is ink" (2.5); inkstone, ink stick and the artist's seal
  (Smithsonian; the seal is a signature, so it is out of 3d); a finished calligraphy stroke cannot be touched up;
  iron gall against carbon ink (British Library 2021, one line only); Taiwan's arts-grant rules that void a receipt
  "written in pencil" (card Q83); the GPO's "OK to print" signed only for what was checked (card Q82); pencil and ink
  in the law of deeds and leases (card Q114); Admiralty chart corrections "in ink" only from one source (card Q141);
  Priestley's rubber of 1770 and the pencil-eraser patent case (cards Q174, Q181); cheques in indelible ink (Q106).
- **Parallels card Q47 lists as misleading (never use):** the IPS or notes as pencil, draft, proof or plate; colour for
  the share fund; bleed for her flexibility; "ink is permanent" as a general truth; a US default as a smudge; inflation
  "fading the ink"; "redraw the page" for a change; the iBond funds as iron gall; "more ink is always better".

## What a row must do

- Each conceit row names the **relation in the plan** it maps, in the "Analogy/use for Laura" column: which figure or
  step, which date, which rule (for example: "the 2031 move: a mark made once, before the meetings, that cannot be
  undone"). A fact that only adds colour is a store row tagged PRESENTATION with "colour only, refused" in the use
  column, never a candidate turn.
- Say where each parallel **stops fitting** (the critique column). A parallel that pushes a reader toward "signed" or
  "promised" for ink is out: ink means owned (the law's own word, UCC 8-503), never signed.
- **Guards (CONCEIT V2.4):** never in tables or figures; never a comics or printing word in Laura's own words to
  co-sponsors (section 3d is plain: no conceit there); nothing about how Laura herself draws or works beyond the case;
  no puns on "issue", "draw" or "drawdown"; the January 2027 purchase is never pencil; the IPS, Trading Notes and WInS
  notes are never pencil, draft or proof.
- One turn per part of the report is the standing rule (voice rounds 2-16): a new candidate says which part it would
  serve and which existing turn it would replace or sit behind as a fact (most good finds sit behind a working word as
  a fact, not as a new turn). The judges marked down trade words a finance reader must decode: a turn must explain
  itself in the sentence that uses it.
- Claims to test are stated so they can be kept, killed or changed. A kill is as useful as a keep.
