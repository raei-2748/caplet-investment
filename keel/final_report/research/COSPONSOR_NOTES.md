## 2. The range and its confidence (NUMBERS 4, MODEL)

- **The range, on the 6 Nov 2026 curve: about $95,000 to $115,000** (unrounded $97,605 to $117,142). Dated in the
  sentence; a later curve is disclosed, never re-booked (the 13 Nov curve gives $105,000 to $125,000, NUMBERS 4 "On a
  later curve").
- **The rule behind each end, one step per clause.** What the late-2032 Treasury fund's own holdings pay if no more
  interest is earned, worked out on the trade day, cut by 0.5% for costs beyond the fee, rounded down to $5,000. The
  bottom is the floor bought in January 2028; the top adds the half of the shares moved into the same fund before the
  2031 meetings (January 2031 in the model).
- **When each end is fixed.** The bottom in January 2028; the top on the day of the move; after the move nothing moves
  either end. Her flexibility (the half kept, and anything the fund pays above the top) moves until January 2033.
- **The confidence, in two layers, for judges (3c, the team's voice).** Once the shares are moved: certain. Both ends
  are then paid by Treasuries held for her, counted as if their cash earned nothing more; no share price or interest
  rate can change them. Seen from today: which top she will reach is uncertain, a top of at least $100,000 in about 91
  of 100 simulated futures and $115,000 in about 57. Laura took a degree in statistics (case l.16), so the first layer is said
  in her own discipline: after the move her confidence is ownership, and the model only says, seen from today, where in
  the range the top lands (GOLDMINE 25, placed today).
- **What "certain" rests on, and what still could leave it short.** Strong words go on the Treasuries ("guaranteed",
  "no market can change what they pay"; Book 19). The top rests on IBTM, a fund that holds Treasuries maturing in 2032
  and pays about 15 December 2032 whatever it then has; its payout is not fixed to the dollar, and the 0.5% cut is the
  answer. What could still leave a stated end short: fund costs beyond the fee of more than 0.5% (about 2% in all would
  break the top, NUMBERS 8u), an early end or merger of the fund while waiting cash earns nothing (at most about $4,600,
  from her flexibility), a broker failure (late, and short only beyond what SIPC fills), or a US default. These belong
  to 3e and Table 3, never to Laura's words.
- **What she gives.** Her contribution is the lower of what the fund pays and the stated top; centrally the fund pays
  $122,503, so she gives $115,000 and $7,503 joins her flexibility. In every tested path the contribution equals the top.
- **Why nothing is said before the move.** A top named a year early would exceed what the move buys in about 11 of 100
  futures (NUMBERS 4: 2.9%, 6.2%, 8.6%, 10.6% for 1, 3, 6, 12 months early). Before January 2031 she can tell the
  residency's own planners "$95,000, held for me since January 2028", with any more named in 2031 (never "at least",
  section 8), and no figure to co-sponsors.

Three sentences carry it: rates before January 2028 set the bottom; shares before the move set the top; after the move
no market moves either end, and only her flexibility still rides on shares. The bottom moves with rates only before it
is bought, which is why the bottom is dated and why Table 2 shows lower bottoms when rates fall before 2028.

## 4. How the range protects the ten payments (case l.119-120)

- **The order of spending does it.** The 2028 deposit first completes the ten (the $2,388 top-up, then $36,774 of extra
  Treasuries that size the operating reserve: the first $39,162), then buys the floor, then VT. No end of the range is
  drawn from money the payments need, and no payment draws on the floor or the shares (CANON A5, A27).
- **The deletion test.** If every share were worth nothing, all ten payments would still be paid on their dates and the
  building would still receive the bottom, $95,000 on the 6 Nov 2026 curve (NUMBERS 4, 8u).
- **One fund, two lots.** IBTM holds both the 2033 payment's rung (bought January 2027) and the floor (January 2028),
  later the moved half; the payment lot comes first (case l.94: the reserve is set aside before the facility; CANON D2).
- **What a co-sponsor reads into it.** Ten years of running money already bought is the part campaigns usually lack
  (Kresge's building-reserves rule, Q11b-022; NFF's evaluation, Q11b-023; Artists Rep's "operating seed funds",
  Q204-014): her whole commitment is the viability signal the case names, not the building figure alone.

## 5. What the drafted fundraising passage must do (case l.123-124)

**Whose voice.** Laura's, in the first person, as she would say it to a funder in 2031: full sentences, the year named
each time, no finance jargon (no "custodian", "draw", "close date"; voice round 8), no conceit word (panel 5, voice round
16: 3d is plain, final), no outside parallel (D6). The team's own lead-in comes before it, in the team's "we", two or
three sentences for the judges: owning shows the money exists, a signed letter says it will be given and is what a gift
office counts; Lehman Brothers' own $10 million pledge to Spelman College (October 2007) paid $3 million when the bank
failed; Laura's money is held before she signs (cards Q200, Q213).

**What a co-sponsor needs to read, in this order (funder test, 16 Nov; panel 5; round 16).**
1. Purpose in two sentences, from the case's own material: who the residency serves ("artists, writers,
   designers, entrepreneurs, and educators", case l.78-79), why Taiwan, why her.
2. The range the case asks for, each end in its own sentence with its date, and then what the bottom is now: the part
   that never depended on her shares; since January 2031 neither end does (voice round 17, section 8).
3. How sure, in one sentence, with the Treasuries as its subject.
4. Why the top rather than the bottom: the half moved in January 2031 was the building's half from the start (IPS l.15:
   its returns "help determine your maximum facility contribution"; the other half is "promised to no one"), and once
   moved it is held, so she gives the top without touching her flexibility.
5. Her whole commitment: $50,000 a year for the running costs, 2033-2042, bought from January 2027 and complete since
   January 2028, and $115,000 for the building; if a total, "$615,000 in all", never "about" (section 8).
6. The date and form: committed in writing in 2031 in a signed pledge letter (amount, purpose, payment date), the
   $115,000 paid in January 2033 (CASE's counting rule, Q200-001; never "intends" or "plans", ASC 958-605-25-10).
7. What she asks of them, and what she does not: no matching condition; she gives "without waiting for anyone to match
   it" (Q11e-031; never "she pays first": she pays in January 2033, section 8).
8. How they can check: the letter names the fund, and co-sponsors see the broker's own statement of the building's
   money at the 2031 meetings and again before the December 2032 payout, with what it will pay beside the day's value
   (aha test 9; Table 3; C11, NUMBERS 8y: at the move it shows a little under $115,000).

**How "how sure" is said so people hear it right.**
- **Mechanism first, then the word.** "Held for me in US Treasuries since ..." before "certain". A founder's "certain"
  is heard through her stake in the answer; a holding a co-sponsor can check is not (card Q235; Q213: in every failed
  pledge read, the money was expected later or existed only on a statement). Measured: "I am certain" makes a figure heard as rough and as "at least"
  (Teigen and Juanchich 2024, Q235 rows 2-4).
- **No odds in her words.** After the move there are none to give; the 91 and 57 in 100 belong to the years before and
  to the team's 3c, for judges. No "95%", no "confidence interval", no "at least $115,000" ("at least" tells a listener
  the speaker does not know the figure; card Q52).
- **No condition in her words.** Listeners keep the figure and drop the condition (the RBA's review, Q94-002, Q94-003):
  so her figure carries none. The 0.5% is already inside it, never named as something that could trim it (funder test,
  readers 1 and 2: "reads as if $115,000 might be trimmed"). The letter's one undertaking, that
  she makes up any shortfall in what the fund pays (C3), removes a condition rather than adding one.
- **One idea per sentence for the figures.** Round 16: the range sentence holding purpose, two figures and two dates
  was followed first time by one reader in six; each end gets its own sentence, and every "it" points at one thing.
- **A range is heard as a margin for error** (funder test, reader 2: "every range we see is one"). So the bottom is said
  as what it is, the part held since January 2028, never as a possible outcome.
- **The currency.** Stated in US dollars, the currency her Treasuries pay, with its NT$ value on a named day for Taiwan
  readers: $115,000 is NT$3.77m at 32.768 (6 Nov 2026), labelled as that day's size, never a promise in NT$ (D9, card
  Q203; NUMBERS 8j). Keeping the NT$ value whole is a one-way intention from her flexibility, never in the letter.

**Never in her words or the lead-in:** "lead gift", "secured", "covers the decade", "escrow", "at least", "intends",
"plans", "guaranteed" on the fund's payout, "the largest share", "matching", "the RBA promised", any conceit word,
"cushion", "what she keeps" where a co-sponsor may read it.
