# Judge 1: blind read of the experience pool

Lens: a Wharton finance professor scoring only on Wharton's guide for "Articulation of Competition Experience"
(teamwork, communication, learning, research and decision process, growth and response to challenges), with
the IPS and Trading Notes read alongside. Scores for all 103 events are in `judge_1.csv` (W fit, V vivid,
C credible and plain, S strategy link; 1-10 each). I scored hard: only 21 events total more than 28 of 40.

## Top 12, in order

Where two ids tell the same story, the stronger one stands for both, so the twelve are twelve different stories.

1. **X076, the model that sank our own first plan (35).** The team kills its own share-heavy plan before a
   single trade because it missed a payment 40.8% of the time without the 2028 deposit. Learning, honesty and the
   origin of the whole strategy in one event.
2. **X100, the "copout" objection (33).** A teammate challenges the one-fund choice, and the team answers with a
   test it wrote before running any numbers (0.8991 against a bar of 0.90, then failing the every-run rule). Real
   disagreement settled by a rule set in advance: exactly the decision process judges ask to see.
3. **X026, our own trade note's warning came true (33).** A written prediction on 7 Oct (a fall of 0.2 points in
   yields pushes the ladder over $300,000) comes true on 22 Oct and becomes the IPS's completion rule. Ties all
   three deliverables together. Stands for X087 and X028.
4. **X018, her career is a stock she cannot sell (33).** The client insight that took six weeks to find its words
   and ended as the first line of the IPS. The strongest piece of client knowledge in the pool. Stands for X101.
5. **X044, no bond for the missing years, and IBTN was INSCORP Inc. (33).** A real gap in the simulator turned into
   the ladder's two-instrument design, plus a near-miss a judge will remember and smile at.
6. **X084, last year's lessons (32).** Four returning members, a 54% loss on FMC, trades outside the rules and no
   feedback; three rules carried into this year. The cleanest "growth" story available, and fully plain.
7. **X013, a bond fund never matures (32).** Reading TLH's holdings (74.7% after 2042) moved the plan from matching
   rate sensitivity to one dated holding per payment. A finance professor will respect this one most.
8. **X103, we wrote our own IPS as a letter (31).** The team takes the pen on the last day and writes "Dear Laura".
   A strong ending with a quotable last line. Use this version, not X008.
9. **X006, the cheapest the payments had been since 2002 (31).** $470,249 in August 2020 against $289,119 on 28 Sep
   2026: the reason the plan buys certainty first. Stands for X020, X089 and X032.
10. **X082, a former Wharton judge says the IPS cannot make it (30).** An outside blow with no reasons given, met by
    diagnosis rather than argument: "Laura is barely in it." A clear turning point. Use this version, not X054.
11. **X063, "metaphors alone does not count as creativity" (30).** The team leader rejects packaging and asks what
    the strategy's biggest strength is; the answer (both ends of the 2031 range owned before she names them) is the
    plan's real creativity. Stands for X068.
12. **X072, the donor study we had read backwards (29).** A source check reversed a claim about seed money (a
    "nearly sixfold increase"), which is why the bottom of the 2031 range is treated as the part co-sponsors can
    book. Fresh research on the co-sponsor side that few teams will have.

Next in line: X059 (which portfolio WInS should show, a real vote with a cost), X053 (the October trade we never
made), X057 (4.22% from a search summary against 5.17% in the Treasury table), X045 (promise only what is already
owned), X098 (the sector rule that was not a rule).

## The arc (seven events)

**X084 -> X076 -> X044 -> X100 -> X026 -> X082 -> X103**

A team that lost last year on stock picks and a rule breach kills its own share-heavy plan, builds a
payment-by-payment ladder from what the simulator actually lists, defends its one world fund against a teammate's
challenge with a test set in advance, watches its own trade note's warning come true and writes the fix into the
IPS, then hears from a former judge that "Laura is barely in it" and answers with a letter to her built on her career.

- Beginning: X084 (what we carried in) and X076 (the plan we threw away).
- Building: X044 (designing around a real gap) and X100 (disagreement inside the team, settled by a rule).
- Challenge met: X026 (the market tests a written prediction, and the rule goes into the IPS).
- Turn: X082 (an outside verdict that the client is missing).
- End: X103 (the team's own letter, opening on Laura's career; X018 supplies the idea's history if space allows).

## The three most likely to hurt

1. **X081, a thousand pieces read in a night.** No student team reads Montaigne, De Quincey and Wollstonecraft at
   hourly intervals through one night. A judge who doubts this one event will doubt every other event in the section.
2. **X079, mock judges on "different models".** It says the team's drafts were scored by machine judges, that one
   model was playing every judge, and that the team's own 5-1 vote was overturned by them. A reader concludes the
   team outsourced both the writing and the judging, which is the opposite of the teamwork and growth the criterion
   asks to see.
3. **X024, handing over the decisions and losing sight of the work.** The team leader writes that he had no idea what
   was happening in the research and later that the team could not follow what had been achieved. Honest, but it tells
   a judge the students did not understand their own plan, which undercuts every claim of learning in the report.

Close behind: X021 (it points judges to two problems in the locked reflections, including inflation risk called
"nonexistent" against the case) and X069 (the team's instructions "just being blatantly ignored"). Events built on
internal machinery (X011, X012, X027, X030, X083, X091, X094, X096) score low for the same reason and add nothing a
judge would credit. X092 is vivid (00:30 in Sydney, a teammate asleep waiting for the open) but only usable if the
wrong-row click is told without the "automated browser" detail.
