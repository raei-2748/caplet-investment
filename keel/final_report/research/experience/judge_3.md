# Judge 3: blind read of the event pool

Lens: a fixed-income portfolio manager at a large asset manager who has run liability-matching books and mentored
young analysts. I reward sound judgement, a decision process I can follow, honesty about mistakes, rules learned the
hard way and real understanding of the client. Scores are in `judge_3.csv` (W, V, C, S out of 10; total out of 40).
I was hard: only about one score in six is above 7, and no event tops 34.

## Top 12, in order

Where two ids tell the same story I kept the stronger one and named its twin in brackets.

1. **X076: the model that sank our own first plan.** The team ran its own share-heavy plan through 200,000 markets, saw it miss a payment 40.8% of the time if the 2028 deposit never came, and dropped it before a single trade. This is the moment a liability manager is born.
2. **X026: our own trade note's warning came true** (pair with X087, merge X028). A written test on 7 Oct ("if yields fall more than about 0.2 points, the 2028 deposit tops up") came true on 22 Oct and became the IPS rule; X087 adds the sharpest line in the pool: the simulator's bonds gained while Laura's real purchase got dearer.
3. **X084: last year's lessons.** Rules learned the hard way (trades outside the rules, a stock that fell 54%, "clarity beats complexity" ignored), turned into three rules this year's eleven trades all kept.
4. **X013: a bond fund never matures.** Reading TLH's holdings (74.7% after 2042) moved the team from matching rate sensitivity to one dated holding per payment; this is the difference between immunisation and cash-flow matching, learned from a primary source.
5. **X101: her career is a share she can't sell, tested after submission** (merge X018, X001). The client idea from day one, put to data after the freeze, with the two lines in the IPS that overpromise named rather than hidden.
6. **X044: no bond for the missing years, and IBTN was INSCORP Inc.** A gap in the listed bonds became the design (iBond funds for 2033-37), a ticker trap was caught, and the team learned that these funds pay net asset value, not par.
7. **X029: the promise we called certain was the weakest one.** Reading the plan like a lender showed the payments, not the gift, were most exposed; a pay-the-senior-claim-first rule cut shortfalls from 8.4% to 0.01% and removed a clash with a submitted reflection.
8. **X100: the "copout" objection.** A real disagreement inside the team, answered by a test written down before any number ran; gold and property missed the bar by 0.001 and the every-run rule, so VT stayed.
9. **X082: a former Wharton judge says the IPS cannot make it** (merge X054). The team treated a blunt "no" as a diagnosis, and the diagnosis ("Laura is barely in it") changed the whole IPS.
10. **X020: certainty on sale, briefly** (merge X006, X089). $300,000 bought the ten payments in about one month in four since 1871 and in none from mid-2007 until this autumn; the 1923 example (shares beat bonds, yet the 1937 and 1938 payments bounce) is sequence risk told as a story.
11. **X033: the stale prices.** Two WInS bond prices sat 111 and 92 basis points off the Treasury curve; the team swapped a bond and wrote a 0.25-point check every later order passed. Exactly what I would want a junior to do before trading.
12. **X103: we wrote our own IPS as a letter** (merge X008). The team kept the plan and rebuilt only how it was explained, around Laura rather than the instruments; the clearest evidence of growth in communication.

Close behind: X072 (the donor study read backwards, so the bottom of the range is treated as seed money co-sponsors can book), X053 (the October trade we never made, a pre-written trigger that did not fire), X045 (promise only what is already owned), X098 (the sector rule traced to a 2015-16 guidebook).

## The arc (seven events)

**X084 -> X076 -> X013 -> X026 (with X087) -> X082 -> X103 -> X101**

One line: a team that broke the rules and lost money picking stocks last year kills its own share-heavy plan, learns how real Treasury instruments behave, watches the market test the top-up rule it wrote in its own trade note, is told by a former judge that Laura is missing from the plan, rewrites the IPS itself around her career as a stock she cannot sell, and then tests that idea honestly after the freeze.

Beginning: last year and the first model (X084, X076). Turn: the judge's "no" (X082). End: the team's own letter and the honest test of its central claim (X103, X101). X013 and X026 carry the middle and give the bond reader the evidence that the team understood its instruments and its main risk.

## Three to avoid

1. **X024 (and X011): handing over the decisions.** A team leader telling someone else to "make your own call", then saying he had no idea what was happening, tells a judge the students did not own their strategy. Wharton says the decisions must be the students'; under "decision-making process" this is the worst signal in the pool.
2. **X021 (and X017): thirteen edits rejected on deadline night.** It shows the team overruling accuracy checks for tone on a locked, judged deliverable, and it points the reader to a submitted reflection calling inflation risk "nonexistent" against the case. A bond reader would mark that down twice.
3. **X081: a thousand pieces read in one night.** No group of students reads 1,000 essays between 23:45 and 05:43; it reads as invented, and one event a judge disbelieves makes every other one suspect. It is also about prose, not Laura.

Also risky as written: X079 and X069 (they describe machine judges and an office ignoring the team's instructions, which no student team would write), and X092, whose Sydney-at-00:30 story is the most vivid in the pool but must not say the wrong click was automated. X034 names a prescription stimulant's label in an investment report.
