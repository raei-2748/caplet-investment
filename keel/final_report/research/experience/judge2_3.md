# Reader 3, second blind pool (Y001-Y142)

Lens: a former finalist who now helps mark reflections. I gave most credit to events an outsider can follow and check: events that show what the team believed, what tested it and what is still unknown, and that a judge could not learn from the Trading Notes or the IPS. Each criterion was scored on its own, and the caps were applied: an event dated after Sat 7 Nov 09:00 Sydney scores at most 3 on A, and an event that mainly retells a Trading Note or the IPS scores 1 on E. Scores are in `judge2_3.csv`.

## Top 12, in order

1. **Y087 (21)**: The team dropped its own growth-first plan before the first trade, after a dated test (3.2%, 13.7% and 40.8% of paths missed a payment). This changed the spine of the plan, and an outsider can follow it in one reading.
2. **Y129 (21)**: A teammate called one world fund a "copout" and argued for picking stocks, a Taiwan fund or gold. A test written before any numbers were run settled it (0.8991 against a bar of 0.90, then the every-run rule failed). It's a real disagreement settled by a rule fixed in advance, and the decision was kept.
3. **Y017 (21)**: Critics called the plan "timid". The team priced smaller floors, and each $25,000 less floor gave about $2,000-3,000 in the middle against about $9,000-10,000 in a bad market. The $150,000 floor was kept, with the rejected option and its cost stated.
4. **Y049 (21)**: The same objection came back after the IPS ("over-insured for an entrepreneur") and was priced from the other side: more shares won about 63 times in 100, but the contribution fell under $95,000 in 26-79 futures in 100 against 3. The event states its own limit (US share history flatters this option). A is capped at 3.
5. **Y054 (20)**: The simulated rival team's plan beat the team's own designer, and the team switched to it. The bad case improved from $168,000 to $182,000 at p5, and from $120,000 to $169,000 in the worst historical window. This is a clear before and after for the recommendation.
6. **Y016 (20)**: The strongest rival plan was priced three times in one day ($9,000, $19,000, then $15,000). The plan ended with one honest price and "$90k v $140k" in a bad market, and the team adopted the rule "in which plan?".
7. **Y003 (20)**: The team found that 55.6% of 585 historical windows fell short and sized the margin to match. It rejected the bond desk's pooled design because pooling would break the IPS's promise to buy all ten in January. It shows what is still unknown (94%, worst case $12,117). A is capped at 3.
8. **Y074 (20)**: The team priced buying the floor in 2028 rather than waiting: waiting wins about 6 times in 10, but the 1929 start leaves about $23,000. The weak reason, "panic", was dropped and Schelling's reason, that others can rely on bonds already bought, was kept. A is capped at 3.
9. **Y096 (20)**: A test asked to support the 50/50 split killed half of its own brief, so the half is defended as a choice and never called "optimal". It's a strong example of learning, and the cap holds A at 3.
10. **Y076 (19)**: A lender's reading showed that the ten payments were the weakest promise in the plan, with 8.4% of paths short. The "gap first" rule brought that down to 0.01% while the median gift held. It names the part of the plan it changed, with figures before and after.
11. **Y009 (19)**: Since 1871, $300,000 could buy the ten payments in only about one month in four, and in no month from mid-2007 until this autumn. This turned "buy the payments now" from a cautious move into a matter of timing. The event also logs a figure it could not trace in its own results file.
12. **Y117 (19)**: The team chose between an all-bond book and a scaled book in WInS. A memo put the strongest case for each side before the vote, and the cost of the choice was stated (explaining every figure, and about $2,000 less cash). Judges see this book but are never told why it looks this way.

## The set for the reflection: Y087, Y129, Y017, Y060

These four each show a belief the team held, a dated test that answered it, and what was given up or owed to luck:
- Y087: growth would pay the payments.
- Y017: more shares would serve Laura better.
- Y129: one fund looks lazy. This is a real teammate disagreement settled by a test written in advance (C 4).
- Y060: the 7-8 Oct fills landed two trading days after the year's cheapest close. That was luck, and the decision was the IPS's rule for the opposite case.

No two of them tell the same story, and none retells the Trading Notes.

Rule checks:
- **Score of 4 or more on C:** Y129 and Y017.
- **A decision kept after a dated test, told with what was luck:** Y060, which covers the ladder's cost through 2026 as its one story. Y017 and Y129 are also decisions kept after dated tests.
- **Retelling the Trading Notes:** Y129 ends by quoting the VT note's principle, but the event is about the October argument and its test, not the note.

## Events I think are mis-described or unclear

- **Y001:** The title "only 12 days of 2026" is called a wrong figure by Y060, which counts 37 of 214 closes on the register. The sheet needs to say that 12 days is the count up to 28 Sep, on that day's model.
- **Y027 and Y110:** They give two different costs for the same 22 Oct close ($302,863 and $302,139), and Y060 says the claim that it crossed $300,000 on 22 Oct was void. Y027's point that "the prediction came true" rests on that crossing.
- **Y081:** It says the ladder's cost was "now over $300,000" on 23 Oct. Y004 gives the cost at 23 Oct prices as $297,636.
- **Y052:** It says $300,000 bought the ten "before this September on no day since 2008". Y095 found 19 Oct 2023 ($299,293), so this line contradicts that fact.
- **Y009 and Y042:** "In no month from mid-2007" is a monthly count, while Y095 counts days. The basis should be stated.
- **Y114 against Y083:** The shortfall "if interest earned nothing" is $22,192 in Y114 and $54,595 in Y083. The sizing each one assumes is not stated.
- **Y012, Y019 and Y050:** A word is missing after "our" or "we", apparently where something was removed for anonymity ("our found it passed", "from our were waiting", "waiting on we", "stopped scoring its own clarity").
- **Y106:** "The name we XR began as" is garbled.
- **Y082:** The text says "an automated browser click" but does not say what did the clicking.
- **Y064:** It reports a vote of 4-1-1 but shows no positions, so its C score cannot rise above 3.
- **Y019 and Y101:** They refer to internal ids (K-06, K-22, K-02, K-11, N-03, K-43) that an outsider cannot resolve. Y012, Y048 and Y079 have the same problem.
