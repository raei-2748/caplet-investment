## 1. The working rule, read at source (the likeliest reading, standing order 22: say which reading was taken)

Caplet's words (Book 18): "I don't mind AT ALL if something 'rested on your work or Keel's', as long as we don't need to

The reading taken, five tests for every sentence of 3f:
1. **A student is named only for an act the team's own record gives that student.** The named acts are Darren's
   objection, Eric's 00:30 night and Ray's sole charge of orders. Caplet's tab names all three, and Caplet's week-one
   summary of 15 Nov (`caplet/FR_WEEK1_2026-11-15.md` l.42, l.56) named Darren and Ray back to Caplet without objection.

   attribution Book 18 forbids.
3. **Nothing that was not a person is described as one.** No "readers", "reviewers", "judges", "outside readers" or "a
   blind test" for an AI process. Calling simulated clients "readers" implies people, which is a false attribution.
4. **The point must land without saying the AI's share.** If it cannot (the "too timid" readers), that half is out.
5. **No invented quote, feeling, scene, vote or reply.** The record holds no vote, no quote from any student except
   Darren's word "copout" and the journal's lines, and no reply to Darren (EXPERIENCE_RECORD section 4: "Feelings, who
   said what in meetings beyond the tab ... [are not in the record]").

Why naming beats hiding, from Wharton's own documents:
- Wharton's title page lists "Finalized team member names" (instructions l.72-73). First names in 3f map onto a page
  the judge has already read.
- Wharton's FAQ says trades are "a team decision" and "We strongly encourage teams to identify different roles" (card
  Q21 row 22). A named role (Ray) is what Wharton asks for.
- The criterion names "collaboration" and "disagreement" (l.148).
- Card Q80 rows 15-16 (Kim et al., 2004): readers weigh signs about integrity more heavily than signs about competence.
  A reflection that looks as if it hides who was in the room costs more than one that names three students.

## 3. Where "something hidden" comes from

1. **A rule in the plan: "never whose work it rested on" (SKELETON 3f l.263).** It was meant for the AI's share. As
   written it reaches the students too: Darren becomes "a teammate" (card Q13 (b) M1: "Avoid: naming who objected ('a
   teammate')"), Eric "one of us" (RANKING_4 l.130), and Ray "one person" (SKELETON 2.5 l.248). Each of those
   replacements hides a name that Caplet's own tab prints. Its source is Keel's gloss in standing order 22 ("It is told
   without saying who did the work") and RANKING_4 section 1. Book 18's words are narrower.
2. **"The plan called too timid in a blind test ... the readers never described" (SKELETON l.263; RANKING_5: "Who the
   readers were is never said").** The concealment is planned on purpose because the readers were simulated. A judge
   asks "by whom?" (the professor), and that is right: the event has no person to name.
3. **"Knockout to overprint" as 3f's frame (SKELETON l.207).** The finalist read it as hiding "the team behind a
   printing term"; the professor as burying "the lesson in print-shop jargon". The conceit is Caplet's call, and one
   turn per part stands. The fault is that in 3f the turn takes the place where people should be.
4. **Passive voice with no actor.** "A bar was written", "it was tested", "the headline was dropped", "the plan was
   called too timid". Caplet's own convention ("We changed strategy") cures most of it at no cost in truth.
5. **"Roles, not names" in the blind pack (BLIND_POOL_3 l.13, RERANK_RUBRIC l.55-56).** This was right for blind
   scoring, but it moved into the plan ("in the roles the record supports").
