## E1: "A copout": the world fund against its strongest objection

**Gate**
- Human: pass. A teammate calls the one world fund "a copout" and the team holds it to a bar written five days earlier; any team could have lived this.
- Purpose: pass. It explains the share fund, so the top of the range, and teaches that a default has to be defended for this client.
- Impressive: pass. Writing the bar for switching before running a number, then reporting a rival that nearly cleared it, is what a judge hopes to see.
- New: pass, narrowly. Trading Note 3 already says why one world fund (no picking winners); the objection, the written bar, the near-miss and the Taiwan concentration are not in it.

**Scores**
- A Influence, 4: names the part tested and kept (the share fund, the $115,000 top), but the before and after are about understanding, not a changed holding.
- B Reasoning, 5: four alternatives rejected against a bar set in advance, the closest named (10% gold, 10% property trusts, 0.8991 against 0.90), and the stake priced ($800-$1,600 of flexibility).
- C Disagreement, 4: the only teammate-against-plan disagreement in the pool, in the objector's own word, settled by a test written before it; but the objector has no role, and "as a normal team would" leans toward a straw man.
- D Uncertainty and learning, 5: belief, test and verdict, plus what is unknown (which fund does better) and how small the choice is beside luck ($800-$1,600 against $9,881-$41,258).
- E New and checkable, 3: dated and sourced, but it walks over Trading Note 3's ground, and "spread ratio", "fourteen of fifteen houses" and "the IPS's two reasons" need inside knowledge on one reading.

**Total: 21**

**Telling**
- The one detail that carries it: the bar dated 30 Sep, five days before the objection, and the closest rival at 0.8991 against 0.90, passing four runs in five when the bar asked for every run.
- What would make it corny: ending on a line like "sometimes the simplest choice is the bravest", reusing Trading Note 3's "vain illusion" as the moral, or making the teammate who said "copout" the fool of the story. (The "[fill in]" journal line is real but reads as untidiness to an outsider; I would leave it out.)

## What separates the top from the bottom

The top events each name a belief the team held, the dated test that broke or kept it, the part of Laura's plan that
came out of it, and one honest line on luck, and each has a single number a tired reader can carry away after one pass:
173 closes in 185, one market in thirty, a bar written five days before the objection. The bottom two are not weaker
work. E2 is the most human event in the pool and the easiest to follow, but no one held a second view, and what it
changed is how the orders were placed, not what Laura was told. E5 has the sharpest reversal in the pool, but it is told
in a lender's vocabulary (paths, coverage tests, who is paid first) that an outsider has to read twice, and it shares
its ending with E3, the 2028 deposit's first job. With fifty reports to read, the gap is whether the point survives one
reading and lands on a part of the plan.
