## 2. Wharton's five criteria

- Investment Strategy: 5. One rule (promised money in Treasuries, unpromised in shares), applied consistently from IPS to notes to report, and the plan refuses to retrofit the IPS.
- Client Knowledge and Objectives: 4. Career-as-a-stock, the 2028 deposit, co-sponsor behaviour, the Taiwan dollar and building costs are all used. Facility need is not estimated (the case allows this), so "responsible" is argued only from what she keeps.
- Portfolio Analysis: 4. Reinvestment pricing, funded-with-zero-cash tests, 585-start replays and a failure-first 3e are above what I usually see. The 7.5% equity assumption and the reliance on forward curves are less strong.
- Articulation of Competition Experience: 3. The planned moments are genuine (Darren's "copout", the first plan failing its own test) but the notes constrain them ("never credited to a named student", "analysis as we"), which risks a thin and over-edited reflection.
- Creativity and Presentation: 4. The pencil-and-ink image is distinctive and defensible, and the exhibits are strong. The risk is density and over-managed wording, which would put a first reader's clarity at about 3.

## 3. Likely?

- Top 50: Yes, likely (about 85%). The content stands out and a consistent strategy with real numbers is what judges reward.
- Top 10: Possible, not yet (about 35%). It depends on whether a first reader gets the point on one reading, and clarity is the gap.

## 4. Claims a fixed-income professional would challenge

1. **"certain" on the 2031 range** (3.0, 3c: "after the move, certain, both ends owned in Treasuries held for her, with no default clause"). Challenge: the range is defined as what the funds hold, so its certainty is by construction; the real uncertainty is which range she can state, and the bottom is set by January 2028 yields. The ends rest on a fund's net asset value at its end, with fees and possible early end. Answered? Mostly. INTENT: "the real uncertainty is earlier, which range she will be able to announce"; Table 2's "rates 3 points lower" row shows $75,000 / $95,000; the subject of "certain" is the range once stated, not the figures; 3d says "stated end". The report should also state, once, in 3c itself that the bottom cannot be known until January 2028.
2. **The ten payments "owned" when five rest on iBond funds** (Table 1: IBTM, IBTO, IBTP, IBTQ, IBTR). Challenge: a fund's terminal payout is NAV, not par, and a board can end, merge or move a fund. Answered: TNA l.15 and the Table 3 note say it; Table 3 row 4 prices an early end of IBTM ("$4,589 short" at worst with cash at zero; nothing short at today's forwards); the 0.5% margin covers trading costs. Residual: the plan never says plainly that one fund (IBTM) carries the 2033 payment, the bottom and the top, a single-instrument concentration and a single sponsor. Table 3 row 4 touches it ("one notice touches all three") but the prose should.
3. **"If cash waiting earns nothing"** is a stress test, not a forecast, and a 0.15% fee on the waiting ETF is charged. Challenge: zero is too kind for a fee-bearing ETF? Answered (8h's "fee" block; "$103 of the waiting fund's 0.15% fee, with $1,177 to spare"). Good.
4. **The ten cost $302,287 against a $300,000 deposit, with the IPS saying "roughly $300,000".** Answered in 3a with the top-up ($2,388) and IPS l.13's "we'll simply complete it with the 2028 deposit". The report must not read as revising the IPS and the plan guards that ("never 'new standard'").
5. **Reinvestment-rate risk "priced at today's rates"** (585 months, median $16,055 when short, $35,000 at 1 in 20). Challenge: this is a replay with a modelled yield path; "forward rates" are not forecasts. Answered: 3a states "today's rates", Table 2's note states yield-move assumptions. Residual: the plan sizes by today's forwards and the actual 2028 deposit buys at 2028 yields, so "$39,000" is itself uncertain (1-in-20 $34,686 to $55,624 in WORDS). The report should print that range once.
6. **"Fund costs beyond the fee" fixed at a 0.5% margin.** Challenge: where does 0.5% come from? Answered: the five ended iBond Treasury funds' worst year beyond the fee is about 0.15% (WORDS/3d note), 0.27% the worst of three measures, so 0.5% is nearly double. Table 3 says the margin pays costs, not lost interest. Good and honest; the plan already says it fails past about 2% of what the late-2032 fund pays.
7. **7.5% a year for world shares** ("J.P. Morgan's 2027 assumption, above the middle of published forecasts"). Challenge: a high-end forecast. Answered: top states $115,000 for any share return from 4% to 10% (3c), because the top is mostly owned Treasuries; the 7.5% only moves her flexibility. The plan correctly dropped the "median" claim.
8. **A "meaningful" contribution of $115,000 of $450,000, flexibility about $21,200.** Challenge: facility cost unknown, so "responsible" cannot be tested. Answered partly: 3e area (1)-(5) and the 25% building-cost cover ("if shares earn the 7.5% assumed"; 23.6% at 6.5%). A skeptic will say flexibility is small and shares-dependent.
9. **Inflation and nominal liabilities.** Nominal Treasuries match nominal payments (TNA l.15: no TIPS needed). Challenge: the building cost rises. Answered by the flexibility and Table 3 row 7 (building-cost band 1.5% to 5.0% a year; DGBAS index).
10. **Early-2031 move of half the shares to Treasuries.** Challenge: this is a rules-based de-risking at a fixed date, with a market-timing flavour (the "2.9% a month early" cost). Answered in 3c's aha.
11. **"Barring a US default" appears only once (3e)** after Caplet ruled it out of other sentences. As a professional I do not mind either way, but "guaranteed" about iBond funds would trouble me. The plan keeps the NAV caveat, so I accept it.

## 5. Three changes that would most raise the score

1. **3b: cut to one answer and one test.** Currently "Her flexibility: half the shares, promised to no one, about $21,200 on today's market rates, growing to about $28,700 with anything above the top ... covers a 10% stronger Taiwan dollar ... in about 93 of 100 simulated futures". Print $28,700 once (Table 2's last column) and give 3b prose only the $115,000, the $21,200 and the one stronger-TWD test. This lifts clarity from 2 to 3 or 4 without losing any analysis.
2. **3c: say the bottom's own dependence in one plain sentence**: "the bottom cannot be known until January 2028, when the fund is bought; at 3 points lower rates it is $75,000, not $95,000." The current line "the bottom said once, as the part held since January 2028" leaves this to Table 2. A professional wants the contingent date in the words next to "certain".
3. **3e/Table 3: make concentration explicit.** The current line "one notice touches all three" (Table 3 row 4 note) should be 3e's first sentence about the funds: IBTM carries the 2033 payment, the bottom and the top, and "not a Treasury" is the one thing that can move all three at once. It also gives the reader a natural lead from an early-end figure ($4,589 at worst) to the 0.5% margin.

(An optional fourth: Section 2.3's BMS 73%-to-23% figure is "directional only" in the plan's own words. Swap it for Laura's own quoted sentence and free the words for diversification evaluation in 2.2.)

## 6. The single biggest risk

Density. The notes show 20 to 44 ideas in 200-word parts, and every row has its "never" list. If the 27 Nov writer carries the guards into prose, the first reader will lose the answer, as panel 3 judged in 3a, 3c and 3e at clarity 2, and the report's best thinking (3a's zero-cash sizing, 3c's ownership range) will be missed. A report that is correct but cannot be repeated back loses to a plainer one.

## 7. The aha moments I would remember tomorrow

- "Ten payments rest on ten Treasuries ... paid even if cash waiting earns nothing." The one real forecast left in a ladder is the rate on reinvested interest, and the extra Treasuries take it out (3a).
- "the first plan's danger was one date, 1 Jan 2033, when all ten had to be bought with whatever the shares had become, not a crash" (3f): a liquidation-date risk, not a drawdown risk.
- "Jerzy Neyman, who coined the term confidence interval" (3c): a forecast range holds or it does not, but a range built of Treasuries held for her is a fact.
- "A comfort letter describes today; what she owns pays in 2033" (3d).
- "$302,287 of them pays $500,000" (2.1), turning TNA l.7's "won't create wealth" around.
- "No fall in share prices can." (3e).

Pencil and ink: it helps in a narrow way, as a definition ("ink is a dollar figure owned in Treasuries that will pay it on its date; pencil is a figure no Treasury she owns will pay yet") that makes the sort of every holding quick (Table 1's Treasury rows ink, VT the only pencil). It gets in the way where it becomes a vocabulary, a gloss to be remembered ("two inkings, two dates", "erase every pencil line", "the printed page shows only ink"), because a bond reader wants the engineering term first. Keep it to the definition, 2.0/2.1, 3c and the one turn in 3f; drop the rest. The plan's guard ("a turn a fresh reader cannot repeat back is said plainly instead") is right.
