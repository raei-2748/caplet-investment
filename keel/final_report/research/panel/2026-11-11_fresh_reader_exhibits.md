# Fresh reader, exhibits (Wed 11 Nov 2026)

Role: a first-round judge with a stack of fifty reports and about five minutes each. I know bonds and funds; I know
nothing about this team beyond what I read here. Each item read once, in order. Notes written after each item,
before opening the next. Reactions, scores and reasons only.

## 1. The case, page 3 (read for context, not scored)

What I carry forward: ten fixed $50,000 payments, 2033 to 2042, funded with "a high degree of certainty" and no
outside money; an operating reserve set aside at the start of 2033; then a facility contribution that keeps her
flexible; in 2031 she names a dollar range and a confidence, and the range must not threaten the ten payments.
Overpromising damages her credibility. That is the test I will hold each exhibit to.

## 2. The opening idea (INTENT notes: governing thought, six things)

**Five-second reading.** Money Laura has promised is held in Treasuries before she promises it, and only money
promised to no one is in stocks.

**Is that what they meant, and how sure.** Yes, very sure. The first sentence of the governing thought is the
clearest thing I have read so far. It answers the case's "high degree of certainty" in one line.

**What I looked at first; what I misread or read twice.**
- First: the opening sentence ("Money Laura has promised is owned in Treasuries before she promises it"). Good.
- Read twice: the parenthesis "half the shares moved into Treasuries before she names a figure; half promised to no
  one". Half of which shares? I assumed a stock fund bought with the second deposit, but nothing says so here.
- Read twice: item 3. It is one very long sentence with a rule inside a rule ("each end at what it pays if no more
  interest is earned, worked out when it is bought"). I came away with "about $95,000 to $115,000" and "lands inside
  it unless the US Treasury defaults", and not much else.
- Item 1: "the escrow verifier's standard" means nothing to me without explanation. The numbers ($444,500 reserve
  against $399,089 of payments, about 111% funded) I understood on one read.
- Item 2 caught me: the book cost $302,287 against a $300,000 deposit, and the team says so. That is unusual and
  it raised my trust.
- Item 5 is crisp ("only a missed deposit or a Treasury default changes the plan").

**One question before I trust it.** If the reserve is about 111% funded, why is the gift range only $95,000 to
$115,000 out of $450,000 deposited? I want to see what the extra safety costs her in dollars, in one place.

**Scores.** Clarity on one reading: 3 (governing thought alone 5; the six items 2, item 3 is the drag).
Want to keep reading: 4 (the rule is simple, defensible and on-case, and the admitted overrun is a hook).

## 3. Table 1, the holdings (image opened)

**Five-second reading.** About 90% of the $300,000 sits in ten Treasury holdings maturing one a year from Dec 2032
to Nov 2041, about 9% in one world stock fund (VT), and the rest is cash and purchase costs.

**Is that what they meant, and how sure.** Mostly, fairly sure (about 70%). The ladder shape is plain. What I am
not sure of is what the ten holdings are meant to pay, because nothing in the table links a row to a payment year.

**What I looked at first; what I misread or read twice.**
- First: IBTM at 32.50%. It is four to five times every other row and it is the first number my eye lands on. I
  thought it was a mistake until I reached the note ("IBTM does two jobs: the 2033 payment and the floor"), and even
  then "the floor" only means something because I had read the opening notes. A judge who skipped them would not know.
- Second: the subtotal "Treasuries for the ten payments and the floor ... 89.55". Useful, and it answers "how much
  is safe" at a glance.
- Read twice, and this is the real problem: the rungs are $15,000 to $23,000 each. A $23,408 Treasury fund maturing
  Dec 2033 cannot pay a $50,000 payment in Jan 2034. So is this the real plan or a scaled copy of it? The opening
  notes said "the ten cost $302,287 ... more than the deposit"; this table says the ten plus the floor cost
  $268,652 and the whole book is exactly $300,000.00. On one reading those two statements do not reconcile, and I
  would stop and doubt both.
- Four of the five T-bonds show "[ID]" instead of an identifier. Wharton's table asks for a ticker or identifier,
  so this reads as unfinished.
- The row "Commissions and accrued interest at purchase" typed as "Cost" is honest and makes the total land on
  $300,000, but accrued interest is not a cost (the note says it comes back). Minor, but a bond reader notices.
- The amounts falling from $23,409 to $14,991 along the ladder read correctly to me as discounting. Good.

**One question before I trust it.** Which payment does each row fund, and how do rungs this size become $50,000
each: is the WInS book the plan at a smaller scale, with the rest coming from the 2028 deposit?

**Scores.** Clarity on one reading: 4 (clean, conventional, totals to $300,000 and 100% as asked; docked for the
unexplained IBTM weight and the [ID] gaps). Want to keep reading: 3 (competent, but it looks like any careful bond
ladder; the one interesting fact, IBTM's two jobs, is buried in the note).

## 4. Figure 1, the reserve (image opened)

**Five-second reading.** Each of the ten $50,000 payments from 2033 to 2042 has its own Treasury holding that
matures just before it; at forward rates each pays exactly $50,000, and if no more interest is earned each falls
$2,700 to $7,800 short, a gap covered by a "backstop" totalling $55,920.

**Is that what they meant, and how sure.** The left half, yes, very sure. The right half, only about 50%: I am
guessing what "backstop" and "at zero interest (after fees)" mean.

**What I looked at first; what I misread or read twice.**
- First: the bars. One bar per payment, each ending at a black tick just before its 1 January, with the 2027 to
  2032 stretch broken off. That is the best single picture of "matched to the payments" I have seen in the stack so
  far. The two dotted "cash waits 10.5 months / 7.5 months" lines are honest and a bond reader respects them.
- Second: the column "At forward rates", which is $50,000 in all ten rows. It tells me nothing on its own; I read it
  twice to check I was not missing a difference. It looks like the holdings were sized to hit $50,000, but the
  figure does not say so.
- Read twice: "Backstop". Backstop from what? Already owned, or a promise? From the 2028 deposit, from the stock
  half, from the extra IBTM? The single most important word on the right-hand side is undefined.
- Read twice: "At zero interest (after fees)". Zero interest on what: on coupons received before maturity, or on
  cash after it? I could not settle it on one reading.
- Noticed: the figure has no title or caption in the image, so there is no sentence telling me the point before I
  start decoding columns.
- Noticed: these are full-size amounts ($500,000 of payments), while Table 1 was the $300,000 WInS book with $15,000
  to $23,000 rungs. The figure half-answers my Table 1 question, but nothing on either exhibit tells me which scale
  I am looking at. Also, the opening notes gave about $444,500 "at today's forward rates" and this figure gives
  $444,080 "at zero interest". Two near-identical totals under different labels made me doubt which is which.

**One question before I trust it.** Where does the $55,920 backstop come from, is it set aside today, and does
setting it aside come out of the gift?

**Scores.** Clarity on one reading: 3 (the bars alone 5; the right-hand columns 2, because the key word is undefined
and one column is constant). Want to keep reading: 4 (it is distinctive and it admits the gap rather than hiding
it, which makes me trust the team; I would read on to find out what the backstop is).

## 5. Table 2, the plan in several markets (image opened)

**Five-second reading.** In every market tested, two historical crises and 585 history replays included, all ten
payments are paid, and the range Laura states in 2031 runs from about $80,000 to $95,000 in bad worlds up to
$115,000 to $140,000 in good ones, with $20,000 to $50,000 left over for flexibility.

**Is that what they meant, and how sure.** Yes, about 80% sure. "All paid" down the whole column is plainly the
point, and here a column that never changes is doing its job.

**What I looked at first; what I misread or read twice.**
- First: the column of "All paid". Second: Bottom and Top. I got the message on one pass.
- Noticed, and liked once I saw it: shares falling (2006 replay) only trims the top by $10,000, but rates falling
  (lower for good, or the 2007 replay) costs $15,000 to $20,000 at both ends. So this plan's real exposure is to
  rates, not to stocks. That is an interesting finding, but the table leaves me to work it out.
- Read twice: the range itself changes from row to row. So the team is not recommending one range today; it is
  recommending a rule that produces a range in 2031. I had to stop and decide whether that is clever or evasive.
  The case asks for a range and a confidence. The confidence answer is hidden in the note ("the contribution equals
  the stated top in every world"), which is the most persuasive sentence on the page and the one I nearly skipped.
- Read twice: "Flexibility (Jan 2033)". The note says "half of VT kept plus the excess", but excess of what? And
  next to $450,000 of deposits, $20,000 to $50,000 looks small; I want to see where the rest of the money goes.
- Minor: the title says "Five Markets" and the table has eight rows (five worlds plus three spread rows). The note
  also says rows 1 and 3 assume shares earn 7.5% a year; I would want that number defended.

**One question before I trust it.** What confidence does Laura state, in one number, and what does she say to a
co-sponsor before 2031, when the table shows the range itself still moves with interest rates?

**Scores.** Clarity on one reading: 4 (simple and well built; docked for the undefined "Flexibility" and for keeping
the confidence answer in the note). Want to keep reading: 4 (payments safe in every replay and a range that holds
up is exactly what I am grading for, and the rates-not-shares pattern makes me want the explanation).

## A. The plan for Laura in two sentences (written without looking back)

The money for the ten payments goes into Treasuries that each mature just before one payment, bought from the first
deposit and finished from the second, so the payments are paid whatever markets do. The rest goes into a world
stock fund, half of which is moved into Treasuries before 2031 so she can announce a facility gift of roughly
$95,000 to $115,000 that she already owns, while the other half stays unpromised as her flexibility.

## B. If I could keep only two

Figure 1 and Table 2. Figure 1 answers the case's first requirement (ten payments, high certainty) in one picture
that no other team in the stack is likely to have, and Table 2 shows the same plan holding in bad markets while
giving the range, the flexibility and, in its note, the confidence. The governing thought's first sentence is the
best line in the material and could ride as Figure 1's title, so dropping the notes loses little. Table 1 would go
first on persuasion, but Wharton requires it, so in practice it stays regardless.

## C. The single change to each that would most help a judge with a stack

- Opening idea: cut item 3 to two plain sentences (the range, the confidence and one clause of why) and say once
  which shares "half the shares" means; the end-cut rule belongs in Section 3.
- Table 1: add a column "Pays for" (2033 to 2042, and "2033 payment and 2031 floor" against IBTM), so the 32.5% row
  explains itself without the note. Must-fix besides: the four "[ID]" placeholders.
- Figure 1: put a title sentence on it and replace "Backstop" with a heading that says where that $55,920 comes from
  and whether it is owned today; the constant "$50,000 at forward rates" column could go.
- Table 2: move "the gift lands at the stated top in every world" out of the note and into the table (a column or
  the title), since it is the confidence answer Wharton asks for, and define "Flexibility".

Across all three: say on each exhibit which scale it is ($300,000 WInS book or the full plan), and make the opening
notes' $302,287 and $444,500 agree visibly with Table 1's $268,652 and Figure 1's $444,080, or explain the gap in a
line. On one reading I could not reconcile them, and a bond judge who cannot reconcile two numbers stops trusting
the rest.
