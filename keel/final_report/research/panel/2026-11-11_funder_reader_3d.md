Reader: a program officer at a private foundation, fifteen years funding artist residencies and arts buildings, and

What I think the plan says, in my words: by the 2031 meetings Laura owns, in US Treasuries in her own account, both the
ten operating payments of $50,000 (2033-2042) and a facility contribution of about $95,000 to $115,000 on today's

and any operating support beyond her $50,000 a year, never for her ten payments. Her gift does not rise with overruns.
She keeps some money back for her own flexibility and says so without offering it.

## A. Would I take the meeting?

**Yes.** The one thing that decides it: ten years of operations already paid for, in Treasuries she owns, before the
doors open. The question that kills most residency proposals on my desk is not the building, it is who keeps the
lights on in year three. This founder has answered it to year ten. That alone gets her the meeting.

It drops to **maybe** if that fact sits as a sub-clause behind the building figure. The plan does not yet agree with
itself on this:
- SKELETON 3d says the passage "opens with the ten-year operating commitment already owned";
- the same row then says the opening paragraph has "the building first and the operating commitment as one dated
  clause in it";
- INTENT says the operations lead 3d; Q22's order puts item 1 first.

Settle it in favour of operations first. A building figure of about $100,000 on its own is a modest founder's gift;
put behind ten owned years of operations, it reads as the second half of a serious commitment.

## B. What I need first, second and third

1. **Item 1, the ten payments already owned, dated to 2042.** It is the credential and the reason I keep reading.
2. **Items 2 and 4 together: the figure I can book, and its status.** How much, owned now, in whose name, and whether
   it is a binding pledge or a statement of intent. I file every founder's gift as secured, committed or expected
   within ten seconds; tell me which.
3. **Item 5, the ask and what is not asked.** What you want from me, and that my money never props up her ten
   payments.

Then, in order: one date (when the money is cash for the residency), item 8 (her gift does not rise with overruns),
item 6 (proof on request, one phrase), item 10 (others will be listed with amounts), item 7, item 12, item 9, item 11.

**What I would cut or move:**
- **Item 11 (named custodian, named funds, the written plan):** cut from the passage; it belongs in the meeting and on
  the statement.
- **Item 3's four dates:** keep two at most (owned since; available January 2033). "Floor bought January 2028", "top
  owned before the meetings" and "paid mid-December 2032" are the team's mechanics, not mine.
- **Item 7's net asset value clause and the fund wind-up:** move to the report. I do not need to learn how an iBond
  fund closes in a funder letter; I need to know the money is in Treasuries and a statement is available.
- **Item 9 (the half kept):** at most one clause, inside the answer on overruns, never sized, never its own sentence.
- **Twelve items in 250 words is too many.** It will read as a disclosure, not a letter. The opening paragraph should
  carry three things (items 1, 2 with 4, and 5), not five; the currency clause and the dates can wait a paragraph.

## C. What I would ask in the first five minutes that the plan does not answer

1. **What does the building cost, and how much is raised?** The case says the team need not estimate it, and the plan
   says "no total cost or gap figure". Right for the team, but without even a pointer to where and when the budget will
   be set, the ask is unbounded and I cannot tell if her gift is 5% or 50% of the job.
2. **What does it cost to run each year, and how much beyond $50,000 are you asking for?** The $50,000 is fixed in
   dollars for ten years, so the operating gap grows every year with Taiwan costs and the exchange rate. Anyone asked
   to fund operations will see that.
3. **What happens in year eleven?** The commitment ends with the 2042 payment. The plan dates it to 2042, which is
   honest; it should expect the question and not look surprised by it.
4. **What if the build is not ready in January 2033, or needs money before then?** Her money is cash about
   mid-December 2032 and cannot move before 2033 (case: no withdrawals before 2033). The plan does not say what holds
   if construction slips a year, or if land or design costs fall due in 2032.
5. **Who do I write the cheque to?** The receiving entity in Taiwan. Out of scope for an investment team, but the
   passage should point to where it is answered, as Q22 item 11 already does for governance.
6. **Is it binding?** The plan leaves this to Laura (item 4), which is right for the team. The passage must still
   choose one; an unclear status is worse than an honest "intent".

## E. The two Tue 10 Nov calls

**Call 1, co-sponsors see the rule and the stated range, never the modelled payout: right on the payout, half right
on the rule.** Once I read $123,476 I remember $123,476, and $115,000 becomes a shortfall. Keep it out. But I do not
want the rule's mechanics in a letter (zero-interest payout, 0.5% cut, rounding). The rule is for the judges in 3c; I
need one plain clause on what each end is backed by.

**Call 2, what returns to Laura named as two kinds of money: right, for the report.** Lumped as one $75,000, it looks
like a founder holding back 40% of her gift. Split, the $45,500 is plainly operating safety money and only about
$29,800 is hers by choice, which is small and defensible. Neither figure belongs in my passage. Any phrase like
"returns to Laura" near the building gift reads to me as a clawback.

**Range with a written rule or one firm figure?** For me, one firm figure I can book is better, every time. A range I
book at its bottom. A written rule beats a bare range, because it was set before the numbers and I can check it. But
here both ends are owned before the meeting and the contribution lands at the top in every modelled path, so a range
of $95,000 to $115,000 costs Laura about $20,000 of booked credit and invites the question in row 3 of the table
above. The IPS itself says "whatever figure you choose is money you already own", and the register already holds a
one-figure framing (NUMBERS 4, option D). The case requires a range, so keep it, but the plan must make the passage
tell me which figure I can count and what, if anything, decides where in the range the gift ends up.
