# Blind judge: former finalist (first-round reader), Thu 19 Nov 2026

Persona: a former finalist of this competition, now a Wharton undergraduate who helps judge the first round. Read only:
Wharton's Final Report instructions, the Competition Guide's criteria, the case, the IPS as submitted, the TNA as
submitted, and the plan (INTENT, SKELETON, WORDS, exhibits/tables.md). I judged the report these notes would produce if
written as planned. Where a SKELETON row carries dated notes, the latest one counts, and struck text is history. The
density rows (SKELETON l.37-47) win over the reservoir rows, as the plan says.

## 1. First read (about twenty minutes, first-round speed)

**What I remember without looking back**

1. The one rule: money Laura has promised is held in Treasuries before she promises it, and only money not yet
   promised takes market risk. I could say it back after one page, and it is the best thing in the plan.
2. Ten Treasuries (five iBond funds, then five T-bonds) pay the ten $50,000 payments. From the 2028 deposit, a small
   top-up finishes the ten and then "extra Treasuries" are bought, so all ten are paid "even if cash waiting earns
   nothing". After that comes a late-2032 Treasury fund (the floor) and VT.
3. In 2031, half of VT moves into the same Treasury fund. Laura states $95,000 to $115,000, co-sponsors count $115,000,
   and the confidence is one word: "certain".
4. Table 2 says "All paid" in every row, and its last line is "In every world and every starting month, the facility
   contribution in January 2033 equals the stated top."
5. Pencil and ink: ink is owned in Treasuries, pencil is not yet.
6. The reflection: Darren called VT "a copout". The first plan was mostly shares and was dropped because of how a
   writer is paid. Eric was asleep before the 00:30 open.

**Where I got lost (the plan's own words)**

- **Which number is the price and which comes back.** "the first $39,162 ... the top-up ... $2,388 ... the extra
  Treasuries ... $36,774 ... about a quarter ... worth about $46,600 in 1 January 2033 money if rates hold ... if cash
  waiting earns nothing, none beyond the 0.5% margin for fund costs." Even with only "about $39,000" and "about $46,600"
  printed, I had to read it twice to see that the second figure is the first one plus interest, coming back to her.
- **Two words for nearly the same thing.** "'the margin' is the 0.5% alone and 'the room under the top' is the margin
  plus the rounding down" (about 2%). The same goes for "the floor" (the fund) and "the bottom" (the figure). A judge
  reading at speed will merge each pair.
- **The size of the operating reserve.** I could not find it on the fast read. Wharton's checklist says "Recommend an
  appropriate operating reserve" and the case asks for "its size", but the plan prints the size only in "Figure 1's
  note item 4" ("the reserve (its size printed once, in Figure 1's note item 4, M1)"). 3a's headline is a price ("about
  $39,000" of the 2028 deposit), not a size.
- **"Certain" of what?** The case asks how confident we are "that her 2033 contribution will fall within that range". The
  plan answers "certain", because the range is stated only once both ends are owned. Then Table 2 says the contribution
  always equals the top. So my first reaction was: the bottom is never paid, so what is the range for? The plan knows
  this is open ("why a range at all when co-sponsors count the top ... the lead's call").
- **Table 1's one description.** "Laura's plan as it will stand after January 2028 with both deposits scaled to
  $300,000, without the top-up and the extra Treasuries (bought in 2028 at the day's rates) and before half the shares
  move into Treasuries ahead of the 2031 meetings; its dollar split is not Section 3's." That is three qualifiers on
  the one exhibit Wharton requires.
- **3c's conceit.** "trim, bleed and safe area; dot gain; non-photo blue". By then I had stopped following.
- **3d's comfort letter.** "Kleinwort Benson v Malaysia Mining (Court of Appeal, 1989)", inside the co-sponsor part.
- **3f's numbers.** "left no room to call 0.8991 against 0.90 a pass". I don't know what 0.8991 measures.
- **Figure 1.** Its only world is the stress test, so the bars show each Treasury paying $42,469 to $47,338 of a
  $50,000 payment. At a glance that reads as a ladder built too small, which is the reading the plan works hardest to
  avoid elsewhere.

## 4. Top 50? Top 10?

- **Top 50: likely.** All three deliverables tell one coherent story, every required element is met with evidence, and
  the IPS's letter voice already stood out.
- **Top 10: not yet.** A top-10 report lets a judge write down, after one read, the reserve's size, the contribution,
  the range with its confidence, and what Laura gives up for that certainty, in four numbers. This plan prints the
  reserve's size only in a figure note, never shows the cost of certainty in facility dollars in prose, and answers
  "how confident" with a "certain" that needs two layers to not sound circular. Its reflection reads like research
  rather than six students.

## 5. The three changes that would most raise the score

1. **Lead 3.0 and 3a with the reserve itself, not its price.** The plan says "the reserve (its size printed once, in
   Figure 1's note item 4, M1)" (3.0) and "Headline: the price, 'about $39,000' of the 2028 deposit at today's rates"
   (3a). Instead, 3a's first sentence should be the recommendation: on 1 January 2033 the operating reserve is the ten
   Treasuries plus the extra Treasuries, about $445,700 at today's rates, about $1.12 for each $1 the payments need, and
   the extra 12 cents is what keeps every payment safe even if cash waiting earns nothing. Then the price ("about
   $39,000 of the 2028 deposit") and what comes back. It answers Wharton's checkbox in the first sentence, and it
   answers "why is the range smaller?" before the judge asks.
2. **Answer 3c's confidence in two layers, with the spread in prose and the print shop out.** The plan says "Two figures
   in prose since Tue 17 Nov evening: the headline range and the margin", "The real uncertainty, in one clause with no
   figures" and "trim, bleed and safe area; dot gain; non-photo blue". Swap the 0.5% margin out of prose (it belongs in
   Table 2's note, as the $120 rate already is) and put the spread in. Today, nobody can know the top she will be able to
   state: in about 9 of 10 starting months since 1962 it fell between $95,000 and $140,000 (Table 2's 1-in-20 rows each
   way), and it is more exposed to Treasury
   rates falling before January 2028 than to a share crash (Table 2: 3 points lower gives $95,000; the 2006 replay gives
   $105,000). Once she states it in 2031, it is certain, because both ends are already owned. Then settle "why a range"
   plainly: the bottom is what she has owned since 2028, and the gap up to the top is what the shares earned her. Keep
   one ink turn ("inked in January 2028", "inked before the 2031 meetings") and cut the five printing terms.
3. **Give 3b the density cut, and cap outside names at one per part, none in 3f.** For 3b, keep "The recommended
   facility contribution in one figure", $115,000 against her $450,000, her flexibility in one test, and make "What the
   shares buy and cost, one sentence with its numbers" mandatory, with no "or cut". The trade-off is the plan's main
   weakness in a judge's eyes, so show it in dollars: an all-Treasury plan states $110,000, and with no margin she could
   state about $125,000-$155,000 "but on a forecast". Move BoardSource, Kresge, Levati, Klein, Arrow and Fisher, Perold
   and Sharpe, Norway, England's lottery and 傾其所有 to References or the store. In 3a, keep at most the state lottery
   (the one picture the plan's own lay reader kept). In 3f, drop "Uhlmann and Cohen (2005)", "Kahneman, Lovallo and
   Sibony 2011", Schulz-Hardt and the SEC bar, say what 0.8991 was in words or cut it, and add one honest line on what
   the team would do differently (for example, sizing the reserve before trading, not after).

## 6. The single biggest risk

**Density on 27 Nov.** The plan is mostly guards ("never X", struck text, dated fixes from ten coherence readers) laid
over parts that each carry 15-40 ideas for 150-250 words. The words are lawyered so tightly (the margin and the room under
the top, the top-up and the extra Treasuries and the first $39,162, the floor and the bottom, "if cash waiting earns
nothing") that writers obeying them will either overstuff every paragraph or produce careful, hedged-sounding prose. A
first-round judge then finishes Section 3 unable to say the reserve's size, the contribution, or why "certain" is not
circular. The plan's own record already contains "I lost the answer" and "about 44 ideas for 200 words". Cutting whole
ideas, as SKELETON says ("cut whole ideas, never squeeze sentences"), has to be done to 3a, 3b, 3c and 3f before the
writers start, not during the rounds.

A secondary risk: a judge holding the IPS beside the report reads IPS l.15 ("Most of the 2028 deposit will fund a
Treasury fund maturing in late 2032 ... The rest will go into a world share fund") and then sees a quarter of the 2028
deposit going first to "extra Treasuries" as a revised strategy. The plan's wording guards (the case asks for the
reserve's size; l.11 says where it is held) are right. But 3a has to make that link in its first two sentences, not
leave it to WORDS.md.

## 7. Aha moments I would remember the next day, and pencil and ink

**What I would remember**

- "$302,287 of them pays $500,000", against the team's own "won't create wealth". This is a clean reversal.
- "Diversify Laura, not the portfolio", with her line "I don't know how my book will do until the day it hits the
  shelves". This is the one I would repeat to another judge.
- Table 2's "All paid" column and "In every world and every starting month, the facility contribution in January 2033
  equals the stated top."
- "If the shares went to zero: the ten unchanged, and she states $95,000 as one figure." Told as "erase every pencil
  line, shares at zero, and the page still reads", this is the conceit at its best, a stress test you can picture.
- The first plan, "about 1 in 31 against none from January 2028", dropped anyway for how a writer is paid. It shows the
  team choosing a standard above the usual one for a client-specific reason.
- "No fall in share prices can." It works as a closing line.
- One the plan under-uses: her 2031 figure is more exposed to Treasury rates in 2027 than to a stock crash (Table 2).
  A judge expects the opposite, so it is a real aha, and it is sitting unspoken in the table.

**What I would not remember:** Neyman (clever, but abstract at speed), the comfort letter and Kleinwort Benson, 26
U.S.C. 130(d)(2), the Antideficiency Act, and the 4%-10% test in words.

**Pencil and ink: helps as two words, gets in the way as a vocabulary.** As a definition (ink is a figure owned in
Treasuries that will pay it on its date; pencil is a figure nothing owned pays yet) it is sticky, and it makes "she
states a figure only once it is owned" memorable in a way the plain rule is not. "Erase every pencil line" and "the
first plan's payments were in pencil" both land. It gets in the way once it becomes a print shop: trim, bleed, safe
area, dot gain, non-photo blue, registration marks, "a click is ink", "ink only goes one way". Each needs a gloss,
each gloss costs the clarity budget, and 3c (the most important answer in the report) is where most of them sit. A
bond-literate judge may also object to calling an iBond fund's net-asset-value payout "ink". Keep the definition in
2.0-2.1, one turn in 3e, the one sentence in 3f, and the verb "inked" in 3c, and cut the rest.
