# Reader 3: a Taiwan arts official (grants officer, Ministry of Culture)

I read the case excerpt and the notes once, as an officer who books an applicant's own funding share against the ministry's rules: the applicant shows its own share, shows proof of other bodies' resources, and raises any overrun itself.

## 1. What you would book

**US$115,000, booked as "committed", payable January 2033.** It is not "secured" and it is not "expected".

- It is **committed** because the notes give me what I normally have to chase: a signed letter with the amount, purpose and payment date, a named holding, and statements at the 2031 meetings and again before payout. Most applicants show me intentions. She shows money that is already held and a signature.
- It is **not secured** because the money stays in her own account until January 2033. A pledge letter is a promise, not a transfer. "Secured" in my files means the money is in the project's own account, in a restricted account, or backed by a bank guarantee.
- **What I would actually enter in the budget is an NT$ figure.** Our budgets are in NT$. I would convert at the rate on the date of the application, not at the 2026 rate in the notes, and I would mark the line "US$-denominated; currency shortfall is the applicant's". Under our rules that shortfall falls on the applicant anyway. Her silence on it does not move it anywhere else.
- **The ten operating payments ($500,000)** go in my notes as committed recurrent support. That is a real strength. I would **not** count them toward her share of the facility. Capital and running costs are separate lines on our forms.
- **If her words lead with "$95,000 to $115,000"**, then by default I book the bottom, $95,000, as committed and file the top as expected. When an applicant gives a range and nothing more, I book the end they cannot fall below. I book $115,000 only because the notes say she signs for $115,000.

## 2. Belief

Yes. Words written to section 5 would make me believe the figure more than most founders' figures I see. That is because of what she can show, not because of what she says. Three things carry it: she pays first, she attaches no matching condition, and she offers statements I can check.

**What would make me believe it more:**
- A dated statement from the broker or custodian **showing the account holder's name**, attached to the application, plus a second one shortly before disbursement. "Held for me" is not the same as a statement with her name on it.
- The letter saying the gift is paid **in cash, to a named receiving body, in a named currency**. It should not read as though she might hand over fund units.
- One written line saying who makes up any shortfall.

**What would make me suspicious:**
- The word "certain" in a founder's mouth. Every applicant is certain. I discount the adjective and look for the document. If the statement is there, she does not need the word.
- "About" anywhere near the signed figure.
- A range beside a signed figure without explaining what the bottom is. Then I wonder whether $115,000 can slide to $95,000.
- Finding out for myself that the named fund's payout is not fixed to the dollar, after being told there are no conditions (see 4).
- Her "whole commitment, about $615,000" offered as if it were her facility share. I would read that as padding the share.

## 3. What is missing

1. **The facility budget and her place in it.** I need the total cost of the building, her US$115,000 as a share of it, what co-sponsors and grants are expected to cover, the gap that remains, and who covers overruns. The notes size her gift but never the thing it is a share of. Our first calculation is her share as a percentage of the total, and I cannot make it from these notes. They also keep her flexibility out of co-sponsors' sight, so nothing in them answers "who pays if the building costs more". Under our rules that answer must be the applicant's.
2. **Proof of funds and the receiving body.** I need a statement in the account holder's name, the legal body in Taiwan that receives the gift, and whether that body is the applicant or a separate one. If it is separate, her money is another body's resource and needs its own proof. "The letter names the fund" does not tell me any of this.
3. **The currency.** I need either a figure she will stand behind in NT$, or a plain written statement that a currency loss is hers or the project's to cover. The notes give an NT$ value "never a promise in NT$" and keep "keeping the NT$ value whole" as an intention left out of the letter. An unwritten intention counts for nothing in my file.

(A fourth, if there were room: the January 2033 payment date set against the building's schedule. If the purchase or construction has to be paid in 2032, a January 2033 gift arrives late.)

## 4. What you would mishear

- **"About $95,000 to $115,000"**: "about" on a pledged figure tells me the applicant does not know the figure. If it reaches her words, I book the bottom.
- **"A range is heard as a margin for error"**: the notes say this themselves, and it is true for me. A range in an application tells me the bottom is what I can count on.
- **"its payout is not fixed to the dollar"** next to "certain" and "No condition in her words": these are not in her words. But the letter names the fund, so I would look it up. If I find out myself that the payout varies after she told me there was no condition, she loses more with me than she would have by saying one sentence: "the figure is already set below what the fund's Treasuries pay, and I cover any difference myself." Naming the fund invites the check. Staying silent about what the check will find reads as concealment.
- **"$115,000 is NT$3.77m at 32.768 (6 Nov 2026) ... never a promise in NT$"**: if an NT$ figure reaches me, I enter it in an NT$ budget. If the NT$ later strengthens and she pays fewer NT$, the project is short, and she said in advance she would not stand behind the number she gave me. A 2026 exchange rate in a 2031 document would also look stale.
- **"about $615,000" as "her whole commitment"**: I would separate it into $500,000 recurrent and $115,000 capital. If it is presented as one sum toward the project, it looks like inflating the own share.
- **"the payment lot comes first"**: if this reached her words, I would hear that the building gift is second in line behind the operating money in the same fund, so it is the gift that absorbs any shortfall. Keep it out, or answer it with a shortfall undertaking.

## 5. Odds and conditions

**Odds: right to leave them out.** The ministry does not book probabilities. It books documents. Once the money is held there are no odds left to give. A founder who quoted "91 in 100" to me would make me think the money was not yet in hand.

**Conditions: half right.** I do not want a list of ways it could fail: fund costs, an early end of the fund, a broker failure, a US default. That belongs to the judges. But under our rules the remaining risk already sits with the applicant, so I want one sentence that says so: if the fund pays less than the pledged figure, she makes up the difference herself. That is not a condition on the figure. It removes the condition. Leaving it out does not make the risk disappear. It only means I have to ask, and an applicant who answers before I ask looks like one who knows our rules.

## 6. Order

**The figure she signs first, then where it comes from.** Whatever comes first is what I write down. If the range comes first, I write $95,000 and treat everything after it as hope. Better still, by 2031 she should not present it as a range at all, but as one figure with its parts: "US$115,000, of which $95,000 has been held since January 2028 and the rest since January 2031." In my file that is a breakdown of what is already held, not a spread of outcomes. It still tells the reader everything the range was meant to tell them.

## 7. Scores

- **How clear it is what she will give: 4.** One signed figure with an amount, purpose and date. I lose some clarity because a range sits beside it, the word "about" appears, and it is stated only in US dollars.
- **How credible it is: 4.** The money is held before she signs, she pays first with no matching condition, and statements are offered twice. That is better than almost any founder pledge I see. It is not a 5 because of the founder's "certain" and the silence on who covers a shortfall.
- **Whether it would survive my committee: 3.** The committee would accept US$115,000 as a committed own share. It would send the file back for four things: the NT$ value and who carries the currency risk, the total facility budget and her percentage of it, a statement in the account holder's name, and the receiving body. None of these is hard to supply, but none is in the notes.

## 8. One change

**Put one bounded undertaking in the letter: "If the fund pays less than US$115,000, or if the NT$ value on the payment date is below [NT$ figure at the letter's date], I make up the difference myself."** If the currency half is too large for her flexibility, she should say plainly that currency loss is the project's to raise. Either version I can book. Silence I cannot. Our rules already put overruns and shortfalls on the applicant. Saying so unprompted turns a pledge I book as "committed, US$, risk unstated" into one my committee passes without sending it back.
