## Wharton criteria

- Investment Strategy: 5. Liability-matched, one idea, consistent from pitch to Table 3; the mechanism is correct and tested.
- Client Knowledge and Objectives: 4. Taiwan dollar, building-cost bursts, co-sponsor behaviour and the 2028 income-timing risk are all used; some Taiwan evidence is peripheral.
- Portfolio Analysis: 4. Zero-interest sizing, history from 1962, a joint recession replay, the 0.5% cut; weakened by tail-figure clutter and by the model's own inputs (200,000 runs, "about four independent rate eras").
- Articulation of Competition Experience: 3. Moments are chosen for purpose and tied to changed decisions, but the plan itself concedes they must be told without saying whose work they rested on; risk of staging.
- Creativity and Presentation: 3. Pencil and ink is intricate and potentially memorable, but the plan runs a turn in nearly every part, and the density (6-12 figures before the point, per its own panel) is the main threat.

All three agree.

## The one change

Cut the apparatus out of the prose of 3a and 3c. Give each a three-sentence statement (the standard, the price, what comes back; the range, who owns each end, the confidence) and move the mechanics (two names for $38,282, rounding, fee, cut, tail figures) to the exhibit notes. The plan's own panel found clarity 2-3 on exactly these parts.

## Contradictions with the frozen IPS and TNA

- IPS: "Even if yields fall ... we'll simply complete it with the 2028 deposit" (about $2,400). Plan: the first $38,282 of the 2028 deposit goes to the payments, sixteen times larger. The plan explains this as the reinvestment-rate risk of TNA l.5, but "most of the 2028 deposit will fund a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032" holds only "at today's rates" (54.6%).
- IPS pitch: "a world stock ETF will determine the ceiling." Plan 3e: the 7.5% assumption moves the top "by less than one $5,000 step". Reconciled by "what shares actually do before 2031 sets it", but it will read as a tension.
- TNA l.7 calls inflation risk to the payments "nonexistent"; plan 3e area (4) makes inflation decide "what they and the contribution buy". Consistent only if always said together, as the plan itself notes.
- IPS: the floor's payout is "the bare minimum ... without doubt." TNA Note 2: "the final payout is not fixed." Plan's bottom of $95,000 sits 8% below the floor's modelled payout, which reconciles them, but the IPS wording is stronger than the plan.
