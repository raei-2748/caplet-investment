## 4. What a judge would remember, and what would most lower the mark
1. The ten payments are owned in Treasuries before the doors open and are paid even if interest earns nothing, barring a US default.
2. Laura's career is a share she cannot sell, so the portfolio diversifies her rather than itself.
3. In 2031 she states about $95,000 to $115,000, both ends already in Treasuries, and co-sponsors count the bottom.

Most damaging: density. Section 3 is planned like a risk-committee pack: each part puts six to twelve figures, several of them tail probabilities, before the point lands. A judge who cannot repeat the reserve, the range and the confidence after one reading marks Presentation and Portfolio Analysis down together. A compliance risk too: Section 2's page arithmetic counts Table 1 but not 2.5's table, against a three-page maximum that is a minimum requirement.

## 5. Where other judges will disagree
- A growth-minded judge may give Investment Strategy 3: about 7% shares and a $38,282 zero-interest top-up look over-insured for an entrepreneur, and about $100,000 on $450,000 looks timid. I hold 4: the career argument and "high degree of certainty" justify it, though the plan never shows what more shares would buy.
- A strict consistency judge may read the post-freeze zero-interest standard, and "maturity-matched, not duration-matched" against Trading Note 1's "duration-matching", as revising frozen work. I read them as the reserve sizing Wharton asks for and an honest correction, if labelled so.

- A generalist impressed by volume may give Portfolio Analysis 5; I hold 4 for false precision.
