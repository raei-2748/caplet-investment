Persona: twenty years raising money for arts buildings and residencies; now on a foundation's grants committee. Some

## 1. First read (about twenty minutes)

**What I remember without looking back.**
- The rule: from January 2028, every dollar Laura has promised is paid by Treasuries held for her before she promises
  it, and only money promised to no one is in shares.
- The opening test: if every share were worth nothing, all ten payments are still paid and the building still gets
  $95,000. I could check that one against the plan in my head, so it stuck.
- Each payment has its own Treasury, and about $39,000 of the 2028 deposit (about a quarter of it) buys extra
  Treasuries so the payments hold even if cash waiting earns nothing. If rates turn out as today's prices say, that
  money comes back to her.
- The building gets the late-2032 Treasury fund plus half the shares, moved before the 2031 meetings. The other half
  is her flexibility, about $21,000 to $29,000.
- Range about $95,000 to $115,000. From today the top is uncertain (about 91 in 100 for at least $100,000, 57 in 100
  for $115,000); once moved, it is held.
- Laura signs a pledge letter for $115,000; her whole commitment is about $615,000 because ten years of running money
  counts. Lehman pledged Spelman $10 million and paid $3 million. Co-sponsors get statements in 2031 and before the
  December 2032 payout.
- Only a missed deposit or a Treasury default changes the plan; a market fall does not.
- Pencil and ink. Darren's "copout". One step further took a missed payment from about 1 in 31 to none.

That is a lot to remember from notes, which is the plan's strength: the spine is memorable. What I did not remember
was anything from 2.3, 2.5, 3e's risk list or Table 2's note, which is where the plan is densest.

**Where I got lost (the plan's own words).**
1. The governing thought itself carries two figures in two money-years for one gap: "In January 2027 money the
   payments' money is $37,499 short of the report's standard (the first $39,162 of the 2028 deposit, in January 2028
   money...)". If any of that reaches the prose, the first thing a judge meets is a reconciliation.
2. The definition of ink: "a dollar figure is in ink once Treasuries held for her, directly or through Treasury funds,
   will pay it on its date, counted as if their cash earned nothing more and after the 0.5% margin". Three conditions
   on a metaphor whose charm is that it is simple. I understood "ink = already paid for" at once; the clauses made me
   doubt I had.
3. 3b: "Each $5,000 more of stated top gives up about $300 of expected return for each $5,000 made certain." I read
   it three times and still cannot say what is traded for what.
4. 3b, the key to aha 6: "because money moved into Treasuries later loses less if cash waiting earns nothing". This is
   the reason fewer shares would give the building less, and as a non-specialist I do not see why. An aha whose key
   is not understood reads as a trick.
5. "The margin" (0.5%) and "the room under the top" (about 2%) are two names for two close quantities; I mixed them
   up twice.
6. 3c puts three things under "the range": today's illustration ("about $95,000 to $115,000 on the 6 November 2026
   curve"), the uncertain figure from today ("$115,000 in about 57"), and the held figure after the move. I had to
   rebuild which number answers Wharton's "level of confidence" question.
7. 3b: "Taiwan's Ministry of Culture shares a building's cost with its owner only on the way down." I do not know what
   this means.
8. 3e: a stated top can break by "fund costs beyond about 2% of what its fund pays, the fund's own swaps of its
   Treasuries for others maturing the same year (unsized...), an early end or merger (...) or a broker failure (a
   delay), in one sentence". Four risks, two sizes and a caveat in one sentence.
9. 3e's claim "Only two things change the plan" sits beside its own "Left out: 'only two things can break the
   plan'". The difference between "change" and "break" is real but too fine for a reader to hold without being told.
10. 2.5: "the book's DV01 (about $159) beside the liability's (about $200)". Shown as a mismatch with no word on
    whether it matters; I stopped there.
11. Section 2 promises "a test a judge can check against the holdings table", but Table 1 prints IBTM as one line
    ("2033 and the floor"), so I cannot see the floor's share in the table.
12. WORDS contradicts itself where the 27 Nov writers will look: its attack-day line says "never 'what the market
    expects'", but its Forward rates row still defines them as "the interest rates the bond market expects today"; its
    top line says "never the figure" is owned, but its conceit row defines ink as "a dollar figure owned in
    Treasuries" and its range row says "both ends owned"; its range row bans "at least" before an amount, while the
    tree has Laura tell planners "at least $95,000" and 3c says "a top of at least $100,000". WORDS also gives 3f one
    conceit turn ("the first plan's payments were in pencil"), while the tree and the shape say 3f is "told plain".

## 4. The three weakest points, and what would fix them (within the submitted IPS)

1. **Too many ideas per word, worst in Section 2 and 3e.** The plan treats every researched fact as owed a place, so
   Section 2's ~580 words and 3e's share of ~1,900 carry more citations, figures and parallels than a judge can absorb
   in one reading, and the authentic voice is what will be lost first.
   *Fix:* in each part keep the claim, its plain reason, one headline figure and at most one aha; move citations to the
   References list with a short phrase in the text ("as bond writers call it, dedication"); in Section 2 drop the DV01s,
   GIW Industries and the background-risk literature to a clause or out, and keep the 25-basis-point check as one
   sentence; in 3e let Table 3 carry the lists and give the prose only the two reasons, one sentence per break route,
   the hard case, and the closing line, with the gilt crisis in two sentences or not at all. Fix WORDS's
   contradictions (point 12 above) before the writers start.

2. **The range Laura communicates, and the confidence in it, can be misread.** Three things are called "the range",
   the lead's "$95,000" has no date while Table 2 shows bottoms of $75,000 and $80,000 when rates fall before 2028, and
   3d gives co-sponsors one signed figure although the case says Laura wants a range "rather than promising one exact
   amount". A judge looking for "the range and our confidence" may find a point estimate and "57 in 100".
   *Fix:* in 3c, one sentence each: the range she communicates in 2031 is the bottom held since January 2028 and the top
   held since the move, whatever those figures are then; on today's curve that is about $95,000 to $115,000; confidence
   that her 2033 contribution falls in it is that it is already held (Table 2's last line: equal to the top in every
   tested world and starting month); the 91 and 57 in 100 are what today can say about where the top will land. In
   3d, Laura states the range and signs its top, with one sentence on why: the case's reason for a range, a 2033 value
   still uncertain in 2031, no longer applies once the money is held (the IPS's own "whatever figure you choose is money
   you already own"). Date the lead's $95,000 ("at today's rates") and say "bottom" for it so it matches Table 2.

3. **The Team Reflection is too thin for a fifth of the marks.** Three moments in roughly 300 words show decisions but
   little of how the team worked together, what it was unsure of, or what it would do differently.
   *Fix:* give 3f about 400-450 words, taken from the outside parallels and citations above; keep the three moments,
   and for each say who argued what, how it was settled and what the team learned; add, if Caplet's own record
   supports it, the moment the team sized the operating reserve after the WInS portfolio was built, which would turn
   the note under Table 1 from a defence into learning without changing the strategy. Settle whether 3f has one
   conceit turn or none (WORDS and the tree disagree).
