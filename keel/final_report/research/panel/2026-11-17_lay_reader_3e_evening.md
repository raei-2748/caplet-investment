## a) What the plan rests on, and what would make each promise fail

What the plan rests on, in my words:

1. **Shares.** World shares earn about 7.5% a year (J.P. Morgan's figure). The notes then say this hardly matters,
   because anything from 4% to 10% moves the top Laura states by less than $5,000. What actually sets the top is what
   shares do before the 2031 meetings.
2. **Bonds.** Interest rates as the market priced them when the plan went in. In the model runs, rates move up and down
   together and independently of shares (the team's own assumption, not a market figure).
3. **Timing.** The $150,000 arrives in full at the start of 2028. It comes from book, speaking and licence money, which a
   bad economy delays first.
4. **Outside money.** None goes to the payments or the bottom of the range. Co-sponsor money comes later, year by year,
   in Taiwan dollars, and public money can be held up by budget votes.
5. **Inflation.** The payments are fixed dollars, so inflation changes what they buy, never whether they are paid. Taiwan
   building costs and the Taiwan dollar are not forecast. They are tested against her flexibility.
6. **Her flexibility.** This is half the shares, about $28,700 in 2033 (Table 3 row 7), and it is not sized to any cost.
   Its honest limit is a share slump before the meetings followed by a jump in building costs.
7. **Safety margins the team chose.** Cash waiting is assumed to earn nothing after January 2028, the range carries a
   one-time 0.5% margin, and each fund is counted at what its bonds pay less its fee.
8. **Laura's career.** The IPS's opening idea is that her income falls when shares fall. The notes treat it as tested and
   only partly true: it held in the financial crisis but not in the pandemic.

What would make each promise fail:

- **The ten payments.**
  - A US default, meaning a Treasury paying more than five business days late (Table 3 row 2).
  - The 2028 deposit never arriving. Table 3 row 1 says the ten would then be short by $3,016 at today's rates, or
    $57,455 if waiting cash earns nothing.
  - A share crash does not touch them. A fund that ends early or costs more is covered from the extra Treasuries.
- **The range she states in 2031.**
  - The same two events: "no range until paid" (row 1) and "the range restated on what was paid" (row 2).
  - Before the 2031 move, falling shares lower the top she can state. Lower rates before the first purchase lower the
    bottom (row 6).
  - After the move, building costs and a stronger Taiwan dollar use up her flexibility, not the range.

**One list or two?** In effect there is one short list of things that break a promise (a missed deposit, a US default),
and it applies to both promises. Beside it is a longer list of things that only move numbers: shares, rates, Taiwan costs
and the Taiwan dollar. The notes never say "the same two events can break both promises" in one sentence. I put it
together from Table 3 rows 1-2 and 3e's "the only two events that can remove a layer the payments rest on", and that
phrase speaks only of the payments. A judge needs that sentence said once.

  any of it leaking into the prose would confuse.

## c) If the 2028 deposit never arrived

- Nothing received by 31 March 2028 counts as a missed deposit.
- Then there is "no range until paid", and the ten are short by $3,016 at today's rates, or $57,455 if waiting cash earns
  nothing.
- "Never: a sale."
- 3e adds that a late deposit still runs in the same order.

What I could tell: there would be no range, because without the deposit there is no floor fund and no shares, so there is
nothing to promise co-sponsors. The payments would be slightly short at today's rates and badly short under the team's
own test.

What I could not tell:

- Who fills the $3,016 (or the $57,455). Does a payment shrink? Does the last one go unpaid? Does Laura find outside
  money, which the case forbids for the payments?
- A sale of what? With no deposit there are no shares to sell. The other rows say "a forced sale" and "a sale of shares",
  so row 1's bare "a sale" reads as a missing word.

It is consistent with IPS l.13 ("we'll simply complete it with the 2028 deposit"). But the notes I read say what breaks,
not what Laura does next. **Half clear.**

## d) What I would watch, and when

- **Each January from 2027:** the year's trades on the first trading day, at prices within 0.5% of the fund's value (row
  5), and the broker statement checked against the plan (3e's custody clause).
- **By 1 Dec 2027:** Laura confirms the 2028 deposit. **31 March 2028:** the cut-off.
- **Every cash date:** each Treasury pays on time. More than five business days late counts as a default.
- **Any debt-limit deadline (the "X-date")** within a month of a cash date: look closer, sell nothing.
- **By 31 October of each fund's final year:** the fund's notice of an early end, a merger or a fee rise. For the fund
  that pays the 2033 payment and the floor, that is October 2032.
- **2031:** half the shares move into Treasuries before the meetings, never after them.
- **2031 to 2033:** the Taiwan dollar (10% stronger than in 2031 costs about $11,500 on a $115,000 top) and Taiwan
  building costs (against a trend band of 1.5% to 5.0% a year).
- **Shares and rates:** no trigger. They move the top until 2031, and her flexibility after that.

This was the easiest part to follow: the table's three levels (change the plan, look closer, just watch) worked for me.
