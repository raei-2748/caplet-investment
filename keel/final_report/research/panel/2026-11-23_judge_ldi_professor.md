## 1. First read (about twenty minutes)

**What I remember without looking back.**
- The lead: if every share were worth nothing, all ten payments would still be paid and the building would still get
  $95,000. A judge can check it against Table 1. It is the best sentence in the plan.
- The structure is a dedicated (cash-flow-matched) Treasury portfolio: one holding per payment, iBond Treasury funds for
  2033-2037 and bonds for 2038-2042, then a late-2032 Treasury fund for the floor and VT for the top and her flexibility.
- The reserve is sized so that all ten are paid even if cash waiting earns nothing. The price is about $39,000 of the
  2028 deposit, and about $46,600 (2033 money) comes back if rates turn out as today's prices imply. The lottery holds
  zero-coupon Treasuries; Laura may not, so her extra Treasuries are what the STRIPS ban costs her. I will remember that
  one for a long time.
- The contribution is the floor plus the half of VT moved into Treasuries before 2031; the other half is her
  flexibility; about $95,000 to $115,000.
- Confidence in two layers: uncertain from today (a top of at least $100,000 in 91 of 100 futures, $115,000 in 57),
  held once the shares are moved.
- Laura signs a pledge letter for $115,000 with her whole commitment, about $615,000. Lehman's pledge to Spelman.
- Two reasons to change the plan: a missed deposit or a Treasury default. The gilt crisis: the forced sellers had
  borrowed; Laura borrows nothing.
- Darren's "copout".

**What I could not recall without looking back:** the forward-rate citations, GIW Industries, Perold, "the room under
the top", the 2% figure, any 3e figure except "about 25%", the 1986-88 currency move, the 3b trade-off ratio. These are
the plan's clutter. If a professor reading slowly cannot hold them, a judge reading many reports in a row will not.

**Where I got lost, in the plan's own words.**
1. 3b: "Each $5,000 more of stated top gives up about $300 of expected return for each $5,000 made certain." I read it
   three times. Is that per year or in total? Measured against what?
2. 3b, aha 6: "an all-Treasury plan holding back the same flexibility would state $110,000, fixed in January 2028,
   against the plan's $115,000, fixed at the move, because money moved into Treasuries later loses less if cash waiting
   earns nothing." This compares a figure fixed in 2028 with the central case of a figure reached (seen from 2028) in
   about 57 of 100 futures. The mechanism it gives is the report's own counting rule, not an economic gain. A
   fixed-income reader will read this as an artifact of how the figures are counted.
3. 3a: "priced at today's rates, reinvested interest would have fallen short more often than not for start dates since
   1981 and far less often before." Fallen short of what? I had to work it out: a ladder sized on today's forward rates
   would have run short. It also rests on one long fall in rates.
4. Figures for the same thing that do not visibly agree. In 3a, "it pays $502,605 at zero interest against $500,000
   owed", against Figure 1's "$444,080 + $55,920 = $500,000" with "$1,177 to spare". In Table 3, "the ten short $3,016
   at today's rates, $57,455 if cash waiting earns nothing", against the $39,162 that fills them. Each may be right on
   its own basis, but printed together without their bases they look like errors.
5. Section 2: "Matched payment by payment, so duration matches too, bond by bond" sits beside "the book's DV01 (about
   $159) beside the liability's (about $200)". Both cannot be printed unless a sentence explains the hedge ratio.
6. 3d: the conceit defines ink as a figure that Treasuries held for her will pay, and the aha in the same part says
   "Owned answers whether she can pay; signed answers whether she will". The reader has learned that ink means owned,
   and here meets literal ink, a signature, that means something else. "The printed page shows only ink" then lands on
   the wrong meaning.
7. 3e: "A stated top: fund costs beyond about 2% of what its fund pays". I could not tell which fund "its fund" means.
   "The room under the top" and "the margin" are near-synonyms the reader must keep apart, and WORDS needs two whole
   rows to separate them.
8. 3c: "Why a range at all, then." Table 2's last line says the contribution equals the stated top in every tested
   future, so the bottom is never what she pays. The plan's answer ("The bottom tells the residency's planners ...")
   reads as defending the case's format, not as a reason.

## 4. The three weakest points, and what would fix each (within the submitted IPS)

1. **Density beyond the budget.** Each part of Section 3 has about 300 words. Into them the plan puts a claim, its
   reason, evidence, one or two ahas, a conceit turn, an outside parallel and citations, and Section 2's 580 words carry
   about fifteen items. Written as planned, it will be compressed, not explained. **Fix:** give each part one claim, one
   headline number, one aha and at most one device (a parallel or a conceit turn, not both). Move secondary figures to
   the exhibit notes or drop them. In Section 2, keep either the implementation detail (GIW, Perold, DV01) or the
   background-risk citations, not both. In 3e, print the two reasons and the three limits with one figure each, and
   point to Table 3 for the rest.
2. **3c and 3d answer a slightly different question from the case.** The case asks for a range "rather than promising
   one exact amount", and for "how confident" the team is that "her 2033 contribution will fall within that range". The
   plan gives one signed figure, and its confidence is spread across 3c and 3e. **Fix:** in Laura's words, state the
   range with its two dates ($95,000 held since January 2028, up to $115,000 held since the move). Commit in writing to
   the top, with one sentence on why she can: IPS l.15, "whatever figure you choose is money you already own". Give 3c
   one sentence of stated confidence, with the remaining risks and what covers each. Keep the conceit out of 3d
   altogether, or say in one clause that a signature is the other kind of ink.
3. **Three analytical claims a fixed-income judge will challenge.** (a) "Duration matches too": state the hedge ratio
   instead (the book's DV01 is about four-fifths of the liability's until the 2028 completion; the early maturities of
   the 2038-2042 bonds are handled by the zero-reinvestment test). (b) Aha 6: present $110,000 against $115,000 as the
   central case, with Table 2's bad worlds beside it and the mechanism said plainly, or replace it as 3b's aha. (c) The
   lead's $95,000 rests on the 6 November curve: date it in the lead, since a 1-point fall before January 2027 gives
   $85,000 (Table 3). None of these changes the strategy.
