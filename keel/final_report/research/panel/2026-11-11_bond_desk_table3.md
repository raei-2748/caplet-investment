## 4. Three definitions

**(a) Treasury default.** Recommend: Treasury does not pay interest or principal on any marketable Treasury within
five business days of its adjusted payment date. Issuer level: the IPS's exception is a US default, and fund holdings
are invisible day to day. Five days is S&P's test where no grace period is stated (ISDA deems three); it separates a
back-office slip (1979: bills due 26 April paid late) from a default. A 2027-28 delay cured inside the window: no, log
it under row 3. Beyond it: yes, though nothing is due until 2033, because Laura must know before stating a range. Row
2 then names the change: re-check payments and both ends on actual receipts, hold, restate; never a forced sale, as
no permitted asset is safer.

**(b) Missed deposit.** Recommend: nothing received by 31 March 2028; anything received by then is a smaller deposit
and runs the order. The case says the start of the year; a quarter is generous. An open-ended "late" leaves the range
unknowable; this leaves three years to re-plan.

**(c) Custody.** One line of prose. The crossing is predictable now ($589,655 at the end, NUMBERS 8k): a set-up
decision, not a signal. In a broker failure segregated Treasuries are rarely lost, but access can freeze near a
payment date. The line names the decision (two custodians or confirmed excess cover) and SIPC's $250,000 cash limit
against December 2032's cash.
