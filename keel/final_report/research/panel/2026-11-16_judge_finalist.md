## 4. What I took away (written before re-reading anything)

- What Laura tells co-sponsors in 2031: ten years of her $50,000 operating commitment are already owned in Treasuries, to 2042. For the facility she will give between about $95,000 and $115,000, in US dollars, all of it already owned in Treasury funds. Co-sponsors should count $95,000, and she intends $115,000. Her figure comes first and has no conditions; theirs are pledged toward one total by one close date.
- The recommended facility contribution: about $115,000 at today's rates, made of the 2032 Treasury fund bought with the 2028 deposit plus the half of the world share fund moved into Treasuries before the 2031 meetings, never more than the top she states. Half the shares, about $21,300 (about $29,800 with anything above the top), stay hers.
- What I could not say from memory: the operating reserve's size. I think about $444,500 in 2033, but the plan puts that figure in the reservoir row, not in 3a's lead. I also could not say why the range is $20,000 wide if she expects to give the top.

## 5. The single change, and where I could not follow

**The one change.** Choose what the report is for and cut the rest by name. The plan stacks a density cut, ten added judgements, eight "must land" ideas, ten ahas and a conceit turn in every part onto about 2,380 words. Give each part one answer sentence, one number and at most one extra thing (an aha or a conceit turn, not both). Keep the conceit in three places only: the definition in 2.0, "ink only goes one way" in 3b, and its absence from Laura's words in 3d. Lead 3a with the reserve's size. Everything else goes to the exhibits' notes or stays in the store.

**Where I could not follow.** SKELETON 2.1: "it splits what is left of the 2028 money as the plan does (about 73% floor, 27% VT) but holds neither the top-up nor the extra Treasuries, which come first in the plan ...; scaled up, Table 1 is not Section 3's dollar split". After three readings I still cannot say what Table 1 is a picture of. Close second, 3c: "the top from the bottom's figure before rounding ... plus the moved half's on the same basis, the sum rounded down once".

## 7. Contradictions with the frozen IPS and Trading Notes

1. **The size of what goes to the payments from the 2028 deposit.** IPS: "Even if yields fall and the total cost of the ladder ends up being above $300,000, we'll simply complete it with the 2028 deposit" and "Most of the 2028 deposit will fund a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032". Plan: "the first $38,282 of the 2028 deposit ... about a quarter of it", of which only "$2,388" is the completion and "the other $35,894" is new, leaving the floor at "54.6% centrally". "Most" survives by a hair, and a reader of the IPS did not expect a quarter of the deposit to go to the payments. This is the report's largest consistency risk under Wharton's "not an opportunity to ... revise the IPS". The plan knows it (INTENT failure 7), but the defence is long.
2. **"Pay what they say" against the need for extra Treasuries.** IPS: "Held to the end, these pay what they say – no price fluctuations for us to fear." TNA: "a nominal Treasury matches the liability perfectly". Plan 3a: "in about half of the starting months since 1962 yields fell enough after purchase that the interest each bond pays along the way, reinvested, would have needed the 2028 deposit's help". However it is worded, that says the ten alone did not match perfectly.
3. **Duration-matching.** TNA: "they will remove uncertainty, through duration-matching". Plan 2.5: "matched payment by payment", with "instruments 7.76 years ... payments 9.94 years" kept for a note. If those two figures are printed anywhere, they contradict the TNA. The plan cuts them from prose; cut them from the notes too.
4. **The career premise.** Pitch: "Laura's career is already a giant stock she can't sell. So we hedge it!" IPS: "In a recession they come later and smaller, and during that same period, shares fall". Plan (rank 4, 3e): "2020 fell while shares rose; her own genre rose in 2020 ... so the risk to the 2028 deposit is a quiet year more than a share-market fall", and "the case's certainty standard does more of the work than the career"; 2.3: "never 'hedge' in Keel's own words". Honest, but it demotes the idea the pitch leads with. Say it as a tested limit in one clause, not as the conclusion.
5. **"The bottom will remain constant."** IPS: "the bottom will remain constant, built on the ever-reliable Treasuries". Plan, Table 2: "bottom $80,000 to $115,000"; Table 3 row 6: "a 1-point fall in yields before January 2027 gives a stated bottom of $85,000". The plan's answer (constant once bought in January 2028) is correct, but the table will be read first.
6. **What sets the top.** Pitch: "a world stock ETF will determine the ceiling". Plan 3e: "the assumed average return moves the stated top by less than one $5,000 step", and the aha "The forecast barely touches the promise". The plan guards this ("what shares actually do before the 2031 meetings sets it"); the aha's short form still reads against the pitch.
7. **Certainty words.** IPS: "barring a US default". TNA: "a near-ironclad guarantee"; Trading Note 2: "the final payout is not fixed"; "a net asset value, as opposed to a promise of par". Plan (WORDS): "'guaranteed', 'locked', 'set in stone' or 'risk-free' may describe the Treasuries, with no exception attached"; 3c: "inside the range is certain, both ends owned, with no default clause attached". Both ends of the range are iBond funds, which the team's own note says do not pay a fixed sum. "Certain" for the range is stronger than anything the team submitted.
8. **Only two reasons.** IPS: "There are only 2 possible reasons we would change our plan". Plan, Table 3 row 4: "swaps that fund for a Treasury maturing before the payment", and a missed deposit is now "nothing received by 31 March 2028". The plan calls the swap "a permitted step ... not a trigger"; a quick reader may count it as a third reason. Minor.

No contradiction found on the holdings, on VT's role, on the half kept and its three uses, or on WInS gains (never used).
