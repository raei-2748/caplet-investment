# Blind judge: the finance professor (Mon 16 Nov 2026)

Persona: a Wharton finance professor who teaches fixed income and behavioural finance and has judged this competition
before. I marked against Wharton's Final Report instructions and criteria. I read only: the Final Report instructions,
the client profile, the IPS as submitted, the TNA as submitted, `INTENT.md`, `SKELETON.md` and `WORDS.md`. I judged the
report these notes would produce if written as planned, with the density cut and Book 17's amendments applied as the
plan says they win.

## Ranking

- **Top 50 of about 2,300: likely.** The analysis is deeper and better tied together across the three deliverables than almost any high-school report. It stays likely only if the page limits hold and Section 3 can be followed on one reading.
- **Top 10: not as planned.** It becomes possible if Section 3's clarity rises from 2 to 4, the range's bottom is explained, and the reflection shows the team arguing.

## Three things a judge has not seen before

1. **"Ink is a dollar figure owned in Treasuries that will pay it on its date, and pencil a figure no Treasury she owns will pay yet; Laura states a figure only once it is inked."** This will land. It is the client's own craft used as a rule about money. It stops landing once the print-shop extensions start ("trim, bleed, safe area ... dot gain ... non-photo blue"); those lose a finance reader.
2. **"The real uncertainty is which range she will be able to state"**, set beside "finance chiefs' 80% ranges held about 36% of the time, so Laura's range comes from owned money, not a forecast". This lands hard with a behavioural-finance judge: confidence is moved from a forecast into a rule about ownership. It needs the bottom-versus-top problem below fixed, or a sharp judge will turn it against the team.
3. **What certainty cost: "No margin: she would state about $125,000 to $155,000; sized for rates 3 points lower, $110,000 to $130,000; at the standard chosen, $95,000 to $115,000"**, together with "closer to a deposit than a fee: about $45,500 comes back if rates hold". This lands. Few teams show the price of their own conservatism as a menu. A runner-up that would also land is "on 4 Aug 2020 the ten cost $470,217, more than both deposits": the plan is feasible only in this rate regime, and it says so.

## The single change that would most raise the report

**Enforce the density rule over the goldmine in Section 3: each of 3a-3e carries one claim, one headline number with its basis, and at most one piece of outside evidence. Everything else stays in the store or the References.** The thinking is already there (four parts score 4-5). What loses marks is clarity 2 in 3a, 3b, 3c and 3e. "Lose nothing" should mean lost from the store, not printed on the page. The same cut should apply to the conceit: keep pencil and ink, and drop the print-shop vocabulary.

## Where I could not follow the plan

The range after the move. 3c says the contribution is "at the top unless the funds' other costs ... take more than 0.5% of their value by the end, and then short by about $120 for each tenth of a percent above that". Yet it answers "if the contribution always lands at the top, why state a bottom?" with "the bottom is the figure that holds whatever the funds' costs, so it is the one co-sponsors count". A $20,000 gap is not explained by a risk worth about $120 per tenth of a percent. Once both ends are owned, the range is in substance one figure, and 3d's "count $95,000, I intend $115,000" under-promises against the report's own Gneezy and Epley evidence ("exceeding a promise buys almost no trust"). The report has to say plainly which it is. Either the bottom is the IPS's constant (l.15) that carries the period before the move and the range narrows to its top at the move, or there is a real risk below the top that I could not find.

A second, smaller one: "splits what is left of the 2028 money as the plan does (about 73% floor, 27% VT)", beside the instruction to "never print $75,001, 'just over half' or any label saying the floor's written minimum binds". I could not find what sets 73%. A rule the report is told to hide is exactly what I distrust in model output. If a floor minimum exists, show it.

## Against the frozen IPS and TNA

1. **The 2028 deposit's use (medium risk).**
   - IPS l.15: "Most of the 2028 deposit will fund a Treasury fund maturing in late 2032 ... The rest will go into a world share fund."
   - IPS l.13 limits the completion to price: "Even if yields fall and the total cost of the ladder ends up being above $300,000, we'll simply complete it with the 2028 deposit."
   - Plan: "the first $38,282 of the 2028 deposit ... about a quarter of it", of which only "$2,388" is the completion. The floor falls to "54.6% centrally", and "'most' fails ... if yields fall more than about 1.3 points before January 2027 ... and then the floor takes all of what is left and VT nothing".
   - The plan frames the $35,894 as the operating reserve Wharton asks for, and that defence is reasonable. But a strict judge reading l.13 and l.15 side by side will see a new allocation of a quarter of the deposit, against "not an opportunity to ... revise the IPS".
2. **Reinvestment and "matches perfectly" (medium risk).**
   - Pitch: "a Treasury ladder, immune to markets".
   - IPS l.13: "Held to the end, these pay what they say".
   - TNA l.7: "a nominal Treasury matches the liability perfectly".
   - TNA l.15: "each of Laura's payments rests on a dedicated instrument (not merely a forecast)".
   - Plan 3a: "in about half of the starting months since 1962 yields fell enough after purchase that the interest each bond pays along the way, reinvested, would have needed the 2028 deposit's help". Also: "priced at today's rates alone, the ten assume waiting money earns about 5.1% a year, the forward rates' average".
   - The plan cites TNA l.5 as having named this risk. But TNA l.5 names only "reinvestment-rate risk on the proceeds held in a short-term Treasury ETF" during "the 10.5-month gap" for one bond, not the reinvestment of six years of interest across all ten. The candour is right; calling it "the risk the Trading Notes already named" overstates what the TNA said.
3. **The recession premise (low risk to the IPS, inconsistent within the plan).**
   - IPS l.9: "In a recession they come later and smaller, and during that same period, shares fall and co-sponsors cut their budgets."
   - Plan 3e: "public co-sponsors fall short through budget votes and rule changes, not recessions (Taiwan's central culture spending rose in 2009-10)". Also: "2020 fell while shares rose".
   - Plan 2.3's turn: "a recession rubs out her advances, share prices and co-sponsors' budgets together", which restates l.9 without the limit that 3e then sets.
   - Testing the premise is good evaluation. Asserting it in 2.3 and qualifying it in 3e is a contradiction a judge will catch.
4. **One figure or two (low risk).**
   - IPS l.15: "whatever figure you choose is money you already own" (Laura chooses a figure).
   - Plan 3d: co-sponsors "count the bottom" and she "intends the top".
   - Not a breach, but the report should say how the IPS's single chosen figure became a two-figure statement.
5. **No contradiction found** on the two exits (IPS l.17 against Table 3, where the fund swap is "a permitted step under IPS l.13, not a trigger"), on inflation (TNA l.7's "nonexistent" kept, "never whether they are paid"), or on VT's role and the 7.5% figure (TNA l.21-23).
