# Independent judge: the co-sponsor's reading (Mon 9 Nov 2026)

Persona: a program officer at a cultural foundation who has funded artists' residencies and read many founders' case
statements, serving as a competition judge. I read the plan the way a prospective co-sponsor reads a founder: is her
own commitment real, is the range credible, is the stated confidence honest, is the flexibility sensible, and would
these words make me write a cheque. I mark only against Wharton's Final Report instructions (WHARTON_NORTH_STAR
section 0), the client case, and the frozen IPS. I read INTENT.md, SKELETON.md and NUMBERS.md sections 4 and 8f, and
nothing else in the folder.

I am judging a plan, so every score is for the report that would result if the plan were followed exactly, with
competent prose. A plan can be sound and still produce a 3 if it leaves the hard decision for 27 Nov.

## 2. As a funder

### What I would need to hear in 2031 to commit

1. That the residency's operations are secured for ten years from money Laura already owns, with dates. Residencies
   fail on operating deficits far more often than on buildings. A founder who arrives with ten years of operating
   payments in Treasuries is in the top one percent of founders I have met. This is the strongest thing in the whole
   plan and the skeleton never names it as the co-sponsor argument.
2. A floor she owns now, named: the instrument, its maturity (late 2032), and the fact that it was bought in January
   2028, three years before she sat down with me. "Bought in 2028" is worth more than any confidence percentage.
3. A top she also owns, since January 2031, and the one condition under which it would not arrive.
4. What my money is for: not the total cost (Wharton excludes it), but the shape: her contribution covers part of the
   facility, co-sponsors cover the rest, operations are not part of the ask.
5. What she holds back, how much, what it is for, and what happens to it if it is not needed. Named and sized, it is
   prudence. Unnamed, it is a reason to wonder what else she has not said.
6. Who else is in, and who governs the reserve. Mostly outside Wharton's scope, but one line on governance (the asset
   manager holds the reserve; the payments are not Laura's to redirect) would reassure a funder and costs nothing.

### What in this plan would make me doubt Laura

- The whole facility contribution comes from the 2028 deposit, and the 2028 deposit comes from advances, speaking fees
  and licences. In 2031 that is resolved, and the report must say so; but if the report is silent on it a careful reader
  will notice that her gift depends on one good year of a volatile income. State it, then close it.
- A range whose bottom she cannot actually land on. If I later learn that in 2031 she already owned the top, the $110,000
  looks like sandbagging, and sandbagging is a credibility cost of its own kind: it tells co-sponsors to anchor low, and
  the case says a meaningful personal contribution is the signal of viability.
- A cushion that is smallest in the year the project most needs it. The plan admits this. Admission without a reason
  reads as a flaw the team found and could not fix.
- Language borrowed from the IPS that a bond reader can puncture ("immune to markets", "without doubt", "pay what they
  say" for a fund that pays net asset value). INTENT already guards this. Hold the line.

### Is "$110,000 to $135,000, inside it unless the US Treasury defaults" credible?

Credible as to the top, and nearly tautological as to the range. After the January 2031 move both ends are Treasuries
maturing late 2032, and the haircuts for the final months' interest are already taken. So the confidence statement is
honest about the money. The problem is not overpromising; it is the opposite. Central case, the gift is about $138,700,
above the stated top. The honest statement in 2031 is therefore "at least $135,000, barring a default". A range of
$110,000 to $135,000 is credible only if the report explains what the bottom is: the figure she could already promise in
January 2028, which holds even if the shares were worth nothing on move day. In 2031 that bottom is history, and a
co-sponsor reads a range as "expect the bottom". Two risks follow. First, the actual gift exceeds the range unless the
report defines the contribution as capped at the stated top with the surplus joining the kept half, which NUMBERS
section 4 (definition D12) implies but the skeleton does not state. Second, judges who read the case's question as
"explain how favourable and unfavourable outcomes could affect the amount" will find the answer "they cannot, after
2031" too neat unless the report shows what Laura would have announced had markets fallen before the move (top about
$111,000, so a range that is essentially the floor) or risen (top about $171,000). Shown that way, the design is
impressive: the team moved the uncertainty from 2033, where it would damage her credibility, to 2028-2031, where only
the size of her top is at stake. Not shown, it reads as a question dodged by construction.

Also say once, plainly, what "unless the US Treasury defaults" does not cover: the floor fund pays net asset value, not
par, and its last months are in short bills, which is why 4.1% and 2.5% are deducted. One sentence turns a slogan into
a statement a bond reader respects.

### How the flexibility should be presented

As a project contingency, not a personal reserve. Four moves make it prudence rather than hedging:

1. Name its purpose in the project's terms, which the IPS already does (overruns, a stronger Taiwan dollar, a co-sponsor
   falling short). Add the order in which it would be used.
2. Size it at the time of speaking. In 2031 Laura can say roughly what it is worth and that it moves with markets until
   2033. A funder would rather hear "about $20,000 to $25,000, held in shares, available to the project" than nothing.
3. State a release rule and a destination: when it is drawn (a named overrun, a named shortfall), who decides, and
   where it goes if unused (to the facility, or to operating support beyond her commitment, both of which the case
   allows).
4. Defend why it is in shares. The reason is in the IPS: it is the only money promised to no one, so it is the only
   place growth can live. The report should add NUMBERS 8f's honest sizes: median about $18,000 when shares fall against
   $27,000 when they rise, about $15,000 after a fall of a fifth, and about $6,500 even from September 1929. A cushion
   that shrinks but never vanishes, kept in the one asset that can also grow, is a reasoned choice. Compare it, without
   estimating the facility's cost, to the contingency that builders routinely hold as a share of a project.

## 3. The five weakest points, most serious first, each with a fix

1. **The range is a range of choice presented as a range of uncertainty (3c).** After the 2031 move the gift is about
   $138,700, above the stated top, and $110,000 is not a possible outcome. Fix: in 3c define what each end means at
   each date (bottom fixed January 2028 and good even if the shares were worthless on move day; top fixed on move day);
   state in one sentence why she gives a range and not the top alone; define the contribution as the stated top with
   any surplus joining the kept half (D12), so "inside the range" is true by construction and said to be; and make
   Exhibit 3 the 2031 announcement under three market outcomes (bad, central, good) so the case's question about
   favourable and unfavourable markets is answered in one picture.
2. **The co-sponsor passage is the least planned part of the report (3d).** Fix now, not on 27 Nov: decide that it is
   in Laura's voice; that its first sentence is the ten-year operating commitment already owned, with dates; that the
   floor is dated (bought January 2028) and named; that the top is dated (owned since January 2031); that the held-back
   contingency is named and sized; that it says what co-sponsors are asked to fund and what is not asked of them; and
   that it is read before 28 Nov by someone who has funded things, judged on one question: would you take the meeting.
   Give it 250 to 300 words and take them from 3a's fourth evaluation method.
3. **The kept half is confessed, not designed (3b).** Fix: a three-line flexibility rule (purpose and order of use;
   size at 2031 and at 2033; release and destination if unused), plus the defence of keeping it in shares from NUMBERS
   8f, plus the builders' contingency comparison. Without this, "half the shares promised to no one" reads as Laura
   keeping money back from her own project.
4. **The WInS book is not literally the IPS's year one (2.1, 2.5).** The rules equate WInS's $300,000 with the 2027
   deposit; the IPS spends the 2027 deposit on the ten Treasuries alone; the book holds a floor stand-in and VT. Fix:
   answer the question in 2.1's first lines: the book is the whole $450,000 plan scaled to $300,000 so that every
   instrument of the strategy, including the floor fund and VT, was implemented and tested in the trading period; the
   rule survives the scaling (promised money's share in Treasuries unchanged); and the ladder alone cost $301,544 at the
   freeze, more than the whole deposit, which is the IPS's "complete it with the 2028 deposit" clause seen live.
5. **The two thinnest parts carry two of the five criteria (2.3, 3f).** Diversification: make the headline that the
   portfolio diversifies Laura, whose career is the share she cannot sell, and that for a fixed dollar liability the
   matching asset is the one without issuer risk, so issuer diversification would add risk to the payments; VT is
   where conventional diversification lives. Reflection: choose the events this week, each with a before-and-after
   number in the recommendation (what the first plan promised, what the model showed, what the final promises), one
   line on the advisor doing administration only so the decisions are visibly the students', and a word budget of 450.
   An outsider must understand every event without knowing the team.

A sixth, smaller: "$427,028 against $500,000 still owed" needs its explanatory sentence (rungs pay face value as they
mature; the margin of $21,149 and the overflow at today's forwards) in the same breath, or a lay judge will think the
reserve is short.

## 4. What would make this the best report among about 2,300

1. Lead with the proposition no other founder can make: ten years of operations owned in Treasuries before the doors
   open; the facility is the only ask. Say it in 3.0 and again in Laura's words in 3d.
2. Show the range narrowing over time in one exhibit: wide in 2028, owned in 2031, delivered in 2033, under three
   market weathers. That is the case's hardest question answered in a picture.
3. Let the honest costs stand: a lower certain gift than all-Treasuries, a cushion that shrinks in bad years. Judges
   trust a team that prices its own trade-off.
4. One definition of the contribution, used in every sentence and number, so no reader can make "inside the range"
   fail.
5. A monitoring table whose third column is "what it never changes".
6. Co-sponsor words a funder would actually take a meeting on, tested on one before submission.
7. A reflection where every event changed a number.
8. Nothing a bond reader can puncture; every Treasury claim carries its haircut.

## 5. Where other reasonable judges would disagree with me

- A portfolio-manager judge will say the strategy is too conservative for a six-year horizon and that "her career is a
  stock she cannot sell" is a story, not a measured correlation; they would score 2.3 lower than I do and want a
  quantified hedge argument. The IPS is frozen, so the report can only defend, not change; I weight the defence.
- A quantitative judge will score 3c higher than my 3: the design that moves all uncertainty before 2031 is, to them,
  the elegant answer, and they will not mind that the bottom is unreachable in 2031. I think the case's co-sponsor
  framing makes the sandbag matter; they may think I am reading a fundraising problem into an investment question.
- Some judges will read "unless the US Treasury defaults" as refreshing plainness rather than as a slogan that needs a
  footnote; they would not insist on the NAV sentence.
- An educator judge will weight 3f more heavily than I have and may score the current plan a 2 for giving the
  Articulation criterion the smallest budget.
- Some will disagree that 3d deserves 250 to 300 words, arguing the instruction says "provide language", not a case
  statement, and that the pages are better spent on analysis. I hold that Wharton scores the communication explicitly
  and that this is where the client-knowledge case is won or lost.
- Judges will differ on whether the all-Treasury benchmark belongs in the report at all; some will read it as the team
  second-guessing its own IPS. I think the honesty earns more than it costs, provided it is one paragraph and ends on
  why the IPS chose otherwise.
