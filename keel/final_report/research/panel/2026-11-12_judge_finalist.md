# Blind judge: the former finalist (Thu 12 Nov 2026)

Read only the seven files named in the brief. Judged as "the report if written exactly to this plan".

## 1. Who I am and how I read

A first-year university student, a finalist here two years ago, now a volunteer first-round reader.
Fifty reports, about five minutes each, marked on Wharton's five criteria only.
For each part I ask what a typical top-50 report does, whether this one beats it, and what is missing.

## 2. The one idea after five minutes

In my words: Laura only ever promises money that is already sitting in Treasuries, and shares hold only money she has
promised to nobody. I got it at once from INTENT, and it matches the IPS.

Less cleanly from SKELETON row 2.0, which asks 100 words to carry five things: the idea, the portfolio as the idea
built, the payments' standard and its price, the two-thirds scale, and the overrun. The idea is clear; the opening is
crowded.

## 4. Part by part (score / clarity)

**Section 2**
- 2.0 Lead: 4 / 3. Right place for the idea; five jobs in 100 words. "Top-up, most of which comes back" loses me.
- 2.1 What the portfolio is: 4 / 3. Answers why a $300,000 portfolio holds VT. "Two-thirds scale" next to 8.84% and 6.63% is two numbers for one thing.
- 2.2 Construction: 4 / 4. Plain.
- 2.3 Diversification: 4 / 4. The best part of Section 2; most reports cannot match it. The 73% to 23% figure does the work. Two citations in 130 words is one too many.
- 2.5 How well it was done: 4 / 2. The targets-against-results table beats any report I read. Then "basis points", "$234 a basis point", "maturity-matched, not duration-matched" lose a first-round reader, and the last one openly contradicts the team's own first trading note.

**Section 3**
- 3.0 One paragraph: 5 / 4. "The facility is the only ask" is the line I would remember from the whole stack.
- 3a Commitment and reserve: 4 / 3. A real definition of high certainty, which most teams skip. Lost on: "full cash or gross refunding", "Regulation 126". $444,500 held for payments worth $399,089 looks like waste unless the reason sits beside it.
- 3b Contribution and flexibility: 3 / 2. The weakest part. In 260 words: a definition, two kinds of returning money, an order of use, five different probabilities for one test (93, 96.9, 99.3, 98.1, 99.77), a psychology study, an all-Treasury comparison, inflation and the currency. Afterwards I could not say what the contribution was.
- 3c Range and confidence: 4 / 2. Both ends already owned beats every "80% confident" range in the pile. "585 windows", "four rate eras", "1 in 790", "$429", "98.35%" bury it.
- 3d Words for co-sponsors: 4 / 3. Opening on ten years already owned is right. As planned it also carries a close date, conditional pledges, yearly programme money, who owns the site, currency and slippage in 250 words. A term sheet, not Laura.
- 3e Assumptions and monitoring: 3 / 2. "Only two reasons to change the plan" matches the IPS. Credit default swap prices, correlations of -0.31 and +0.22, and the X-date in 150 words are for someone else.
- 3f Team Reflection: 3 / 3. Unbuilt. 600 words is the right weight.

**Exhibits**
- Table 1, holdings: 4 / 4. Meets the rule. The extra row for money that left at purchase is not in Wharton's example; I would look twice.
- Figure 1, the reserve: 4 / 3. One holding per payment reads fast. "If nothing earns interest" columns need a second look.
- Table 2, several markets: 4 / 4. Answers Wharton's "favorable and unfavorable outcomes".
- Table 3, monitoring: 3 / 2. Seven rows by five columns, single-spaced. I would skim two rows and move on.

## 6. Where others will disagree with me

A finance professor would score Portfolio Analysis higher than I do for the career-risk literature and the stated
limits. They would mark harder on the simulation's zero
link between shares and rates, and on overlapping history counted as 585 tests.

A practising wealth manager would rate Table 3 and 3d above my scores, since defined exits and a countable figure are
how real mandates work. They would rate 3a and 3b below mine: sizing for zero interest ties up about $45,500, and about 7% in shares for a
young client with a six-year wait is hard to call balanced.
