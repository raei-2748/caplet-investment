# Funder test reader 1 (US private foundation; framing B), Mon 16 Nov 2026

**1. Figure in my board paper.** $115,000, filed as **committed**, not secured. The assets are already bought, they are Treasuries, and the top is stated after costs. That is better than most founder pledges I see, which are "I'll put in what I can when the house sells." But in my office "secured" means the money has been received or is covered by a signed pledge agreement or a restricted account. Treasuries held for her at her own broker are earmarked, and she can still redirect them. I would move the figure to secured once she signs a pledge or moves the money into a restricted building account.

**2. Meeting?** Yes. What decides it is that the founder's own money is in, and the market can't move it. "Has the founder put her own money in first, and is it real?" is the first question any capital-campaign funder asks, and here the answer is plainly yes.

**3. How the range reads.** At first it read as a margin for error, because that's what a range usually means to me. Then it read as a statement of when each part was secured: "the range shows when each part was secured", "held since January 2028", "the rest since half her shares moved". I came round, but it took a second read. A board member skimming it will ask, "So why give a range at all?"

**4. Promise and hope.** Promise: "Treasuries she already holds when she speaks." Hope: nothing in the text reads as hope, which is rare. The nearest is "a margin held back for the funds' running costs." It's honest, but it makes me ask how big the margin is. "Before shares had any say" is a bit too clever for a funder letter.

**5. First-five-minutes question.** "What does the building cost in total, and who pays if it runs over?"

- Clarity: 3
- Credibility: 4
