## Context every reader gets

Laura Gao, a graphic novelist, is founding an artists' residency in Taiwan. From her own investments she has already
bought US Treasuries that pay ten fixed operating payments of $50,000, one each January 2033-2042. Separately she will
contribute to the building, which co-sponsors (foundations, companies, public bodies) will help fund. In 2031 she must
tell co-sponsors her building contribution as a dollar range with how confident she is; over-promising would damage
her credibility. Her contribution is paid in mid-December 2032, before the residency's first operating year.

How the money is held, in plain words: in January 2028 she bought a US Treasury fund (a fund that holds US Treasury
bonds and pays out its value in December 2032). Before the 2031 meetings she moves half of her shares into Treasuries
of the same kind. Both purchases are held for her at a US broker. The other half of her shares is promised to no one:
it is her own flexibility (building costs that overrun, a stronger Taiwan dollar, a co-sponsor falling short) and is
never offered to co-sponsors. Each end of the range is worked out as if the money waiting inside the funds earned no
interest, after the funds' 0.07% yearly fee, less a further 0.5% for other fund costs, rounded down. The five funds of
this kind that have already ended showed about 0.27% of such other costs; the contribution would fall short of the top
only if those costs passed about 3% of the funds' value with no interest earned (about 7% at the interest markets
expect), and then by at most about $120 for each further 0.1%.

## Framing A (what co-sponsors were told on Wed 11 Nov)

"In 2031 Laura states her contribution as $95,000 to $115,000. Co-sponsors count the bottom, $95,000: it has been held
for her in US Treasuries since January 2028, before shares had any say. The top, $115,000, is also held in Treasuries
once half her shares move before the 2031 meetings; she intends to give it, and reaches it unless the funds' other
running costs are far higher than any such fund has shown."

## Framing B (tonight's candidate)

"In 2031 Laura states her contribution as $95,000 to $115,000, and she gives the top, $115,000. Both ends are US
Treasuries she already holds when she speaks, so the range shows when each part was secured: $95,000 of it has been
held since January 2028, before shares had any say, and the rest since half her shares moved into Treasuries before the
2031 meetings. Co-sponsors count $115,000, with a margin held back for the funds' running costs."

## What each reader writes (about 300 words, notes only)

1. The figure you put in your board paper, in dollars, and whether you file it as secured, committed or expected. Why.
2. Would you take the meeting? Yes, maybe or no, and the one thing that decides it.
3. Does the range read as a margin for error, a statement of when each part was secured, or something else? Quote the
   words that made you read it that way.
4. One word or phrase that reads as a promise and one that reads as a hope, if any.
5. The one question you would ask the founder in the first five minutes.
6. Scores 1-5: clarity; credibility; would this founder's figure survive your board's questions.
