# Funder test reader 3 (US private foundation; framing A), Mon 16 Nov 2026

**1. Board-paper figure.** $95,000, filed as committed, with the further $20,000 footnoted as expected. I would not call it secured. In my file, secured means cash received, money in escrow, or a signed irrevocable pledge. Treasuries "held for her" at her own broker are still her asset, and she could redirect them. That said, this is the best-backed founder figure I have seen in fifteen years, and a signed pledge letter for $95,000 would move it to secured. The $20,000 stays expected because the passage says "she intends to give it", and intent is exactly what we file as expected.

**2. Meeting?** Yes. What decides it is that the founder bought her floor in 2028, years before she asks anyone else for money. In capital campaigns, the gift that sinks the others is a founder's gift that arrives late or shrinks. This one already sits in bonds that pay out before the building bill comes due.

**3. How the range reads.** It reads as a statement of when each part was secured, not as a margin for error: "held for her in US Treasuries since January 2028, before shares had any say" and "also held in Treasuries once half her shares move". That raised an eyebrow. If both ends are in Treasuries by the time she meets us, the $20,000 gap is about her willingness to give, not about whether the money exists. The cost caveat ("far higher than any such fund has shown") covers a few hundred dollars, not $20,000.

**4. Promise / hope.** Promise: "Co-sponsors count the bottom, $95,000." Hope: "she intends to give it."

**5. First question.** "If the full $115,000 is already in Treasuries when we meet, what would lead you to give less than all of it, and will you put the $95,000 in a pledge letter?"

**6. Scores.** Clarity 4 (the tense in "is also held ... once" trips me, and "before shares had any say" needed a second read). Credibility 5. Survives my board 4 (the board's first question will be "is it restricted to the project?").
