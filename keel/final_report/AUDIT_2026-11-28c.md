### 2. Table 2's $57,070 of extra receipts: which Treasuries, and is each payment met on time? (confirmed)

**What the $57,070 is.** It is the total the extra Treasuries pay towards the ten payments. They are bought from the first

The $35,213 is $36,774 in January 2028 money.

**How each payment is met.**
- Every coupon, fund payout and principal repayment from these extra holdings waits in the Treasury-bill ETF until the next
  payment is due. Any surplus carries forward.
- Under the draft's own standard, every row of Table 2 matches the register's section 8zb exactly. That standard is: no
  interest, a 0.15% a year fee on waiting cash, and every fund payout 0.5% smaller.
  - The extra cover runs $2,899 for 2033, $3,503, $4,089, $4,796, $5,732, $6,805, $6,939, $6,957, $7,820, and $7,531 for 2042.

  falls due.

**Timing.**

- The five bonds mature between ten and a half and one and a half months early: Feb 2037, May 2038 and Nov 2039-2041,
  against January 2038-2042. "Matures ten and a half months early" for the 2038 payment is right.

**Sufficiency was rebuilt independently.**

### 4. The 1.5% below $95,000, and how it differs from the reliability of the 2031 range (confirmed)

**The 1.5% is projected proceeds in the 200,000 simulations.** On the register engine (`below95.py`), 1.4585% of futures have
Table 3's projected facility proceeds under $95,000.

**Every one of them has yields falling before 2028.**
- The average fall among them is about 2.1 points.
- With no early move in yields, none falls below $95,000.
- 1.15% still fall below even with shares at the median, so this is a rates effect, not a share effect.

**This is not the reliability of the range.**
- The range is set in 2031 from holdings already bought, and the 2028 purchases are sized on the yields of that day.
- Once the range is stated, no simulated future leaves the bottom or the top short at the 0.5% allowance.
- So the note's sentence is right: "Outcomes below $95,000 reflect falling yields before 2028, not shortfalls against the
  range set in 2031."

**One point for the writers.**
- The $95,000 bottom is today's figure. In 26.5% of futures, the floor bought in 2028 pays less than $95,000, because yields
  have fallen by then.
- In those futures the 2031 range would simply be stated lower.
- The draft already ties the range to "today's curve", which covers this.

### 5. Monte Carlo assumptions, return conventions, volatility and stress results (confirmed; two optional disclosures)

**The note's inputs match the code.**
- 200,000 paths, seed 2033.
- World shares lognormal, with a median growth of 7.5% a year and volatility of 17.88%. These are J.P. Morgan's 2027
  long-term assumptions for AC World equity in US dollars: compound 7.50, arithmetic 8.94, volatility 17.88.
- Yields move in parallel by 0.9 points a year, in three steps (now to January 2027, 2027 to 2028, 2028 to 2031), floored
  at 0.1%.
- Fees are 0.06% for VT and 0.07% for the iBond funds.

**Return convention.**
- The 7.5% is used as the median (compound) growth rate, which is how J.P. Morgan states it.
- At 17.88% volatility, the lognormal's implied arithmetic mean is about 9.2%, a little above J.P. Morgan's 8.94%. The
  draft's "7.5% median annual equity growth" is the right description.

**Stress results in the draft.**
- "About one simulated future in ten" short with fund costs of 0.75%: confirmed. It is 10.9% in the audit.
- "The largest shortfall was roughly $450": confirmed ($447 in the register, $419 in the audit). It happens only when
  waiting cash earns nothing.
- "57%" for the top: this is the register's figure. The audit's rebuild gives 61.8%, so the draft's figure is the cautious one.
- The worst history window (mid-1927 start) and the Taiwan cost line were fixed earlier and still hold.

**Not disclosed (optional, no change made):**
- Shares and yields are drawn independently.
- J.P. Morgan's figures are 10-15-year averages, applied here to 2028-2033.

Neither changes a figure. One clause in the note would cover both if a judge is likely to ask.
