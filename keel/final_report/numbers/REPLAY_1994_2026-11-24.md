# The 1994 replay, with a script (Tue 24 Nov 2026)

**MODEL; Keel arithmetic; a history replay, never a plan figure.** Card Q232 rows 11-12; NUMBERS 9's open item
("1994 replay"). Script `numbers/replay_1994.py` (run `python3 -I keel/final_report/numbers/replay_1994.py`), results
`numbers/results_replay_1994_2026-11-24.json`, data `numbers/data/fred_1994/` (`SOURCE.txt`).

**What was run.**
- **Data.** FRED's daily constant-maturity par yields: DGS1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 20 and 30 (H.15). They were read at
  source on 24 Nov 2026 through WebFetch, because the proxy refuses curl to FRED. Every row is identical to the
  repo's 27 Sep mirror (4,528 rows, no differences). There are 500 trading days from 31 Dec 1993 to 29 Dec 1995.
- **Curve.** The register's method A (`engine.Curve`). Each day's par yields are interpolated linearly on a half-year
  grid, with the 1-year yield held flat below 1 year. They are then bootstrapped to discount factors and interpolated
  log-linearly. Time is days / 365.25.
- **A, the payments' value** (row 11's method). Ten $50,000 payments due 1 Jan 2000-2009, each valued as a zero.
  They sit six to fifteen years ahead, as Laura's do from 2027. Duration is 9.97 years (Laura's payments: 9.94,
  NUMBERS 1).
- **B, a holding** (row 12's method). Ten $50,000-face Treasuries maturing 31 Dec 1999-2008, each bought at par on
  31 Dec 1993 (coupons 5.37%-6.15%, cost $500,000). Its value is the bonds' full price plus coupons received, with
  the coupons held as cash at zero. Duration is **7.76 years, the same as Laura's own holdings (NUMBERS 1)**.
- **Both ladders.** Held unchanged and marked every trading day, so their terms shorten. A weekly mark gives A the
  same low; B's weekly low is 4 Nov 1994 at -9.11%.

Variant from late January (31 Jan 1994, the last close before the Fed's 4 Feb move; ladder set six to fifteen
years out that day):
- **A** fell 14.5% to 4 Nov 1994 and was back on 9 May 1995.
- **B** fell 10.9% to 9 May 1994 and was back on 5 May 1995.

Adding the 3-month and 6-month yields (from the mirror) moves no figure by more than about $25.

No world share index is included: MSCI's level service refuses dates before 1997, and the repo's Shiller series
covers US shares only.

**Against the card.**
- **Row 11 is reproduced.** Every dollar figure agrees within $0.41. The dates (12 Jan, 4 Nov, 4 May) and the 309
  days all agree.
- **Row 12 is reproduced:** -9.15% on 9 May 1994, back on 20 Apr 1995, -7.06% and +13.8%. The one exception is the
  total paid: $809,008 here against the card's $809,058. The card set its coupons on an exact half-year grid, and
  the script checks this (on that grid the total is $809,058). The difference is $50 and moves no fall or date.
- **One wording catch.** "Fell up to 14.7% by 4 Nov 1994 and was back by 4 May 1995" mixes two bases. The 14.7% is
  measured from the 12 Jan high, but 4 May 1995 is the return to the 31 Dec 1993 value. The value got back to the
  high only on 9 May 1995. So pair the figures like for like: either 12.6% with 4 May 1995, or 14.7% with 9 May 1995.

**Fit for the register?** Yes, as a history replay labelled MODEL and Keel arithmetic, now that a script and the
data are filed. Lead with B, because it has Laura's holdings' duration. Never write "a ladder like hers lost about
15%": that was the value of the promises, which she cannot hold as zeros (STRIPS are banned).

**One sentence the register could carry:** "In 1994 (FRED H.15 par yields; `numbers/replay_1994.py`; MODEL, Keel
arithmetic), ten $50,000-face Treasuries bought at par on 31 Dec 1993 and maturing six to fifteen years later, the
same 7.76-year duration as Laura's holdings, were worth at most 9.15% less (9 May 1994) and were back to cost on 20
Apr 1995. Valued as zeros, the ten $50,000 payments they were bought for were 12.6% below their 31 Dec 1993 value on
4 Nov 1994 and back to it on 4 May 1995. Not one dollar either ladder would pay on its dates moved."
