**Why it changed (8 Nov evening).** The old plan researched for nine days before anything said what shape the report
would take, re-ran the numbers on day 9, asked for Caplet's sentences on day 14 and drew the charts on day 16. A report
written in one day needs its skeleton, numbers and exhibits settled long before. So: numbers first (Mon 9), so every
note rests on one set of figures; the skeleton the day Wharton's instructions land (Tue 10), so the research answers
the report's questions rather than our own; the co-sponsor and fundraising questions forward, because the store is
thinnest there and Wharton scores co-sponsor communication; Caplet's sentences asked five days earlier, so their voice
is in hand before the voice studies; exhibits a week earlier, so each can be tested on a fresh reader; a rehearsal of
the review machinery on the IPS Caplet already wrote, so the 24-hour session does not test its tools for the first
time on the real thing; check-ins during the session, so twelve rounds are paced rather than hoped for; and a review
week to the deadline, so Keel is still useful after handing over. Logged in `../pm/ASK_HUMANS.md`.

## The fourteen research questions
Questions 1-7 each come from a sentence in the FINAL IPS: 1. Is Laura's career really a stock, and what kind? 2. Who
else promises fixed payments, and how did they keep or break them? 3. Floors that held and floors that broke. 4. The
range: what co-sponsors need to hear, and how to say how sure we are. 5. Flexibility: why keep half, and is half right?
6. The shares: what VT is for, and how far to trust 7.5%. 7. Rules and time.

Questions 8-14 cover the rest of the case (Book 15, Ray 8 Nov: every part of the report rests on research, not only
the investing): 8. What "a high degree of certainty" means outside finance (engineering reliability targets, Solvency
II's 1-in-200, ALARP). 9. What makes a residency last (Black Mountain College, MacDowell, Yaddo, Taiwan's residencies,
nonprofit reserves). 10. Taiwan: building costs, the Taiwan dollar, earthquakes, public arts funders. 11. The
fundraising text (assurance contracts, case for support, gift range charts, real campaign documents). 12. Laura's
working life (advances, royalties, school and library market, speaking, licensing; the 2028 deposit's risk). 13. How
the team learned (Articulation: pre-mortems, decision journals, Tetlock; real events only). 14. How the report shows
its work (Creativity and Presentation: Cleveland and McGill, icon arrays, the SEC Plain English Handbook, McCloud).

Pairs by day: Tue 10 Q1 (deepened) + Q12; Wed 11 Q4+Q11; Thu 12 Q2+Q8; Fri 13 Q3+Q9; Sat 14 Q5+Q10; Sun 15
Q6+Q7 + digest; Mon 16 Q13+Q14 + exhibits. Q1's first pass ran on Sun 8 Nov.

Every idea is judged on five questions before it can reach the report (what is it; did it work; how Laura's case is
like it; how it is not; what we take), in `research/`.
