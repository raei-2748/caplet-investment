## The governing thought (one idea the whole report serves)

Money Laura has promised is owned in Treasuries before she promises it; only money promised to no one takes market
risk. The IPS set this rule (the payments in Treasuries; the bottom of the gift a Treasury fund; half the shares moved
into Treasuries before she names a figure; half promised to no one). The report shows that the WInS book put the rule
into practice, and applies the same rule to each thing Wharton asks Caplet to recommend: the operating reserve, the
facility contribution, the 2031 range and its confidence, the words for co-sponsors and what to watch.

## The argument in three sentences (EXPECTATIONS 2.9: the strategy, its biggest strength, its main trade-off)

1. The strategy: ten Treasuries pay the ten operating payments; the 2028 deposit completes them, then buys a Treasury
   fund maturing late 2032 for the bottom of the gift and a world share fund for the top and for flexibility.
2. Its biggest strength: every promise Laura makes, to the residency in 2033 and to co-sponsors in 2031, is backed by
   money she already owns, so the confidence she states comes from structure rather than from a forecast.
3. Its main trade-off: a lower certain gift than an all-Treasury plan at the bottom, and a smaller cushion in the years
   shares fall, in exchange for a share of world growth, half of which is never promised to anyone.

## Five things a judge should be able to say after the first page

1. The ten payments rest on ten Treasuries, each paying out just before its payment; from 2033 they are the operating
   reserve, about $427,000 on 1 Jan 2033 at today's forward rates against the $399,000 the payments are worth at the
   same rates, about 107% funded (NUMBERS 8h, MODEL).
2. In 2031 Laura can tell co-sponsors a range of about $110,000 to $130,000 (central, NUMBERS 4; framing under review), and once half the
   shares have moved, both ends are Treasuries she owns: the 2033 gift falls inside it unless the US Treasury defaults.
3. Half the shares are promised to no one, for overruns, a stronger Taiwan dollar or a co-sponsor falling short; that
   cushion is smallest in the years it is most likely to be needed, and the report says so (NUMBERS 8f).
4. Laura watches many things, but only a missed deposit or a Treasury default changes the plan; everything else changes
   a number, never a payment.
5. The team changed its own first plan on its own evidence, and its recommendation came from disagreements it settled
   on reasons.

## Ways the report could fail (each has a scorecard row)

1. **It reads as the IPS again.** Wharton: "without repeating the content of your IPS". Guard: every paragraph
   evaluates or recommends; the IPS is cited, not restated.
2. **A bond reader catches a loose claim** ("immune to markets", "without doubt", "pay what they say" for an iBond
   fund). Guard: the canon's exact meanings (`CANON.md`); NAV not par; reinvestment priced; the margin explained.
3. **The confidence statement overpromises or is vague.** Guard: one definition of the contribution (floor plus moved
   half, CANON D12); the confidence said in numbers and words; what could move it named.
4. **The reflection is generic or retells the Trading Notes.** Guard: three or four real events from the blind
   ranking, each tied to a change in the recommendation.
5. **It is too dense to follow at speed.** Guard: the stack test on the first page; word budgets per part
   (`SKELETON.md`); cut whole ideas, never squeeze sentences (EXPECTATIONS 3.2).
