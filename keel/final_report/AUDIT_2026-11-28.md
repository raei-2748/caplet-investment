# Independent audit of the Final Report's numbers (Sat 28 Nov 2026)

Audited: the "big w's big w" tab of the team's Final Report Google Doc, as it stood at 21:15 Sydney on Sat 28 Nov,
read only (nothing in any Caplet document was changed). The IPS tab was read for coherence. This file is the
quantitative audit only; the editorial review is separate.

How: every figure was rebuilt from primary data with new code written for this audit
(`audit_2026-11-28/`, which imports nothing from `numbers/`). `NUMBERS.md` was used only afterwards, as the comparison
point, to explain differences. Where the existing engine made a simplifying choice, the audit made a different,
more literal one, so that agreement means something:

## The verdict in one paragraph

The headline numbers hold. The stated range of $95,000 to $115,000, the $57,070 shortfall, the $445,655 reserve, the
$28,700 Laura keeps and Table 3's comparison all come back within about 2% on an independent rebuild, and every
difference runs in the cautious direction: the draft slightly understates what the plan delivers. Five things in the
words need fixing, and one of them matters a great deal: Table 3 is labelled as money held in January 2031, but its
figures are what the facility Treasuries will pay by December 2032, so the sentence beneath it says the opposite of
what the numbers are. The sensitivity sentence also understates how often a 0.75% fund cost would bite (about one
future in ten, not one in seventeen), though by at most about $450 and only if waiting cash also earns nothing.

## What needs fixing, in order of importance

**1. Table 3 measures 2032 payouts, not 2031 holdings (the 2031-to-2033 link).** Its six figures are what the
Treasuries held for the facility after the January 2031 move (the late-2032 fund bought in 2028 plus the half of VT
moved in 2031) will pay by 15 December 2032, with their interest reinvested at expected rates. The audit's rebuild
of exactly that quantity reproduces the table (below). They are not market values in January 2031. So:
- the title "Facility Assets in 2031" and the row label "Money held for the facility, January 2031" mislead;
- "These figures measure the assets Laura holds in 2031, rather than what she can contribute in 2033" is the wrong
  way round. They are the closest thing in the report to what she can contribute in 2033;
- how they connect to the range: the stated range is the same money measured more strictly (no interest on anything
  waiting, fund payouts less 0.5%, rounded down to $5,000). In the central case Table 3's quantity is about $122,500;
  the strict measure gives $117,142, stated as $115,000; the contribution is $115,000 and the $7,500 between them goes
  back to Laura (it is part of the $28,700). Table 3's "below $95,000" rows therefore compare a payout at expected
  interest with the stated bottom.
- Suggested wording (the team's to change): title "What the facility Treasuries will pay by December 2032"; row
  label "Paid by December 2032 by the Treasuries held after the January 2031 move"; the sentence "These figures are
  what Laura's facility Treasuries would pay by the end of 2032 with interest earned as expected. The range she
  states is the same money counted more strictly: no interest, fund payouts 0.5% lower, rounded down."
- In the text, "leaves Laura with less than $95,000 at the meeting" would be accurate as "leaves Laura, at the
  meeting, with Treasuries that will pay less than $95,000".

**2. The 0.25-point sensitivity is understated.** "A 0.75% reduction in fund payouts rather than 0.5% causes a
shortfall against at least one of the announced amounts in about one in seventeen simulated futures." The audit
finds: the top falls short in 6.2% of paths (about 1 in 16), the bottom in 5.0%, and at least one of the two in
10.9% (about 1 in 9). The register agrees on the parts (top 5.8%, bottom 4.8%; its "1 in 17" is the top alone). The
largest shortfall in any path is about $420 (register: $447). It needs cash waiting to earn nothing as well: at
expected interest no path falls short, and the floor alone earns about $5,000 more than the strict measure counts.
The cause is only the rounding: once 0.5% is used up, the cushion is the distance down to the next $5,000 step.
Suggested wording: "If fund costs ran to 0.75% rather than 0.5%, and waiting cash also earned nothing, one of the two
figures would fall short in about one simulated future in ten, by at most about $450. Any interest at all on waiting
cash closes that gap." This fits as a monitoring trigger (check the two funds' tracking each January from 2028), as
both reviewers suggested, rather than a new subsection.

**3. Table 3's note mixes its two models.** "Treasury rates follow the 6 November 2026 forward curve" is true of the
history rows only; in the 200,000 simulations yields move (0.9 points a year in the register; the draft does not say).
And the history uses 1,770 five-year windows (January 2028 to January 2033: three years to the move, two more for
the half kept), not "1,770 rolling three-year US equity return periods". Confirmed: Jan 1871 to Jun 2023 gives
exactly 1,770 five-year starts, the last in June 2018. Suggested note: "200,000 simulations (world shares 7.5% a year
median growth, 17.88% volatility; yields moving about 0.9 percentage points a year) and every five-year window of US
shares with dividends since 1871 (1,770 windows), the latter with Treasury rates on the 6 November 2026 forward curve."

**4. The 1929 stress.** "Under a historical stress test based on 1929, she would retain only about $12,500." The
lowest window starts in mid-1927 (shares bought then are hit in 1930-32): about $11,800 in the audit, $12,542 in the
register. A July 1929 start leaves about $14,100. Say "in the worst window in US history, the Great Depression, she
would keep only about $12,000", or keep $12,500 and drop "based on 1929".

**5. The purchasing-power sentence uses a US inflation rate for a Taiwan building.** $115,000 / 1.025^7 = $96,746,
so the arithmetic is right. But the register's US inflation figure is 2.4% (J.P. Morgan; $97,400), and what the money
buys in Taiwan follows Taiwan's construction costs (3.19% a year over the five years to October 2026; at 3% the
$115,000 is about $93,500). Either name the 2.5% as an assumption or use the building-cost rate, which also suits the
sentence's own point.

## Where the draft is cautious (no change needed, but know it)

**6. The extra Treasuries are sufficient, not the minimum.** $36,774 pays all ten under the stress assumptions.
The audit's exact minimum under the engine's own rule (each of the ten keeps its receipts for its own payment) is
$35,454. If every receipt may pay whichever payment comes next, which is how cash actually works when it earns
nothing, $32,153 is enough (checked by simulating one cash account: every payment met, nothing left). On both models
that saving would lift the stated range by one $5,000 step at each end ($100,000 to $120,000 in the audit's rebuild,
and the register's bottom of $97,605 would also pass $100,000). This is a design choice, not an error, and changing
it now would change the headline range, so the audit does not recommend it without re-running the main engine and
re-checking every downstream figure. It does mean the report should never call $36,774 the minimum (it does not now).
The register's note that pooling gains only $523 is not reproduced here; the numbers owner should reconcile that
before anyone acts on either figure.

**7. The first deposit's shortfall ($2,388) is method-sensitive.** The audit's curves give $1,960 to $2,066 (in
January 2028 money). The gap is curve interpolation between 10 and 20 years (the engine interpolates par yields in
straight lines; Treasury's own curve and the audit's are smooth). The funds' notes also traded about 0.1-0.2 per 100
below the par curve on 25 Nov, so a market purchase would cost slightly less again. "Falls $2,388 short" can stay; it
is the cautious end.

**8. The odds of a $115,000 ceiling.** "Roughly 57%" comes back as 61.8% (59% to 66% across yield volatility 0.6 to
1.2 points a year, and 59.6% if the 7.5% is read as an arithmetic mean). It is lower in the register because its
extra Treasuries and its fund model are both more cautious. "Roughly 57%" is defensible as the register's figure;
"a little better than half" is safe on every run.

## WInS against the real plan (how the book compresses two deposits)

ChatGPT's qualification is right: the WInS book is not a proportional scaling of Laura's plan. It is a compression
of both deposits into one $300,000 portfolio, built before the extra Treasuries were sized.
- Each operating rung is held at 62.5% to 67.7% of its full size (proportional to $300,000 of $450,000 would be
  66.7%).
- The facility is over-weighted: in the book the floor stand-in (3,361 of the 4,503 IBTM shares) is 24.3% and VT
  8.8%; in the plan on 1 January 2028 the floor is 17.5% and VT 6.4% of about $463,000. Scaled by 1.5, the book holds
  $293,830 for operations, $109,148 for the floor and $39,787 in VT, against the plan's $339,162, $81,244 and $29,594.
- The extra Treasuries ($36,774) are not in the book at all.
- So IBTM's 32.5% in Table 1 is two jobs at book weights, not the plan's weights. Table 1 itself is right: every
  amount is quantity times price paid, the Treasury subtotal is $268,652.18 (89.55%), VT 8.84%, cash 1.17%, and the
  total is $300,000.00.
- Suggested sentence (the team's call): "WInS gave us one $300,000 portfolio for a plan with two deposits, so Table 1
  holds each operating payment at about two-thirds of its full size and gives the building slightly more than its
  share; the extra Treasuries described below came after trading closed."

## What could not be verified this session

- **Whether 0.5% is enough for fund costs beyond the fee.** It rests on the register's reading of the five ended
  iBonds Treasury funds (about 0.27%, card Q3c). Their final payouts were not re-read. The model's results are
  conditional on it, and finding 2 shows what happens a quarter-point beyond it.
- **The 17.88% volatility.** Taken from the register's reading of the J.P. Morgan 2027 USD matrix (9 Nov); the
  matrix was not re-read. The 7.5% was.
- **Market prices of the five bonds on 6 Nov.** Treasury's FedInvest price file for that date could not be fetched.
  The audit priced the bonds on the curve; the funds' own notes were 0.1-0.2 per 100 cheaper than the curve on 25 Nov.
- **IBTQ's holdings** are as of 21 Sep 2026 (the file ishares.com served); the other four funds' are as of 25 Nov.
- **The history series** is the Shiller data mirror already in the repo (`keel/models/data/`), not re-fetched from
  Yale. Its prices are monthly averages, which slightly soften the deepest falls.
