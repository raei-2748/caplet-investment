## 1. The governing thought

**From January 2028, every dollar Laura has promised is paid by Treasuries held for her before she promises it; only
money promised to no one takes market risk.** In 2027 the payments' money is about $39,000 short of the report's
standard, and the 2028 deposit fills that gap before it buys anything else (D20's dated form; IPS l.11's rule, applied,
never quoted as the report's own words).

Situation, complication, question (Minto), one line each, which the report never prints as a set-up:
- Situation: $450,000 arrives in two deposits, 2027 and 2028; ten fixed $50,000 payments run 2033-2042; a facility
  contribution follows; in 2031 she must tell co-sponsors a figure with a confidence.
- Complication: she must state a figure two years before she pays it, from a portfolio that must also grow, and her
  income moves with the economy, so the second deposit may come later and smaller.
- Question: how can she promise with a high degree of certainty and still give the building a share of growth?
- Answer: the governing thought. Each promise is bought before it is made; the order in which the money is spent does
  the work that diversification would do elsewhere.

**The main trade-off, said once (INTENT's three sentences, sentence 3):** a lower certain contribution at the bottom
than an all-Treasury plan, and less flexibility for her in the years shares fall, in exchange for a share of world
growth, half of which is never promised to anyone.

### Branch 1 (Section 2). The WInS book is the rule built at $300,000, and it was bought carefully

- **Claim.** Our portfolio holds the plan's instruments, built from the $450,000 plan and scaled to $300,000: ten
  Treasury holdings, each paying just before its payment, the late-2032 Treasury fund for the bottom of the range, and
  VT for the top and her flexibility.
- **Why it is built that way (2.1-2.2).** Promised money needs the asset of the same date, so each payment gets the
  latest Treasury before it: iBond Treasury funds for 2033-2037 (forced for 2033-2036, since WInS listed no bond
  maturing Feb 2031 to Feb 2036; a choice of wrapper for 2037), bonds held to maturity for 2038-2042 (no iBond fund ends
  2037-2043). Matched payment by payment, so duration matches too, bond by bond, and nothing needs rebalancing (D13).
  The mix changes only on three dated steps, January 2027, January 2028 and January 2031, each buying a promise before
  it is stated (GOLDMINE 27; never "glide path" for her plan).
- **Diversification (2.3).** Across dates, not issuers: the risky money is spread across VT's thousands of companies;
  the promised money sits in the one asset that needs no spreading, spread across dates as the law reads diversification
  for a fixed promise (GIW Industries: the loss "could have been avoided by staggering maturity dates"). The layers that

  recession "can" bring both, as 2008-09 and 2020 did (one-person artists' and writers' receipts -7.8% in 2009). Laura's own words, once: "until the day it hits the shelves" (GOLDMINE 36).

### Branch 2 (3a). The ten payments are paid by Treasuries held for her, even if cash waiting earns nothing

- **Claim.** All ten payments are paid from January 2028 even if cash waiting earns nothing; the report holds IPS
  l.11's promise to that standard (D14, "gross funding", MSRB's "full cash" refunding named once for bond readers).
- **Why the standard, before the replay.** Bond interest arrives years before the payment it helps pay, and the rate it
  will earn in the meantime is not known. Forward rates are the rates implied by today's prices, and they have been a
  poor guide to the rates that followed (Fama and Bliss 1987; Campbell and Shiller 1991); a forward rate can be locked in
  only by someone who can borrow at it (Shiller and co-authors 1983, card Q205), and Laura only lends, so zero is the one
  rate on waiting cash she can count on, and the extra Treasuries are the price of that lock. Then the replay (method
  named: history's moves in the one-year yield applied in proportion to today's forward rates): priced at
  today's rates, reinvested interest would have fallen short more often than not for start dates since 1981 and far
  less often before (585 starting months since 1962; never a bare 55.6%). The test runs each of the ten with its own
  interest and pools only the extra Treasuries (bond 9, answered).
- **The price, in the next breath.** The first $39,162 of the 2028 deposit at today's rates (about a quarter of it): the
  top-up ($2,388) completes the ten; the extra Treasuries ($36,774) answer the case's ask to size the operating reserve.
  What comes back if later rates turn out as today's prices imply: about $46,600 in 1 January 2033 money, all of it hers
  by 2042; if cash waiting earns nothing, none beyond the 0.5% margin.
- **The reserve as a schedule (D18).** Year, instrument, payout counted at zero further interest, the extra Treasuries'
  face; it pays $502,605 at zero interest against $500,000 owed; market value (about $445,700, 111.7% of the $399,089
  owed at that date) a by-product in Figure 1's note. One release rule: each 1 January, once that year's payment is
  made, anything above what the remaining payments need at zero interest is hers.
- **Why the two pots are separate.** Funders' own warning: even well-planned organisations "often find it difficult to
  protect these funds from covering cost overruns, fundraising shortfalls" (Oman, Grantmakers in the Arts, 2018; card
  Q202). The plan meets it by construction: the payments' Treasuries are not the building's money.
- **Headline number.** $39,162, about a quarter of the 2028 deposit, as the price of the standard.
- **Aha.** Row 5: each payment rests on its own instrument, and the one forecast the trade notes left (the rate on
  reinvested interest, "reinvested coupons fund the rest") is taken out by the extra Treasuries.
- **Conceit.** The one line that waited in pencil, the interest on cash waiting, inked in January 2028 by the extra
  Treasuries.
- **Outside parallel (one).** The California State Lottery's rule: investments mature "in amounts sufficient to meet
  required prize payouts on dates immediately prior to" each prize, with extra bonds held against reinvestment risk
  (store Q16-018; named by all four judges as surviving).
- **Left out.** "A forecast in disguise" for the ten (TNA l.15 says "not merely a forecast"); "what the market expects";
  structured settlements (26 U.S.C. 130(d)) and Florida's prepaid plan (store, behind the lottery); "covers the decade".

### Branch 3 (3b). The facility contribution is the floor plus the moved half; her flexibility is promised to no one

- **The currency (D9, card Q203).** Her figure is stated in US dollars, the currency her Treasuries pay, with its NT$

- **The design not taken (one clause).** Portfolio insurance, which sold shares as prices fell and broke in 1987; her

- **Headline number.** $110,000 against $115,000 (row 6).
- **Conceit.** Ink only goes one way; her flexibility is the pencil never inked, glossed in dashes with what it shows

### Branch 4 (3c). The range: uncertain from today, held once the shares are moved

- **Claim, in one form (D1).** Two layers in two dated sentences. Seen from today, which figure she will be able to

- **Why a range at all, then.** The bottom tells the residency's planners what markets could not move after January

  the payments: a figure not yet promised may wait on a date; a promise may not (professor 27).
- **The rule behind each end.** Each end is what the fund's own holdings pay if no more interest is earned, worked out
  on the trade day, cut by 0.5% and rounded down to $5,000, one step per clause (round 11's fifth kind of failure). What
  a fund share is, at first mention: a Treasury fund that holds Treasuries maturing in 2032 and, about 15 December 2032, pays
