## 1. The five things judges score (verbatim; the only marking scheme)

> **Investment Strategy:** Presents a clear and creative investment thesis; demonstrates disciplined planning across
> Laura's changing time horizons and cash-flow needs; uses appropriate diversification; and maintains consistency with
> the team's IPS.
>
> **Client Knowledge and Objectives:** Tailors the strategy to Laura's circumstances, priorities, risk considerations,
> and residency goals; demonstrates a thoughtful understanding of the client; and presents recommendations that can
> earn her confidence.
>
> **Portfolio Analysis:** Demonstrates understanding and effective use of investment concepts and tools; integrates
> quantitative and qualitative analysis; and uses reasonable assumptions and projections to evaluate funding
> reliability, the facility contribution, and financial flexibility under varying market outcomes.
>
> **Articulation of Competition Experience:** Demonstrates teamwork, communication, and learning; clearly explains the
> team's research and decision-making process; and reflects meaningfully on the team's growth and response to
> challenges.
>
> **Creativity and Presentation:** Presents a compelling, well-organized narrative in an authentic team voice; uses
> data effectively to support conclusions; demonstrates original thinking and meaningful reflection; and communicates
> Laura's potential facility contribution and investment uncertainty clearly and credibly to prospective co-sponsors.

The Trading Notes Analysis, the IPS and the Final Report are judged together on these five.

## 2. What the IPS is for (updated IPS instructions, verbatim)

> Your investment strategy is the cornerstone of the Wharton Global High School Investment Competition, and your IPS
> is where you clearly articulate that strategy.
>
> Your strategy should explain how you will invest your client's money to support their financial goals and future
> cash flow needs while managing risk and uncertainty. It should reflect your client's goals, time horizons,
> liquidity needs, desired degree of funding certainty, and need for financial flexibility.

**The questions to answer:**
> - What is the central idea behind your investment strategy?
> - What principles will guide your investment decisions?
> - How does your strategy balance growth, risk, liquidity, funding reliability, and financial flexibility?
> - How will the portfolio's asset allocation and composition change as future funding needs approach and payments
>   are made?
> - How will your strategy protect the operating commitment while preserving flexibility for a responsible facility
>   contribution?
>
> Always remember who your strategy is designed to serve. Your client's profile and financial goals should remain at
> the center of your investment decisions.

**The pitch:** "Quickly summarize your team's investment strategy. Clearly communicate the central idea behind your
approach and how it is designed to support your client's financial goals and future funding needs."

**The IPS:** "Clearly explain your team's investment strategy and the reasoning behind it. Your IPS should demonstrate
how your strategy is tailored to your client's financial goals, time horizons, and needs. Explain how your team will
approach risk and return, liquidity, portfolio construction and diversification, and planned changes in the portfolio
as future funding needs approach." It should "establish your strategic approach to preparing for the operating
commitment, managing the operating reserve's asset composition over time, determining a responsible facility
contribution, and preserving appropriate financial flexibility."

> Focus on the strategy and decision-making framework that guide your portfolio rather than describing individual
> investments or presenting detailed financial calculations.
>
> Your IPS should make it clear:
> - What your team is trying to accomplish for the client
> - Why your strategy is appropriate for the client and their financial goals
> - How your strategy balances growth, liquidity, funding reliability, risk, and financial flexibility
> - How your strategy will guide portfolio construction and investment decisions as the client's needs change
> - How your approach will protect the required operating payments while supporting a responsible facility
>   contribution
>
> You do not need to address these points separately. Your goal is to present a clear, cohesive, and well-supported
> investment strategy.

**Not expected in the IPS** (they belong to the Final Report): "final operating-reserve calculations, detailed
portfolio projections, a final facility-contribution range, or the draft communication to co-sponsors."
**Sources:** "Teams should use research and analysis to develop their strategy, but sources do not need to be cited in
the IPS." No charts, links or citations. **Once submitted, the strategy may not be revised.**

## 3. What Wharton says judges look for (Competition Guide, verbatim)

> The competition recognizes thoughtful strategy, research and analysis, client understanding, disciplined decisions,
> management of risk and uncertainty, communication, and creativity.
>
> **Judges want to understand not only what your team decided to do, but also the reasoning, assumptions, and
> tradeoffs behind those decisions.**
>
> The goal is not simply to identify investments you think will perform well. The goal is to develop a cohesive
> strategy in which your investment decisions work together to support the client's objectives across a range of
> possible market outcomes.
>
> A long-term strategy may include planned adjustments as funding dates approach, but it should not be rewritten
> simply because markets move or hindsight reveals a different outcome.

## 4. Wharton's advice (halfway email, "Tips for competition success", the parts about the work)

> - Let your investment strategy lead. It connects every part of the competition: from your WInS portfolio and Trading
>   Notes Analysis to your Investment Policy Statement and Final Report.
> - Know your client. Review the Client Case Study carefully so you understand Laura's goals.
> - Use WInS to demonstrate your strategy. Your WInS portfolio should reflect the strategy your team has developed.
> - Do not focus on portfolio performance. You are not evaluated on whether your WInS portfolio gains or loses money.
>   WInS results should not be added to or subtracted from Laura's long-term portfolio projections.
> - Explain your decisions. There is no single correct strategy. Use your research and analysis to explain why each
>   investment belongs in your strategy and how it supports Laura's goals.
> - Follow the instructions.

## 5. The one hard limit on substance
Permitted for modelling both contributions and in the IPS and Final Report: stocks, ETFs and Government/Treasury bonds
available on WInS. Not permitted: margin, short selling, stock-secured debt, crypto, derivatives (options, futures,
swaps), individual TIPS, individual STRIPS, anything else. (Our plan uses only Treasury bonds, iBond Treasury ETFs and
one world stock ETF.)
