### Kai · near · structured settlements and lottery annuities · door: the phone shop's payment plans · Haiku 5.5
- **K1. The sixty-day birthday in US tax law.** 26 U.S.C. §130(d), Cornell LII, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/130,
  accessed 28 Oct 2026. Quote: a qualified funding asset is "any obligation of the United States" that "is purchased by
  the taxpayer not more than 60 days before the date of the qualified assignment and not later than 60 days after the
  date of such assignment", and whose periods of payment "are reasonably related to the periodic payments" it funds.
  **Breaks:** it's a tax rule for injury settlements; it says nothing about rates moving or who carries a shortfall, and
  a coupon isn't addressed. **Mechanism:** when a court turns a payout into a schedule of fixed cheques, US law wants
  the Treasuries that fund it bought within 60 days of the promise, with payment dates that match the cheques. The
  promise and the purchase are meant to share a birthday. **Could touch:** the birthday rule has a statutory twin; the
  cheques bought in Jan 2027 sit inside the spirit of the window. **2042 sentence:** "We bought on the window's first
  day, and nobody tested whether the coupons inside the funding still lined up with the cheques."
- **K2. The lottery promises what it bought, not what it advertised.** OAR 177-098-0060 (Oregon Lottery, Mega Millions
  prize payment), via oregon.public.law (aggregator), https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_177-098-0060, accessed 28 Oct
  2026. Quote: "Neither MUSL nor the party lotteries shall be responsible or liable for changes in the advertised or
  estimated annuity prize amount ... and the actual amount purchased after the prize payment method is actually known
  to MUSL." Also: "may set a minimum guaranteed annuity Jackpot Prize amount". **Breaks:** the lottery hands the gap
  to the winner; Laura can't hand a gap to her co-sponsors. The rule doesn't say how a guaranteed minimum is funded if
  the bonds buy less. **Mechanism:** the jackpot on the billboard is an estimate; the prize is whatever the bonds bought
  at the auction. The binding number is set on the purchase date, never the advertising date. **Could touch:** the
  2031 top. It becomes a promise on the day it's bought, which is the birthday rule from the other side of the counter.
  **2042 sentence:** "We advertised the amount; the purchase set what we could buy; the rule put the difference on the
  cheque."
- **K3. Zero-coupon funding: no coupons, nothing to reinvest.** TreasuryDirect, "History of Marketable Securities:
  STRIPS", https://maint.treasurydirect.gov/research-center/history-of-marketable-securities/strips, accessed 28 Oct
  2026. Quote: "STRIPS are called "zero-coupon" securities since the only time an investor receives a payment from
  STRIPS is at maturity." **Breaks first and fatally:** STRIPS are banned for both contributions (SMApply FAQ, 20 Oct;
  Wharton week 4 email). **Mechanism:** a bond with no coupons has no coupon gap; the whole rate is locked on the
  purchase day. It is the exact cure, and the one we may not use. **Could touch:** it explains *why* the coupon gap
  exists in our plan at all (Wharton's list, not our design), and makes the Window's thin-coupon twin (W-01) the
  nearest legal cousin. **2042 sentence:** "The cure was on the shelf in 1985; the rulebook took it off."

### Felix · adjacent · construction retainage, contingency and escalation · door: his mum's audit files · Sonnet 5.5
- **F1. Securities in lieu of retainage.** California Public Contract Code §22300, via california.public.law,
  https://california.public.law/codes/public_contract_code_section_22300, accessed 28 Oct 2026. Quote: "The contractor
  shall be the beneficial owner of any securities substituted for moneys withheld" (and keeps the interest).
  Eligible securities "shall include those listed in Section 16430 of the Government Code". **Breaks:** retainage
  guards an owner against a contractor's default, an event; Laura is both parties and her risk is arithmetic, not
  behaviour. The escrow is released on "completion", not a date. **Mechanism:** the money held back for a cost that
  might come is not left as cash; it is swapped on signing day for securities that the builder still owns and earns on,
  and they only go to the owner if the cost arrives. **Could touch:** the coupon reserve could be bought as its own
  Treasury on the day the cheques are bought (or the 2028 deposit day), still Laura's, released to her flexibility in
  2042 if never used. A cure with a birthday. **2042 sentence:** "The reserve had been bought on day one and held to the
  end; the owner drew on it, as the contract said."
- **F2. Who keeps the contingency nobody used.** Skyler Santomartino (Peckar & Abramson), "The Contractor's
  Contingency: What Contractors and Construction Managers Need to Know and Be Wary Of", ConsensusDocs, 3 Nov 2023,
  https://www.consensusdocs.org/the-contractors-contingency-what-contractors-and-construction-managers-need-to-know-and-be-wary-of/,
  accessed 28 Oct 2026. Quote: "Alternatively, some contracts may provide that unused contingency reverts back to the
  owner or some other funding source." **Breaks:** a contractor's contingency sits inside a guaranteed maximum price,
  so a second party eats the overrun; Laura has no second party. The page is a law firm's article, not contract text.
  **Mechanism:** good contracts decide on signing day who owns the leftover (shared savings, or back to the owner), so
  nobody argues about it at the end. **Could touch:** our rule that unused reserve goes to Laura's flexibility, never
  to the gift; and the article's warning that spending the contingency early forces someone "out-of-pocket" is our
  ban on the reserve ever touching the 2031 bottom. **2042 sentence:** "The contingency rule was written for savings and
  never for a shortfall."
- **F3. Escalation clauses: the one cost left floating.** FAR 52.216-4, Economic Price Adjustment, acquisition.gov,
  https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.216-4, accessed 28 Oct 2026. Quote: "There is no percentage limitation on the
  amount of decreases that may be made under this clause." (Increases "shall not exceed 10 percent of the original
  unit price".) **Breaks:** it re-prices the contract; Laura's $50,000 can't be re-priced, so it is our opposite, not
  our twin. **Mechanism:** the government's contract fixes everything it can and writes one named clause, with a cap,
  for the cost it can't fix. **Could touch:** the IPS can name the coupon gap the way a contract names its escalation
  clause: the one cost not fixed on signing day, with its own cap and its own fund. **2042 sentence:** "The adjustment
  was symmetrical on paper, but the only cost left floating was one the contract never priced."

### Tess · far · theatre cue sheets and the stage manager's "go" · door: lighting the school play · Opus 5.5
- **T1. Tracking and the block.** ETC Eos Family Online Help, "About Cues and Cue Lists" and "Block",
  https://www.etcconnect.com/WebDocs/Controls/EosFamilyOnlineHelp/en/Content/12_Cues_and_Cue_Lists/About_Cues_and_Cue_Lists.htm
  and .../05_Flags/Block.htm, accessed 28 Oct 2026. Quotes: "If your console is set in Track mode (default), changes
  move forward through the cue list until a block or a move instruction is encountered." "Any changes upstream in the
  cue list will not impact this blocked cue data." **Breaks:** "Block is an editing function. It has no impact on how
  the data is played back": on a console a block is free and only changes the record; in bonds a block costs real
  money. And a block stops only what comes *before* it; drift after it still tracks forward. **Mechanism:** a small
  change in an early cue silently carries into every later cue until a hard value stops it. A coupon reinvested at a
  lower rate is exactly that: a 2028 shortfall tracks into every later cheque. **Could touch:** the Jan 2033 re-match is
  a block. It stops 2027-32 drift, not 2033-42 drift, which is where the Lab's L-10 found most of the shortfall.
  **2042 sentence:** "Cue 10 played short because an upstream value tracked through; the only block sat at 2033."
- **T2. A missed cue is fixed in the programming before the next show, not on the night.** Central Connecticut State
  University Theatre, *Stage Management and Assistant Stage Management Handbook* (2025), pp. 19-20,
  https://www.ccsu.edu/sites/default/files/2025-08/Updated%20SM%20and%20ASM%20Handbook%202025%20mf.pdf, accessed 28 Oct
  2026. Quote: "If the issue was technical, find out how to correct the issue in programming before the next
  performance." **Breaks:** theatre always has a next performance; cheque ten is the last show, so a fix only works if
  the fault is found before an earlier cheque. **Mechanism:** the night only spots and writes down the fault; the cure
  goes into the programming before the next show. **Could touch:** a check-and-fix rule decided in 2027 and run before
  *every* cheque from 2033 (nine chances to fix before the tenth), not one check in 2033. **2042 sentence:** "The drift
  was in the show reports from 2034 on, and never went into the programming before the final performance."
- **T3. The pre-show check finds only the lamps already dead.** Utah State University Theatre Arts, Student Handbook,
  "Electric Work", https://artsci.usu.edu/theatre-arts/students-and-alumni/current-students/student-handbook/production-assignments/electric-work,
  accessed 28 Oct 2026. Quote: "Conducts a dimmer and instrument check before every event and replace lamps and/or
  repair instruments before the event." **Breaks:** a check finds what has failed, not what will. **Mechanism:** the
  check sits where spares can still be swapped in, before the house opens, and runs before every event. **Could touch:**
  the 2033 set-aside must be sized on the gap still to come (the Archive's reserve R), not the gap seen so far.
  **2042 sentence:** "Dimmer check in 2033 passed clean; the lamp failed in Act 2."
