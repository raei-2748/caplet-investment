### Felix · near · IPO bookbuilding, the file price range · door: his cousin's float, priced at the bottom of its range · Haiku 5.5
- **F1. About half of US IPOs land outside the range their banks filed, and the ones above it jump most on day one.**
  Jay R. Ritter, "Initial Public Offerings: Price Revision Statistics Through 2025", University of Florida, 2026,
  https://site.warrington.ufl.edu/ritter/files/IPOs-price-revision.pdf, accessed 30 Oct 2026. Quotes (verified, Table 7,
  below / within / above the file range): "2001-2019 33% 45% 22%"; "2020-2025 16% 55% 29%"; average first-day returns
  "2001-2019 3% 12% 38%"; "2020-2025 6% 21% 51%". **Breaks:** an IPO range is a forecast that a live book settles within
  weeks, with nothing bought behind its bottom; Laura's bottom is owned, and her gift is capped at the top, so it can
  never land "above". US deals only. **Mechanism:** the bank names a range before it knows demand; the book then lands
  below, inside or above it, and the size of the first-day jump says how wrong the range was. **Low side / high side:**
  priced below, the issuer raises less in a cool market; priced above, the issuer leaves money on the table for the new
  buyers (38% on day one, 2001-2019). **Could touch:** the case asks how sure we are the gift lands *inside* the range;
  for an IPO that's about a coin toss (45-55%), for Laura it is near-certain by construction (bottom owned, top a cap),
  so the number worth stating is the top's chance, not "inside". **Ninety-second rule:** "Half of all floats miss their
  own range. Ours can't miss the bottom, so the only honest number left to give is how often we reach the top."
- **F2. Where the final price lands against the range predicts what the issuer leaves on the table: the range only
  partly moves to what the book reveals.** Kathleen Weiss Hanley, "The underpricing of initial public offerings and the
  partial adjustment phenomenon", *Journal of Financial Economics* 34(2), 1993, 231-250,
  https://business.lehigh.edu/sites/default/files/2019-08/7%20hanley_1993_jfe_0.pdf, accessed 30 Oct 2026. Quotes
  (verified): "the mean initial return for firms going public at a price above the anticipated range is 20.7%"; "The
  remaining issues, representing offerings within the anticipated range, have an average initial return of 10.0%";
  below the range, "an average initial return of 0.6%, which is not significantly different from zero"; the range
