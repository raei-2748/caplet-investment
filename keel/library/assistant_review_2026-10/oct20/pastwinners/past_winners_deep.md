## 2. The one public Top 50 report (Mochi, 2023-24): how it is built

Client that year: Hilary Ash. She had $25k to grow to $40k by 2028 for real estate and $75k for a women-led
consulting firm in about 15 years (Mochi midterm). Page limits: 10-13 pages plus Works Cited; reports outside the
range were "not reviewed" ([DL] page 3768). Mochi: 11 pages (Scribd metadata).

### 2.1 Running order (what it led with)

| Order | Section | What is in it (paraphrase; short quotes) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title page | As required |
| 2 | **Elevator Pitch** | Team name as an acronym ("Mastering Opportunities, Crafting Holdings, and Inspiring Growth"). Core claim: "Our team quantifies your goals to provide a tailored investment strategy" |
| 3 | **Our Unique Approach** | "we studied her qualitative goals and quantified them to specific IRRs for long and short-term payouts. This drove the selection of stocks and ETFs" |

| 8 | Analysis of Client Needs | Turns the vague goal into a number: it costed the consulting firm at $110k, interviewing two founders of women-led consulting firms. With 15 years of inflation and tax that becomes **$245k** ("see diagram below") |

My check of its numbers: $100k paying $40k at year 5 and $245k at year 15 gives an IRR of **8.38%** (matches the
final's 8.4%). $75k growing to $245k in 15 years gives **8.21%** (matches the midterm's "8.21% on the $75,000").
Both are right but on different bases, and the report never says so (INFERENCE: a consistency gap that a reader
of both documents would notice).

### 2.2 How it presented risk and certainty (the most useful part for Caplet)

- Disclosure first: "We strongly recommend telling the client upfront about risk scenarios, including scenarios in
  which she is unable to achieve her financial objectives. While this could make it harder to win the client, we
  believe that honesty is key".
- The test comes from history, not from a model: "We analyzed S&P 500 from the past 92 years. The tenth percentile
  five-year return was -16.5%". After that, "it would then require a 400% return over ten years" (their figure; my
  rough re-check: ($100k x 0.835) - $40k = $43.5k, and getting to $245k needs about 5.6x, so roughly a 460% gain.
  Same message).
- It states the conclusion plainly: "under this conservative market scenario, it is unlikely the client would achieve
  all of her goals, even if we were able to outperform the market."
- Where it overreaches: a stated aim that "our portfolio outperforms the market" and "our stock selection process
  has the potential to outperform". It still made the Top 50. INFERENCE: the honest downside and the quantified goal
  outweighed this.

### 2.3 Charts and visuals (preview)

Used: a diagram of the needs calculation ("see diagram below"); a portfolio target table; a team-process graphic
("The graphic above shows how we assigned a sector to each team member"); and the two required portfolio snapshots.
Not used in the preview: performance charts, return tables, efficient frontiers or Monte Carlo fans.

### 2.4 Midterm to final

The midterm already held the core idea: the quantified $245k and an "annual ROI of 8.21%" (with the "consulting
firm" cost model and interviews). It said the portfolio was "currently negative (but still above the current market
average)". The final dropped all WInS performance talk. The idea stayed; the evidence and the risk section grew.

## 4. Patterns, with evidence

### 4.1 Structure: Wharton dictates it, and qualifiers follow it exactly

- In 2022-23 and 2023-24 the page order was fixed: title; summary (75-word pitch, 75-word uniqueness, 150-word advisor
  reflection); trading notes analysis; Portfolio-at-a-Glance ("tables or charts, but please do not use sentences or
  paragraphs"); then "Report Details", which was "the bulk of your report ... Be creative!" ([DL]). Mochi follows it
  line for line.
- The 2025-26 section names are the same in two unrelated reports (C3, and the Phoenix stub in KB). Title; Team
  Reflection plus Advisor Reflection; Trading Notes Analysis (an ETF, a stock and a "Team Choice"); Portfolio-at-a-Glance;
  Report Details with "Investment Strategy", "Team Decision-Making", "Team Growth and Learning", "Challenges and
  Roadblocks", "Biggest Takeaways". INFERENCE: that was the official 2025-26 template. The 2026-27 one comes on 9 Nov ET.
- Inside "Report Details", the Top 50 report opens with a roadmap and then runs **needs, then rules, then process**
  (2.1).

### 4.2 Lead: the strategy up front, as a rule, tied to one client number

- "if you provide us with a team strategy that is buried somewhere in your experience, rather than clearly stated,
  defined and articulated up front, you will not be among the strongest teams" (FROM KB [W6]).
- "Your strategy is a set of rules and guidelines; a plan of attack" [W3]. "Will certain triggers dictate whether you
  buy or sell a security?" [W3].
- Mochi led with the method that produced its number. Champions led with a tagline ("Ignite the flame for your
  financial future", 2024, [N10]).

### 4.3 Risk and certainty

- An honest downside, in dollars, in the client's terms, is in the only public Top 50 report (2.2).
- 2024 semifinal judge Marcus Shaw (AltFinance): "How do you develop risk-monitoring metrics for different changes in
  the macro environment and other constraints ...?" (2024 finalists post, VERIFIED).
- 2024 finale judge Balchunas urged teams to consider "their clients' emotions during a market downturn" (paraphrase
  in [N10], VERIFIED).
- 2026-27 adds it to the rubric: "communicates ... investment uncertainty clearly and credibly to prospective
  co-sponsors" (`deliverables.txt:59`), and the case warns about overpromising (case:114-116; FROM KB).
