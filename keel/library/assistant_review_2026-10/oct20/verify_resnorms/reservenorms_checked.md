## 0. Five-line summary

1. **The case's "operating reserve" is not what nonprofits call an operating reserve.** Nonprofit practice means a rainy-day fund for *unexpected* shocks, often sized in months of expenses (NORI: 3-month minimum; Propel: 3-6 months common, ceiling 2 years; Kresge/NFF: "rainy day" definition only, no months benchmark) [fixed 20 Oct, E7]. Propel says such reserves "are not intended to replace a permanent loss of funds or eliminate an ongoing budget gap". Laura's reserve pre-funds a *known, scheduled* commitment (case:88-89, 94-95). The closest professional terms are a **dedicated (cash-matched) portfolio**, a **sinking fund** and an **escrow "to maturity"**, all of which run off to zero.
2. **The team's own notes misuse the nonprofit benchmark.** IPS Report Doc, Tab 7 (`S/oct19/drive/ips_report_live.md:184`) cites "NORI 2008; Candid" for "3-6 months … no more than 2 years". In fact NORI suggests a 25% (3-month) *minimum* and tells boards to set their own floor and ceiling. Its sample policies use 2-month minimums and 5-month maximums (other examples use a 3-month target). The 3-6 months and the 2-year cap appear in Propel Nonprofits. Candid could not be checked (403), so it is UNVERIFIED [fixed 20 Oct, E6]. The note also applies the band to $50k, but $50k is only Laura's payment *toward* the residency's costs (case:88), not the residency's budget.
3. **"High degree of certainty" has ready-made professional language.** Each regime states its standard as a **measure + confidence + horizon**:
   - Solvency II: 99.5% over one year ("ruin … once in every 200 cases").
   - APRA (Australia): 75% "level of sufficiency" for insurance liabilities (the greater of 75% sufficiency or central estimate + ½ s.d.; general insurers) [fixed 20 Oct, E9], 99.5% for capital.
   - UK Pensions Regulator: "not required to eliminate … with certainty", the "likelihood … is small", tested under "most reasonably foreseeable scenarios", with, as an example, a 1-in-6 one-year stress and six years to recover [fixed 20 Oct, E10].
   - US actuaries: "funded status", discounting with "US Treasury yields" (ASOP 4 §3.11), and scenario, sensitivity, stress and stochastic tests (ASOP 51).

   Caplet's three options in RS §6b (C1 matched, C2 funded ratio, C3 probability) map one-to-one onto these.
4. **One benchmark bites.** TPR's DB funding code (Nov 2024, in force 12 Nov 2024) says that, for a DB scheme's *notional* low dependency investment allocation, "we expect schemes to target a minimum level of interest rate and inflation hedging of at least 90%". This is an expectation in a code, and "Codes of practice are not statements of the law"; trustees are "not required to invest in line with" that allocation. Analogy only [fixed 20 Oct, E1]. The ladder's rate hedge is **about 78%** at purchase (LDI: PV01 $226 vs $291, 19 Oct curve). 3r adds **about 4 points (≈81%)**. On RS's flat-5% approximation the ratio drifts up as the ladder runs off (ladder alone ≈80-86% in 2028-36; with 3r ≈84-88%), and passes 90% only near the end (≈2039) [fixed 20 Oct, E2]. Leibowitz's classic "dedicated portfolio" abstract says "In many cases the objective will be to assure fulfillment even under totally passive management—that is, no reinvestment", and that reinvestment can lower costs "without substantially increasing risk or complexity". Caplet's rungs are sized assuming coupons are reinvested (RS §2 rule 2). It is therefore "cash matching with reinvestment": name the assumption and stress it [fixed 20 Oct, E5]. Expect a judge to ask about this.
5. **Arts-funding sources support the structure, not a number.** Kresge's capital challenge grants were built to give "cachet and credibility" to fundraising campaigns, which matches case:111-113. GIA found that large arts groups that put capital into endowments and buildings have "money … trapped in inflexible assets" [fixed 20 Oct, E20], which matches case:101-103. Residency endowments fell an average 29% in 2008-10 (GIA/Alliance of Artists Communities), which supports keeping the payments out of equities. Mellon's general operating grants sampled run 24-36 months, against Laura's 10 years. These are three examples, not a field statistic.

## 1. What the case asks, and the vocabulary trap

- **The requirement (case:94-99).** Laura sets aside "a portion of the portfolio to fund the ten payments. This set-aside is called the operating reserve." Teams must give "its size and initial asset composition", say "how its composition should change, if at all", "define what they consider a high degree of funding certainty, explain how they evaluated that level of certainty, and identify the assumptions".
- **Constraints.** The payments are fixed at nominal $50,000 (case:88-90). They must be funded "with a high degree of certainty" with no outside money (case:91-93). No separate "contingency fund or endowment" is expected (case:106-107). The facility cost and the residency's business plan are out of scope (case:163-166), and so are taxes (case:167-169).
- **The trap.** In nonprofit finance, "operating reserve" is a technical term for an *emergency cushion* (§2a). The case defines its own meaning: a set-aside that *funds the ten payments*. A judge with a nonprofit background may hear "3-6 months". A finance judge may hear "a dedicated bond portfolio". **Use the case's definition, and if useful show that you know the difference.** That is a Client Knowledge point. The wording is the team's own.

## 5. Cross-check with RS (and its verifier banner) and LDI §11-12

**Agreements: the professional sources support these RS/LDI positions.**
1. **The three ways to define certainty (RS §6b: C1, C2, C3) are each a recognised professional form.** C1 is cash matching (E1, E3, R7e). C2 is funded status on a low-default-risk basis (R9b, R10, E2). C3 is a probability of sufficiency or confidence level (R1, R3, R6). The UK code (R7, R7b, R7d) combines all three: matched assets, plus scenarios, plus a calibrated stress, plus a "small likelihood" judgement. That combination is what LDI §11 recommends: a testable definition, four measures and a stress table.
2. **LDI §11's matching tolerance** ("a US Treasury holding maturing within about 11 months before it") is the textbook "on or before a liability date" (E3). The longest gap is Feb-37 at 10.5 months (RS §8).
3. **RS's banned words** ("guaranteed", "risk-free", "cannot miss") agree with R7: even pension regulators do not require certainty.
4. **Run-off with no trading** as the answer to "if at all" (RS §5; case:96-97) is supported by E3b and by R2b condition (a).
5. **LDI §12 concepts 1, 4 and 5** (funding priority, surplus release test, risk only the surplus) have direct professional counterparts: E2/E2b (surplus), N2b (floor and ceiling) and E4c (surplus management).

**Tensions: new points from this pass.**
6. **The hedge-ratio benchmark.** LDI §2b's 78%, set against TPR's "at least 90%" (R7c) and CFA's BPV condition (E4, condition 2), gives an outside yardstick that the ladder alone falls short of. With 3r it stays below 90%: about 81% like-for-like at purchase (3r adds about 4 points); 84-88% only on the at-date, flat-5% series for 2028-36, where most of the rise is the ladder ageing (§3 row C3) [fixed 20 Oct, E2]. This adds evidence for the 3r vote (RS R1, Tue 27 Oct). It does not decide it: TPR's 90% is set for a different kind of fund, measured on a "low dependency funding basis". It is an expectation in a code that is not law, for a DB scheme's *notional* low-dependency allocation, and it covers interest rate and inflation hedging together [fixed 20 Oct, E1].
7. **Reinvestment vs "dedicated".** Leibowitz describes no reinvestment as a common objective of cash matching ("In many cases", E1b), and reinvestment as acceptable if named (E1c) [fixed 20 Oct, E5]. RS sizes every rung assuming reinvestment at its own yield (RS §2 rule 2; LDI §2b: about $49k, 10%, from interest on interest). If the team calls the ladder "dedicated" or "cash-matched", a well-read judge may ask about reinvestment. The honest answers are already in RS: test M (corrected by VR to **$3.0k short**, not $4.2k), the 3r reserve, and option 4 (a dedicated rebuild, 0.0% short in the MC, RS §6c).
8. **Escrow / defeasance framing.** In municipal practice an escrow is sized so the obligation is fully provided for. If it falls short, the issuer is still liable (E5b). Without 3r, RS's own cover order makes Laura's kept money the backstop (RS T9-G2: $9.7k taken from her). That is exactly the flexibility concern in case:101-103. With 3r, the backstop sits inside the reserve.

## 7. What not to claim

- "Meets Solvency II / APRA / TPR standards", or "Solvency II-grade". These are analogies with different measures, horizons and legal settings. Say "uses the same kind of standard: measure, confidence, horizon".
- "Immunised". The BPV is not equal (E4). Use "cash-matched" or "dedicated" with the reinvestment caveat.
- "Defeased" in the legal sense. Laura keeps ownership and liability (E5b).
- "Endowment" for the reserve. It spends principal, and the case excludes an endowment (case:106-107).
- "Within NORI guidelines" for the reserve or for Laura's kept money. This is a category error (§3 rows A1-A2).
- Any months-of-expenses figure for the residency. Its budget is unknown and out of scope (case:163-166).
- "Guaranteed", "risk-free", "cannot miss" (RS §6b; supported by R7).
