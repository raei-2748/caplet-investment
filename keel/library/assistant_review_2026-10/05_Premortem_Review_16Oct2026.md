## 4. Trading-note reflections: blueprint (content only; students write)

**The picks are right: IBTR, IBTM, VT.** Order them roots → floor → branch. All three judge reviews ranked this trio
first (14 Oct), and together they use the instructions' three verbs.

### Design the three as one set
- Each reflection names its part of the system (payments, floor, growth), so the three read as one strategy. The TNA
  instructions ask that "collectively" they show "a cohesive portfolio strategy".
- Each earns **one verb**, clearly: IBTR = **tested**, IBTM = **refined**, VT = **supported**.
- Each carries **one dated number** at most, and links to one IPS rule and one case fact.
- **No sentence repeats the note.** The judge has just read it. Every sentence adds why, fit, client, or what changed.
- Translate the notes' jargon ("reinvestment-rate risk", "defined-maturity") into plain words.
- Use the same words as the IPS (roots, branch, floor, range), and only after vote set A.

### IBTR (filled 7 Oct, 4:42:53 AM ET): **tested**
- **What was tested:** the IPS's load-bearing claim, that the first deposit alone buys all ten payments. It was priced
  on the 6 Oct curve using only WInS-listed holdings: about $293k (MODEL), with room for about 0.2-0.23 points of
  falling yields.
- **The rule set in advance:** if yields fall past that line, the second deposit completes the payments first, and the
  facility gift absorbs the cost, never the artists. This makes the ranking of her two goals visible.
- **The live result (dated):** the room had shrunk to about 0.15 points by 14-15 Oct (MODEL, 15 Oct check). Re-run it on
  Wed 21 Oct and quote it with its date. Either outcome works: crossing the line would be the rule working, not the
  plan failing.
- **The most impressive point available (promote it from "optional"):** falling yields *raised* our WInS bond values
  but made Laura's real January purchase *more expensive*. Our trade gained while her plan got dearer, which is exactly
  why the FAQ says WInS gains don't count. It is counterintuitive, concrete and true. Use it as an illustration, never
  as a "gain".
- **Client link:** "high degree of certainty" and no reliance on co-sponsors (case p.3). Her tolerance for a missed
  payment is low.
- **Traps:** don't mention the cancelled note's "0.3". Don't say the ladder "can't" cost more than $300k.

### IBTM (filled 6 Oct, 10:01:41 PM ET): **refined** (confirm the order of events first, gate G3)
- **One fund, two jobs:** 1,142 shares for the 1 Jan 2033 payment, 3,361 for the facility floor (Portfolio tab).
  WInS lists no Treasury maturing Feb 2031-Feb 2036.
- **The refinement:** the 6 Oct Wharton email limited Laura's real plan to WInS-listed securities. IBTM went from a
  WInS stand-in to her actual floor holding. Its payout isn't fixed, so the floor is now quoted rounded down
  (model: typical about $149.4k, worst about $146.3k; REBASE, pending lab item 03). "Never below the floor" became
  "rounded down". A rule change produced a design change, which is what "refined" means.
- **G3 check:** did the email arrive before the 10:01 PM ET order? If yes, "the decision changed the strategy". If no,
  "the email confirmed it". Check Gmail and the doc's version history.
- **Client link:** in 2031 co-sponsors hear a bottom figure that is money she already holds. The case says overpromising
  damages credibility, and that a meaningful contribution "may signal" viability (p.3).
- **Why the note says "risk management":** this holding sets the worst case for the gift. Say so in plain words.
- **Traps:** no fixed payout, and no 2031 range (the TNA isn't expected to give one).

### VT (filled 7 Oct, 8:27:19 PM ET): **supported**

  holding, so it carries the rubric's "appropriate diversification").
- **Why so small (the note leaves this out):** about 9% of her money, because the payments and the floor come first.
  State the trade-off: a lower middle-case gift in exchange for a much better bad case. Floor grid: each $25k less floor
