# Firm E

## 1. How the ten payments are protected
A portfolio of 60% stocks and 40% bonds is rebalanced once a year. It moves to 55/45 in 2029, 50/50 in 2030 and 40/60 in 2031-2032. On 1 January 2033 the firm buys a Treasury ladder covering the ten $50,000 payments (about $395k at today's forward rates).

## 2. What "high certainty" means and how it was checked
High certainty means at least a 95% probability that the portfolio covers the reserve on 1 January 2033. Checked by a 200,000-path Monte Carlo on JPM's 2026 long-term assumptions (U.S. stocks 6.7% compound, Treasuries 4.0%) and a one-point uncertainty in 2033 interest rates. Model estimate: funded in 98.9% of paths; 90.4% if the 2028 deposit is $75k.

## 3. How growth is pursued and with what money
Growth comes from the whole portfolio's stock share, split 60% U.S. and 40% international; bonds are a broad U.S. bond fund. The average stock share over 2027-2032 is 51%. Model estimate: median money left after the reserve is $213k.

## 4. What Laura can tell co-sponsors in 2031 (range and confidence)
The range runs from the 10th percentile to the median of the model's 2033 facility contribution, re-run with 2031 values. Model estimate from today: $66k to $170k. The model gives a 90% chance of reaching at least the bottom and a 50% chance of exceeding the top.

## 5. Flexibility after 2033
The facility receives 80% of the money above the reserve. The other 20% is kept in Treasury bills for project changes.

## 6. What the competition portfolio (WInS) holds
The 2027 allocation: VTI 36%, VXUS 24%, BND 39%, cash 1%. Three ETF trades at $25 each. Pending WInS availability check.

## 7. The main risks the firm accepts
- Payments depend on markets until 2033: model estimate 1.1% miss, 9.6% if the 2028 deposit is $75k, 42.5% if it does not arrive.
- The bond fund holds corporate and mortgage bonds and is not matched to the payment dates.
- A one-point rate fall by 2033 adds about $16k to the reserve's cost (model estimate).
