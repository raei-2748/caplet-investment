[Inference: my own bootstrap, re-run in the fact-check. It converts the par curve into a discount rate for each date. The yield the plan needs is 5.09% a year with annual compounding ($300,000 today against ten $50,000 payments). Treasury yields are quoted with half-yearly compounding (bond-equivalent), and on that basis the need is 5.02%. [Inference: recomputed, 5.087% annual and 5.024% half-yearly]
On 6 Nov the curve's spot rate (the interest rate for a single payment at that date, half-yearly basis) was about 4.70% at 6 years (2033) and 5.18% at 15 years (2042). Payments from 2033 to 2039 (4.70–5.00%) cost more than the 5.02% they need. Only the 2040–42 payments (5.06–5.18%) are cheaper. [Inference: recomputed]

3. iShares iBonds Treasury ETFs
All five funds charge 0.07% and pay monthly distributions. [Verified: iShares fact sheets as of 30 Sep 2026]
The fact sheets say the funds "terminate on or about October or December 15 of the year in each Fund's name". These are the Dec-series funds, so the date is about 15 December. In their final months they move into cash and cash-like instruments, so the yield drifts toward money-market rates. [Verified: fact sheet risk text. The earlier quote left out "October or"]
The final payout is net assets less liabilities. It is "not predictable at the time of investment", and the funds do not aim to return a set amount. [Verified]
Precedent from IBTF (2025): the last trading day and final NAV were both on 15 Dec 2025, and liquidation was on 18 Dec 2025. Proceeds arrive "on or around" that date depending on the broker, which is about two weeks before a 1 January payment. [Verified: BusinessWire/BlackRock release of 15 Oct 2025, read directly]

5. History: reinvestment risk and how long high yields last (FRED GS10, monthly, Apr 1953–Oct 2026)
Since 2000 the 10-year has been at or above 4.86% in only 11% of months. One spell above 4.75% started in Feb 1999 and lasted 31 months, to Aug 2001 (20 of those months fall from Jan 2000). Every later spell lasted 7 months or less. Before Sep 2026, the most recent month above 4.75% was Oct 2023 (4.80%), a single month. The longest gap ran from mid-2007 to 2023. [Verified data, recounted; Inference for the counting]
I looked at the 90 months where the 10-year started between 4.5% and 5.5% and measured the average yield over years 6 to 15 afterwards, which is Laura's reinvestment window. It fell by a median of about 2.0 points, and fell by more than 1 point in 63% of cases. [Inference: recomputed, median −1.98, 63%]
That result depends on the era:
1990s starts: −0.7 to −2.0 points
2000s starts: −1.9 to −3.0 points
1950s–60s starts: +1.5 to +4.6 points [Inference: recomputed]
