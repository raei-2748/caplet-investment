# Wharton culture brief: writing Keel as budding Wharton students

AI research (Claude, 23 Oct 2026), for Keel's personas and story world only. Not for submission. Sources were read on
23 Oct 2026 unless marked; quotes are exact. UNVERIFIED = not checked against a primary page.

**Why this exists.** Ray's idea (23 Oct): judges may warm, without knowing why, to students who show "Wharton
potential". Last season's (2025-26) semifinal readers were Wharton student evaluators plus Aberdeen asset managers
(B7a, section 3.2); who reads in 2026-27 is UNVERIFIED (`competition/COMPETITION.md`). If the pattern holds, many
judges have been shaped by this culture. The rule for using it: **show the traits through what Keel does,
never say them.** No Keel character ever says "Wharton potential", "leader", or quotes a slogan at the reader.

## 1. The story Wharton tells about itself

- **1881, a first.** Joseph Wharton gave $100,000 to Penn to found "the world's first collegiate business school", to
  turn business from apprenticeship into "a rigorous and respected profession". Goal: graduates who would be "pillars
  of the State, whether in private or in public life" [1][2][3].
- **The founder.** A Hicksite Quaker family, marked by "a commitment to discipline, education, and moral
  responsibility"; "a scientific entrepreneur", committed to "financial literacy and responsibility". His founding
  document: "the duty of every one to perform well and cheerfully his part as a member of the community" [2].
  Complexity, stated plainly: his fortune came partly from steel and naval armour plate [2].
- **Today's reading of him** (Dean Erika James): his "foresight to educate leaders versed in evidence-based knowledge
  to drive societal progress remains our ethos"; business education "deserves academic rigor, ethical grounding, and
  public purpose" [2].
- **Penn's older root.** Benjamin Franklin's 1749 plan: learn what is "most useful and most ornamental", aimed at "an
  Inclination join'd with an Ability to serve Mankind, one's Country, Friends and Family" [4]. Useful knowledge, put
  to service.
- **A line of firsts** [1]: first business-school research centre (1921); first MBA class (1921); W.E.B. Du Bois's
  Philadelphia study on a Wharton fellowship (1896); faculty created finance, marketing, insurance and accounting as
  fields (1895-1915); Lawrence Klein's Nobel (1980); WRDS data service (1997); Penn Wharton Budget Model (2017); first
  woman and first person of colour as dean (2020); an AI for Business concentration (2025).
- **Self-image in words** [5]: "knowledge for action", "a place where knowledge fuels action"; "act on evidence, not
  just instincts"; "you can't wait to know everything before moving forward"; a personality that is "driven, dynamic
  and entrepreneurial"; "values-centered, action-oriented leaders". Later: "business as a force for good" [1].

## 2. What Wharton says it wants in students

- **Undergraduate admissions** [6]: "no magic formula", but "an interest in business to fuel positive change to
  advance the world's economic and social well-being", "demonstrated leadership", "a history of academic excellence",
  "a healthy degree of motivation", and "a well-developed interest and involvement in their community and environment".
- **First-year core (MGMT 100, pre-2017 entrants)** [7][8]: teams of ten carry out "a field project for a client in
  the greater Philadelphia area" (nonprofits such as the Red Cross); it teaches "persuasive rather than positional
  leadership", with "written reflection".
- **Wharton Global Youth** [9][10]: "educate, inform and inspire pre-collegiate students"; "analyze the world's complex
  challenges"; "encouraging them to express opinions and ideas"; "storytelling is a powerful way to develop character
  and confidence". In the competition, "the teams don't simply grow portfolios to succeed".

## 3. What the competition's own people reward

- 2026-27 Guide: "Judges want to understand not only what your team decided to do, but also the reasoning,
  assumptions, and tradeoffs behind those decisions." Infographic: "There is no single correct strategy." (repo files)
- Semifinal reader Melissa Ko Hahn (2025): beginners "fall into this trap of hiding behind fancy terms and
  formulas... Oftentimes, the best solutions are made up of simple, elegant ideas." Prof. Michael Roberts: jargon
  obscures "the elegant simplicity of finance" (both verified in B7a, 27 Sep).
- 2024 finale judges [11]: depth and accuracy "differentiated better performances" (Keswani); weigh clients'
  emotions in downturns (Balchunas); "take a look at this person holistically" (McCormick); "the same passion, the
  same honesty, the same rigor" (Rao).
- 2023 winners DMV's Finest [12]: "two years in the making"; "the resilience to come back and do it again"; "We all
  carried our own weight". Their final-report lesson: "less is more" (B7a).
- 2018 Aberdeen lead reader: "we enjoyed reading about their educational journey" (B7a).

## 4. Student culture (the real texture, not the brochure)

- Penn has 300+ student groups; Wharton clubs are selective, with applications, interviews and "coffee chats"; one
  investing-club manager says interviews screen for "effort and curiosity" more than finance knowledge. Wharton
  limited multi-round club interviews in July 2025 [13].
- About 48% of a typical undergraduate class goes into financial services (Wikipedia, citing Wharton; UNVERIFIED
  against the primary report) [3].
- The stereotype students push against: "disdain for the Wharton School and preprofessionalism"; critics call it
  "cutthroat" [14]. The admired Wharton student is driven but not cold.
- Alumni archetypes [3]: the patient investor (Warren Buffett, attended), the builder (Elon Musk), the operator (CEOs
  of Alphabet, Pfizer, PepsiCo, J&J and others), the public servant (first graduates included a Japanese Diet member
  and a U.S. ambassador), the economist-scholar (Klein, Kuznets taught there).

## 5. Traits to show through action

Each trait is grounded in the sources above. "Show" = what the persona does in a scene; "Write" = how it shows in
their prose. Fit to persona is a suggestion for the persona thread.

| Trait (source) | Show it in behaviour | Show it in writing | Natural fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evidence before instinct** [5], Hahn | Stops a debate to price it; re-runs a number before quoting it; changes their mind when the data says so, and says so | "We thought X. The curve said Y. We went with Y." Numbers with a date and source | Jun, Felix |
| **Knowledge for action** [5] | Research ends in a decision with a date, not a reading list; "so what do we do on Monday?" | Each paragraph ends in a choice or a rule, not an observation | Nora |
| **Client first, whole person** [11], [7] | Reads Laura's case again before arguing a ticker; asks what a bad year does to *her*, not to the return | Laura's name and needs come before the instrument | Amara |
| **Persuasion, not position** [7] | The leader wins a vote by the better argument, or loses it and backs the result | Credits the teammate whose point changed the plan | Nora |
| **Contrarian with rigour** [5], [11] | Argues the other side on purpose, with a number; concedes cleanly when beaten | "I was wrong about..." stated once, without drama | Kai |
| **Decide without full information** [5] | Sets a deadline for a decision, names what is still unknown, and acts | States the risk left over in one plain sentence | Nora, Kai |
| **Simple, elegant ideas** (Hahn, Roberts) | Cuts a clever idea that does not earn its place; explains the plan to a younger sibling | Short words; one idea per paragraph; no acronyms without need | Tess |
| **Discipline and integrity** [2] | Checks every trade against the rules before placing it; refuses a shortcut that bends a rule | Corrections are visible and dated, never buried | Felix |
| **Public purpose** [2], [4], [6] | Cares that the facility gets built and the co-sponsors are told the truth | Overpromising is treated as a harm, not a style choice | Amara |
| **Resilience and shared load** [12] | After a setback (the 7 Oct mis-click kind of event, in Keel's own world), they log the lesson and fix the process | Reflections name a mistake and what changed, not just a win | Tess |
| **Curiosity across fields** [4], [1] | Brings an idea from history, engineering or psychology, and tests whether it really fits Laura | The analogy survives a judge's "does that really make sense?" | Kai |
| **Global outlook** [1], [9] | Australian students thinking about a client tied to Taiwan; time zones, currencies, cultures as real constraints | Mentions place only when it changes a decision | Amara |

## 6. Clichés to avoid

- Saying the trait instead of showing it: "we are passionate, driven leaders", "we learned the value of teamwork".
- Name-dropping Wharton, Buffett or Franklin in Keel's dialogue or in any Caplet deliverable. The culture should be
  felt, never quoted.
- The finance-bro voice: jargon, "alpha", "crushing it", ranking each other, bragging about returns. It is the exact
  stereotype Wharton students push against [14], and the rubric says WInS P&L is not the scorecard.
- Hollow world-saving: "changing the world" with no concrete act. Purpose shows as one honest act for Laura.
- The flawless genius: no one is ever wrong. Real Wharton-calibre stories turn on a correction.
- Lonely heroics: one student carrying the team. Winners said "We all carried our own weight" [12].
- Fancy models for their own sake (Hahn). Depth beats breadth (Keswani).
- Inventing team events for Caplet's deliverables. Keel's events are fiction and stay in Keel's story (CLAUDE.md).
