## 3. Findings

### INT-01 - The 16 Oct rule change lets AI write deliverable text (critical, new)

**What is wrong.** Today's public commit 412e9e3 replaces the rule "Do NOT write the Trading Notes, IPS or Final Report" with: "the assistant may edit the IPS, Trading Notes and Final Report, but only as Google Docs SUGGESTIONS ... each labelled "[AI]" ... the team records accepted AI wording in the Final Report's Works Cited" (branch `claude/fervent-edison-sqk1ry`, CLAUDE.md:27-30; COMPETITION.md:142-144). The first batch exists: `research/ips_numbers_2026-10/IPS_v5_suggestions.md:7-21` gives 13 "Current text -> Suggested text" rewrites of IPS V5 sentences, written by Claude Code. The new docs/AI_USE.md row (line 77) says the AI "drafted [AI]-tagged Google Docs suggestions for IPS Version 5".

**Why it costs us.**
- Wharton's line is "You may not submit any work generated by an AI program as your own". The team's plan assumes that citing accepted AI wording makes it acceptable. Rules & Roles does say AI material "included in your report must be properly cited". But a cited quote is shown as a quote. Here the AI sentences would be blended into the team's own prose and judged as the team's voice ("authentic team voice", deliverables.txt:59).
- The IPS bans "formal citations" (IPS-FINAL.txt:123). So any AI sentence accepted into the IPS is submitted on 6 Nov with **no credit at all**, and the IPS "becomes the official record" (IPS-FINAL.txt:73). The credit would only arrive with the Final Report on 4 Dec.
- The rule also covers "Trading Notes", i.e. the TNA reflections due in 8 days. Reflections are the most personal part (see INT-11).
- The public record now shows the team first wrote the stricter rule (old CLAUDE.md:23-25; COMPETITION.md:142 at 2affd4c: "**The team writes every deliverable.**"; docs/ai_authorship_compliance.md:30 "AI_GENERATED ... PROHIBITED"), then relaxed it. If AI wording is later found in a submission, that history reads as a knowing choice, not an accident.
- Accepting suggestions into V5 also rewrites text that is itself AI-made (INT-02). The result is AI text edited by AI.

**Likelihood / severity.** Breach happens only if AI wording is accepted and submitted. Detection odds are unknown (UNVERIFIED whether Wharton runs similarity or AI checks), but the exact AI sentences are now public on GitHub, so a match is easy to prove. Severity: dismissal is possible ("grounds for dismissal"); a quieter mark-down on "authentic team voice" is likely if judges sense AI style.

**Fix (what it must achieve).**
- Whole team decides by **Sat 17 Oct**: return to "students write every submitted sentence". AI may give numbers, facts, checks and lists of problems, never replacement sentences. Write the decision down with names of who agreed.
- Reject every [AI] suggestion already placed in the Google Doc (if any were placed; UNVERIFIED). Keep a record that they were rejected, rather than deleting the history.
- If the team still wants AI wording anywhere, the only safe place is a clearly marked, cited quotation in the Final Report, never the IPS or TNA.
- Check the SMApply TNA and IPS forms for any honesty or AI checkbox before submitting (UNVERIFIED whether one exists). If there is one, the answer must be true.

**Evidence:** branch 412e9e3 files CLAUDE.md:27-30, :40; COMPETITION.md:142-144; docs/AI_USE.md:77; research/ips_numbers_2026-10/IPS_v5_suggestions.md:1-24; HEAD COMPETITION.md:141-143; docs/ai_authorship_compliance.md:13, :26, :30.

### INT-03 - The rule covers AI "work" (models, numbers, charts), not only sentences (high, new)

**What is wrong.** The repo's AI framing treats numbers as safe: "AI **may**: check facts and numbers" (15 Oct brief §8) and "Numbers, charts and bullet memos only; the team writes the report" (ops/claude_lab/BACKLOG.md:3). **Why it costs us.** Portfolio Analysis asks the team to show "understanding and effective use of investment concepts and tools". The Code of Conduct also requires the team to "acknowledge ownership of all images and other media ... that it ... did not solely develop", so AI-made charts need a credit line. At the semifinal, "All team members must participate" in a live presentation to judges (faqs.txt:89); students who cannot rebuild their own key numbers will be exposed.

**Likelihood / severity.** Real breach only if AI analysis is presented as the students' own without credit. Likely to be partly true today. Severity high.

**Fix (what it must achieve).**

- Plan by Sun 25 Oct; rebuild before the Final Report instructions arrive (Tue 10 Nov Sydney).

## 7. Learning note (for Ray's learning goal; do not reuse the wording)

In real asset management, a manager who lets a vendor's model choose the strategy must still disclose that to the client and be able to explain the model. The CFA Asset Manager Code calls this including "material facts ... regarding ... the investment process". The competition's AI rule is the same idea: using a tool is fine, hiding it is not.
