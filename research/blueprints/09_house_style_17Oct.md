## The rules
1. **Headings are principles, in the imperative.** Fund the promise first. Match risk to purpose. Buy the payments,
   then the floor, then growth. Hold to maturity. Promise only what is owned. Name the residual risks.
2. **Decision first, then the reason, then the number.** "Treasuries currently yield about 5.15%, so we buy on receipt
   rather than wait."
3. **Certain words only for arithmetic.** "Because the floor is owned, a fall in stocks can move the contribution only
   within the range" is structural, so it is stated flatly. Market outcomes get odds ("about three of four").
4. **No adjective without a number.** Not "little margin" but "5.1% required, about 5.15% available".
5. **Name what matters.** For co-sponsors, the number that matters is the owned minimum, not the expected gift.
6. **End a key paragraph with a short sentence.** "No payment changes." "We decline that trade." "The minimum she
   quotes is one she already owns."
7. **One claim per sentence; no colons or semicolons; no metaphors in the body** (the name lives in the pitch).
8. **The team is the actor.** "We buy", "we decline", "we did not trade" (the Fed's "the Committee decided").
