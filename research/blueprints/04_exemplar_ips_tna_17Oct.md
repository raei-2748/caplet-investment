## Elevator pitch

Laura's ten operating payments must come from her portfolio alone, so Root-and-Branch grows roots first: Treasuries maturing as each payment falls due, and more for a minimum facility gift. One global stock fund is the branch, growing the gift within a range she can keep.

## Investment Policy Statement

Laura funds this portfolio with $300,000 in January 2027 and $150,000 in January 2028. Its first objective is ten $50,000 payments to her Taiwan residency, each January from 2033 to 2042, funded with high certainty from the portfolio alone. We define high certainty as each payment matched at purchase by Treasuries maturing shortly before it, with any reinvestment gap covered by her other holdings. The second objective is a facility contribution with a minimum Laura can state in 2031 and room to grow above it.

With living costs covered elsewhere and no withdrawals before 2033, her capacity for risk is high. She has taken thoughtful risks throughout her career, but the residency needs reliable operating support, so the payments carry no stock-market risk. Because her 2028 deposit depends on publishing and speaking income, the first deposit alone is planned to buy the payments.

Our roots are Treasuries. In January 2027 the first deposit buys the payments, longest-dated first, because distant payments move most with interest rates, using defined-maturity Treasury funds where no single Treasury matures in time. If falling yields push the cost above $300,000, the 2028 deposit completes the payments first. What remains buys a Treasury fund maturing in December 2032, the floor of her contribution. The rest is the branch: one global index fund of thousands of companies, assumed to return about 7% a year. The portfolio is then about two-thirds payment Treasuries, a quarter floor and under a tenth stocks: only what both promises leave. Treasuries are held to maturity, so none is sold in a falling market. The stock fund stays untouched until 2033, not shifted into bonds, since the floor already protects her gift's bottom. Each January we review with Laura and act only if a payment is no longer covered.

In 2031 Laura gives co-sponsors a range: its bottom is the floor, rounded down because a fund's payout is not fixed; its top adds half the stock fund's value that day, less any expected payment shortfall her half cannot cover. In 2033 she contributes the floor plus half the fund, capped at the top, so the gift lands inside the range unless the payments need the floor itself. In our tests, the gift reached that top in about three of four market paths. The other half stays with Laura as financial flexibility. The payment Treasuries become the operating reserve, each maturing before its payment, so the reserve runs down by 2042 without sales.

Beyond a US default, the main risk is reinvestment: if yields fell two points for good, reinvested income would leave the payments about $21,000 short. Any shortfall is covered by Laura's half first, then the gift above the floor, and only then the floor. Inflation or a stronger Taiwan dollar would raise facility costs, which her half helps absorb. Investing the floor in stocks would raise the expected gift, but we accept a smaller one for a range Laura can keep.

## Trading Notes Analysis reflections

Each note must be pasted from WInS itself (these copies are from the 14 Oct export).

### 1. VT, 10/07/2026 08:27:19 PM ET

VT spreads Laura's growth money across about 10,000 companies worldwide for a 0.06% fee, so one cheap fund diversifies it without our picking stocks. It is the branch of our Root-and-Branch plan: only money the payments and floor do not need takes market risk, under a tenth of the portfolio, untouched until 2033. We kept it small on purpose: more stocks would raise her expected gift but shrink the minimum she can quote. Half its 2031 value sets the top of her range for co-sponsors; the other half stays with her as flexibility.

### 2. IBTR, 10/07/2026 04:42:53 AM ET (the re-placed order)

IBTR holds Treasuries maturing in 2036 and funds Laura's 2037 payment. We bought the ladder longest-dated first, because distant payments move most with interest rates. Our note named the risk: if yields fell about 0.2 percentage points before January, $300,000 would no longer buy all ten payments. Within ten days yields fell nearly that far, and our model cost rose from about $292,000 to $298,000. We held, because our rule already answers this: the 2028 deposit completes the payments first. If yields keep falling, the payments stay whole and only the facility floor shrinks.

### 3. IBTM, 10/06/2026 10:01:41 PM ET

Wharton's week-2 guidance limited Laura's real plan to WInS-listed securities, ruling out the zero-coupon Treasury we had planned for her facility floor. WInS lists no individual Treasury maturing in 2032, so IBTM, a fund holding 2032 Treasuries that pays out in December 2032, became the floor itself. It also funds her first $50,000 payment, two weeks later. Because a fund's payout is not fixed, as our note says, Laura will quote co-sponsors the floor rounded down. This refinement keeps her 2031 minimum credible, and the payments still rank ahead of it.

## Why it is written this way (what to copy: the choices, not the sentences)
- **Certainty is defined, not asserted** ("paid for at purchase ... not by future market returns"), and the leftover
  risks are named with the order that covers them, ending with the floor so the payments rank first.
- **No absolutes:** no "never", "cannot", "secure", "guaranteed". The floor is "rounded down because a fund's payout
  is not fixed", which matches the IBTM trade note word for word in meaning.
- **Laura-specific:** the 2028 deposit comes from her publishing and speaking income, so the first deposit alone is
  planned to buy the payments; "thoughtful risks" is the case's phrase.
- **Shape and trade-off stated once:** two-thirds / one quarter / one tenth, and "a smaller expected gift for a range
  Laura can keep".
- **Time order:** objectives, profile, 2027-2028 and holding, 2031 and 2033, risks.
- **Evidence kept from the team's live V5 (17 Oct combined version):** the tested reinvestment risk (yields 2 points
  lower for good: about $21,000 short; rab/numbers.yaml reinvest.bookL_delivered, own yield -2pp = $479,175, MODEL),
  the 7% stock return assumption (JPM 2026 LTCMA world equities, 7.0% compound), the inflation / Taiwan dollar line,
  the WInS-scaled sentence and "a range built to hold". Fixed from V5: "lands inside the range about 99 times in 100"
  (the gift stays inside by design; 99% is the chance it lands at least halfway).
- **The IBTR reflection is the "tested" example:** the note predicted a risk and the market moved most of the way
