## Per firm

**1. Firm D (8.0)**
- Earns confidence:
  - All ten payments are bought with Treasuries in January 2027, and the firm states what "certain" means: bought at
    market prices, nominal dollars, barring a U.S. default. Case p.3: "must be funded ... with a high degree of
    certainty". Her quote D13a-N01 says letting down "people who were your earliest supporters" is worse than letting
    herself down.
  - The 2031 bottom ($150k) is already owned, so she can never promise more than she has. Case p.3: "If Laura
    promises more than she can ultimately contribute, she could damage her credibility". D13c R11.
  - Money is kept back for "project changes" (case p.3 "financial flexibility as the project develops"; D13c V10).
  - It names its own costs: less upside, a 1-in-3 chance the ladder costs more than $300k, and what happens with no
    2028 deposit (D13c V1 "names the cost first"; quote B8b-Q18; D13c R5 "honest numbers").
- Loses confidence:
  - Stocks average 7.5% of the portfolio over 2027-2032. Against her own rule, "you should always take the jump
    because you're always going to regret not doing it" (B1b-Q11), and the case's "willing to take thoughtful risks"
    with an "appropriate balance between pursuing growth and protecting the capital" (p.2), this looks one-sided.
  - When the ladder costs more than $300k, the latest payments wait for the 2028 deposit. That deposit comes from
    income she called "unstable" (B8b-Q18).
  - It earmarks $150k in 2028 as the "minimum facility contribution", five years before she has to decide (p.3: "no
    predetermined facility contribution").
  - Inflation is not mentioned, although the case asks for its "effect ... on portfolio projections and facility
    costs" (p.4).

**2. Firm A (6.5)**
- Earns confidence:
  - A plain glide path (stocks fall from 65% to 20% of the portfolio), which reads as the case's "appropriate balance"
    (p.2).
  - Treasuries maturing on the payment dates are bought in stages from 2030.
  - A 99.3% funding estimate, with the model named.
  - Broad, low-cost funds and five trades (D13c V11, V12).
- Loses confidence:
  - The payments depend on markets until 2033, and the model misses 44% of the time if the 2028 deposit never comes.
    The case says the payments must not rely on outside money (p.3), and that deposit comes from income she called
    "unstable" (B8b-Q18).
  - The firm admits its 2031 range is wide and that its bottom is less than half the median. At 80% confidence, 1 year
    in 10 ends below the promised bottom. Case p.3 on overpromising; D13a-N01.

**3. Firm E (6.0)**
- Earns confidence:
  - The simplest plan: 60/40, three funds, three trades (D13c V12).
  - The 2031 range runs from the 10th percentile to the median, so she would underpromise. The firm states both

  - It keeps 20% for flexibility, the largest share of the six firms (p.3).
- Loses confidence:
  - The bond fund holds corporate and mortgage bonds and is "not matched to the payment dates". The firm says so

  - The whole reserve is bought on one day, 1 January 2033, at whatever rates hold then.
  - With no 2028 deposit the payments miss 42.5% of the time (B8b-Q18).
  - With the top set at the median, about half of all outcomes land above the range, so the range says little about
    where her contribution will actually fall (p.3 asks for a range and a confidence that the amount falls within it).

## What Firm D must change before she would sign (SIMULATION)

1. **Keep the payments independent of the 2028 deposit.** The ten payments should not wait on the 2028 deposit
   (B8b-Q18; p.3 "may not rely on ... outside funding"). The plan should say plainly which payments go unbought if
   the ladder costs more than $300k, and what the fallback is if the deposit arrives smaller or not at all.
2. **Show she is not being made timid.** State the price of certainty in dollars next to one growth-heavier
   alternative. Consider giving the post-floor growth money a real stock share rather than 7.5% of the whole
   portfolio (B1b-Q11; p.2 "appropriate balance"; D13c V1).
3. **Stop fixing the facility gift in 2028.** Call the $150k zero-coupon bond the bought floor of the 2031 range, and
   leave the actual facility decision to 2033 (p.3 "no predetermined facility contribution"; flexibility "as the
   project develops").
4. **Name both certainty gaps.** Payments are certain in U.S. dollars, but the costs are in Taiwan and a fixed $50k
   buys less each year. Also state the inflation assumption for projections and facility costs (p.4).
5. **Use plain words.** Define "zero-coupon". Explain why the WInS portfolio copies the January 2028 plan rather than
   the 2027 one (D13c V12; p.4 says WInS is the implementation of the strategy).

## What this teaches

A client does not pick the plan with the most return or the cleverest model. She picks the one that keeps the promise
other people will rely on, that she can follow and check herself, and that names its own costs. The runner-up plans
were not bad. Each lost her confidence on one of those three points: payments that depend on markets (A, E, B), a
model she cannot check (F), or a portfolio that flatters her (C).
