## Firm by firm

### 1. Firm D (7.5)
Earns her confidence:
- The payments are bought in January 2027, and "certain" is defined by what can be checked, with its one exception
  named (U.S. default). Case L91; D13c V9 (define certainty by what is observable, not by a model percentage).
- It names its own weak spots: a 1-in-3 chance the ladder costs more than $300k, and a $12k-$31k joint-tail gap.
  This matches how she labels her own results "[unfinished]" (B9b-Q09) and names the cost before a leap (B8b-Q18).
- The 2031 bottom ($150k) is owned, not forecast, so it cannot be walked back. Case L114-116; D13a-N01.
- A $150k floor is a "meaningful personal contribution" (case L111). Growth money comes on top, and more is offered
  if markets allow. This is her festival pattern: more "as the festival grows" (B8b-Q39; D13c E3).
- Flexibility is kept, not everything goes to the building (case L102-103), and its WInS book uses four trades.

Loses her confidence:
- 7.5% average stocks over 2027-2032 for a client "willing to take thoughtful risks" who wants "an appropriate
  balance between pursuing growth and protecting the capital" (case L70-72). She tells herself "you should always
  take the jump because you're always going to regret not doing it" (B1b-Q11). The file never says where the leap
  is (D13c N1).
- Locking $118k of the 2028 deposit into the floor five years early is the main cause of the low stock share, and
  the file does not weigh it against a smaller floor or a later purchase.
- The payments still depend partly on the 2028 deposit if rates fall first. That money comes from "publishing
  advances, speaking engagements, licensing" (case L44), income she herself called "unstable" (B8b-Q18).
- The confidence statement answers the wrong question. The case asks how confident the team is that the
  contribution "will fall within that range" (case L117-118). D gives only the chance of reaching the top (73%).
- Nothing on inflation or facility costs (case L147). "Certain in nominal dollars" is not the same as the same
  residency in 2042 (D13c N4 gap ii).

## What Firm D must change before I would sign (simulation)

1. **Answer the case's confidence question.** Under D's own cap rule, the 2033 contribution always lands between
   the owned bottom and the top unless the U.S. Treasury defaults (my reading of D section 5). Say that as the
   confidence the contribution falls within the range. Keep the 73% as the chance of reaching the top. Case
   L117-118.
2. **Say where the leap is, and what certainty costs.** The residency is the leap. The payments and the floor are
   bought for the people relying on them, and the growth money takes the risk. Put the price in one line: D's
   median is about the same as A's ($207k v $206k), and the give-up sits at the far upside (D's p95 $250k). Case
   L70-72; B1b-Q11; D13c N1.
3. **Show you tested a bigger growth share.** Either keep more of the 2028 money in stocks (a smaller floor bought
   in 2028, or the floor bought in 2031, when she first speaks to co-sponsors), or show with one number why locking
   $118k in 2028 is worth it. At 7.5% stocks, "appropriate balance" (case L72) is not yet shown.
4. **Close the 2028-deposit gap with a rule.** If rates fall before January 2027 and the deposit is late or smaller,
   state what gives way first. The payments come first and the minimum contribution shrinks, in dollars. That
   income is "unstable" in her own word (B8b-Q18; case L44).
5. **Name what nominal certainty does not cover.** A fixed $50k buys less each year, and building costs are in
   Taiwan. One plain sentence, as the case asks about inflation and facility costs (case L147; D13c N4).
6. **Explain the WInS book in the Trading Notes.** It mirrors the 2028 plan (8.7% VT), not the 2027 book. One note
   must say why so little is in stocks. Every security remains PENDING WInS AVAILABILITY + POSITION-LIMIT CHECK.

## What this teaches
- A client with two risk ideas does not need one "risk level". She needs each idea to govern different money: her
  own leap can take risk, but money other people rely on should be bought, not forecast.
- Compare medians before you compare stories. Five of six plans end up near the same middle outcome. They differ
  mainly in who carries the bad cases.
- A "range with confidence" is a promise. The number that matters is the chance of landing inside it, not the chance
  of reaching the top.
