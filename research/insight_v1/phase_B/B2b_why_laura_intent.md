### Tier 1: WInS now, the Trading Notes and the tone of everything (Oct 23)

**Q1. Since Wharton replaced "shape the winning investment strategy", "compelling and clever pitch" and "would win
him/her over as a client" with "develop the investment strategy that Laura ultimately chooses" and "recommendations
that can earn her confidence", should every deliverable aim to earn trust rather than to persuade?**
- Anchor: R-C8, R-S25, R-AN5. New: 2025-26 case and old judging page (VERIFIED-PRIMARY, section 1.1).
- Hypothesis (GUESS): yes. "Chooses" survives, but the persuasion scaffolding around it was removed. Signs of trust
  that a reader can check:
  - claims are stated with their assumptions;
  - uncertainty is stated honestly;
  - each note names the risk of its own trade (Wharton's example note does this, R-T24);
  - there are no superlatives and no claims about beating other teams.
- Would change (sentence): the tone rules for TN reflections, the pitch and the IPS. Ban "best", "optimal",
  "guaranteed" and "unlike other teams". Every claim carries its basis. The North Star is restated as "the plan she can
  trust".
- Deliverables: TN, IPS, FR. Domain: D7. Seed: 1.
- North Star: Laura does not choose the most persuasive firm. She chooses the one whose claims hold up.

**Q2. What will a semifinal reader look for FIRST, given that Wharton's Guide never says "choose" or "win" and asks
teams to explain "why your strategy is appropriate for the client"?**
- Anchor: R-new (Guide p.5, "explaining why your strategy is appropriate for the client"; "What Are We Looking For?"),
  R-I17, R-T9.
- Hypothesis (GUESS): a reader first checks whether the team has found Laura's binding constraint. Here that is a
  fixed, nominal promise that no one else may fund. The reader then checks whether each decision follows from it. In
  the TN that means the first line of each reflection names which of her needs the trade serves. In the IPS it means
  the pitch's first clause names the promise.
- Would change (decision): the order of content in each TN reflection and in the 50-word pitch, with the constraint
  first and the instrument second.
- Deliverables: TN, IPS. Domain: D7. Seed: 1.
- North Star: a firm that names her real constraint in its first sentence shows it listened.

**Q3. Wharton's recent pull quotes state each season's value theme ("impact" in 2024-25; "sustainability ... strong
partnerships" in 2025-26). Does "The only person who needs to believe in something is yourself" signal self-reliance,
meaning that the part only Laura must believe in (the ten payments) is funded by her alone and the part others join
(the facility) is where co-sponsors come in?**
- Anchor: R-C21, R-AN30, R-C48 ("Teams may not rely on co-sponsors..."), R-C44. New: the 2024-25 and 2025-26 pull
  quotes (VERIFIED-PRIMARY).
- Hypothesis (GUESS): yes. It turns the tension in R-AN30 into a design:
  - private conviction = the promise she funds herself, locked;
  - public proof = a range others can rely on.
  - In the TN this reads as "we secure what only Laura can promise".
- Would change (sentence): the IPS central idea (prompt 1, R-I9) and the order of the fundraising draft. Check the
  quote's attribution first (Q4).
- Deliverables: TN, IPS, FR. Domain: D7. Seed: 15.
- North Star: a plan built on her own stated principle, that the promise rests on her alone, is one she would see as
  hers.

**Q4. Until the pull quote is traced to a primary source, how may our deliverables use it, given that Wharton's past

- Would change (decision): add a one-line cost and fee disclosure to the FR projections, with a Laura-specific reason,

- Deliverables: TN, IPS, FR. Domain: D8. Seed: 18.

## What this teaches

1. **Compare this year's words with last year's.** One word can carry the whole season's intent: "shape the
   *winning* strategy" became "develop the strategy", and "win him/her over" became "earn her confidence". You only see
   it by putting the two versions side by side. The old versions were still on Wharton's own server, and the site's
   public data feed showed them.
2. **Separate house style from signal.** "[Client] believes ..." opens every recent case, so it is template, not a
   clue. A risk sentence that appears for the first time in nine seasons is a clue.
3. **Date your evidence.** The case numbers were fixed when rates were lower. A strategy that only works at today's
   rates is lucky, not robust, and a careful client (or judge) will notice.
4. **Use a real person's record with restraint.** Her public professional facts (trading and regulator internships,
   teaching high schoolers about money, designing a case competition) explain how she will read our work. Her private
   life does not belong in an investment plan.
