## 0. Summary (read this first)

1. **The case's pull quote is not verified as Laura's words.** The line is "The only person who needs to believe in
   something is yourself." (R-C21). I looked for it on 14 primary pages where she speaks (her site, 5 PDFs and pages
   on it, 9 interviews and features). I also searched all 195 pages cached by this run and ran two web searches. The
   phrase was not found anywhere. The nearest verified idea is from Wharton Magazine (2022): "it’s not for anyone else
   to judge". Until D13 finds a source, treat the line as Wharton's sidebar copy. Do not present it as "Laura said".
2. **Her biggest career risk was taken floor-first.** She named the "unstable income" of self-employment herself
   (2021). Input reported (2022) "So when she got the book deal with HarperCollins, she gave Twitter her notice." She
   had also planned to "teach art part-time". So her "thoughtful risks" (R-C37) were jumps made **after** a contract
   secured the downside. This mirrors lock-early (promise bought first, risk only with the surplus). It is the single
   strongest "she would recognise this as hers" link I found. That sentence is interpretation; the facts are
   VERIFIED-PRIMARY.
3. **"No more gimmicks."** (Nerd Daily, 2025.) She scrapped three drafts of her second book because she "lost the heart
   of my writing—authentic, personal connections". This is her own test for inauthenticity. It supports the design
   principle directly: themed branding, book puns and extra model layers are exactly what she discards.
4. **She writes a purpose statement for her work.** Pulse Spikes (2021): "I can’t think of many art pieces that I
   didn’t also write some kind of purpose statement for." An IPS is a purpose statement. A trading note that opens with
   the holding's job fits how she already thinks.
5. **Her books ran late, and she paused writing in 2025.** Messy Roots was announced for "summer 2021" and published 8
   March 2022. Kirby was hoped for "a 2024 release" and came out by March 2025. In March 2025 she said "I’m taking a
   short break from writing". I found no public announcement of a new book deal as of 2026-09-27; absence of evidence
   is not evidence. The case says the $150k "will" come from publishing advances and other sources in January 2028
   (R-C27). This gives a Laura-specific size for the "late deposit" stress case (BS-13): about 9-12 months. The case
   base stays unchanged.
6. **She has already built a community institution with partners, with free access.** Pride in Panels is "free for
   exhibitors and attendees", run with the San Francisco Public Library, and gave "mini grants to 20 comic creators".
   Her stated rule: "Our No. 1 priority is to get as many diverse voices and stories in the room as possible, no
   matter the grounds that they start off on". Her speaking guide offers "Pro-bono visits". Implication for the
   case's list of outside sources (R-C44): the residency's "program fees" may be deliberately low. More of the
   extra operating support would then fall on co-sponsors, which argues for keeping flexibility.
7. **Her institutions start small and scale after proof.** On The Sign.al: "Only after we realized EVERY student
   deserved to hear their words of wisdom did we decide to build something". On the festival: "All of it is way
   beyond our initial passion project." Her advice: "Always start small". This suggests a staged facility
   contribution (a firm floor now, more "as the project develops") rather than one lump. D5 to test.
8. **What she would find inauthentic** (from her own words and choices): polish over substance ("I don’t need to be
   perfect to be impactful"), gimmicks, numbers without the human behind them ("it’s just a number"), pressure to
   conform to an "ideal Whartonite" (P&Q, snippet-level), and borrowing her identity-context advice for finance.
   One verified quote maps perfectly onto "buy the floor before the 2031 reveal", but it is coming-out advice. Using
   it as a finance metaphor is the tokenism trap. Echo her **career** sequencing instead.

## What this teaches

A client's own words are data. Read them the way you would read a fact sheet: exactly, with a date, and with a check
of who is actually speaking (her, a reporter, or a publisher). Three lessons from this lens:

1. **Behaviour beats adjectives.** The case says she takes "thoughtful risks". Her record shows what that means: she
   jumped only after a contract secured the downside. Behaviour tells you how to design a plan; adjectives do not.
2. **Verify before you quote.** The most quotable line in the case could not be traced to her, and a misattributed
   quote would hurt us with exactly this client.
3. **Understanding someone is not borrowing their identity.** The best-fitting quote for our 2031 idea is personal
   advice about coming out. Using it would be tokenism, so we leave it out and use her career story instead.
