Plain-English terms used below
- **Human capital:** the value of a person's future earnings. Laura's books, talks and licences are her human capital.
- **Wrong-way risk:** two bad things tending to happen together. Here: her 2028 deposit shrinking in the same bad
  market that shrinks the portfolio.
- **Correlation (rho, ρ):** a number from -1 to +1 saying how closely two things move together. 0 means unrelated.
- **Advance:** money a publisher pays an author before a book earns royalties, usually in instalments.
- **Idiosyncratic risk:** risk specific to one person or book (a deal comes late) and not to the whole market.
- **Growth sleeve:** the part of the portfolio left after the Treasury ladder is bought; the only part that takes
  market risk.

## What this teaches

A client's income is an asset too, and it has its own risks. Before assuming "when markets crash, her income crashes",
look at the evidence for each income stream.
- Books held up in 2009.
- Graphic novels grew through 2020.
- The recent shocks to her markets came from politics, not the stock market.

The biggest lesson is structural. If the promise is paid for with money she already has, uncertainty about money she
has not earned yet can only change the size of the gift, never the safety of the promise. And when you describe a
real person, precision is respect: "finalist" is not "winner", and "closing" is not "acquired".
