## 0. Summary (read this first)

Twenty-three questions follow (section 2). The eight that I think most teams will miss:

1. **The equity "premium" over Laura's own Treasury ladder is thin in 2026.** The S&P 500's forward earnings yield
   (1/19.1 = 5.24%) is about equal to the 10-year Treasury (5.17%). The CAPE earnings yield (1/41.48 = 2.41%) is
   below the 10-year TIPS real yield (2.83%): a negative "excess CAPE yield" of about -0.4pp. JPM's 6.70% for U.S.
   large caps is only 1.2-1.6pp above the ladder's forward zero rates (5.08-5.48%). "Thoughtful risk" in 2026 means
   taking equity risk for a small expected reward, and the team should price that out loud (Q1, Q2).
2. **A "U.S.-only" index is not Taiwan-free.** About 20% of the S&P 500 by weight is in companies whose leading chips
   are made mainly by TSMC (NVDA 8.18, AAPL 7.37, AVGO 2.51, AMD 1.55, MRVL 0.34, QCOM 0.31; SPYM holdings 2026-09-24;
   the supplier link is an ASSUMPTION to verify). Add VT's direct 3.5% Taiwan weight. Laura's portfolio, her residency
   and much of the world index all depend on one island (Q3, Q4).
3. **The Taiwan dollar tends to move in Laura's favour in bad times and against her in good times.** USD/TWD rose
   (TWD weaker) by 7.2% in the 1995-96 Strait crisis, 6.4% in Aug-Sep 2022 and 14.5% in 2008-09. In calm boom times
   it can jump the other way: -6.7% in two trading days (1-5 May 2025) and -13.0% from 1 Apr to 3 Jul 2025, which
   raised the USD cost of a NT$ budget by 15%. Monthly USD/TWD changes correlate -0.38 with S&P 500 returns
   (2016-2026; FRED, computed). So the facility contribution may be **less** uncertain in NT$ than in US$ (Q6, Q7).
4. **The TWD looks "held weak".** Taiwan's current-account surplus was 19.6% of GDP in 2025 and the U.S. Treasury keeps
   Taiwan on its monitoring list; life insurers have cut currency hedges and the central bank leaned against
   appreciation (SECONDARY-READ). The jump risk is skewed toward a stronger TWD (Q8).
5. **The wrong inflation index is the default.** The $50k is spent in Taiwan, mostly on people. Taiwan's minimum wage
   rises 4.745% on 2027-01-01 (11th straight rise) and regular earnings grew 3.18% y/y in June 2026, versus Taiwan CPI
   ~2%. At 3.5% a year the 2042 payment is worth about $28.8k in 2026 money; at Taiwan CPI 2.07%, about $36.0k
   (ASSUMPTION rates). U.S. CPI (JPM 2.5%) is neither (Q9, Q18).
6. **The September 2026 cheap ladder is partly an AI-boom side effect.** Hyperscaler capex is heading past $690bn
   in FY26, now partly debt-financed (debt = 32% of capex, LTM mid-2026; FactSet); the FOMC says "capital investment
   is robust"; the 10-year term premium hit 0.97% on 2026-09-16, the highest since 2011-02-11 (FRED). An AI bust before
   January 2027 is a growth shock: yields and stocks would likely fall together, making the ladder dearer exactly when
   the sleeve shrinks (Q10).
7. **The stock-bond relationship has flipped.** Daily correlation of S&P 500 returns with 10-year yield changes:
   +0.41 in 2016-2020 (bonds hedged stocks), -0.44 in 2026 (stocks fall when yields rise, so bonds and stocks fall
   together). JPM's long-run -0.01 sits in between. Wharton's own sample note justifies a Treasury ETF "to reduce
   portfolio volatility" - the weakest reason in this regime; matching the liability is the strong one (Q11, Q19).
8. **An EM tilt is a double bet on the Strait.** Taiwan is now 26.85% of MSCI EM (TSMC alone more than 14%), China is
   also a large weight, and JPM's highest equity return (EM 7.8%) comes with that concentration. Russia 2022 shows
   what "uninvestable" means for an index: MSCI removed it "at a price that is effectively zero" (Q14).

## What this teaches
Macro facts matter only when they reach the client's numbers. In 2026 the same story (AI) explains three things at
once:
- why U.S. stocks are concentrated;
- part of why U.S. bond yields are high (heavy borrowing for AI and a higher term premium);
- why Taiwan is booming while its currency is held down.

For Laura, those three facts meet in one place: her payments are safest in U.S. Treasuries precisely because
everything else she has (her income, her stocks, her Taiwan project) is tied to the same cycle. A good question does
not ask "what will markets do?". It asks "when this moves, which of her numbers moves with it, and in which
direction?".
